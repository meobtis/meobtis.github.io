The *Viśeṣastava* is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) addressed to the Buddha. The digital text runs to seventy-eight numbered verses and opens with the salutation "namo buddhāya" and a verse in which the speaker declares that he has abandoned other teachers and taken refuge in the Buddha, asking what reason there could be other than that the Buddha is faultless and full of good qualities.[^dsbc939] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the hymns to Śākyamuni.[^dsbc939]

In the verses it transmits, the hymn sets the Buddha against rival divinities: it observes that the qualities extolled by devotees of gods such as Vāsudeva or Īśvara appear as faults in the Buddha's teaching, and it contrasts Rudra's burning of the three cities with the Buddha's burning of anger itself with the great arrow of knowledge.[^dsbc939] It closes with the speaker dedicating the merit of the praise so that the whole world may be devoted to awakening, and a colophon records the end of the work.[^dsbc939]

Little reliable information on the work's authorship, date or transmission could be verified. The 84000 catalogue lists a Tibetan-canon text at Toh 1109 titled "Praise of Exaltation" in the eulogy section, which corresponds in title to *Viśeṣa-stava*, but the page gives no author or summary and the identification has not been checked against a scholarly source here.[^84000toh1109] The edition records give no printed source either, so the history of the Sanskrit text, any manuscripts, and its relation to the Tibetan version remain undocumented in the sources consulted.[^dsbc939]
