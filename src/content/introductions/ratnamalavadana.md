The *Ratnamālāvadāna* is a Sanskrit collection of *avadāna* (edifying narrative) tales told in verse. Takahata's edition subtitles it "A Garland of Precious Gems or a Collection of Edifying Tales, Told in a Metrical Form, Belonging to the Mahāyāna".[^gretil-rm] The work opens with King Aśoka bowing to the monk Upagupta and asking him to teach further, and the elder then relates the stories; the edition numbers them in Roman numerals, with titles such as *Kauśīdyavīryotsāhanāvadāna*, *Snātāvadāna* and *Cakrāvadāna*, running to at least thirty-eight items.[^gretil-rm] Encyclopaedic overviews of the avadāna literature list it among the popular avadānas and describe it as a collection of stories about travelling merchants.[^wiki-avadana]

Little reliable information about the work's date, authorship, translations or manuscripts could be verified in the sources consulted here. A doctoral thesis presents an annotated English translation with a critical introduction,[^chandima2015] but only its catalogue record, which gives no abstract, could be read, so no claims are made from its contents.
