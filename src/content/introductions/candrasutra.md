The Candrasūtra ("Discourse on the Moon") is a short sūtra in Sanskrit, known from manuscript fragments that Klaus Wille published in 2008 under the title "Neue Fragmente des Candrasūtra" ("New fragments of the Candrasūtra").[^gretil-candra] The text opens with the usual formula "Thus have I heard" and places the Buddha at Śrāvastī, in Anāthapiṇḍada's park in the Jeta Grove. Rāhu, lord of the asuras, covers the whole orb of the moon; the deity who dwells in the moon, frightened, goes to the Buddha, bows, and speaks a verse taking refuge in him and asking that Rāhu release the moon. The Buddha answers with a verse addressed to Rāhu, who lets the moon go, and a further verse is then spoken by the asura Bali Vairocana.[^gretil-candra]

This story corresponds in outline to the Pali Candima Sutta (Saṃyutta Nikāya 2.9), in which the moon deity Candima is seized by Rāhu, calls the Buddha to mind, and is freed when the Buddha speaks to Rāhu; that discourse is also used as a protective (*paritta*) text in the Theravāda tradition.[^ati-sn29] Two Chinese *Saṃyukta-āgama* versions correspond to it: sūtra 583 in the Taishō edition of Guṇabhadra's translation (T 99) and sūtra 167 in the second, "alternative" translation (T 100); the text is also quoted in the *Mahāprajñāpāramitāśāstra* (T 1509).[^84000toh331] Waldschmidt's earlier edition of the Sanskrit rests on four fragments from Xinjiang now in Berlin, to which Wille added five more, two from Turfan and three from the Pelliot collection.[^84000toh331] The Tibetan translation was made from Sanskrit, probably by the early ninth century, since the text appears in the Denkarma catalogue; this is the second of two Tibetan texts titled *Candrasūtra* (Toh 331).[^84000toh331] Date and school affiliation of the Sanskrit are not stated in the sources consulted.
