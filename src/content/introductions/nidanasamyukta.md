The *Nidānasaṃyukta* is the name given to a collection of discourses on causal conditioning (*nidāna*) in Sanskrit, belonging to the "connected discourses" (*saṃyukta*) of an early Buddhist Āgama collection. The digital text contains the first twenty-five sūtras, as edited from fragmentary manuscripts by Chandrabhāl Tripāṭhī in 1962.[^gretil-nidsa][^wiki-samyukta] Each sūtra is numbered and given a short title, for example the first, "vṛkṣa I" (Tree), as in the edition's headings.[^gretil-nidsa]

The opening sūtras present the twelvefold chain of dependent origination (*tṛṣṇā* leading to *upādāna*, *bhava*, *jāti*, and finally ageing and death) and its cessation. They use similes such as a young tree that grows when it is tended and withers when it is cut at the root.[^gretil-nidsa]

The sources consulted for this introduction give little detail on the manuscript's date, provenance or school affiliation. The edition belongs to the series of Sanskrit texts from the Turfan finds, and the Sanskrit fragments are generally studied as parallels to the Chinese Saṃyuktāgama.[^wiki-samyukta] Tripāṭhī's edition has been described as the earlier and less reliable one, and a new edition of the first twenty-five sūtras by Jin-il Chung and Takamichi Fukita appeared in 2020.[^wiki-samyukta] Little else could be verified, so readers should consult those editions for the manuscript evidence.
