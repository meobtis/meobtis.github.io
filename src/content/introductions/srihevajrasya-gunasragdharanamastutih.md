The *Śrīhevajrasya Guṇasragdharanāmastutiḥ* is a short Sanskrit hymn in praise of the blessed Hevajra, a wrathful tantric deity (Heruka) of the Yoginī tantra class. The text consists of 34 verses in elaborate metres. The opening verses declare the inadequacy of any being to praise the deity fully, and the hymn goes on to extol his fearsome form as an expression of the union of wisdom and means (*prajñā* and *upāya*), his power over proud gods, and his ability to cut through the faults of beings and lead them from saṃsāra to liberation. It closes with a dedication of the merit of composing the praise to the attainment of Heruka's state for the benefit of beings.[^dsbc926]

The colophon calls the work the composition of the Mahāpaṇḍita Śūnyasamādhivajra and the heading names it as the praise "garland of qualities" of Hevajra.[^dsbc926] No further information about the author, date or place of composition, or about Tibetan or Chinese translations and manuscripts, could be verified for this entry, and no established English title was found. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among its commentaries on Yoginī tantras (*yoginītantraṭīkā*), although the text itself is a devotional hymn (*stuti*) rather than a commentary in the strict sense.[^dsbc926]
