This is a Sanskrit commentary (*ṭīkā*) by Candrakīrti on the *Catuḥśataka* (Four Hundred Verses) of Āryadeva, the work indexed here under the title *Bodhisattvayogācāracatuḥśatakaṭīkā*.[^gretil-catuhsataka] The *Catuḥśataka* has sixteen chapters: the first eight are usually described as preparing the practitioner for the path, and the last eight as demonstrating the insubstantiality of phenomena. Only fragments of the Sanskrit verses survive, and a complete commentary by Candrakīrti is preserved only in Tibetan.[^wikipedia-aryadeva] The digital text accordingly reproduces the surviving Sanskrit portions of the commentary, beginning with chapter I, titled in the text *Nityaviparyāsaprahāṇopāyasandarśana*, in which Candrakīrti glosses verses on the impermanence of conditioned things and on the pain that follows from attachment to what is pleasant.[^gretil-catuhsataka]

Little further could be established from sources consulted for this introduction. A 1931 edition by Vidhushekhara Bhattacharya, published by Visva-Bharati in Calcutta, offers the Sanskrit and Tibetan texts of the *Catuḥśataka* with extracts from Candrakīrti's commentary, and is one of the printed sources for the surviving Sanskrit.[^bhattacharya1931] Which printed edition the GRETIL text follows is not stated in its front matter, so it should be checked against the text's own page markers before being cited.
