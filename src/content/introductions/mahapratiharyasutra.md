The *Mahāprātihāryasūtra* ("Sūtra of the Great Miracle") is a short Sanskrit text of the sūtra (āgama) class, preserved in a state that the editors' angle brackets and parentheses mark as incomplete or conjecturally restored. In the digital edition it consists of a single chapter of roughly 380 lines.[^dsbc967] No established English title was found in the sources consulted.

The text opens with the Buddha staying at the Veṇuvana in Rājagṛha, honoured by kings, brahmins and householders and well supplied with the requisites, while the rival teachers (*tīrthika*) receive no such honour. Māra then decides to trouble the Buddha by troubling these teachers, and the text names six teachers, beginning with Pūraṇa and Maskarin Gośālīputra, who claim omniscience. The teachers resolve to match the Buddha's superhuman displays of miraculous power (*uttaramanuṣyadharma-ṛddhiprātihārya*), doubling or tripling whatever he shows. Later passages involve King Prasenajit of Kosala, a pavilion built for the display, and a request that the Buddha show such a miracle to humble the rival teachers and delight gods and humans.[^dsbc967] This summary rests on a reading of the Sanskrit text itself; no scholarly study of this particular work was available to the author of this note, and little about its date, provenance, manuscript source or translations is documented in the sources consulted.
