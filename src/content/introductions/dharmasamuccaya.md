The *Dharmasamuccaya* is a Sanskrit anthology of verses on Buddhist moral and religious practice. The opening of the text names it as put together by the monk (*bhikṣu*) Avalokitasiṃha, and its introductory verses state that the stanzas are drawn from the vast *Saddharmasmṛtyupasthānasūtra*.[^gretil-dhs] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon edition divides the work into 36 sections (*varga*), beginning with the "Jitavarga" and ending with a section of praise (*stutivarga*).[^dsbc231]

The opening verses list themes such as heedfulness, faith, effort and steadfastness as the six bases of liberation, and generosity, ethical conduct, restraint, patience and the four immeasurables among the causes of attaining peace, while contrasting them with the causes that lead to hell.[^gretil-dhs] The collection thus functions as a compendium of edifying verse arranged by topic rather than a systematic philosophical treatise.

Little reliable information on the compiler's date and place or on the manuscript tradition could be gathered for this introduction, and none is asserted here. The standard critical edition and French translation is by Lin Li-kuang, *Dharma-samuccaya, compendium de la loi: recueil de stances extraites du Saddharma-smṛty-upasthāna-sūtra*, published in several volumes from 1946 in the Annales du Musée Guimet series; one volume covers chapters XIII to XXXVI.[^lin1946]
