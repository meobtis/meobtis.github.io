The *Nairātmyaparipṛcchā* ("Questions on Selflessness") is a short Mahāyāna sūtra in Sanskrit. Its edition by P. L. Vaidya appears in the first part of the *Mahāyāna-sūtra-saṃgrahaḥ*, a collection of Mahāyāna sūtras, on pages 174 and following.[^gretil-nairatmya] The text is cast as a dialogue in which non-Buddhist questioners (*tīrthika*) ask the Mahāyāna speakers how the body can be without a supreme self (*paramātman*) when it nevertheless exhibits laughter, weeping, anger and pride.[^gretil-nairatmya]

In the Sanskrit text, the Mahāyāna speakers reply that one can say neither that a supreme self exists nor that it does not, and compare the phenomena in question to a dream, an illusion and a magical display. They then set out two levels, conventional (*saṃvṛti*) and ultimate (*paramārtha*), and present verses on them.[^gretil-nairatmya] Beyond the edition records, little reliable information on the work's date, authorship, Tibetan or Chinese translations, or manuscript sources could be found in the sources consulted, so none is asserted here.
