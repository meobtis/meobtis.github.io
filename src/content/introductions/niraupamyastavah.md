The *Niraupamyastava* ("Hymn to the Peerless One") is a short Sanskrit stotra in verse addressed to the Buddha. The text in this index opens by saluting the one who teaches that things lack intrinsic nature (*niḥsvabhāva*) and who acts for the benefit of a world that has fallen into wrong views, and its verses go on to praise the Buddha's vision of reality, his understanding of the profound nature of things, and his attainment through seeing sameness, without a duality between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa.[^dsbc155]

The hymn is classed here with the Madhyamaka stotras. It is generally listed as one of the hymns of the *Catuḥstava* ("Four Hymns") attributed to Nāgārjuna, alongside the *Lokātītastava*, *Acintyastava* and *Paramārthastava*.[^wiki-nagarjuna] The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes that scholars debate which hymns make up the "four hymns" of the commentarial literature, and that the hymns, unlike Nāgārjuna's argumentative treatises, present ultimate truth in a more positive way.[^westerhoff] Little further detail about the manuscript transmission, the Tibetan and Chinese translations or the date of this particular hymn could be verified in the sources consulted, so those matters are left open here.
