The *Śrīghanācārasaṃgraha* ("Compendium of the conduct of Ghana") is a short Sanskrit work in verse on the rules of behaviour for a Buddhist novice (*śrāmaṇera*). The text as now available is divided into four chapters: homage to the Buddha (*buddhanamaskāra*), the status of the novice (*śrāmaṇerabhāva*), the means of becoming a novice (*śrāmaṇerabhāvopāya*), which is the longest chapter and is arranged under ten numbered training rules, and further observances (*anye cārāḥ*). It opens with a homage to the Buddha and an announcement that the conduct of novices will be compiled, and it lists the five precepts together with the further abstentions that make up the ten novice rules.[^gretil-ghanacara]

The work is not preserved as an independent Sanskrit manuscript in the form read here. The digital text is a reconstruction by Sanghasena Singh, made from the Sanskrit of Jayarakṣita's commentary, the *Śrīghanācāraṭīkā*, which is catalogued separately in the same collection; the reconstruction was discussed in his 1992 article on the restoration of the work.[^gretil-ghanacara] I could not find reliable published information on the author, date, or Tibetan and Chinese translations of the work, and these are therefore not stated here.
