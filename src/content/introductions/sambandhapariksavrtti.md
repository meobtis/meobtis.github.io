The *Sambandhaparīkṣāvṛtti* is the prose commentary on the *Sambandhaparīkṣā* ("Analysis of Relations"), a short work of the Buddhist epistemologist Dharmakīrti. Modern surveys count the *Sambandhaparīkṣā* among his seven works and, with the *Saṃtānāntarasiddhi*, as one of two "minor" ones that are harder to place in the sequence of his writings than the major treatises.[^tillemans2026] The text in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon opens with the first verse of the treatise, followed by a gloss, and is a single-chapter work that proceeds verse by verse.[^dsbc921]

The first verse states the thesis: a relation is a dependence on something else (*pāratantrya*), yet if the related terms are already established there is nothing left for them to depend on, so no real relation exists among things.[^dsbc921] Tillemans notes that Dharmakīrti's arguments against inherence (*samāvāya*), the Nyāya relation tying universals to particulars, turn in part on the unreality of relations.[^tillemans2026] Frauwallner published an edition and German translation of the *Sambandhaparīkṣā* in 1934.[^tillemans2026]

Little else about this commentary could be verified. The sources consulted do not say who wrote the *vṛtti*, nor when, and the question of authorship and its relation to Dharmakīrti's verses should be checked in specialist literature before being relied on.
