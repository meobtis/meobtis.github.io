The *Aśokāvadāna* ("Narrative of Aśoka"), known in English as *The Legend of King Aśoka*, is a Sanskrit Buddhist avadāna describing the birth and reign of the Mauryan emperor Aśoka as a devoted Buddhist ruler. In Sanskrit it survives as part of the *Divyāvadāna*, an anthology of Buddhist narratives.[^wiki-ashokavadana] John S. Strong's *The Legend of King Aśoka: A Study and Translation of the Aśokāvadāna* is the standard English-language study and translation.[^strong1983]

The narrative moves between several themes. It includes the life of the monk Upagupta, later Aśoka's spiritual guide; Aśoka's previous birth as the child Jaya, who offered the Buddha a handful of dirt; his cruel early career and conversion; his building of 84,000 stūpas and his pilgrimages; the blinding of his son Kuṇāla; and the final gifts of his reign, restricted by his ministers, ending with half a myrobalan fruit. The cycle closes with the persecution of Buddhists by Puṣyamitra, whom the text wrongly presents as Aśoka's descendant.[^wiki-ashokavadana] The text itself opens by announcing that it will relate a pious tale about the elder Upagupta.[^gretil-asoka]

The work is probably by Buddhist monks of the Mathura region, judging by its praise of that city and its monasteries. Proposed dates range widely, from the fifth to the sixteenth century CE for the extant form, though an earliest finished form may go back to the second century CE.[^wiki-ashokavadana] Two early Chinese translations, the *A-yu wang chuan* (阿育王傳; attributed to An Faqin, about 300 CE) and the *A-yu wang ching* (阿育王經; attributed to Sanghapāla, about 512 CE), suggest that the legend once circulated independently of the *Divyāvadāna*. Jean Przyluski translated the first into French in 1923.[^wiki-ashokavadana] Strong's translation was published by Princeton University Press in 1983, and was reissued in the Princeton Legacy Library.[^nii-strong]

Little more could be verified from the sources consulted here; Tibetan versions and details of the manuscript tradition are not covered.
