The *Yuktiṣaṣṭikārikā* ("Verses of Sixty Reasonings") is a short Madhyamaka treatise in verse, known in English as the *Sixty Verses on Reasoning*. In the Tibetan canon it is catalogued as Toh 3825 in the Middle Way section of the Tengyur.[^84000toh3825] The Sanskrit text transmitted here opens with homage to the "chief of sages" as the teacher of dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*), and its first verses speak of a view that is free of both existence and non-existence.[^dsbc255]

The work is traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna, and modern scholars such as Christian Lindtner and David Burton are reported to count it among the works most securely his, a group usually called his "reasoning" (*yukti*) corpus, which defends emptiness against substantialist views.[^wikinagarjuna]

Beyond this, little reliable information about the text, its date, translations, manuscripts and printed editions could be verified in the sources consulted, and the account above should be read as provisional.
