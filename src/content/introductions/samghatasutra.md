The Saṃghāṭa Sūtra (*Āryasaṃghāṭasūtra*) is a Mahāyāna scripture that opens in the usual way, with the Buddha at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak (Gṛdhrakūṭa) among a vast assembly of monks, bodhisattvas, gods and nāgas, after which the bodhisattva Sarvaśūra rises to ask the Buddha for a teaching.[^gretil-sa] One summary reports that the text circulated widely in north-western India and Central Asia, and that scholars have often found its contents confused or cryptic; the meaning of its title is debated, and in the text the Buddha is said to call it a treasury of Dharma.[^wiki-sanghata]

According to the same summary, the sūtra survives in several languages: Sanskrit manuscripts from Gilgit, Khotan and other Silk Road sites; Khotanese, the oldest vernacular translation; fragments in Sogdian; two Chinese versions (Taishō 423 by Upaśūnya and Taishō 424 by Dānapāla); and a ninth-century Tibetan translation.[^wiki-sanghata] This account comes from a general reference article, and the dates and attributions have not been checked here against specialist sources, so they should be treated with caution. Little further reliable information on the text's authorship, date and teachings could be gathered for this introduction.

The two modern Sanskrit editions reflected in the digital texts are those of Oskar von Hinüber and of Giotto Canevascini, whose 1993 edition of the Khotanese version also gives the Sanskrit.[^gretil-alt][^wiki-sanghata]
