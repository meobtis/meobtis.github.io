The transcription records a manuscript of *caryāgīti* ("songs of practice"), the repertoire of ritual songs that the Newar Vajrācārya priests of the Kathmandu Valley sing, and in part dance, in tantric worship; in Nepal Bhasa the songs are called *cacā*.[^shrestha2016][^kitada2021] The text is in mixed language. Its verses are in Sanskritised and vernacular forms, set out with headings that name a *rāga* (for example Bhairavī, Madhumat or Lalita) and sometimes a *tāla*, and with a recurring refrain marked *dhruva*; between the songs, directions in Newar prescribe the order of the rite, such as worship of the guru, the offering of fees and the vajra consecration.[^archive88] The scan is dated by Commons to the eighteenth century.[^commons88]

The songs invoke the deities of the Newar tantric ritual: the dākas and ḍākinīs, the goddess Vajravārāhī, the mothers (*mātṛkā*) and the goddesses of the charnel ground placed in the directions, and Bhairava; the last page of the transcription ends with a mantra.[^archive88] A Newar scholar of the tradition describes the corpus as having a "secret" (*guhya*) part and a public (*bāhya*) part, and calls it an essence of Vajrayāna doctrine in song and dance.[^shrestha2025] Its language, he writes, is prominently Sanskrit with so-called vernacular words and "twilight language" (*sandhyābhāṣā*).[^shrestha2016]

This Newar tradition is related to, but should not be confused with, the Old Bengali *Caryāpada* or *Caryāgīti*, the fifty songs of the *Caryācaryaviniścaya* that Haraprasād Śāstrī found in the Darbar Library in Kathmandu in 1907 and published in 1916; Kvaerne's 1977 study dates them to about the eleventh century or earlier.[^shrestha2016] The same author notes that only one song of that collection, *trihaṇḍā*, belongs to the Vajrācārya ritual practice, and that the two bodies of songs differ in language even though they share doctrinal concepts.[^shrestha2016] Another study, of one Newar song, argues that the *cacā* songs have links with Bengali through old Bengali words.[^kitada2021]

The manuscript's identity within the printed Newar collections (such as the compilation *Cacā Munā* published in 1999) could not be checked, and which song cycle it preserves, who compiled it, and when, are not stated in the sources consulted.[^shrestha2016]
