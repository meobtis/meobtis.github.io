The *Pañcarakṣādevīstotrāṇi* ("hymns to the five protective goddesses") is a short collection of five Sanskrit stotras. In the digital edition they are numbered and titled as hymns to Mahāpratisarā, Mahāmantrānusāriṇī, Mahāmāyūrī, Mahāśītavatī and Mahāsāhasrapramardinī, and each is introduced by a salutation and closed with a colophon naming the goddess as a "rakṣādevī", a protective goddess.[^dsbc168] The first hymn consists of five verses, each ending with a refrain of homage to the goddess, and the others are of similar brevity.[^dsbc168]

The names correspond to the five goddesses of the Pañcarakṣā group of protective texts, which are better known as dhāraṇī and *vidyā* scrolls than as hymns. The stotras themselves are devotional and apotropaic: the first, for instance, praises Mahāpratisarā as a bringer of victory in battle and a remover of fear of weapons and of obstacles, recalling stories of Indra, King Brahmadatta and merchants returning from the sea.[^dsbc168]

Little reliable information on the authorship, date or transmission of these five hymns could be consulted for this entry. The editions record only that the text derives from Pandey's 1994 anthology of Buddhist stotras; no earlier manuscript source is named in the front matter, and no established English title is known to the compiler.[^dsbc168]
