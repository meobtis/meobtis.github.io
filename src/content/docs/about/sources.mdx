MEOBTIS indexes the following collections. Each one has been converted to Markdown in
its own repository. **{both}** texts are available from more than one source; they are
listed once in the index, with a link to each edition.

## GRETIL

The [Göttingen Register of Electronic Texts in Indian Languages](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil.html)
(GRETIL) is maintained by the Göttingen State and University Library (SUB Göttingen). It
offers standardised, machine-readable texts in Sanskrit and other Indian languages
contributed by many scholars and projects. Most texts are encoded in TEI XML with full
bibliographic headers.

The Markdown repository has **all {src.gretil.editions + src.gretil.skipped} Sanskrit texts** that GRETIL serves, in folders that follow
GRETIL's own sections, each marked as Buddhist or not. MEOBTIS lists the texts in GRETIL's two Buddhist sections, **Buddhist
religious literature** (sūtras, Vinaya, avadānas, tantras, stotras) and **Buddhist philosophy** (Abhidharma, Madhyamaka,
Yogācāra, Pramāṇa), and does not list the other {src.gretil.skipped}: the Veda, epics, Purāṇas, poetry, other philosophy,
law, medicine and so on. GRETIL files a few Buddhist works under poetry, and MEOBTIS lists these too: Aśvaghoṣa's
*Buddhacarita* and *Saundarananda* (two editions each) and the Buddhist dramatic fragments edited by Klaus Wille.

- **{src.gretil.editions}** Markdown editions of **{src.gretil.works}** texts
- Markdown repository: [meobtis/gretil](https://github.com/meobtis/gretil)
- Licence: given in each text, mostly
  [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/)
- Credit: {src.gretil.credit}

## DSBC

The [Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon](https://www.dsbcproject.org) (DSBC) is a project of
the University of the West (Rosemead, California), begun in 2003 with the Nagarjuna
Institute in Kathmandu. It digitises Sanskrit Buddhist texts, many of them preserved in
Nepal, and arranges them by the divisions of the canon: Sūtrapiṭaka, Vinayapiṭaka and
Śāstrapiṭaka. Most texts are published in both Roman transliteration (IAST) and
Devanāgarī.

- **{src.dsbc.editions}** Markdown editions of **{src.dsbc.works}** texts, in IAST
  (`DSBC`) and Devanāgarī (`DSBC देवनागरी`). Devanāgarī books that DSBC has no IAST edition of also have an IAST
  version transliterated with Aksharamukha (`DSBC (transliterated)`)
- Markdown repository: [meobtis/dsbc](https://github.com/meobtis/dsbc)
- Terms: non-commercial educational and research use; see the DSBC
  [usage policy](https://www.dsbcproject.org/pages/usage-policy)
- Credit: {src.dsbc.credit}

## SARIT

[SARIT](https://sarit.indology.info) (Search and Retrieval of Indic Texts) is a library of TEI-encoded Indic texts. From
2013 to 2017 it was funded by the German Research Foundation (DFG) and the US National Endowment for the Humanities
(NEH). Its Buddhist content concentrates on Buddhist epistemology and logic (*pramāṇa*). The texts are critical editions,
each keyed in from a printed edition that its header names, and they carry that edition's apparatus (variant readings,
parallels and the editor's remarks). In the Markdown editions the apparatus is shown as footnotes.

The Markdown repository has **all {src.sarit.works + src.sarit.skipped} texts** of SARIT, each marked as Buddhist or not.
MEOBTIS lists the Buddhist ones, and does not list the other {src.sarit.skipped}: Hindu and Jain philosophy, medicine,
law, grammar, poetry, the Mahābhārata and Purāṇas, and one Buddhist text (the *Buddhacarita*) whose SARIT version is coded
for linguistic analysis and is not readable as it stands (DSBC has the text).

- **{src.sarit.editions}** Markdown editions of **{src.sarit.works}** texts: in Devanāgarī (`SARIT देवनागरी`), in IAST
  as edited (`SARIT`), and IAST versions of the Devanāgarī texts transliterated with Aksharamukha
  (`SARIT (transliterated)`)
- Markdown repository: [meobtis/sarit](https://github.com/meobtis/sarit)
- Licence: given in each text, Creative Commons
  [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) or
  [3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/) (attribution and share-alike)
- Credit: {src.sarit.credit}

## Schøyen Collection

*[Buddhist Manuscripts in the Schøyen Collection](https://www.hermesbooks.no/downloads)* (general editor Jens Braarvig;
Hermes Publishing, Oslo, 2000–2016) publishes editions of manuscript fragments from the Bamiyan region. The publisher
offers the four volumes as free downloads; they remain in copyright. Almost everything in them is fragmentary, or
duplicates works MEOBTIS already has from other sources, so we index a single text: the *Sarvadharmāpravṛttinirdeśa*,
a Mahāyāna sūtra not otherwise available here, edited by Jens Braarvig in volume i.

The Markdown edition gives the Sanskrit only: the transliteration of the surviving folios and the Sanskrit of the
editor's reconstruction. It leaves out the edition's introduction, footnotes, Tibetan and Chinese parallel texts and
English translation, for which see the book. The other texts in the volumes are listed on the
[Other collections](/about/collections/#manuscripts) page.

- **{src.schoyen.editions}** Markdown edition of **{src.schoyen.works}** text (`Schøyen`), in IAST as edited
- Markdown repository: [meobtis/schoyen](https://github.com/meobtis/schoyen)
- Terms: the edition is © Jens Braarvig, all rights reserved. The transcription is offered for research and study;
  no licence is granted for it. See the repository's
  [rights statement](https://github.com/meobtis/schoyen/blob/main/LICENSE.md), and cite the printed edition.
- Credit: {src.schoyen.credit}
