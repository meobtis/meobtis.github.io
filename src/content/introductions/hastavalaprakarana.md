The *Hastavālaprakaraṇa* is a short treatise in verse on the Madhyamaka theme that things lack an intrinsic nature. The Sanskrit text opens with a salutation to Mañjuśrī and a statement that the work is composed so that beings, who cling to ordinary conventions as if they were ultimate, may reach non-erroneous knowledge by examining the nature of things. Its verses draw on stock images of illusion, such as mistaking a rope for a snake.[^dsbc238] A prose commentary, the *Hastavālaprakaraṇavṛtti*, accompanies it and is a separate work in this collection.

Its authorship is disputed. The Tibetan tradition ascribes it to Āryadeva, whereas the Chinese tradition attributes it to Dignāga; modern scholars cited in a general reference article are said to consider Dignāga the more likely author, but that article gives the attribution only in outline.[^wikiaryadeva] Little else about its date, place or reception could be verified from the sources consulted, and further research in specialist literature on the Chinese and Tibetan versions would be needed.
