*Paścāttāpaḥ* ("Remorse" or "Regret") is a short Sanskrit poem of fifteen verses, classed under *kāvya* (poetry) in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon.[^dsbc874] It opens with a salutation to a certain Dharmaratna Mitrācārya, wishing him health and long life through the power of the Three Jewels and for the growth of the Buddhist teaching. The speaker then describes his own old age.[^dsbc874]

The verses dwell on the decay of the body: trembling limbs, failing eyesight and memory, laboured breathing, and a pen lying idle because poetic composition has ceased. Old age is personified as a *yakṣiṇī* who devours his flesh and blood, and the speaker notes that books he once read untiringly now lie in the library, where worms and mice handle them. A closing reflection observes that everything in saṃsāra ends in decay, and the final verse states that, afflicted by age (the text gives eighty-seven years), he can no longer compose verse full of sentiment.[^dsbc874] Little reliable information about the poem's author, date or place of composition could be found; the DSBC record names *Buddhist Studies Review* (2003) as its bibliographic source but does not identify the author or the specific item, so the attribution and the identity of the addressee remain unestablished here.[^dsbc874]
