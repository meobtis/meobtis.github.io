The *Vajrasattvastuti* ("Praise of Vajrasattva") is a very short Sanskrit hymn of five verses. In the digital text it opens with a prose line stating that the blessed tathāgatas praise the blessed Mahāvajrasattva, and it closes with the colophon "śrīmahāvajrasattvastutiḥ samāptā".[^dsbc210]

Each of the first four verses addresses Vajrasattva as embodying one of the poisons or defilements (delusion, slander, desire, envy) as a "vajra" nature, and pays homage to him as body, mind, speech or the holder of the vajra bond. The fifth verse praises him as the nature of all buddhas and lord of the maṇḍala.[^dsbc210] Little reliable information about the work's author, date or provenance, or about Tibetan or Chinese translations, could be gathered, so none is claimed here. The text appears in the same collection and section of the DSBC as a separate eight-verse *Vajrasattvastotram*.[^dsbc210]
