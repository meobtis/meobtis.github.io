The *Sarvaśabdabhāvacarcā* is a short Sanskrit treatise of Buddhist epistemology (*pramāṇa*). In the edition on which the digital text is based, the title is given as *Sarvaśabdābhāvacarcā*, "discussion of the absence of all words". The work is attributed to Jñānaśrīmitra, a Buddhist philosopher of the epistemological tradition who is dated to about 975–1025 CE and is described as a "gate-scholar" (*dvāra-paṇḍita*) of the Vikramaśīla monastic university.[^wiki-jnanasrimitra] No established English title was found. Its opening sentences, as read in the digital text, take up an argument for the absence of every word and consider a proof based on the non-perception of the effects of cognition (*jñānakāryānupalabdhi*), which they set against the non-perception of the nature of a thing (*svabhāvānupalabdhi*).[^dsbc732]

Very little further information about the work could be gathered from the sources consulted. It is usually grouped with Jñānaśrīmitra's *Anupalabdhirahasya*, and the printed edition used for the digital text presents the two together; a critical edition with a survey of Jñānaśrīmitra's theory of non-perception (*anupalabdhi*) is listed as Kellner (2007), published in the series Wiener Studien zur Tibetologie und Buddhismuskunde (no. 67) in Vienna.[^wiki-jnanasrimitra] The date, the manuscript basis and any translations or commentaries were not verified here, and readers should consult that edition for them.
