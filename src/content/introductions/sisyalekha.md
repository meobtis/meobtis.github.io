The *Śiṣyalekha* ("Letter to a Disciple") is a Sanskrit poem in numbered verses, ascribed in its colophon to Ācārya Candragomin.[^gretil-sisya] The digital text opens with an invocation of the Three Jewels and verses in praise of a compassionate being, and runs to 115 numbered verses, some of them damaged or incomplete in the edition used.[^gretil-sisya] It is usually described in English as the *Letter to a Disciple*.[^wiki-chandragomin]

Little reliable information on the work could be gathered for this introduction. Wikipedia lists it among the surviving Sanskrit works of Candragomin, whose dates are uncertain (estimates range from the fifth to the sixth century CE), and notes an eleventh-century manuscript of the text found in Nepal and held by Cambridge University Library (MS Add. 1161).[^wiki-chandragomin] Details of its contents, its Tibetan and Chinese translations and its place in later tradition are not documented in the sources consulted here, and should be checked against specialist scholarship.
