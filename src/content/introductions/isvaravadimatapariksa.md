The *Īśvaravādimataparīkṣā* ("Examination of the Doctrine of Īśvara", here given in English as "Examination of the Doctrine of God") is a short Sanskrit work of Buddhist epistemology and logic attributed to Jitāri.[^wiki-jitari] The title is not transmitted with the text: the SARIT edition marks it as reconstructed, and the work survives as one of five small texts edited together by Gudrun Bühnemann in 1982, alongside the *Vedāprāmāṇyasiddhi*, *Sarvajñasiddhi*, *Nairātmyasiddhi* and *Jātinirākṛti*.[^sarit-isvara]

The text opens by announcing that the view of those who affirm Īśvara is to be examined, and it takes as its opponents the Naiyāyikas and others who hold that everything that is an effect, such as plants and mountains, was made by Īśvara. Their proof is an inference: whatever is an effect is seen to have an agent, like a pot, and the disputed objects are effects too. The Buddhist author contests the pervasion (*vyāpti*) on which this inference depends, arguing that things like grass are seen to arise without any person's activity, so that the general rule "every effect has an intelligent maker" cannot be established.[^sarit-isvara] Jitāri is generally dated to the later tenth century and is said to have been a teacher at Vikramaśilā in the Dharmakīrti tradition, but little further has been documented about this particular treatise, and no reliable information on Tibetan or Chinese translations was found.[^wiki-jitari]
