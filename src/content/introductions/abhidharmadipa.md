The *Abhidharmadīpa* ("Lamp of Abhidharma") is a Sanskrit Buddhist treatise on Abhidharma, consisting of verses (*kārikā*) and a prose autocommentary, the *Vibhāṣāprabhāvṛtti*. It was unknown, in the original or in Chinese or Tibetan translation, until photographs of an incomplete palm-leaf manuscript taken in Tibet by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana were published.[^jaini1977] The edition on which the digital texts rest is Padmanabh S. Jaini's critical edition, published at Patna in 1959 by the Kashi Prasad Jayaswal Research Institute and based on that manuscript; it grew out of the editor's University of London thesis and was reprinted as a second edition in 1977.[^cinii-jaini] The manuscript is incomplete, and the photographs of it were also incomplete.[^jaini1977]

The work is a critique of Vasubandhu, the author of the *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya*. It sets the Sarvāstivāda and Vaibhāṣika positions against the Sautrāntika leanings of the *Kośa*, and Jaini's introduction discusses a series of controversies between the "Kośakāra" and the author of the *Dīpa* (the "Dīpakāra").[^jaini1977] The incomplete manuscript preserves no author's name. Jaini argued that the author is most probably Vimalamitra, a scholar from Kashmir who, according to Xuanzang, wrote a refutation of Vasubandhu's views; on that basis the work is placed roughly between 450 and 550 CE.[^jaini1977-intro] This is an editorial hypothesis rather than a certainty. Contemporary reception included a review of the edition in the *Bulletin of the School of Oriental and African Studies*.[^sasaki1962]

Little else about the digital texts is documented. The database records for the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon versions do not name a printed source, whereas the GRETIL files do.
