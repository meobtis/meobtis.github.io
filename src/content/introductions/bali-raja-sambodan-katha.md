The manuscript is headed *Vali rājā saṃbodhanakathā, mūla bhāṣā sahitaṃ, patra 40*, that is, roughly "the story of the addressing of King Bali, together with the original language, forty folios".[^archive95] The text is in mixed language: each Sanskrit verse is followed by a paraphrase in Newar (Nepal Bhasa), so that the Sanskrit "original" and its vernacular rendering stand side by side.[^archive95] The scan is dated by Commons to the eighteenth century.[^commons95]

The story opens with the bodhisattva Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin asking the Buddha Śākyasiṃha for teaching on the merit of the true Dharma. The Buddha then tells how Lokeśvara, the son of the Tathāgata, goes down to the underworld (*pātāla*) to rescue beings, and arrives before the demon king Bali, who is attended by a retinue. Bali, seeing the bodhisattva, rejoices and asks for instruction; the later pages give Bali's words of homage and his vow.[^archive95]

This is the Bali episode known from the *Kāraṇḍavyūha*, the sūtra of Avalokiteśvara's compassionate activity. In the Tibetan version translated by 84000, the Buddha's account includes Avalokiteśvara's meeting with Bali, who recounts how Viṣṇu in the form of a dwarf tricked him, and the sūtra is introduced by Sarvanīvaraṇaviṣkambhin.[^84000] The same translation notes a later Nepalese version in verse, the *Guṇakāraṇḍavyūha*, which has importance in Nepalese Buddhism.[^84000] The manuscript here seems to be a Newar-language retelling of that episode on the verse model, but a verse-by-verse comparison with the Nepalese text was not made, and no source consulted names this particular work or its compiler.

The manuscript identifies no author or date. The sūtra itself is recited in Newar Buddhism on major occasions.[^samhita]
