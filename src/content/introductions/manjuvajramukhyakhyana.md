The *Mañjuvajramukhyākhyāna* is a Sanskrit Buddhist work in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which files it under the commentaries on Mahāyoga tantras and gives as its source Kimiaki Tanaka's edition, published by Watanabeshuppan in Tokyo in 2018.[^dsbc854] No established English title for the work was found in the sources consulted.

The text opens with a salutation to Vajrasattva and a verse of homage, and then prescribes a visualisation in which the practitioner purifies the body with syllables and coloured light and identifies with "Mañjuvajra". It continues with instructions such as the purification of the hands, so it reads as a ritual or meditation manual rather than a discursive commentary.[^dsbc854] This description is based on the opening of the edition only.

Mañjuvajra is a form of Mañjuśrī that, in the Jñānapāda tradition of the *Guhyasamāja Tantra*, is the central deity of a mandala of nineteen deities.[^wiki-gst] The edition's classification suggests a connection with that tradition, but the sources consulted do not state the work's author, date, place of composition or Tibetan and Chinese counterparts, and little else about it could be verified.
