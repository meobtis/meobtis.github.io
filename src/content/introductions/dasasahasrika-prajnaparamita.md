The *Daśasāhasrikā prajñāpāramitā* is one of the Perfection of Wisdom (*prajñāpāramitā*) sūtras, available in English as *The Transcendent Perfection of Wisdom in Ten Thousand Lines* (Toh 11). In the Tibetan Kangyur it is counted among the "six mothers" of the Perfection of Wisdom section, and it runs to 33 chapters in 34 fascicles.[^84000toh11]

According to the 84000 introduction, the sūtra is a compact and coherent restatement of the longer Hundred Thousand, Twenty-Five Thousand and Eighteen Thousand Line versions, whose general structure it follows, and it has much more in common with them than with the *Eight Thousand Lines*. Its first two chapters summarise the 367 categories of phenomena and attributes that the work goes on to examine, and its material is organised around the eight topics of the bodhisattva path set out in Maitreya's *Ornament of Clear Realization*.[^84000toh11] The Sanskrit passage in the digital text opens with the usual setting at Vulture Peak near Rājagṛha, where Śāriputra asks the Buddha what the perfection of wisdom is.

No Sanskrit manuscript of this version is extant, and no Chinese translation of it exists. The Tibetan version is the only surviving form; the Degé Kangyur catalogue by Situ Paṇchen credits Jinamitra, Prajñāvarman and Yeshé Dé with it, and it is already listed in the ninth-century Denkarma catalogue. Edward Conze suggested the text might have been composed in Tibet, but the 84000 translators consider this highly unlikely on grounds of style and content.[^84000toh11]

Because the Sanskrit is lost, the Sanskrit text in this collection is a reconstruction of the first two chapters made from the Tibetan, published by Sten Konow in 1941 together with an analysis and index.[^gretil-dsp]
