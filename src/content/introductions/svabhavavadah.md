*Svabhāvavāda* ("the doctrine of own-nature") is a short Sanskrit text, only a few kilobytes long, filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the Madhyamaka treatises (*śāstrapiṭaka*).[^dsbc721] It is a single continuous piece with no chapter divisions, mixing prose with a few verses.

From the text itself, it begins by stating that the many sufferings of *saṃsāra* are wholly independent of causes and arise from *svabhāva*, which it defines as nothing other than causal independence. It argues that the variety of beings, the heat of fire and the coolness of water have no maker and are simply fixed by their nature. It also argues that positing causes leads to an infinite regress, and that a causal sequence would equally allow the opponents' creator god. This is the argument of a naturalist position; the passage reads as an exposition of that view, and whether the author endorses or refutes it is not clear from the sections read.[^dsbc721]

Little reliable information about the work could be found. Neither the digital record nor the sources consulted name an author, date, school or printed edition, and no established English title, Tibetan or Chinese translation, or manuscript is documented here. The two digital editions give no bibliographic details beyond the proof-reader, supplier, sponsor and input date.[^dsbc721][^dsbc722]
