The *Gaṇeśastotram* ("Hymn to Gaṇeśa") is a short Sanskrit devotional poem of fourteen verses in praise of Gaṇeśa, also called Gaṇapati, Heramba and Vināyaka. The text opens with verses that describe the god's elephant face, large belly and a single tusk, and calls on him as the giver of success and the remover of obstacles. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) files it under the *stotra* (hymn) division of the *śāstrapiṭaka*, in a sub-list devoted to Gaṇeśa.[^dsbc151]

The text lists many of the god's names, among them Gaṇapati, Heramba, Vighnarāja and Vināyaka, and the last verses promise that anyone who recites or hears the sixteen names at the start of study, at a marriage, on entering or leaving, in battle or in danger will meet no obstacle. These contents were read directly from the DSBC text; the edition records no author, date or place of composition, and little independent scholarship on this particular hymn could be found, so its origin and its relation to Buddhist practice remain undocumented here.[^dsbc151]

The DSBC record names the printed source as *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and published by Motilal Banarsidass in Varanasi in 1994, where the hymn is the first item of the relevant section.[^dsbc151] Romanized and Devanāgarī versions of the text are both available.[^dsbc638]
