The *Dharmadhātuvāgīśvaramaṇḍalastotra* is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) in forty-five verses, mostly in the *anuṣṭubh* metre, ending with the colophon "śrī dharmadhātuvāgīśvaramaṇḍalastotraṃ samāptam". The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the *stotra* devoted to Vāgīśvara, "Lord of Speech", an epithet of Mañjuśrī.[^dsbc203]

The verses are a litany of salutations rather than a continuous argument. The text itself identifies Mañjuśrī and Mañjughoṣa with Vāgīśvara and with the *dharmadhātu* in its opening verses. It then names, one after another, the five Jinas (Akṣobhya, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi, with Vairocana implied in the central figure) together with their vajra attendants, goddesses such as Locanā, Pāṇḍarā, Māmakī and Tārā, the ten bodhisattva stages, the perfections, the *dhāraṇī* goddesses and the four analytical knowledges.[^dsbc203] The hymn goes on to the great bodhisattvas (Samantabhadra, Kṣitigarbha, Ratnapāṇi, Lokeśvara and others), the wrathful protectors, the offering goddesses, the world-guardians and Hindu deities, planets, nāga kings, yakṣas and the lunar mansions, and returns at the end to Vāgīśvara as the bestower of knowledge.[^dsbc203] This shape suggests that it was composed as a praise of the whole retinue of a Mañjuśrī maṇḍala, in the manner of the tantric literature.

I could not find reliable published information on the authorship, date or place of composition of the hymn, on any manuscript, or on translations into Tibetan or Chinese, and none is claimed here. The source records give only that the text appears in a modern printed anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc203]
