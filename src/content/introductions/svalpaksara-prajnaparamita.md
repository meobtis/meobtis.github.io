The *Svalpākṣarā prajñāpāramitā*, "The Perfection of Wisdom in a Few Syllables" (Tibetan *Yi ge nyung ngu*), is a very short Mahāyāna sūtra. In the Tibetan Degé Kangyur it is Toh 22, and 84000 gives its Sanskrit title as *svalpākṣarā bhagavatī prajñāpāramitā*.[^84000toh22] The Sanskrit text is only a few paragraphs long.

It is set on Vulture Peak at Rājagṛha. Avalokiteśvara asks the Buddha for a condensed teaching of the perfection of wisdom, and the Buddha enters a samādhi called "liberating all beings", emits light that assures the awakening of those it touches, and then teaches. According to the 84000 summary, the Buddha gives a heart mantra and a dhāraṇī that invokes victory and protection and asks for the purification of karmic obscurations, and he presents the teaching as the source of all buddhas.[^84000toh22] In the Sanskrit text, the Buddha explains the title by saying that the teaching is brief so that even beings of dull faculties can hold, recite and copy it and reach awakening with little effort.[^vaidya1961]

The Sanskrit colophon, as reported by 84000, says that Nāgārjuna retrieved the text from the realm of the nāgas, which is a tradition about its origin rather than evidence for its date. The Tibetan translator is unknown, but the text is listed in the early imperial catalogues (the Denkarma and Phangthangma), so a Tibetan version existed by the mid-ninth century.[^84000toh22]

Several Sanskrit witnesses exist, and editions were published by Edward Conze (1956), P. L. Vaidya (1961) and Akira Yuyama (1977). A Chinese translation (Taishō 258) was made in 982 CE by Devaśāntika (Tianxizai), and English translations were made by Charles Willemen (1973) and Yuyama (1977).[^84000toh22] The digital editions here follow Vaidya's printed text. Little else about the text's history is documented in the sources consulted for this introduction.
