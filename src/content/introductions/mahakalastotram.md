The *Mahākālastotram* is a short hymn of praise (*stotra*) addressed to Mahākāla, "the Great Black One". In the digital edition it consists of thirteen verses and closes with the colophon "śrīmahākālastotraṃ sampūrṇam". The first eleven verses salute the deity, most ending with the refrain *mahākāla namo'stu te*, "homage to you, Mahākāla", and the last two verses state the benefits of reciting it.[^dsbc158]

The verses describe the deity as a dark, two-armed, one-faced hero adorned with snakes, a skull crown and a garland of severed heads, wearing a tiger-skin, and as terrifying, with fangs, a lolling tongue and reddened eyes. He is also invoked as a protector of accomplished practitioners and devotees and as a "boat" across the ocean of *saṃsāra*, and the closing verses promise that one who recites the hymn with devotion is freed from fear and the worry caused by enemies.[^dsbc158] Mahākāla is known in Vajrayāna Buddhism as a wrathful *dharmapāla* ("protector of the Dharma"), often shown as black or blue, with a crown of five skulls and a garland of fifty heads, and regarded in different traditions as a manifestation of figures such as Hevajra or Avalokiteśvara.[^wiki-mahakala]

Little reliable information on this particular hymn could be found. Its author and date are not given in the edition, and no translation, manuscript or commentary was identified in the sources consulted. The only edition information available is that of the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which draws on Pandey's printed anthology.[^dsbc158]
