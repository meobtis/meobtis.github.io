The *Nyāyabinduṭīkāṭippaṇī* is a Sanskrit *ṭippaṇī*, a set of notes on words and passages, on the *Nyāyabinduṭīkā*. It is introduced in the text as a *ṭippaṇī* "of the *Nyāyabinduṭīkā* composed by Ācārya Dharmottara", and it begins by commenting on Dharmottara's opening verse in praise of the Buddha.[^dsbc296] The *Nyāyabinduṭīkā* is Dharmottara's commentary on Dharmakīrti's *Nyāyabindu*. Dharmottara is placed in the eighth century (about 740–800), and the *Nyāyabinduṭīkā* is the only one of his works that survives in Sanskrit.[^wiki-dharmottara] Later passages of the notes discuss epistemological questions such as whether a mental perception (*mānasa*) can be established alongside sense perception.[^dsbc296]

Little reliable information about this particular *ṭippaṇī* was found: its author, date and manuscript sources are not stated in the edition's record and could not be established from the sources consulted, so they are left open here. The digital text is based on the 1909 St Petersburg edition by Stcherbatsky.[^dsbc296] The parent commentary was published in Sanskrit in the Bibliotheca Indica series in Calcutta in 1889.[^archive-nbt1889]
