The *Acintyastava* ("Hymn to the Unthinkable One") is a Sanskrit hymn in praise of the Buddha, traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna. It is usually counted among the four hymns of the *Catuḥstava* cycle, together with the *Lokātītastava*, *Niraupamyastava* and *Paramārthastava*.[^jakubczak2024] The text opens by paying homage to the one who taught that things arisen in dependence have no intrinsic nature, and who is himself unequalled in knowledge, unthinkable and beyond illustration.

A recent study describes the hymn as a vehicle for Madhyamaka philosophy. It draws out themes that are less explicit in Nāgārjuna's treatises, above all the natural tendency to attribute real existence to the objects of experience, which it calls an "obsession with reification" (*bhāva-graha-grahāveśa*).[^jakubczak2024] The Sanskrit verses themselves liken dependently arisen things to illusions, mirages and dreams, and take apart the idea that things arise from themselves, from others or from both.

The authorship of the hymns attributed to Nāgārjuna has been debated, and the question is treated differently for each hymn. Little else about the date, transmission and translations of the *Acintyastava* could be verified here, so those matters are not described. The same article by Jakubczak gives a recent annotated translation of the hymn, together with references to earlier editions and studies.[^jakubczak2024]
