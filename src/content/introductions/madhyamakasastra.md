The *Madhyamakaśāstra* is the Sanskrit title under which this text is catalogued; it is the work more usually called the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* ("Root Verses on the Middle Way"), and in English the *Fundamental Wisdom of the Middle Way*. It is the most important work of Nāgārjuna and consists, according to the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, of 450 stanzas.[^sep-nagarjuna] The digital editions here divide it into 27 chapters (*prakaraṇa*), the first being the "Examination of Conditions" (*Pratyayaparīkṣā*).[^dsbc242]

The verses open with a dedication to the Buddha as the one who taught dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) and the cessation of conceptual proliferation, and then examine in turn concepts central to Buddhist and Indian philosophy, beginning with causal conditions.[^gretil-ms] The treatise is generally read as arguing that phenomena lack intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*) and are empty, a position that became the foundation of the Madhyamaka school.[^sep-nagarjuna]

Nāgārjuna is placed by modern scholarship at roughly 150–250 CE, probably mainly in southern India. The same source cautions that more than one author named Nāgārjuna may have existed and that later works were sometimes attributed to earlier authorities, although the attribution of his principal works is largely uncontested.[^sep-nagarjuna] Many later authors wrote commentaries, including Buddhapālita, Bhāviveka and Candrakīrti, and in Tibet Tsongkhapa and Mabja Jangchub Tsöndrü; Ye Shaoyong's edition is noted as a useful guide to the Sanskrit, Tibetan and Chinese recensions.[^sep-nagarjuna] Beyond these points, little further detail on the transmission could be verified for this introduction.
