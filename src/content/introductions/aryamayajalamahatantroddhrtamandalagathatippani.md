The *Āryamāyājālamahātantroddhṛtamaṇdalagāthāṭippaṇī* is a short Sanskrit commentary or annotation (*ṭippaṇī*) on the maṇḍala verses ("extracted" from the *Āryamāyājālamahātantra*, as the title says). The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon classifies it among the *advayatantraṭīkā*, the commentaries on non-dual tantras, within its *śāstrapiṭaka* division.[^dsbc836] The text is a single unit; its opening line carries the editorial note "sampā-ṭhākurasena negī", which appears to name the editor of the source from which it was keyed, though the record itself supplies no bibliographic details for a printed edition.[^dsbc836]

The commentary begins by quoting a verse that assigns the six perfections (giving, ethical conduct, patience, effort, meditation and wisdom) to the everyday acts of preparing a maṇḍala, such as cleaning with cow dung and water. It then glosses the verse term by term and cites the *Hevajra* and other scriptural passages to explain how the perfections are understood by practitioners of different capacities.[^dsbc836] Beyond this, little reliable information on the work's author, date, translations or manuscripts could be found for this introduction, and none is asserted here.
