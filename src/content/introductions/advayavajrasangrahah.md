The *Advayavajrasaṅgraha* ("Collection of Advayavajra") is a compilation of short Sanskrit treatises, mostly on Buddhist philosophy and tantric practice, ascribed to the master Advayavajra. It was edited by Haraprasad Shastri and issued in 1927 as volume XL of Gaekwad's Oriental Series; a publisher's description counts about twenty-one or twenty-two short works.[^exotic] The Tokyo manuscript catalogue likewise describes the edition as containing 21 works by Advayavajra.[^iic]

The romanized text on this site divides the collection into twenty-one sections, among them the *Kudṛṣṭinirghātana*, the *Tattvaratnāvalī*, the *Caturmudrā*, the *Tattvaprakāśa*, the *Yuganaddhaprakāśa*, the *Mahāsukhaprakāśa*, the *Tattvaviṃśikā*, the *Mahāyānaviṃśikā*, the *Tattvadaśaka* and the *Amanasikārādhāra*.[^dsbc82] The titles show a mix of doctrinal verse sets (of five, six, ten or twenty verses) and short prose or mixed treatises on initiation, the four seals and non-conceptual realization.

Advayavajra is also known as Avadhūtipā or Avadhūtapāda and is dated to about 1000 CE; he is recorded as the teacher of Lalitagupta.[^iic] Shastri found the manuscript in the Darbar Library in Nepal, where it was catalogued as *Tattva-dasaka*; he gave no translation and noted that the readings are often corrupt and the sentences elliptical.[^exotic] A composite manuscript in Tokyo (Matsunami 151) preserves at least fifteen works by him, with many folios lost.[^iic] Little else about the collection is documented in the sources consulted here.
