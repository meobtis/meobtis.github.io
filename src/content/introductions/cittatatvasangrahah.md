The *Cittatatvasaṅgraha* ("Compendium of the Reality of Mind") is a short Sanskrit treatise in verse, filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the Yogācāra division of the śāstra section. The text is numbered in about fifty verses and opens with a salutation to the Blessed One, the worthy and perfectly awakened Buddha.[^dsbc329]

From the verses themselves, the work analyses mind (*citta*) as the combination of the four mental aggregates of feeling, perception, formations and consciousness, which it treats together as "citta". It discusses the states of waking and deep sleep in relation to these aggregates, the senses of the words *citta* and *manas*, and concludes that mind is mere cognition (*vijñaptimātratā*), momentary and impermanent, so that the notion of a self is illusory.[^dsbc329]

The closing verses are a colophon rather than a traditional attribution: they say the work was composed by a learned monk, Priyānanda, described as a son of Sri Lanka and a Dharma messenger in America, and give the work the alternative title *Vijñaptimātratāsiddhi-pañcāśikā* ("Fifty verses establishing mere cognition"). It therefore appears to be a modern composition rather than a classical Indian śāstra. This reading rests solely on the text's own colophon, and no independent scholarly discussion, date, translation or manuscript tradition could be found in the sources consulted, so little else is documented here.[^dsbc329]
