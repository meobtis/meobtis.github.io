The *Maṅgalāṣṭakam* ("Eight Verses of Auspiciousness") is a short Sanskrit stotra, or hymn of praise, in eight verses. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the hymns to the eight great bodhisattvas and gives as its source Janardan Shastri Pandey's *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, published by Motilal Banarsidass in 1994.[^dsbc117] No English title for the work was found in the sources consulted.

Judging from the text itself, each verse names a group of figures and ends with the same refrain, asking that the "bodhisattvas, pleased, grant the success of all aims" and auspiciousness. The first verses list bodhisattvas and wrathful and tantric deities such as Mañjuśrī, Lokanātha, Jambhala, Vajrasattva, Maitreya, Vajrapāṇi, Akṣobhya and Heruka; the later verses name goddesses such as Mārīcī, Tārā and Vārāhī, then holy places (Vaiśālī, Gṛdhrakūṭa, Śrāvastī, Lumbinī) and auspicious emblems such as the parasol, lotus, banner, conch, pair of fish and full vase.[^dsbc117] Its authorship, date and tradition are not documented in the sources consulted, and the reading of some names is uncertain in the edition itself, where one word is marked with an alternative in parentheses.[^dsbc117] Little else about the work could be verified.
