The *Śikṣāsamuccaya* ("Compendium of Training", also rendered "Training Anthology") is one of the two works that survive under the name of the Indian Buddhist monk Śāntideva, the other being the *Bodhicaryāvatāra*.[^wiki-shantideva] It is a long prose text interspersed with verses, and it is above all an anthology: it is built from quotations drawn from over a hundred Mahāyāna sūtras, many of which are preserved in Sanskrit only through this work.[^goodman2016]

The text is organised as a commentary on twenty-seven root verses (*kārikā*) and is divided into nineteen chapters.[^wiki-shantideva] The digital editions open with the chapter on the perfection of giving (*dānapāramitā*), and the sequence of chapters follows the topics of the bodhisattva's practice, including patient endurance (chapter 10) and emptiness (chapter 14).[^goodman2016][^gretil-siks] Śāntideva's own role is chiefly that of compiler and arranger, supplying a framework of verses and brief comment around the extracts he selects.

Śāntideva is placed in the late seventh to mid-eighth century, since Śāntarakṣita quoted him in a work composed by 763 CE; biographical traditions are late and disagree about his origins.[^goodman2016][^wiki-shantideva] The relative order of his two works is debated: some scholars, among them Harrison (2007), have argued that a shorter *Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra* came first, then the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, and that the longer *Bodhicaryāvatāra* was formed later by adding verses, a sequence that is plausible but unproven.[^goodman2016]

The work was translated into Chinese but seems to have been little read there; its main influence was in Tibet, and a number of lost sūtras are known only through the extracts it preserves in Sanskrit and in Tibetan and Chinese translation.[^goodman2016] The standard Sanskrit edition is that of P. L. Vaidya (Darbhanga, 1960), on which the DSBC text is based.[^dsbc269] An earlier English translation by Cecil Bendall and W. H. D. Rouse appeared in London in 1922.[^wiki-shantideva] Little further detail about the manuscripts and translation catalogue numbers could be verified in the sources consulted.
