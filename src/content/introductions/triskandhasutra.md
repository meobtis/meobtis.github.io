The *Triskandhasūtra* (*Āryatriskandha sūtram*) is a short Mahāyāna text whose Tibetan version, *Phung po gsum pa'i mdo*, is catalogued as Toh 284 and translated into English by 84000 as *The Sūtra of the Three Heaps*.[^84000toh284] A general reference source describes it as the scripture that sets out the practice of purifying wrongdoing through confession and prostration to a group of buddhas, and so as the textual basis of the Thirty-five Confession Buddhas; the same source gives the Sanskrit title as *Triskandhadharmasūtra* and classes the work with the *Ratnakūṭa* collection, a claim not checked here against a scholarly source.[^wiki35]

The Sanskrit text in the editions listed here is brief. It begins with the speaker taking refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha, followed by a long series of homages (*namo* …) to Śākyamuni and other tathāgatas, among them Ratnārciṣ, Nāgeśvararāja and Vīrasena. The speaker then asks the buddhas of the ten directions to take notice of the harm he has done in this and earlier lives, including offences against stūpa and monastic property, the five heinous deeds and the ten unwholesome courses of action, and declares these before them with a vow of restraint in future. A second passage gathers all the merit of giving, ethical conduct and the aspiration to awakening and dedicates it to supreme perfect awakening, after the manner of the buddhas of the three times. The text closes with the formula of confessing all wrongdoing, rejoicing in all merit and requesting all buddhas to teach, and with verses of homage.[^dsbc34] The title "three heaps" apparently refers to these three elements of confession, rejoicing and dedication, though the editions themselves do not explain it.

Little reliable information on the date, authorship, manuscripts or Chinese versions of the work could be gathered for this introduction, and the Sanskrit text is documented here only through the single printed edition on which both digital texts rest. The 84000 record itself offers no further background beyond the title and catalogue number.[^84000toh284]
