The *Mahābodhivandanāṣṭaka* ("Eight Verses of Homage to the Great Awakening") is a short Sanskrit hymn of eight verses, followed by a closing colophon stating that the hymn is complete. It opens with the salutation "om namo buddhāya", and every verse ends with the same refrain, "śrīmanmahābodhimahaṃ namāmi" ("I bow to the illustrious Mahābodhi"). The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the *stotra* (hymns of praise) under a group labelled *vajrāsana*.[^dsbc207]

The verses praise the Buddha through a series of epithets and images: a body of golden hue and a voice like that of the kalaviṅka bird and of Brahmā; a descendant of the Śākya lineage who cut through craving and defeated Māra; a physician and a guide who serves as a bridge across the ocean of existence; and one who sends down the rain of the Dharma on beings scorched by passion. The last verse states that he became a refuge for those who take refuge, as Buddha, Dharma and Saṃgha alike.[^dsbc207]

Little reliable information about the hymn's author, date, place of composition or transmission could be found. It does not appear to have an established English title or a documented Tibetan or Chinese translation in the sources consulted, and the only source for the text identified here is the printed anthology and the digital records described above.[^dsbc207]
