The *Avalokiteśvarāṣṭottaraśatanāmastotram* ("hymn of the one hundred and eight names of Avalokiteśvara") is a brief Sanskrit stotra in sixteen verses. The text opens with a salutation to Avalokiteśvara and then addresses him in fifteen verses, each of which strings together vocatives such as Padmasattva, Lokeśvara, Dharmarāja and Vajranetra and ends with the refrain "namo'stu te" ("homage to you"). A closing verse says that whoever meditates on or praises these hundred and eight names will gain lordship over the world, and the colophon calls the work an *adhyeṣaṇāstotra*, a hymn of request.[^dsbc125]

Little reliable information about the work's author, date or transmission could be found. The edition's front matter names only the printed source, Pandey's 1994 collection of Buddhist hymns,[^dsbc125] and no study or catalogue entry identifying this particular Sanskrit hymn was located. Tibetan and Chinese literature does contain several texts titled "The Hundred and Eight Names of Avalokiteśvara"; 84000, for example, translates two Degé Kangyur texts of that title (Toh 705 and Toh 706) and notes a Chinese parallel to the latter (Taishō 1054), but states that Toh 706 does not appear to survive in Sanskrit.[^84000toh706] These sources do not link those texts to the present hymn, so no relationship between them is asserted here.
