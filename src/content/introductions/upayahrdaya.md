The *Upāyahṛdaya* is a short treatise on the theory and practice of debate (*vāda*). No Sanskrit original is known to survive: the Sanskrit text in circulation is a modern reconstruction by Giuseppe Tucci, who retranslated the work into Sanskrit from its Chinese version and made clear that this was a retranslation, not a recovery of the lost original.[^tucci1929] The Chinese text is catalogued as Nanjio 1247, and Tucci regarded the Sanskrit title as uncertain, rejecting Nanjio's proposed restoration *Upāyakauśalyahṛdaya*.[^tucci1929] I found no established English title for the work.

The Sanskrit text, as reconstructed, opens by asking whether debate should be undertaken at all and answers that it should, for the sake of protecting the true Dharma and not for fame or gain.[^gretil-upaya] The treatise then goes on to the characteristics of sound and unsound argument. Tucci observed that its lists of categories are less systematically arranged than those of the later *Nyāyasūtras* and stand closer to the debating categories of the *Carakasaṃhitā*, and he considered it related to the *Tarkaśāstra* and the *Vādavidhi*.[^tucci1929] It also contains lists of Buddhist doctrine of an early type alongside some statements, for example on emptiness, that sound Mahāyāna and that Tucci thought might have been interpolated.[^tucci1929]

The author's name is lost. Some scholars have attributed the work to Nāgārjuna, but Tucci rejected this, noting that many works circulated under that name.[^tucci1929] He judged it to be a very ancient text, reflecting older stages of Indian argumentation than the developed Nyāya manuals.[^tucci1929] According to Tucci, it was translated into Chinese twice: Buddhabhadra's version of the Eastern Jin period is known only by name, and the surviving translation was made under the Northern Wei.[^tucci1929] I could not verify further details of the translators or dates in the sources available for this entry.

The reconstructed text appeared, together with a Sanskrit retranslation of the *Tarkaśāstra*, in a 1929 volume of Pre-Diṅnāga Buddhist texts on logic published at Baroda.[^tucci1929]
