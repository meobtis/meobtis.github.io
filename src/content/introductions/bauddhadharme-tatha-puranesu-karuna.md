*Bauddhadharme tathā purāṇeṣu karuṇā* ("Compassion in Buddhism and in the Purāṇas") is a modern prose composition in Sanskrit, classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the recent (*adhunātana*) works of the *śāstrapiṭaka* section. The record lists it as a single chapter and names Phra Suntharnadhammathada as editor; no author, publisher, place or date of publication is given.[^dsbc823] No separate scholarly description of the work, and no established English title, could be found, so the account here rests on the edition record and on the opening of the text itself.

The text begins with an outline of Buddhism (*bauddhadharmasya saṁkṣiptā rūparekhā*), explaining the word *buddha* as "one who knows rightly", the teaching as truths discovered rather than invented, and the Triple Jewel (*ratnatraya*) of Buddha, Dhamma and Saṅgha. It then gives a short life of the Buddha, from the Śākya kingdom with its capital at Kapilavatthu to his marriage, renunciation and awakening, and passes to the character of his teaching, which it presents as aimed at the welfare of all beings. It refers to the Pāli *Aṅguttaranikāya* and sets out features of the teaching under headings such as change (*parivartana*), reinterpretation and restatement, with the Four Noble Truths among the points discussed.[^dsbc823] How the work treats the Purāṇas, as its title promises, is not documented in the sources consulted and is not described here.
