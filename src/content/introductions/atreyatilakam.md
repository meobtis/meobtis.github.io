The *Ātreyatilaka*, also titled *Pratimāmānalakṣaṇa* or *Pratibimbalakṣaṇa* ("characteristics of the measurement of images"), is a short Sanskrit treatise in verse on the proportions of sacred images, belonging to the śilpaśāstra literature. Its opening verses state that it gathers what earlier sages had said about the measures and characteristics of images, in a Buddhist treatise and elsewhere, and collects it in one place before turning to the marks of the image of the omniscient one.[^dsbc312] The verses that follow set out a system of units (the *tāla*, span and finger-breadth) and the shapes of faces, which illustrates its concern with iconometry.[^dsbc312]

Little reliable scholarly literature on the work could be found. A bookseller's description of a modern edition with English translation reports that the editio princeps was prepared from a unique manuscript of Nepalese origin in the Visvabharati library at Santiniketan, that the text survives also in the Tibetan Tanjur, and that the editor used the Tibetan version to restore faulty Sanskrit readings; these details come from a commercial listing and should be checked against the edition itself.[^exotic] Its date, authorship and school are not established in the sources consulted.
