The *Madhyamakāvatāra* (commonly rendered "Introduction to the Middle Way") is a treatise in verse by the Indian Madhyamaka philosopher Candrakīrti, who is placed by modern scholars in the early seventh century, though little is known of his life. It is an independent work with the author's own prose commentary, and the entry on Madhyamaka in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* reports that it survives only in Tibetan translation. It has ten chapters, one for each stage of the bodhisattva path, and the sixth chapter, on the perfection of wisdom, contains Candrakīrti's fullest exposition of Madhyamaka philosophy.[^hayes2023] Lotsawa House presents it as his classic treatise and as an exposition of the Middle Way view in which appearance and emptiness are understood through dependent origination.[^lotsawa]

The digital text described here is the verse portion (*kārikā*) of chapter 6 only; verses are numbered 6.1 onward. The sources consulted do not say how the Sanskrit of this chapter was established or from what materials, beyond the attribution of the edition to Xuezhu Li, so nothing further is claimed here about the Sanskrit transmission, the Tibetan translators, or Chinese versions. Tibetan scholastics later classed Candrakīrti among the Prāsaṅgikas, because he preferred to expose the untenable consequences of rival positions rather than to build independent arguments; the same entry notes that Indian Mādhyamikas did not use that label.[^hayes2023]
