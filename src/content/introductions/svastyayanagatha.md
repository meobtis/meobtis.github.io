The *Svastyayanagāthā* ("Verses of Well-being") is a short text in verse that opens with homage to the Buddha and to liberation, and then invites the beings gathered, whether on the earth or in the sky, to listen to a blessing (*svastyayana*) spoken by the Jina. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the *nirdeśa* subdivision of the sūtra section and gives no bibliographic details for it, so its source manuscript or printed edition is not stated.[^dsbc788]

The surviving verses, which declare that no treasure in this world or the next equals the Tathagata and conclude with the wish that all be well "by this truth", closely resemble the Ratana Sutta, a Pali protective discourse found in the Sutta Nipāta (Snp 2.1). That discourse praises the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Saṅgha as three jewels, and Pali commentaries tell that it was recited in Vesālī to dispel disease, harmful spirits and famine.[^wikiratana][^thanissaro1994] A parallel in the *Mahāvastu* is reported to have nineteen verses in Sanskrit, against seventeen in Pali.[^wikiratana] Which version the digital text reproduces is not stated in the edition's record, and no further information on its date, authorship or transmission could be found in the sources consulted, so the correspondence is noted here only as a likely relationship.
