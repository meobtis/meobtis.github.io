The *Svayambhūstotram* is a brief Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) of six verses, each of the first five ending with the refrain "I bow to Svayambhū" (*svayaṃbhuvaṃ namāmyaham*). It praises the self-arisen (*svayambhū*) Buddha, the object of worship at the great stūpa on its hill in the Kathmandu Valley, now known as Swayambhunath.[^wiki-swayambhunath] The verses describe him as a thousand-petalled lotus and as brighter than a thousand suns, and the last verse promises the attainment of the three aims of life and then liberation to those who recite the hymn regularly.[^dsbc198]

The colophon of the digital text describes the hymn as taken from the *Śrībṛhatsvayambhūpurāṇa* and composed by Śikhin (*śikhinirmitam*); the identity of this Śikhin is not explained in the sources consulted.[^dsbc198] The *Svayambhū Purāṇa* is the Nepalese Buddhist scripture on the origin and development of the valley and of the site.[^wiki-swayambhunath] The reliable scholarly literature on this particular hymn, its date, its relation to the Purāṇa's recensions and any Tibetan or Chinese translation could not be verified in the sources available, so these are left undescribed; little more is documented.
