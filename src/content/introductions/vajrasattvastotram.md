The *Vajrasattvastotram* ("Hymn to Vajrasattva") is a short Sanskrit devotional poem in the Buddhist stotra genre. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) it is classified among the *stotra* in the *śāstrapiṭaka* division, under a Vajrasattva heading, and it is taken from Janardan Shastri Pandey's anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc209]

The text comprises eight verses and ends with a colophon calling it the "Śrīvajrasattvastotra". It opens with homage to Vajrasattva as a teacher of the world and a buddha, and goes on to praise him as a dispeller of ignorance and delusion. Later verses pay homage to Lokanātha (Avalokiteśvara), to the female figures Māmakī, Locanā, Tārā and Padminī, to Tārā by name, and finally to Śākyasiṃha, the Buddha Śākyamuni. The content is drawn from the text itself as given in the DSBC edition.[^dsbc209]

Little reliable information about the hymn's author, date, place of composition, manuscripts or translations could be found in the sources consulted, and none is asserted here. Its title and verses are known here only from the DSBC edition and the bibliographic record of the printed anthology it reproduces.[^dsbc209]
