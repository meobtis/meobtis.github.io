The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") is a short Sanskrit stotra, a verse hymn of praise addressed to the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha. The title carries the qualifier *brahmaṇā kṛtam*, "made by Brahmā", so the hymn presents itself as the praise of the Buddha offered by the god Brahmā. The text is in numbered verses and opens with a homage to the lord of the Śākyas.[^dsbc179]

Judging from the opening verses, the hymn praises the Buddha as a sun for the lotus of the true teaching, as one who has attained boundless knowledge that gods and sages could not reach, and as one who set the wheel of the teaching in motion, at which the earth is said to have trembled.[^dsbc179] Little else about the work appears to be documented in the sources available for this introduction. No reliable information was found on its date, its authorship beyond the attribution in the title, or on any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The site's catalogue also lists a similarly titled hymn with a different qualifier (*chando'mṛtoddhṛtam*), which is a separate entry.
