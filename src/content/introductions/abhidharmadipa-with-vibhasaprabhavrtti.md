The *Abhidharmadīpa* ("Lamp of Abhidharma") is a Sanskrit treatise on Abhidharma philosophy, composed as verses and accompanied by a prose commentary, the *Vibhāṣāprabhāvṛtti* ("Commentary Illuminating the Vibhāṣā"). It survives in a palm-leaf manuscript reported to have been found in Tibet by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana in 1937, where it was at first named *Abhidharma-pradīpa*.[^oeaw-jaini] The standard edition is the critical edition by Padmanabh S. Jaini, first published in 1959 and reissued by the Kashi Prasad Jayaswal Research Institute in Patna in 1977 as volume 4 of the Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series.[^oeaw-jaini]

Jaini's doctoral thesis, on which his edition is based, presents the work as a Kashmiri Vaibhāṣika text modelled on, and directed against, Vasubandhu's *Abhidharmakośa*, and as the only surviving work of Vasubandhu's critics preserved in its original language.[^jaini1958] The author is not securely known. Jaini proposed Vimalamitra, a disciple of Saṃghabhadra, and the work is commonly dated to roughly the fifth to sixth century CE, though both attribution and date rest on indirect evidence.[^jaini1958] Very little else could be verified in accessible sources for this introduction; details such as the Tibetan and Chinese transmission of the work, and newly found manuscript folios, are not covered here.
