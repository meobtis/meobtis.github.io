The *Dveśavajrasādhana* ("Sādhana of the Vajra of Hatred") is a short Sanskrit work of about 2,900 words, catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the commentaries on yoginī tantras.[^dsbc951] It opens with an introductory section (*upodghāta*) that pays homage to Hevajra and then sets out a *sādhana*, a ritual and meditative procedure.[^dsbc951] No established English title appears in the sources consulted, and little independent scholarship on the work could be found.

By its own account the text is drawn from the *Hevajraḍākinījālamahātantrarāja*, and its author names himself as a descendant of Saroruha and a person known as Bhadra, who writes at the instruction of Vajraḍākinī. The practitioner is instructed to prepare under a teacher, to visualise Heruka, to make offerings to a circle of goddesses, and to perform a sevenfold offering, confession and the generation of the awakening mind.[^dsbc951] These details are taken from the opening of the digital text only; the author's identity, the date and place of composition, manuscripts and any Tibetan or Chinese translations could not be verified from the sources available.
