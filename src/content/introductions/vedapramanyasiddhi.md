The *Vedāprāmāṇyasiddhi* ("Establishing the Non-validity of the Veda") is a brief Sanskrit treatise attributed to Jitāri, a Buddhist philosopher of the Dharmakīrti tradition who is generally placed in the later tenth century.[^wikipedia-jitari] It is a polemical work against the claim that Vedic utterances are an authoritative source of knowledge about matters beyond the senses.

The text opens with an inference: whatever has no necessary connection (*pratibandha*) with a given object has no validity with regard to it, and Vedic words have no such connection with external objects. The argument follows the standard Buddhist view that such a connection can rest only on identity of nature or on causal origin, neither of which holds between words and their referents. It then considers and rejects objections, for example that words may be suited to their objects by a natural fitness comparable to that of the eye for colour, or that a convention (*saṅketa*) supplies the link.[^sarit-vedapramanyasiddhi] Little else about the work's transmission, translations or reception could be verified in the sources consulted, and it is known here mainly through the edition named below.
