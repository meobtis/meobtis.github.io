The *Bodhicittotpādasūtraśāstra* is a short Mahāyāna treatise on the arousing of *bodhicitta*, the resolve to attain awakening for the benefit of all beings. It is divided into twelve sections (*varga*), and an English-language page on it gives the title as "The Treatise on Discourses about Giving Rise to the Bodhicitta".[^tbe] The Chinese translator's attribution names Vasubandhu as author, whereas Nanjio's catalogue records a different attribution, to Maitreya.[^tbe]

The twelve sections begin with the motivation to teach and the arousing of the aspiration itself, followed by the vows, then one section on each of the six perfections (generosity, ethical conduct, forbearance, energy, meditative attainment and understanding). The last three sections introduce the teaching on reality (*tathatā*), define emptiness, and treat the acquisition of merit.[^tbe] Its editors note that its method, an ordered series of reflections and an exhaustive set of vows, differs from the ritual format of worship used in Śāntideva's *Bodhicaryāvatāra*.[^tbe]

The attribution to Vasubandhu would place the work in the fourth century CE, close in time to its Chinese translator Kumārajīva (334–413 CE). That translation is described as a sense translation rather than a literal one, and is therefore of limited use for reconstructing any Sanskrit original.[^tbe] No original Sanskrit survives. The Sanskrit text now known is a modern rendering made in 1949 by Bhadanta Śāntibhikṣu at Visva-Bharati from the Chinese; it has unusual syntax and some misprints, and its translator did not claim to restore Vasubandhu's own wording.[^tbe] It was published as "Fa Fu T'i Hsin Ching Lun: Bodhicittotpāda-sūtra-Śāstra of Vasubandhu" in *Visva-Bharati Annals*.[^tbe]

An English translation by Kate Crosby and Andrew Skilton, made from Śāntibhikṣu's Sanskrit, covers only the first three sections (motivation, arousing bodhicitta and vows). It is presented as a reading text pending a more faithful translation from the Chinese.[^tbe] Little else about the text's transmission or reception could be established from the sources consulted.
