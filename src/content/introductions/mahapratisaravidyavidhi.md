The *Mahāpratisarāvidyāvidhi* is a short Sanskrit text of ritual instruction. It opens with homage to the noble goddess Mahāpratisarā and then prescribes how a practitioner, after offerings, is to draw a protective diagram (*cakra*) on birch bark or cloth, with a central syllable surrounded by concentric squares and lotus-petal bands, and to write a specified mantra in each part. The text closes with a colophon stating that the *vidyāvidhi* (spell-manual) of the noble Mahāpratisarā is complete.[^gretil-mpvv] Its edition carries the subtitle "the spell-manual of the great amulet".[^gretil-mpvv]

Mahāpratisarā is a female deity of Vajrayāna and East Asian Buddhism, the principal figure of the group of five protective goddesses (Pañcarakṣā), and is associated with protection and the averting of calamity.[^wiki-mahapratisara] Beyond the edition's own front matter, little is documented about this particular manual: its author, date and manuscript source could not be established from the sources consulted, and no English title or translation is known to the compiler.
