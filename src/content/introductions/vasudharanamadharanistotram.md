The *Vasudhārānāmadhāraṇīstotram* is a Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) addressed to Vasudhārā, the Buddhist goddess of wealth and abundance. It opens with a salutation to the blessed lady, the noble Śrī Vasudhārā, and then proceeds in verse through a long series of epithets and names of the goddess, beginning with forms such as Vasudharī, Vasudhāraṇī and Vasuśrī. The list goes on to identify her with several of the perfections (*pāramitā*), such as the perfection of giving and the perfection of wisdom.[^dsbc218] No English title is established in the literature I could consult; "The Names of Vasudhārā" is a descriptive rendering of the Sanskrit title.

Vasudhārā ("stream of gems") is a goddess of prosperity venerated in Mahāyāna and Vajrayāna Buddhism and especially popular among the Newars of the Kathmandu Valley, according to a general encyclopaedic account.[^wiki] Her Buddhist cult is associated with a dhāraṇī text, and the Tibetan canon contains works on her, including a dhāraṇī (Toh 662) and a practice text (Toh 663).[^toh662][^toh663] Little reliable information could be found about this particular hymn: its author, date and place of composition, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not stated in the edition's record and are not documented in the sources consulted here.[^dsbc218]
