The *Guhyāvalī* ("Series of Secrets") is a short Sanskrit work in verse. The text as transmitted in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon consists of twenty-five numbered stanzas in ornate metres. It opens with the salutation "om namaḥ vajrayoginyai" (homage to Vajrayoginī) and ends with the colophon "iti dauḍīpādīyā guhyāvalī samāptā", that is, the *Guhyāvalī* of Dauḍīpāda is complete.[^dsbc343] The Canon classifies it under the Yoginītantra commentaries (*yoginītantraṭīkā*), which suggests a work attached to the Cakrasaṃvara or a related Yoginī tradition.[^dsbc343]

The verses themselves speak of the experience of joy (*ānanda*) arising in union with a consort (*prajñā*, *mudrā*), of the guru's blessing or empowerment (*adhiṣṭhāna*) as the means by which the practitioner reaches the highest state (*anuttarapada*), and of the devotion to the wheel (*cakra*) that the author says inspires the work. These remarks are taken directly from the Sanskrit text and are not a scholarly analysis.[^dsbc343]

Little reliable information about the work could be found in the sources consulted. The attribution to Dauḍīpāda rests on the colophon alone, and it was not possible here to verify the author's identity, the work's date, any Tibetan or Chinese translation, the manuscripts on which the text is based, or any printed edition. The bibliographic fields for both digital versions in the Canon are empty, so no editor, publisher or year is recorded.[^dsbc343]
