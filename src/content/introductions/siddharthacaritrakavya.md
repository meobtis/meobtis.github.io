The *Siddhārthacaritrakāvya* is a Sanskrit poem in ten cantos (*sarga*) on the life of the Buddha, whose colophons describe it as the great poem (*mahākāvya*) named *Padyacūḍāmaṇi*, composed by Buddhaghoṣa.[^gretil-siddhartha] It is written in the ornate court-poetry style, with a numbered verse sequence in each canto, and opens with an invocation before the narrative begins.[^gretil-siddhartha] This Buddhaghoṣa is a Sanskrit poet and should not be confused with the well-known Pāli commentator of the same name; the electronic text itself carries no further information about him.

The cantos follow the Bodhisattva from the period before his birth through his youth, marriage and the episodes that lead to his renunciation, and the tenth canto ends with his awakening at the foot of the Bodhi tree after the defeat of Māra's assault.[^gretil-siddhartha] I have not found reliable published scholarship describing the work's date, place of composition, manuscripts or printed editions, so these are left unstated here rather than guessed. Little is documented in the sources consulted.
