The *Bauddhatantrokta Siddhauṣadhisaṅgraha*, the title of which may be rendered "Collection of Accomplished Medicines Taught in the Buddhist Tantras", is a short Sanskrit compilation of remedies and ritual procedures. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is catalogued under the category *adhunātana* ("modern") of the *śāstrapiṭaka*, and its record gives the title in a slightly different spelling, *Boudhatantrokta Siddhauṣadhi Saṅgrahaḥ*.[^dsbc876] The record lists no editor, publisher, place or year for a printed source.

The text itself is in verse with occasional prose instructions. It opens with a rubric naming the *Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa Tantra* and then gives short recipes: purgatives and herbal decoctions, preparations from plants such as *triphalā*, sandalwood and musk, and remedies for ailments of phlegm and bile, sometimes combined with a mantra to be recited over the preparation. Later sections are headed as extracts from other tantras (for instance a colophon-like line naming a "tenth" section of a *mahākalparāja*) and include a section on oil preparation (*taila-vidhi*). The orthography is irregular, and several of the Sanskrit forms look non-standard, which suggests the text was copied from a manuscript or notebook rather than from a critical edition.

Nothing could be found in scholarly reference works or library catalogues about this compilation as a distinct work: its compiler, date and place of origin are not documented in the sources consulted. Because the digital text carries the label "modern" and was keyed by a Nepalese scholar in 2020, it is probably a recent compilation or a transcription of a Newar tradition of tantric medical recipes, but this is an inference and not a documented fact. The *Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa Tantra* to which the opening rubric refers is a separate, well-attested Buddhist tantra, dated by its modern translator to about the tenth or eleventh century and preserved in Sanskrit manuscripts, including a Newari-script copy of about 1620.[^84000toh431] A Tibetan translation exists in the Kangyur (Toh 431).[^84000toh431] The medical recipes in the present compilation should not be assumed to appear in that tantra; this has not been checked.
