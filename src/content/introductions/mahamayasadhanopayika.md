The *Mahāmāyāsādhanopāyikā* ("Means of Accomplishing Mahāmāyā") is a very short Sanskrit prose text of the sādhana type, a practice manual for visualising a tantric deity. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is classified among the commentaries on the yoginītantras, and its colophon names the author as Kukkuripāda.[^dsbc346] As transmitted there, it describes the opening preliminaries (purification, a seed-syllable visualisation on a sun disc, offerings, confession, going for refuge), the generation of a four-armed, four-faced wrathful figure called Karuṇācalavajra surrounded by four ḍākinīs (Vajra, Ratna, Padma and Viśva), an exposition of the syllables of the name *hevajra* as aspects of the emptiness of all phenomena, the recitation of mantra, and a closing instruction on how the practitioner then acts.[^dsbc346]

Little reliable information about the work could be found. Kukkuripāda is the name of a tantric adept (mahāsiddha) of legend, to whom some songs in the *Caryāgīti* collection are ascribed, but the sources consulted give no dates for him and do not mention this sādhana, so its attribution, date and place of composition, any Tibetan or Chinese translations, and any printed edition remain undetermined here.[^wiki-kukkuripa] The digital edition's own bibliographical fields are empty, so the manuscript or printed source of the text is not stated.[^dsbc346]
