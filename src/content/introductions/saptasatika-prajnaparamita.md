The *Saptaśatikā prajñāpāramitā* is a short Mahāyāna sūtra of the Perfection of Wisdom (*prajñāpāramitā*) literature. Its name means "[Perfection of Wisdom] in seven hundred lines", and it is also known as the *Mañjuśrīparivarta*, as the opening lines of the digital editions indicate. English translators render the title as *The Perfection of Wisdom in Seven Hundred Lines*.[^84000toh24]

The sūtra is set at Jeta's Grove in Śrāvastī and consists chiefly of dialogues among Mañjuśrī, the Buddha and Śāriputra. It develops the practice of the perfection of wisdom through negation, paradox and wordplay, with the themes of non-arising and non-duality, and it presents a samādhi called "the single array".[^84000toh24]

No firm date of composition is given in the sources consulted. The Chinese translations provide a lower limit: the earliest known version was made by Mantuoluo (Taishō 232) in the early sixth century, followed shortly by one by his collaborator Saṅghapāla (Taishō 233) and, in the seventh century, by Xuanzang (Taishō 220).[^84000toh24] Xuanzang's large Prajñāpāramitā collection, begun in 660, is reported to contain a 700-line sūtra among its parts.[^wiki-prajnaparamita]

The Tibetan translation dates from the late eighth or early ninth century. It appears in the Kangyur both in the Perfection of Wisdom section (Toh 24) and among the Heaps of Jewels (Toh 90), and is listed in the imperial catalogues of the Denkarma and Phangthangma; two Tengyur commentaries attributed to Vimalamitra and Kamalaśīla also exist. The earliest Sanskrit manuscript is a late eleventh-century palm-leaf copy from the Pāla kingdom, and later manuscripts of the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries come from the Kathmandu Valley.[^84000toh24] The digital texts here follow Vaidya's printed Sanskrit edition of 1961.[^gretil-sapta]
