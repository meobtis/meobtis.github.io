The *Sāmānyadūṣaṇadikprasāritā* ("The Exposition of the Direction of the Refutation of Universals"), also known in shorter form as the *Sāmānyadūṣaṇa*, is a short philosophical tract attributed to the Buddhist logician Paṇḍita Aśoka. Its opening verse announces its aim: to refute the universal (*sāmānya*) that others have conceived as pervasive, eternal and one, in order to cut the knot of delusion.[^dsbc301] No established English title for the work was found.

Aśoka is described in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* as a later Indian Buddhist writer inspired by Dharmakīrti and Dignāga, who in this work ridiculed the idea of real universals with the example of the five fingers of one's own hand, which are plainly perceived without any additional "fingerhood".[^sep-dharmakirti] The text accordingly belongs to the Buddhist epistemological (*pramāṇa*) tradition and argues against realist theories of universals, in particular that a single universal is perceived in many individuals. The printed edition by Thakur pairs it with another tract by the same author, the *Avayavinirākaraṇa*, a refutation of the whole (*avayavin*).[^sarit-samanya]

Little else about the work could be verified in the sources available for this introduction. Its date, Aśoka's biography, and any Tibetan or Chinese translations are not documented in the records consulted here, and are therefore left out rather than guessed.
