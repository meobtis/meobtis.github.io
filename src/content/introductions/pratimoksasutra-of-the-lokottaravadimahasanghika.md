This is the *prātimokṣa* (the core code of monastic rules, recited at the fortnightly *poṣadha* observance) of the Lokottaravādins, a branch of the Mahāsāṃghika school. The digital text opens with an invocation and introductory verses, followed by the *nidāna* and the announcements for the fortnightly recitation. It then sets out the categories of offence in the usual sequence: *pārājika* (4 rules), *saṃghātiśeṣa* (13), *aniyata* (2), *niḥsargika-pācattika* (30), *śuddha-pācattika* (92), *pratideśanīya* (4), *śaikṣa* (the training rules), and the seven procedures for settling disputes (*adhikaraṇaśamatha*). It closes with verses attributed to successive Buddhas and a colophon naming the recitation as that of the Lokottaravādin Mahāsāṃghikas of the Madhyoddeśika school.[^gretil-prmo]

Little reliable secondary information could be gathered on this particular recension beyond what the edition's own record states. The Lokottaravāda is described as a sub-school of the Mahāsāṃghika whose best-known work is the *Mahāvastu*, which serves as a preface to its Vinaya.[^wiki-mahasanghika] Manuscripts of a Prātimokṣa-vibhaṅga from a Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravāda monastery at Bamiyan are held in the Schøyen Collection.[^wiki-mahasanghika] The Mahāsāṃghika codes are among the *prātimokṣa* traditions that survive complete, and Charles Prebish's *Buddhist Monastic Discipline* (1996) presents the Sanskrit Mahāsāṃghika and Mūlasarvāstivāda *prātimokṣa* texts.[^wiki-pratimoksa]
