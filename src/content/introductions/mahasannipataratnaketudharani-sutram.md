The *Mahāsannipātaratnaketudhāraṇī* is known in English as *The Ratnaketu Dhāraṇī* (Tohoku 138 in the Tibetan canon). The 84000 project presents it as part of a large body of Mahāyāna sūtras called the Great Collection (*Mahāsannipāta*).[^84000toh138]

According to the introduction to the 84000 translation, the text recounts Māra's attempt to destroy the Buddha, split the Saṅgha and annihilate the Dharma, which the Buddha overcomes. It opens with a version of the conversion of Śāriputra (Upatiṣya) and Maudgalyāyana (Kolita), and includes past-life stories, teachings, miraculous events set largely in Rājagṛha, prophecies of buddhahood, and the dhāraṇīs from which it takes its name.[^84000toh138] The Sanskrit text opens with a short dhāraṇī before the usual "Thus have I heard".

The Sanskrit is incomplete: the English translation was made from the Sanskrit as critically edited by Yenshu Kurumiya (1978), with lacunae supplied from the Degé Tibetan.[^84000toh138] Dutt's edition of the Gilgit manuscripts, on which the digital text is based, marks many gaps with square brackets.[^dsbc17] A Gilgit manuscript of the work is listed as held in the National Archives of India.[^84000toh138] The Tibetan translation is recorded in the Denkarma catalogue of 812 CE, and its colophon names the Indian preceptor Śīlendrabodhi and the translator Yeshé Dé; the 84000 introduction also mentions two Chinese translations.[^84000toh138] Little else about its date and origin is documented in the sources consulted.
