The *Abhisamayālaṅkāravṛtti Sphuṭārthā* is a commentary by Haribhadra on the *Abhisamayālaṅkāra*, the verse treatise on the perfection of wisdom (*prajñāpāramitā*) attributed to Maitreya. The Sanskrit text introduces itself as the work of the teacher Haribhadra on the *Prajñāpāramitopadeśaśāstra*, and is divided into eight chapters, beginning with the chapter on the knowledge of all aspects (*sarvākārajñatā*).[^gretil-sphut][^dsbc278] Its title is sometimes translated "Blossomed Meaning" (Tibetan *'grel pa don gsal*).[^wiki-aa]

As a commentary, it follows the verses of the root treatise and explains the topics into which they organise the perfection of wisdom teachings. The printed edition on which both digital texts rest includes the Sanskrit text of the *Abhisamayālaṅkāra* itself.[^upenn-sphut]

Haribhadra is dated variously: library catalogues describe him as active in the eighth or ninth century.[^upenn-sphut] He also wrote a separate commentary, the *Abhisamayālaṅkārāloka* ("Light for the Ornament"), and an abridgement of it. Wikipedia describes the *Sphuṭārthā* as even more influential than the commentary of Ārya Vimuktisena, and Haribhadra as the most influential Indian figure in the study of the perfection of wisdom in Tibet.[^wiki-aa] The Tripathi edition appears in the Bhoṭa-Bhāratīya granthamālā series (no. 2), with Sanskrit and Tibetan text and a Sanskrit preface.[^upenn-sphut] Little else about the transmission of this particular text could be confirmed from the sources consulted.
