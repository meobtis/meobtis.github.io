The *Pratītyasamutpādahṛdayakārikā* is a very short work in verse. The Sanskrit text available in the digital corpora consists of seven verses, introduced as composed by Nāgārjuna.[^gretil-pshk] The Tibetan canon contains a work of corresponding title, translated by the 84000 project as *Verses on the Heart of Dependent Origination* (Toh 3836), listed in its Middle Way section.[^84000toh3836]

The verses summarise the twelve members of dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) taught by the Buddha. They group the twelve into three categories, afflictions (*kleśa*), actions (*karman*) and suffering (*duḥkha*), and show how these three give rise to one another so that the wheel of existence keeps turning. The text then states that all of this is only cause and effect, with no further being behind it, that empty phenomena arise from empty phenomena, and that one who imagines annihilation even in the subtlest thing fails to understand dependent origination; its closing verse says nothing is to be removed and nothing added, and that one who sees what is real as real is liberated.[^gretil-pshk]

The attribution to Nāgārjuna is stated in the text itself; little reliable documentation of its authenticity, dating or transmission is available in the sources consulted. The editions of the Sanskrit text are described by their front matter, which names a 1997 Sarnath edition that pairs this work with the *Dharmadhātugarbhavivaraṇa*.[^dsbc248]
