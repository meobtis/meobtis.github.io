The *Sekoddeśa* is a short Sanskrit text on consecration or initiation (*seka*, *abhiṣeka*) belonging to the Kālacakra tradition. In the edition keyed here it begins with a salutation to Kālacakra and opens as a dialogue in which Sucandra asks the Blessed One to explain, in brief, the sevenfold and threefold consecrations, and the Buddha replies in verse.[^gretil-seko] The Kālacakra literature is closely tied to initiation: the third of the five chapters of the *Kālacakratantra* deals with preparation and consecration.[^wikikc] The *Sekoddeśa* is described in a general reference work as the most significant fragment of the original tantra, which Nāropa is said to have commented upon.[^wikikc]

Nāropa (Sanskrit also Naḍapāda) is a figure whose dates are disputed, with proposals of about 956–1040 and 1016–1100; the Indian tradition credits him with major works on the Kālacakra, a point largely absent from Tibetan hagiography.[^wikinaropa] A commentary on the *Sekoddeśa* is known as the *Sekoddeśaṭīkā*, whose quotations have been studied by Nihom.[^wikinaropa] Modern scholarship dates the Kālacakra texts to the early eleventh century.[^wikikc] The sources consulted say little more about the *Sekoddeśa* itself, so its precise authorship, date and Tibetan translations are not discussed here. The Sanskrit text has been edited by Raniero Gnoli, and Giacomella Orofino published a critical edition of the Tibetan translations.[^gretil-seko]
