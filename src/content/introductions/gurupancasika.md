The *Gurupañcāśikā* is a short Sanskrit work in verse on the proper attitude towards the spiritual teacher in tantric Buddhism, known in English as the Fifty Verses of Guru Devotion. Its Tibetan title is given as *Lama Ngachupa* (*bla ma lnga bcu pa*), and it is attributed to Aśvaghoṣa.[^wikiguruyoga] The Sanskrit text opens with a salutation to the Buddha and states that the author, having bowed to the teacher's feet, will briefly set out the reverence due to the guru as it is taught in many tantras. It goes on to speak of the vajra master (*vajrācārya*) who has received the highest consecration, and of worshipping the teacher three times a day with flowers and offerings.[^gretil-guru]

Beyond what the text itself says, little reliable information about it was found. The attribution to Aśvaghoṣa is given by the GRETIL record and in Tibetan-tradition sources, but no scholarly study of its authorship, date or Sanskrit manuscripts was available for this introduction, and the name Aśvaghoṣa is borne by several authors, so the attribution should be treated with caution. The Sanskrit text used here is taken from Pandey's 1997 collection of short Buddhist works published at Sarnath.[^dsbc221] A more detailed account of its Tibetan translation and its place in the Tengyur could not be verified.
