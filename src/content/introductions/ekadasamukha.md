The *Ekādaśamukha* is a short dhāraṇī text, called in English the Eleven-Faced Avalokiteśvara Heart Dhāraṇī Sūtra, in which the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara presents to the Buddha a "heart" (*hṛdaya*) formula associated with his eleven-faced form.[^wiki-ekadasamukha] In the Sanskrit text the Buddha is staying at Śrāvastī when Avalokiteśvara approaches him, recalls hearing the formula from earlier buddhas, and then recites it. The text goes on to list benefits of reciting it and gives a series of further mantras for ritual uses such as bathing, offerings, a fire rite and dismissal of the deity.[^gretil-ekadasamukha]

The Sanskrit text survives in a manuscript from Gilgit, discovered in 1931 and dated roughly to the fifth or sixth century; before that the sūtra was known only through Chinese and Tibetan versions. Nalinaksha Dutt edited the text in the first volume of his *Gilgit Manuscripts* (1939), and the printed edition used for the digital texts is a later Delhi reprint.[^wiki-ekadasamukha][^dutt1939] According to the same account, Xuanzang is reported to have translated the sūtra into Chinese in 656 CE and Amoghavajra produced a version of the dhāraṇī around 750 CE, though the catalogue numbers given in that source are not consistent with each other and I could not verify them elsewhere.[^wiki-ekadasamukha] Little else about the origin and transmission of this text was found in sources that could be checked, so details of authorship and date should be treated as uncertain.
