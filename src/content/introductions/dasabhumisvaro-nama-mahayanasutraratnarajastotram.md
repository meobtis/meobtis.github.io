The *Daśabhūmīśvara* is a short Sanskrit text in verse, titled in its heading and colophon a "stotra" (hymn) of the "king of jewels among Mahāyāna sūtras". The text runs to fifty-four numbered stanzas and closes with a brief prose passage. It opens by calling on the listener to hear about the excellent bodhisattvas who are calm, self-controlled and established in knowledge of the path.[^dsbc139]

The closing prose states that the "son of the Jina" has briefly set out the ten bodhisattva stages (*daśabodhisattvabhūmi*), and describes the world-system shaking and heavenly rejoicing as a result. The colophon says the work is taken from the *Bodhisattvacaryāprasthāna*.[^dsbc139] These features suggest a verse summary of the ten-stage teaching known from the *Daśabhūmika*, but no scholarly source on this particular text could be consulted, so its date, authorship, relation to other works and any Tibetan or Chinese translations are not established here.

The digital text is known only from the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which gives it in both romanized and Devanāgarī forms and bases it on a modern printed anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc139]
