The *Nyāyapraveśa* ("Entrance to Reasoning") is a short Sanskrit treatise on Buddhist logic, which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files among its works on *pramāṇa*, the theory of valid knowledge.[^dsbc77] It opens with a verse summarising its subject, which it presents as the proof and refutation of a thesis, together with the faulty counterparts of each, and perception and inference together with their faulty counterparts. The prose then defines the thesis (*pakṣa*), the reason (*hetu*) with its three characteristics, and the example (*dṛṣṭānta*), and goes on to classify fallacious theses, reasons and examples.[^dsbc77]

Reference works connect the text with Śaṅkarasvāmin, who is named as its author in encyclopaedic accounts of the Chinese reception of Indian logic, with a Chinese translation by Xuanzang; it is also reported that Jain scholars wrote commentaries on it, among them Haribhadra.[^wiki-eay][^wiki-haribhadra] These accounts are tertiary, and the questions of the work's authorship, date and relation to Dignāga's logic were not checked in specialist scholarship, so they should be treated with caution. Little more could be verified about the transmission of the work, and the information given here is limited to what the digital editions and these general sources record.
