The *Maṅgalaṣoḍaśastuti* ("sixteen-verse hymn of auspiciousness") is a short Sanskrit *stotra* in sixteen verses. It opens with a salutation to Samantabhadra and each verse ends with a petition that a Buddha, named by an epithet, protect the speaker. According to the colophon of the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) text, it is excerpted from the *Bhadrakalpāvadāna* and is dedicated to Śākyasiṃha, that is, the Buddha Śākyamuni.[^dsbc195]

The verses are a series of brief allusions, apparently to episodes from the Buddha's previous lives and ministry, each paired with a kind of protection sought, for example from famine, poverty, disease, fire, poison or fear. The final verse refers to a journey of Nepalese travellers from Kapilavastu to visit Svayambhū.[^dsbc195] The text is classed under *stotra* in the Svayambhū group of the DSBC. Little reliable scholarly information about the hymn's author, date or transmission could be found, and no English title, translation or discussion was located, so these matters are left open here.[^dsbc195]
