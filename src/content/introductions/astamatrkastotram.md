The *Aṣṭamātṛkāstotram* ("Hymn to the Eight Mothers") is a very short Sanskrit stotra. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed among the stotras and is taken from Pandey's anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc118] The text opens with a salutation to Śrī Vighneśvara (Gaṇeśa) and the eight mothers, then gives one verse for each goddess in turn: Brahmāṇī, Vārāhī, Indrāṇī, Māheśvarī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Cāmuṇḍā and Caṇḍikā. Each verse places the goddess in one of the eight directions and names her mount, her colour and a characteristic attribute, and a closing verse salutes the eight mothers together with their eight sacred seats, trees and Bhairavas.[^dsbc118]

Very little reliable information about this particular hymn could be found: no author, date or place of composition, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation could be identified. The eight Mātṛkās are, however, known in Buddhist ritual practice in the Kathmandu Valley. For example, an undated Nepālākṣara manuscript of an *Aṣṭamātṛkā Pūjā Vidhi*, a manual for worshipping the eight mothers, is catalogued as a Buddhist ritual text.[^samhita] That context suggests how a hymn of this kind may have been used, but it does not establish the origin of this stotra.
