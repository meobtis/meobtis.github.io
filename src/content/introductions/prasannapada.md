The *Prasannapadā* (*Madhyamakavṛtti*, "Clear Words") is a commentary by Candrakīrti on the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*, the foundational verse work of Nāgārjuna.[^hayes2023] Encyclopaedic sources give its Sanskrit title as *Mūlamadhyamakavṛtti-Prasannapadā* and also record its Tibetan and Chinese titles.[^wiki-prasannapada] It follows the twenty-seven chapters of Nāgārjuna's work, as the digital editions here reflect.[^dsbc247]

The commentary opens with a criticism of Bhāviveka and a defence of Buddhapālita, and this opening has made it a key source for the later distinction within Madhyamaka between consequence-based (*prāsaṅgika*) and independent-inference (*svātantrika*) methods.[^hayes2023][^wiki-candrakirti] Candrakīrti refers readers repeatedly to his *Madhyamakāvatāra* for full arguments, which suggests that he wrote that work first.[^hayes2023]

Candrakīrti is placed at the beginning of the seventh century by Hayes, and Wikipedia gives c. 600 to c. 650 (or c. 670).[^hayes2023][^wiki-candrakirti] The *Prasannapadā* survives in Sanskrit as well as in Tibetan translation.[^hayes2023] Several parts of it have been translated into European languages: selected chapters into German by Schayer (1931), five chapters into French by de Jong (1949), twelve chapters into French by May (1959), and the essential chapters into English by Sprung, Murti and Vyas (1979, as *Lucid Exposition of the Middle Way*).[^hayes2023] Little more is documented here about the transmission of the Sanskrit text; the digital editions on this site derive from Vaidya's 1960 Darbhanga edition.[^gretil-prasannapada][^dsbc247]
