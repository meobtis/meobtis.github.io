The *Satyadvayāvatāranāma* is a brief Sanskrit work in verse on the two truths, conventional (*saṃvṛti*) and ultimate (*paramārtha*). The text consists of numbered verses, and the surviving Sanskrit text is short: the GRETIL file is only a few hundred words long.[^gretil-sda] No established English title was found in the sources consulted; the name can be rendered literally as "Introduction to the Two Truths".

The opening verse states that the Buddhas' teaching rests on two truths, the worldly conventional truth and the ultimate truth. The following verses go on to divide conventional truth into two kinds, the false and the "suchness-like" (*tathatā*) conventional, using the example of the moon's reflection in water for the former.[^gretil-sda] The work is therefore a compact treatment of how conventional truth is to be classified.

Both editions give the author as Dīpaṃkara, which is the name of the Indian master Atiśa Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, who is dated to about 982–1054 CE and was a senior scholar at Vikramaśīla before going to Tibet.[^wiki-atisa] A *Satyadvayavatara* appears in a list of his writings on Wikipedia, which cites no sources for that list, so the attribution should be treated with caution.[^wiki-atisa] Little else about the work's date, transmission or reception could be verified from the sources available, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation details were confirmed here.

The digital texts derive from a modern printed edition by Sonam Rabten, published at Sarnath in 2000 under a title meaning "the *Satyadvayāvatāra* and three related works".[^dsbc251]
