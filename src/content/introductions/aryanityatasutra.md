The *Āryānityatāsūtra* ("Noble Sūtra on Impermanence") is a very short Sanskrit discourse, filed in the digital corpora among the Mahāyāna sūtras. In its Sanskrit text it opens in the usual way, with Buddha staying at Śrāvastī in Jetavana, in Anāthapiṇḍada's park, with a large assembly of monks, and then addresses the monks directly.[^gretil-anityata]

The teaching is a sustained statement that all conditioned things (*saṃskārāḥ*) are impermanent, unstable and subject to change, and that life ends in death for every being. The text works through a series of social and cosmic ranks in turn: wealthy householders, kings, ascetics, gods of the desire realm, form and formless gods, then arhats, pratyekabuddhas and fully awakened tathāgatas. For the last three it says that they too have a body that must be laid down. It compares beings to unfired pots made by a potter that are bound to break, and closes with verses on the transience of conditioned things, the fear of death, and the end of every accumulation, union and life.[^gretil-anityata]

The sources consulted here (the edition records and the Sanskrit text itself) do not give an author, date, place of composition or any information about Tibetan or Chinese translations or manuscripts, and no reliable scholarly discussion of the work was found. It is therefore not possible to say more about its history or its parallels in other Buddhist collections. No established English title was found.
