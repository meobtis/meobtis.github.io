The *Dharmadhātudarśanagīti* ("Song on Seeing the Dharmadhātu") is a short Sanskrit verse composition of roughly 148 numbered stanzas, opening with a salutation to the omniscient one and a homage to the dharmadhātu as that on which all beings rest. Its early verses describe the dharmadhātu as hidden, peaceful, free of conceptual elaboration, unborn and unceased, and in its verse 5 compare the unseen purity of the dharmadhātu, mixed with the defilements, to clarified butter that cannot be seen while it is still mixed with milk.[^dsbc901] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the treatises (*śāstrapiṭaka*).[^dsbc901]

The colophon of the text states that it was composed by the great master Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna and translated by the same Indian preceptor, as translator into Tibetan, together with the monk Tshul khrims rgyal ba (Śīlavijaya).[^dsbc901] Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna is the Indian scholar better known as Atiśa (c. 982–1054), who left Vikramaśīla in 1040 and died in Tibet.[^wikipedia-atisa] That attribution rests on the colophon alone: little else about this work could be found in the sources consulted, and no scholarly study, English title, manuscript or printed edition was identified, so its date, transmission and place in Atiśa's corpus are left open here.
