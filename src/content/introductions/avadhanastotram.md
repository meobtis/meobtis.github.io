The *Avadhānastotram*, also given the alternative title *Vandanāstava* ("Hymn of Homage"), is a short Sanskrit stotra in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, where it is filed among the hymns to Avalokiteśvara and taken from Pandey's anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc119] It opens with a salutation to Lokanātha ("Lord of the World"), a name of Avalokiteśvara, and the closing line calls it the *avadhānastotra* of the venerable noble Avalokiteśvara.[^dsbc119]

The first ten verses share a single refrain, in which the speaker bows to the compassionate one. They praise his marks and radiance, his ornaments and lotus seat, and his role in rescuing beings from the ocean of existence and from bad rebirths, and the speaker finally asks to be lifted from suffering. The eleventh verse gives a date in coded numerical terms and the twelfth promises that those who recite the hymn will gain their wishes and reach Sukhāvatī.[^dsbc119]

Very little reliable information about this particular hymn could be found: no author, secure date or place of composition, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation could be identified, and no scholarly discussion was located. What can be said comes from the text itself and from the front matter of the digital edition.[^dsbc119]
