The *Vādasthānāni* (sometimes rendered "Topics of Debate") is a short Sanskrit work attributed to Jitāri, a Buddhist philosopher of the Dharmakīrti tradition who is placed in the later tenth century and is said to have been associated with Vikramaśilā.[^wiki-jitari] The SARIT text occupies only pages 72–84 of its printed edition and opens with a verse announcing that "some topics of debate" are to be set down, followed by a refutation of the universal (*jāti*, *sāmānya*) as something neither identical with nor different from its instances.[^sarit-vadasthana]

According to a summary of Junjie Chu's work on Jitāri, the *Vādasthānāni* follows Dharmakīrti's consciousness-only system and mainly refutes non-Buddhist theories from a Yogācāra standpoint.[^wiki-jitari] The opening section engages Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, Sāṅkhya and Jaina positions on universals and quotes the Nyāya authority Uddyotakara, which is consistent with that description.[^sarit-vadasthana] Little else about the text's date, transmission or translations could be verified from the sources consulted here, and the date and attribution rest on secondary summaries. Interested readers should consult Chu's specialist studies of Jitāri, which could not be read for this note.[^wiki-jitari]
