The *Buddhastotram* ("Hymn to the Buddha") is a short Sanskrit praise poem (*stotra*) of eleven numbered verses addressed to the Buddha Śākyamuni. It begins with the homage "om namo buddhāya" and then salutes the Buddha as one of pure awakening, as a compassionate Tathāgata, and as the banner of the Śākya clan, a rare *audumbara* flower among beings; it speaks of his teaching of the true Dharma to all beings and of his entry into final peace, and the closing verses ask for his protection and for all beings to be happy through his grace.[^dsbc177]

Little reliable information about the work could be found. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon classifies it under *stotra* in the section on Śākyamuni and gives as its source the modern anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey (1994).[^dsbc177] The colophon of the text itself describes it as a "Buddhastotra" composed by a Kuladevatā (a family deity) and belonging to a work called *Bodhisattvasamuccaya*, which suggests that it may be an extract from a larger composition; no date, author, translation or manuscript tradition for it was found in the sources consulted, and these questions are left open here.[^dsbc177]
