The *Sarvabuddhaviṣayāvatārajñānālokālaṁkāra* is a short Mahāyāna sūtra, known in English as "The Ornament of the Light of Awareness That Enters the Domain of All Buddhas" (Toh 100 in the Degé Kangyur) and sometimes abbreviated *Jñānālokālaṁkāra*.[^84000toh100] It is set at Vulture Peak in Rājagṛha, where the Buddha dwells in the Womb of Dharmadhātu Palace.[^84000toh100]

The sūtra proceeds through questions put by Mañjuśrī. It first presents nine analogies for the activity of a buddha, then turns to the nature of awakening. Its summary on 84000 states that the Buddha and all phenomena share the same empty nature, and that an illusion-like buddha teaches beings according to their dispositions, spontaneously and without conceptual elaboration, out of great compassion.[^84000toh100] The translation's introduction links it thematically to the *Laṅkāvatāra*, the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* and the Heart Sūtra, and notes that its symbolism seems closer to the third turning of the wheel.[^84000toh100]

The sūtra was quoted in the *Ratnagotravibhāga* (4.83) and cited by later authors including Bhāviveka, Candrakīrti, Kamalaśīla and Kūkai, who is said to have brought it to Japan from China in 806.[^84000toh100] The English translation was made from Sanskrit chiefly on the basis of the 2005 edition by the Study Group on Buddhist Sanskrit Literature, with reference to Kimura's 2004 transliteration of the manuscript, which is proposed to date from the eleventh to thirteenth century, and was compared with the Tibetan.[^84000toh100] Little else about its date, authorship or Chinese versions could be established from the sources consulted.
