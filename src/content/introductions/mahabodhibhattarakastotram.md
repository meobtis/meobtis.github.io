The *Mahābodhibhaṭṭārakastotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*). In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is catalogued among the stotras of the "vajrāsana" group, a heading that evokes the Vajrāsana, the seat of the Buddha's awakening at Bodh Gayā; the title itself names the "great awakening" (*mahābodhi*) and the venerable lord (*bhaṭṭāraka*).[^dsbc206] The digital text consists of eight verses, each ending with the same refrain, *śrīśākyasiṃhaṃ praṇamāmi nityam* ("I always bow to the glorious Śākyasiṃha"), preceded by the salutation *oṃ namaḥ śākyasiṃhāya*.[^dsbc206]

The verses praise the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha, the "lion of the Śākyas". They dwell on his bodily marks and beauty, such as his hair, forehead, eyes, arms and the "eighty minor marks", and on his epithets, including teacher, son of Māyā, compassionate victor and son of Śuddhodana. The final verse refers to his bringing the world beyond the three kinds of suffering.[^dsbc206]

Little reliable information about the hymn could be found. The DSBC catalogue entry gives no summary, and no author, date, place of composition, manuscript source or translation was identified in the sources consulted, so these remain unknown here.[^dsbc206] The Devanāgarī and romanized files are two renderings of one digital text.[^dsbc684]
