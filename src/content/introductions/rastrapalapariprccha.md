The *Rāṣṭrapālaparipṛcchā*, rendered in English as *The Questions of Rāṣṭrapāla*, is a Mahāyāna sūtra belonging to the Heap of Jewels (*Ratnakūṭa*) collection, where it is numbered Toh 62 in the Degé Kangyur. It is regarded as one of the earliest Mahāyāna sūtras and as one of the few texts of that collection whose Indian original survives.[^84000toh62]

In the sūtra a newly ordained monk, Rāṣṭrapāla, asks the Buddha about bodhisattva conduct. The Buddha answers with sets of four points on qualities such as renunciation, perseverance and meditation on emptiness, and illustrates them with stories of past lives. The central story is that of the prince Puṇyaraśmi, who gives up pleasure, kingdom and riches to follow the bodhisattva path.[^84000toh62] The Sanskrit text used here opens, in the usual manner, at Vulture Peak near Rājagṛha, with a large assembly of monks and bodhisattvas.[^gretil-rastrapala]

The translators of the 84000 version describe the work as having at least two layers: an earlier, mostly prose layer with parallels in the earliest Chinese translation, and a later verse layer found only in the later Chinese and Tibetan versions and in the Sanskrit text.[^84000toh62] Its author and place of composition are not stated in the sources consulted.

The earliest complete Sanskrit manuscript is a Nepalese manuscript now at Cambridge University, edited by Louis Finot and first published in St. Petersburg in 1901; Finot also made the first Western-language translation, and English translations followed from Jacob Ensink (1952) and Daniel Boucher (2008). The sūtra was translated into Chinese at least three times, the earliest extant version being Dharmarakṣa's of 270 CE (Taishō 170), and into Tibetan in the early ninth century by Yeshé Dé with Jinamitra, Dānaśīla and Munivarman.[^84000toh62] The edition behind the digital texts here is the one by P. L. Vaidya (1961).[^gretil-rastrapala]
