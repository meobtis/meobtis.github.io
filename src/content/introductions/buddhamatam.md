*Buddhamatam* ("The Buddhist doctrine") is a very short Sanskrit prose piece of about 5,000 characters. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon lists it among its drama (*nāṭaka*) texts, but the text itself is not a play. It opens with a passage on the performing arts (*nāṭya*), mentioning Bharata's *Nāṭyaveda* and Abhinavagupta's commentary, and then gives a short account of how Buddhism arose in India and a brief biography of the Buddha.[^dsbc270]

In the text, the Buddhist teaching appears at a time when caste rules had hardened and mutual regard among the social classes had declined. It describes the doctrine as a relief for those oppressed by caste, lists its core ethical values (non-violence, truthfulness, non-stealing, compassion and the like), and notes that it shares with the Brahmanical tradition the ideas of heaven, hell, rebirth and liberation while condemning animal sacrifice. The biography then runs from Siddhārtha's birth to Śuddhodana and Māyā, through his marriage, renunciation, austerities and awakening, to his teaching and death, and ends by noting that the Purāṇas regard him as an avatāra of Viṣṇu.[^dsbc270]

The DSBC record leaves the bibliographic fields (title, editor, publisher, year) empty, and the page numbers embedded in the text, which run from 79 to 83, suggest that it is an excerpt from a larger printed work. Its author, date and source could not be established, and no other documentation of the work, translation or English title was found; the Sanskrit gives dates in the Kali era, which indicates a modern composition, but this is only an inference from the text.[^dsbc270]
