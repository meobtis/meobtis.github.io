The *Nṛtyaprasūtiḥ* is a short dance-drama in Sanskrit. The opening line of the text describes it as a "nṛtyanāṭaka" composed by Śrī Subrahmaṇya Dīkṣita (*śrīsuvrahmaṇyadīkṣivena viracitaṃ nṛtyanāṭakam*).[^dsbc273] It begins with a benedictory *nāndī* in praise of the Buddha as a teacher seated at the foot of a tree and turning the wheel of the teaching, and is then divided into scenes (*dṛśya*). Verses and prose stage directions alternate with sung pieces, each headed by the name of a melodic type (*rāga*) such as Gaula, Pantuvarālī or Nītimati.[^dsbc273]

In the scenes available in this edition, the gods, led by Indra, are first troubled by the demons of Tripura and appeal to Śiva. In a second scene Śiva dances at Kailāsa at dusk, the sage Nārada sings the story of the burning of Tripura, and Śiva then announces a newly created science of expression (*abhinaya*) and instructs in the vigorous *tāṇḍava* dance, followed by the gentler *lāsya*.[^dsbc273] Beyond what the text itself states, little reliable information about its author, date, place of composition, manuscripts or printed editions could be found for this introduction, and no scholarly discussion of the work was located; the attribution and dating should therefore be treated as unverified.
