The *Buddhabalādhānaprātihāryavikurvāṇanirdeśasūtra* (the full title in the sources begins *Āryabuddhabalādhānaprātihāryavikurvāṇanirdeśanāma Mahāyānasūtra*) is a short Mahāyāna sūtra, about ten pages in the printed edition. It is known in Sanskrit from the Gilgit manuscripts, which Nalinaksha Dutt edited in the fourth volume of his *Gilgit Manuscripts* (1984).[^gretil-bubal] No established English title was found.

The opening of the text sets the scene on the bank of the Nairañjanā river, where the Buddha is gathered with seven thousand bodhisattvas, including Avalokiteśvara, Vajrapāṇi, Maitreya and Mañjuśrī, and with great śrāvakas and gods. The Buddha enters a samādhi called *buddhāvataṃsaka* and then sends Avalokiteśvara and Vajrapāṇi to the world systems to benefit beings. In the text as read in the digital edition, they enter a samādhi, the earth shakes in six ways, and beings are roused and give up wrong views and pride. The sūtra also uses medical similes for the skilful means (*upāyakauśalya*) of the Buddhas.[^gretil-bubal]

Little else about the work could be verified: its authorship, date and place of composition, Tibetan and Chinese translations and any translations into English were not established from the sources consulted, and further research is needed.
