The *Vajrasattvaniṣpādana Sūtra* ("Sūtra on the Accomplishment of Vajrasattva") is a short Sanskrit work preserved in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon as a single-chapter text, filed under the Mahāyāna sūtras. Its opening lines, however, show that it is a tantric manual rather than a sūtra in the usual sense: a gloss in the first line gives the alternative name *vajrasattvasādhana*, and the text says that practitioners who have heard the *Śrīguhyasamājamahāyogatantra* compose various methods of practice (*sādhana*) based on it.[^dsbc818]

According to the text itself, different vajra masters understand the production of Vajrasattva through different sequences, such as the moon-vajra sequence, the sequence of arising, and the sequence of five-fold awakening, and they also differ on whether the maṇḍala has thirteen, nineteen or twenty-five deities. The compiler then says that, following the lineage of Nāgārjuna and the Guhyasamāja explanatory tantras, he is gathering ("*melāpaka*") the Vajrasattva-accomplishment teaching from the first seventeen chapters of the tantra in order to resolve doubts about the generation stage.[^dsbc818] Beyond what the text says of itself, little reliable information about its author, date, manuscripts or Tibetan and Chinese translations could be found in the sources consulted, so these are left unstated here.

The edition details in the DSBC records point to a modern printed edition by Luo Hong and Toru Tomabechi, published in Beijing by the China Tibetology Publishing House; no year is recorded.[^dsbc818]
