The *Munimatālaṃkāra* (Tibetan *thub pa'i dgongs rgyan*) is a treatise attributed to the Indian Buddhist scholar Abhayākaragupta, who is placed around 1064 to 1125 CE and is said to have lived under King Rāmapāla.[^wiki-abhaya] It is described as based on Maitreya's *Abhisamayālaṅkāra* and as presenting Mahāyāna thought in the light of the Prajñāpāramitā, Madhyamaka and Yogācāra traditions. It survives in Tibetan, where it was widely studied until the fourteenth century, and Ruegg is cited as calling it one of the last major comprehensive treatises of Indian Buddhism.[^wiki-abhaya]

According to the same summary, the text treats the two truths as the most crucial teaching, under which other doctrines can be subsumed, and its last chapter argues for the superiority of the Mahāyāna.[^wiki-abhaya] A composition date is not given there, and little further detail was found in the sources consulted; this account rests on a tertiary summary and should be checked against the scholarship it cites.[^wiki-abhaya]
