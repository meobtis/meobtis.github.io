The *Jñānaprasthāna* ("Establishment of Knowledge") is a Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma treatise, one of the seven canonical Abhidharma works of that school. It is attributed to Kātyāyanīputra; the *Mahāvibhāṣā* treats it as the "root" text, with six other treatises as its "legs". Its main sections concern fetters (*saṃyojana*), knowledge (*jñāna*), action (*karman*), the faculties (*indriya*), the great elements (*mahābhūta*), meditation (*dhyāna*) and views (*dṛṣṭi*), preceded by a section of miscellaneous topics. The work is also said to have introduced the doctrine of the six kinds of conditions.[^wiki-jnana]

It was composed in Sanskrit, but the sources consulted here do not say whether any Sanskrit manuscripts survive, and it is known chiefly through two Chinese translations: Xuanzang's *Apidamo fazhi lun* (Taishō 1544, twenty fascicles) and the earlier *Apitan bajiandu lun* (Taishō 1543, thirty fascicles), which may reflect a different recension.[^wiki-jnana] Xuanzang's tradition places Kātyāyanīputra about three centuries after the Buddha, but the text was probably revised over several centuries.[^wiki-jnana] Little else could be verified, and no established English title was found beyond the literal rendering of the Sanskrit name.
