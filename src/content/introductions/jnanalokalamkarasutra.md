The *Jñānālokālaṃkārasūtra* (full title *Sarvabuddhaviṣayāvatārajñānālokālaṃkāra nāma mahāyānasūtra*) is a Mahāyāna sūtra preserved in Sanskrit, Tibetan and Chinese. In the Tibetan Kangyur it is Toh 100 in the General Sūtra section, and the 84000 project renders its title as *The Ornament of the Light of Awareness That Enters the Domain of All Buddhas*.[^84000toh100]

The sūtra is set on Vulture Peak at Rājagṛha, in a palace called the Womb of the Dharmadhātu. The Buddha speaks to a large assembly of arhats and tenth-level bodhisattvas, and the teaching is announced by a display of light. Mañjuśrī is the principal interlocutor. He asks what is meant by "nonarising" and "noncessation", and the Buddha answers that these terms designate the Tathāgata.[^84000toh100]

The Buddha then uses nine analogies to show how a buddha can appear to beings without truly arising or ceasing: among them a palace reflected in beryl, a cloud, sunlight, a wish-fulfilling jewel, an echo and space. The discourse goes on to describe awakening as peace, freedom from reference points and nonduality, and to distinguish nonconceptual awareness (*jñāna*) from ordinary consciousness (*vijñāna*). It closes by saying that the Tathāgata's nonconceptual awareness gives rise to great compassion that has no reference point.[^84000toh100]

According to the introduction to the 84000 translation, the sūtra is quoted in the *Ratnagotravibhāga*, which draws on its analogies in discussing buddha nature, and it also influenced Madhyamaka authors, Mantranaya literature, Kūkai's visualisation practices and Tibetan Vajrayāna figures.[^84000toh100] The same source proposes an eleventh- to thirteenth-century date for the Sanskrit manuscript on which the modern editions rest, and notes that the Tibetan translation was made centuries earlier and generally agrees with it. I found no source giving the date or authorship of the original composition, or the names of the Tibetan and Chinese translators, so these are left out here.[^84000toh100]
