The *Abhisamayālaṃkāra* ("Ornament of Clear Realization", or "Ornament of Realizations") is a Sanskrit treatise in verse that condenses the Perfection of Wisdom (*Prajñāpāramitā*) sūtras into a systematic account of the path to full awakening. It is counted among the five Sanskrit Mahāyāna treatises that Tibetan tradition ascribes to Maitreya, and it is also known under the fuller title given in this edition, *Abhisamayālaṅkāra-prajñāpāramitā-upadeśa-śāstra*.[^wiki-aa][^bnf1929]

According to one reference summary, the work has 273 verses in eight chapters, matching eight "realizations" or categories divided into seventy topics. The first three categories concern the objects of practice (the three knowledges), the next four the stages of practice, and the eighth the resulting Dharmakāya; the fourth chapter is the longest.[^wiki-aa] The text is not a sūtra but an outline that organises the Perfection of Wisdom literature, and it is read together with commentaries that connect its verses to the sūtras.[^wiki-aa]

Tibetan tradition holds that Maitreya revealed the work to Asaṅga, around the fourth century CE, but this is a tradition rather than an established historical fact. The same source notes that Xuanzang, who studied at Nālandā in the early seventh century, does not mention it, and that the earliest known commentaries are by Ārya Vimuktisena and Haribhadra (late eighth century), so authorship and date remain uncertain.[^wiki-aa] Scholars also disagree on how far the text leans towards Madhyamaka or Yogācāra.[^wiki-aa]

In Tibet the work was translated, with the Indian paṇḍita Amaragomin, by Ngok Lotsawa Loden Sherab, and all Tibetan lineages study it.[^wiki-aa] No early Chinese translation is recorded: Fazun is reported to have translated it only in the 1930s.[^wiki-aa] The Sanskrit text was edited from two Cambridge manuscripts, with Haribhadra's *Āloka* as a further witness, by Stcherbatsky and Obermiller in the series Bibliotheca Buddhica (vol. 23, 1929).[^bnf1929] Conze later published a translation (1954), and Sparham, Brunnhölzl and others have since translated the work with commentaries.[^wiki-aa] The sources consulted are tertiary and bibliographic, so details of the work's history should be checked against specialist scholarship.
