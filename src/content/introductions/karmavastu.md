The *Karmavastu* ("Section on Formal Acts") is one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, a major part of the monastic code (Vinaya) of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school. The Sanskrit text is a short section, edited from a Gilgit manuscript by Nalinaksha Dutt, whose pagination and the manuscript folio references (such as GBM 6.883) are marked in the digital text.[^gretil-karmavastu] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is one of the three Vinaya traditions that survive, alongside those of the Dharmaguptaka and the Theravāda; it is extant in Tibetan and Chinese translations and only partly in Sanskrit.[^wiki-msv] The origins of the school and its relation to the Sarvāstivāda remain debated.[^wiki-msv]

The text is organised, like other Vinaya sections, as narrative episodes introduced by a mnemonic verse (*uddāna*), each ending with a ruling by the Buddha. In the opening episode, as it appears in the Sanskrit, the monk Senāñjaya of Vāsavagrāma is supported by local householders, and the group of six monks (*ṣaḍvargika*) become resentful and impose on him a suspension (*utkṣepaṇīya*) act without first charging him or reminding him of the offence. The Buddha then lays down that such acts must not be carried out improperly, and further rulings in the section treat formal acts performed "not in accordance with the Dharma" (*adharmeṇa*), whether by a divided or a united assembly.[^gretil-karmavastu] Beyond what the text itself and the edition's front matter show, little specific scholarship on this section could be consulted, so its date, Tibetan and Chinese parallels and commentarial reception are not described here.
