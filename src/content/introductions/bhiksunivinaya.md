The *Bhikṣuṇīvinaya* is the Sanskrit rulebook for fully ordained Buddhist nuns (*bhikṣuṇī*) of the Ārya-Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravāda school. It is known chiefly through the critical edition by Gustav Roth, published in Patna in 1970 as volume 12 of the Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series. The edition is subtitled as including the *Bhikṣuṇī-prakīrṇaka* and a summary of the *Bhikṣu-prakīrṇaka*, so that it presents more than the core rules; its Sanskrit text is in Roman script, with an introduction and critical apparatus in English, and the volume runs to 413 pages.[^openlibrary1970]

The text opens with an invocation and a statement that it follows the recitation of the Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravādins of the Madhyadeśa (the "middle country"), and then begins with the story of Mahāprajāpatī Gautamī, who asks the Buddha for permission to go forth and is helped by Ānanda in repeating her request.[^gretil-bhiksunivinaya] The language of the text is a Buddhist hybrid Sanskrit with many Middle Indic forms, which is typical of Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravāda works.

Little further reliable information on the manuscript, the date of composition and the relation of the work to the Chinese and Tibetan vinaya literature could be verified from accessible sources, so these are not described here; readers should consult Roth's introduction in the printed edition for the manuscript evidence and for comparisons with other vinaya traditions.
