The *Ṣaṭtriṃśatsaṃvarastuti* ("Praise of the Thirty-six Saṃvaras") is a brief Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) that the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files under its Cakrasaṃvara section, and it is known there from a single printed source, the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc145] No English title for it is established, and the sources consulted give no description of the work beyond its bibliographic record. No scholarly study, translation, Tibetan or Chinese counterpart or manuscript description was found, so its authorship, date and transmission remain unknown here.

The opening verses of the text itself invoke Śrī Saṃvara, the great hero, together with the yoginī Vārāhī, and then the four attendant goddesses (ḍākinī, lāmā, khaṇḍarohā and rūpiṇī) holding vessels of nectar filled with the awakening mind. The verses that follow each praise a form of Saṃvara associated with a named place, such as Pullīramalaya, Jālandhara, Oḍiyāna, Arbuda and Godāvarī, together with a consort and a place on the body where the deity is visualised.[^dsbc145] This pattern suggests a verse catalogue of the deities of the Cakrasaṃvara maṇḍala and their sacred sites, but that reading is drawn from the Sanskrit text alone and has not been checked against a secondary source.
