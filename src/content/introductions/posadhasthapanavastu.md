The *Poṣadhasthāpanavastu* ("Section on the Suspension of the Poṣadha") is one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, a major part of the monastic code (Vinaya) of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school. It is a short text edited from a manuscript of the Gilgit collection, and the digital text marks both the printed page numbers and the manuscript folios.[^gretil-posadhasthapana] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is extant in a Tibetan translation of about the ninth century and a Chinese one of the eighth century, and only to some extent in Sanskrit.[^wiki-msv]

The section begins with mnemonic verses (*uddāna*) that refer to a monk expelled by Maudgalyāyana from an impure *poṣadha*, the fortnightly recitation of the monastic code (*prātimokṣa*). It then concerns the circumstances in which a monk may or may not "suspend" (*sthāpayati*) the *poṣadha*, that is, halt the recitation by raising a charge against another monk. The text sets out numbered lists of invalid (*adhārmika*) and valid (*dhārmika*) suspensions, distinguishing, for example, a charge made with or without a real basis, and charges of faults in morality, view, conduct or livelihood. It also lists conditions under which a suspension is improper, such as not having seen, heard or suspected the offence concerned.[^gretil-posadhasthapana] The edition's front matter gives no author or date for the section, and I could not consult scholarship on it, so its date, its Tibetan and Chinese counterparts and its reception are not described here.
