The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Śākya Lion", that is, the Buddha) is a short Sanskrit praise poem in nine verses. The qualifier *chando'mṛtoddhṛtam* in its title marks it as "drawn from the *Chandomṛta*", which suggests that it is an extract taken from a work with that name rather than an independent composition; this reading rests on the title alone, and I found no source that describes the *Chandomṛta* or the extract itself.[^dsbc180] No established English title is known.

The verses praise Siddhārtha (Sarvārthasiddha), son of Śuddhodana and Māyā, as the lord of the Śākya line who conquered Māra and is revered by Maheśvara and other gods. The closing verses turn to a narrative about Brahmā offering praise to the Buddha with his four faces, after which the text is declared complete.[^dsbc180] Little else is documented about its author, date, school or place of composition, and no translations or manuscripts could be traced in the sources consulted. The digital text derives from a 1994 printed anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc180]
