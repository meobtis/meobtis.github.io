The *Bhaktiśatakam* ("Hundred Verses of Devotion") is a Sanskrit stotra, a poem of praise, in the Buddhist tradition. The text opens with homage to the Blessed One, the worthy and fully awakened Buddha, and its heading names the author as the *mahāpaṇḍita* Rāmacandrabhāratī.[^dsbc174] The early verses praise a being whose knowledge extends to all things and whose speech is faultless, and they treat the Buddha as the proper object of devotion in contrast to other deities.[^dsbc174]

Little reliable information about the work could be found beyond this. The only documentation consulted here is the edition record, which places the poem first in the *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, a 1994 anthology of Buddhist stotras edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and published by Motilal Banarsidass.[^dsbc174] Its date, the author's biography, its exact length, and any translations into English or other languages have not been established from sources read for this introduction, and the questions are left open rather than guessed at.
