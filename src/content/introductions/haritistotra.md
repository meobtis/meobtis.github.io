The *Hārītīstotra* is a brief Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) in eight verses, each ending with the refrain "I bow with devotion to the queen of the yakṣas, mother of many sons" (*yakṣeśvarīṃ bahusutāṃ praṇamāmi bhaktyā*), followed by a ninth verse promising benefits to whoever recites it. The text is headed *hāratīstotram* and opens with homage to the goddess Hāratī (Hārītī); it describes her as richly adorned, embracing her children, seated within a fire-circle on a jewelled throne, attended by yakṣas, gandharvas and kinnaras, and devoted to the Buddha's teaching. It asks her to dispel fear, sickness and epidemic, and to guard children. The closing colophon says that the hymn "came forth from Śrībhavaratna" and "protects children".[^dsbc152]

Hārītī is a figure of Buddhist legend, a yakṣī or rākṣasī who preyed on children until the Buddha converted her, after which she became a protector of children and of childbirth.[^wiki-hariti] Nothing reliable about this particular hymn's date, author or transmission was found beyond what its editions record: it appears in Pandey's modern anthology of Buddhist hymns, and the name given in the colophon may refer to the compiler or source of the verses, which could not be confirmed. No established English title was found.[^dsbc152]
