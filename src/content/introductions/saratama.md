The *Sāratama* (more often *Sāratamā*) is a Sanskrit pañjikā, a detailed explanatory commentary, on the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā*, the Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines. The opening lines of the text give it the title "the pañjikā called Sāratamā on the noble Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā" and attribute it to Ratnākaraśānti.[^gretil-saratama] No widely used English title is established, so the Sanskrit name is used here.

Ratnākaraśānti was a Yogācāra philosopher and tantric scholar of the late tenth to mid-eleventh century who is associated with Vikramaśīla, and the Sāratamā is counted among his commentaries on Perfection of Wisdom literature, written from his Yogācāra standpoint.[^wiki-rs] In a comparative study, Jaini notes that the Sāratamā and Haribhadra's *Ālokā* are the only two commentaries on the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* that survive in the original Sanskrit. He dates the *Ālokā* to the ninth century and the Sāratamā to the eleventh.[^jaini1972] The commentary is arranged by the chapters of the sūtra; the GRETIL text begins with the first chapter, on the practice of omniscience (*sarvākārajñatācaryā*), and takes up the sūtra's topics through summarising verses.[^gretil-saratama]

Jaini reported that the Sanskrit text was then awaiting publication in the Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series at Patna, and that it rested on two incomplete manuscripts photographed by Rāhula Sāṃkṛtyāyana in 1937, now in the library of the Bihar Research Society.[^jaini1972] The printed edition appeared in 1979.[^gretil-saratama] The GRETIL text marks lacunae and doubtful readings with dots and question marks, reflecting the damaged state of the manuscripts. Beyond these points, little reliable information on the work's contents, transmission or reception was found in the sources consulted, and nothing is claimed here about a Tibetan or Chinese translation.
