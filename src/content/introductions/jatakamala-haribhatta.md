The *Jātakamālā* ("Garland of Birth Stories") of Haribhaṭṭa is a Sanskrit retelling of tales of the Buddha's previous lives. It is composed in the *campū* form, which mixes prose with ornate verse, and it is generally described as modelled on the older verse *Jātakamālā* of Āryaśūra, which in turn influenced Haribhaṭṭa and Gopadatta, who each wrote a *jātakamālā* of their own.[^wikipedia-jatakamala] The first tale in the digital text is the Prabhāsajātaka, which opens with a verse in praise of the aspiration to buddhahood and then begins the story of King Prabhāsa in the city of Prabhāvatī.[^gretil-haribhatta]

Little reliable information about the work could be gathered for this introduction. A modern edition of seventeen legends from Haribhaṭṭa's collection, edited by Michael Hahn, appeared in New Delhi in 2011 under the title *Poetical Vision of the Buddha's Former Lives*.[^wikipedia-jatakamala] The date of the author, the full extent of the collection and the state of its manuscripts are not documented in the sources consulted, and they are left unstated here rather than guessed.
