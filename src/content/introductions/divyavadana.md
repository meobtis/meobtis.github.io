The *Divyāvadāna* ("Divine Narratives", known in English as *Divine Stories*) is a Sanskrit anthology of Buddhist *avadāna* tales, which the DSBC and GRETIL texts divide into thirty-eight numbered stories.[^dsbc364][^wiki-divy] Many of the stories are drawn from Mūlasarvāstivādin *vinaya* literature. In them the Buddha typically explains how a person's earlier actions led to their present fortunes, with particular emphasis on the merit of offerings to awakened beings and at stūpas.[^wiki-divy] The collection opens with the Koṭikarṇāvadāna, a tale set in Śrāvastī at the Jetavana monastery.[^gretil-divy]

Dating the work is difficult. Some of its stories may be as old as the second century CE, while the collection in its present form is not attested before about the seventeenth century; one component, the Aśokāvadāna, is said to have been compiled in the fourth or fifth century.[^wiki-divy] This summary rests on a tertiary source and should be treated cautiously.

The first Western edition, by Cowell and Neil (1886), was prepared from Nepalese manuscripts in Cambridge and Paris, and P. L. Vaidya published a later edition in Darbhanga in 1959.[^wiki-divy] English translations include Joel Tatelman's *Heavenly Exploits* (2005; stories 1, 2, 30 and 36), and Andy Rotman's *Divine Stories* (2008; stories 1–17) and *Divine Stories, Part 2* (2017; stories 18–25, 31, 32 and 34–37).[^wiki-divy] Little further reliable documentation (for example on Tibetan and Chinese parallels) could be verified for this introduction.
