The *Avalokiteśvarastava* is a brief Sanskrit hymn of praise addressed to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. The text opens with a salutation ("om namo 'valokiteśvarāya") and runs to nine verses, each of four lines in a flowing, uniform metre.[^dsbc122]

The verses give a portrait of the bodhisattva in the manner of a visualisation. They describe an image of Avalokiteśvara with the Buddha Amitābha in his crown, a golden lotus in his left hand, matted hair, a full moon-like face, lotus-shaped eyes, a deerskin over his left side and ornaments of jewels. They go on to praise his qualities: he removes fever and illness, defeats the forces of Māra, brings about the ten perfections, awakens the understanding of non-dual suchness, and delights in dwelling at Potalaka with compassionate eyes.[^dsbc122]

The closing line of the text states that it is a hymn to the venerable noble lord Avalokiteśvara composed by the nun (*bhikṣuṇī*) Candrakāntā; the title in the DSBC record repeats this attribution.[^dsbc122] No independent information about Candrakāntā, the date or place of composition, or a Tibetan or Chinese translation was found in the sources consulted, so these matters should be regarded as unknown here.

The romanized and Devanāgarī texts held by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon were prepared in 2004 from the printed collection of Buddhist hymns edited by Pandey (1994) as named in the romanized record.[^dsbc122][^dsbc608]
