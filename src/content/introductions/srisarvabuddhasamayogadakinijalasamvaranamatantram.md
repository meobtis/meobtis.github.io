The *Śrīsarvabuddhasamāyogaḍākinījālasamvaranāmatantra* ("the tantra named Ḍākinījālasaṃvara, the union of all the Buddhas") is a Buddhist tantra classified in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the Yogottara tantras.[^dsbc933] Its Tibetan counterpart is catalogued by 84000 as Toh 366, "The Higher Tantra of the Deity Ḍākinījālaśaṃvara, Who is the Union of All the Buddhas", among the Unexcelled Yoga Tantras.[^84000toh366] The Sanskrit text is a single unit in verse, with numbered stanzas that run past 160 and a numbered series of sections.[^dsbc933]

From the verses themselves, the text opens with homage to Vajrasattva and presents him as the supreme deity in whom all buddhas are united, and later passages describe a series of hand-gesture mudrās, each followed by promised results such as purification of sin and the attainment of buddhahood.[^dsbc933] Little else about the work is documented in the sources consulted: no reliable account of its authorship, date, place of composition, manuscripts, printed editions or translations beyond the 84000 catalogue entry could be verified, so none is given here.
