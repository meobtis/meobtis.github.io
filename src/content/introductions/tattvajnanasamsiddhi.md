The *Tattvajñānasaṃsiddhi* ("Perfect Accomplishment of the Knowledge of Reality") is a compact Sanskrit work of Buddhist tantric ritual. It opens with homage to the blessed Vajravārāhī and to the lineage, and its verses state that the author sets out a *sādhana* (a practice of visualisation and recitation) of Vajravārāhī, composed in modest terms for the benefit of practitioners.[^gretil-tjs] No established English title was found in the sources consulted.

The text divides into short sections on the procedure of meditative cultivation (*bhāvanāvidhi*), on the rite of favouring disciples, including its benefits (*śiṣyānugrahavidhi*), and on the extraction of mantras (*mantrauddhāravidhi*). A closing colophon calls it the *Tattvajñānasaṃsiddhi*, also described as *svādhiṣṭhāna*, which suggests it concerns self-generation as the deity; this reading is tentative.[^gretil-tjs]

Attribution is uncertain. The GRETIL record names Śūnyasamādhipāda as author, whereas the opening verses of the text name the writer as a monk Jñānendra (or Jñānendracandra) Vasuśarman, a devotee of the feet of Vajradevī; how these names relate was not established.[^gretil-tjs] Date, place and any Tibetan or Chinese translation could not be verified from the sources available, and little reliable information about the work was found. The digital texts derive from Janardan Pandey's printed edition, which presents the root text with a commentary by Vīryaśrīmitra, the *Marmakalikāpañjikā*.[^gretil-tjs][^dsbc352]
