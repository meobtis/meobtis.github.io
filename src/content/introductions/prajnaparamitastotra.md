The *Prajñāpāramitāstotra* is a brief Sanskrit hymn in praise of the Perfection of Wisdom (*prajñāpāramitā*), addressed as a female figure. The edition keyed from Pandey's anthology numbers twenty-one verses, mostly in the *anuṣṭubh* metre, and closes with a colophon saying that the hymn is complete.[^dsbc170] The GRETIL record attributes it to Rāhulabhadra, whereas the DSBC text, in its opening and closing lines, ties it to a figure called Lakṣā or Lakṣābhagavatī; the two records therefore do not name the author in the same way.[^gretil-psp][^dsbc170]

The verses open with homage to the Perfection of Wisdom as free of conceptual construction, and then develop familiar themes of the Prajñāpāramitā literature in devotional language. She is likened to space, without elaboration and beyond words, and whoever sees her truly is said to see the Tathāgata. She is called the mother and nurse of the heroic bodhisattvas and, because the buddhas are her sons, the grandmother of all beings; she is the single path to liberation followed by buddhas, pratyekabuddhas and śrāvakas. Other verses stress that she neither comes nor goes and that she can be spoken of only conventionally, so that praising her is, in the poet's own paradox, praising what cannot be praised. The final verse dedicates the merit of the praise to the wish that the whole world may be devoted to the far shore of wisdom.[^gretil-psp]

Beyond what the two digital editions themselves record, little is documented here: I did not find reliable sources on the date and identity of the author, on Tibetan or Chinese translations, or on commentaries, so these are left unstated rather than guessed. The GRETIL edition is based on Michael Hahn's 1988 study of two texts from the Phudrag Kanjur, which suggests that the hymn is also known in the Tibetan tradition, but this is an inference from the edition's source line only.[^gretil-psp]
