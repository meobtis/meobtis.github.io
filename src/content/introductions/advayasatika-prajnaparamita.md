The *Advayaśatikā prajñāpāramitā* is a very short Perfection of Wisdom sūtra in Sanskrit. It opens in the usual way, with the Buddha at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak with a large assembly, and then consists of a brief dialogue between the Buddha and Subhūti. The Buddha says that those who wish to train should hear and master the perfection of wisdom, because all the qualities of the path are contained in it. These include the perfections, the kinds of emptiness, the factors of awakening and the qualities of a buddha. Subhūti twice exclaims that this perfection of wisdom is hard to fathom, and the Buddha replies by calling it utterly free and utterly pure. He adds that it is difficult to accept for those with bad companions or little merit, and that those who teach it will be upheld by the buddhas of the three times. The closing colophon calls the text a *dhāraṇī sūtra*.[^gretil-advaya]

Little reliable information about the work's date, authorship, manuscripts or translations could be found in the sources consulted, and no English translation or Tibetan or Chinese catalogue entry could be identified. It should not be confused with the *Adhyardhaśatikā prajñāpāramitā*, a different text with a similar-sounding title. The digital editions listed here both derive from the same printed edition, by M. B. Shakya, published in Nepal in 1988.[^dsbc67]
