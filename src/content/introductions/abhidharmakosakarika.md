The *Abhidharmakoṣakārikā* (Verses on the Treasury of Abhidharma) is a verse summary of the tenets of the Sarvāstivāda Abhidharma, arranged in eight chapters of roughly 600 verses in all.[^tbe] It is the root text of Vasubandhu's *Abhidharmakoṣa*; the prose auto-commentary, the *Abhidharmakoṣabhāṣya*, expounds the verses and often criticises them from a Sautrāntika standpoint, and a ninth, prose chapter refutes the Pudgalavāda notion of a "person".[^tbe] The work is traditionally dated to the fourth or fifth century CE.[^tbe]

The Sanskrit text of the verses and the commentary was long known mainly through Chinese and Tibetan translations; the Chinese versions are attributed to Paramārtha (sixth century) and Xuanzang (seventh century).[^tbe] In the 1930s Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana reconstructed the verses in Sanskrit from Tibetan, and on a later journey he found a palm-leaf manuscript of 367 leaves containing the verses together with the commentary.[^tbe] P. Pradhan published the commentary, including the verses, in 1967, with a revised second edition in 1975 in the Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series.[^mejor2024] The textual state of the verses was earlier discussed by V. V. Gokhale (1946).[^mejor2024] Complete English translations of the work exist by Leo M. Pruden (1988), Gelong Lodrö Sangpo (2012) and Masahiro Shōgaito (2014), the last made from the Uyghur version of Xuanzang's Chinese.[^tbe]
