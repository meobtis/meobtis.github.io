The *Paramārthastava* ("Hymn on the Ultimate") is a short stotra of eleven verses addressed to the Buddha. The text in this index opens by asking how one can praise a lord who is unproduced, without foundation, and beyond the reach of speech, and then proceeds by negation: the Buddha is neither existent nor non-existent, neither eternal nor impermanent, neither far nor near, and abides in neither saṃsāra nor nirvāṇa. Towards the end the hymn questions the act of praise itself, since all things are empty and there is no one who praises and nothing praised, and it closes by dedicating the merit so that the world may attain a good rebirth.[^dsbc156]

The hymn is classed here among the Madhyamaka stotras. Hymns of this kind are commonly associated with Nāgārjuna and with the "four hymns" (*Catuḥstava*), but the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy notes that it is debated which of the hymns ascribed to him make up that group, and it does not discuss the *Paramārthastava* individually.[^westerhoff] Little reliable information on this hymn's date, authorship, manuscript transmission, or Tibetan and Chinese translations could be found in the sources consulted, so those matters are left open here.
