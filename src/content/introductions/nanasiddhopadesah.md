The *Nānāsiddhopadeśa* ("Teachings of Various Siddhas") is a very short Sanskrit text, only a few lines per entry. It is arranged as a series of brief instructions, each introduced by the name of a teacher in the genitive, such as Padmāṅkura, Prajñārakṣita, Nelīpāda, Ravigupta, Kṛṣṇācārya, Sarahapāda, Mahīdhara, Bhūripāda, Vīṇāpāda, Virūpācārya, Nāgabuddhi, Diṅnāga, Mātali and Matsyendra. A closing section headed "uddeśa-samuccaya" gathers the instructions into a single summary, and the text ends with a colophon naming it.[^dsbc79]

The instructions concern meditative yoga of the subtle body: they speak of the "wheels" (*cakra*) of the body, the syllables *oṃ*, *haṃ* and *hūṃ*, the inner fire called caṇḍālī, the melting of *bodhicitta* in the form of a drop (*bindu*), and breath control, ending in union (*samarasa*) and bliss. The Sahajayāna ("innate vehicle") classification is that of the digital canon.[^dsbc79] The named figures belong to the group of Buddhist tantric adepts known as mahāsiddhas, who are conventionally counted as eighty-four and are usually placed in medieval India, roughly 750 to 1150 CE, though they remain elusive to historians.[^wiki-mahasiddha] The text's author or compiler, date and place are not stated in the edition, and I found no reliable scholarly discussion of this work, so nothing further can be said with confidence about its origin, manuscripts or translations.
