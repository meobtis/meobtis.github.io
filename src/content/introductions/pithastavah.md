The *Pīṭhastavaḥ* ("Praise of the Seats") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) preserved in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, where it is classified under the *stotra* literature of the Cakrasaṃvara cycle.[^dsbc144] The romanized text runs to some fifty-odd numbered verses and closes with the colophon "śrīpīṭhastavastotraṃ samāptam", that is, the hymn of the glorious seats is complete.[^dsbc144]

Judging from the text itself, the hymn opens with a verse invoking the Mothers (*mātaraḥ*), including Brāhmaṇī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Aindrī and Cāmuṇḍā, and then salutes Gaṇapati. It goes on to pay homage to a series of goddesses, each tied to a seat (*pīṭha*) in a particular direction or region, such as the eastern, northern, south-western and north-eastern seats, in the refrain "namo 'stu te" ("homage to you").[^dsbc144] This pattern of directional seats and attendant goddesses is characteristic of tantric mandala-based worship, which is consistent with its placement in the Cakrasaṃvara section, but the present account rests on the edition alone.

Little reliable information about the work could be found. Its author, date, place of composition, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not documented in the sources consulted, and no established English title or scholarly study was located, so none is asserted here. The only bibliographic information available comes from the DSBC record, which names the printed source as the *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and published by Motilal Banarsidass at Varanasi in 1994.[^dsbc144]
