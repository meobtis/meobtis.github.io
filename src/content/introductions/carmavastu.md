The *Carmavastu* ("Chapter on Leather") is the fifth of seventeen chapters of the Vinaya of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school, known in Tibetan translation as *The Chapters on Monastic Discipline*. In English it is called *The Chapter on Leather*.[^84000toh15] The Sanskrit is preserved in the fragmentary Gilgit manuscript, which was found in the 1930s in what is now northern Kashmir and is dated to roughly the sixth or seventh century.[^84000toh15]

The chapter opens in the frontier region of Aśmaka, where the monk Mahākātyāyana sends his pupil Śroṇa Koṭīkarṇa to the Buddha with a message describing five difficulties faced by monks in border regions. The Buddha responds with five allowances: ordination by a quorum of five monks, regular bathing, single-lined sandals, leather ground-spreads, and no blame for robes lost in transit. The chapter goes on to relax rules concerning beds, seats, carriages and footwear for the sick, the elderly and others in need, and includes stories of Upananda and the group of six monks.[^84000toh15] The text of the Sanskrit edition begins with the birth and early life of Śroṇa Koṭīkarṇa, the son of the householder Balasena.[^gretil-carmavastu]

The frame story of Śroṇa Koṭīkarṇa also appears in the *Divyāvadāna*, where it is among the avadānas drawn from the Vinaya chapters.[^84000toh15] The chapter is further known to have been translated into Chinese by Yijing (635–713), who translated seven chapters of the Vinaya, and into Tibetan around the first decade of the ninth century.[^84000toh15] Nalinaksha Dutt's edition of the Sanskrit manuscript is the printed basis of the digital text.[^gretil-carmavastu] Little further scholarship specific to this chapter was consulted for this introduction.
