The *Garbhasaṃgraha* is a very short Sanskrit work in verse. In the digitised text it consists of fourteen numbered stanzas, an opening salutation to the greatly compassionate one, and a colophon naming the work.[^gretil-gs] Little documentation about the text could be found beyond the edition records, and no established English title was identified.

The opening verses describe the suffering of the three worlds, which they trace to actions, afflictions such as desire and aversion, and wrong ways of grasping at things. The later verses turn to training in the path, the cultivation of compassion and the mind of awakening (*bodhicitta*), and end with an exhortation to rely on a good spiritual friend and to take up the "garbha" so as to liberate oneself and others.[^gretil-gs] The text calls itself the "heart of all buddhas".

The colophon attributes the work to the master Dīpaṅkaraśrījñāna, that is, Atiśa, and the printed edition from which both digital texts derive is a collection of eleven works ascribed to him.[^gretil-gs][^dsbc265] Atiśa is usually dated to about 982–1054 and is remembered as a teacher from Bengal who taught at Vikramaśīla and later in Tibet.[^wiki-atisa] Whether the attribution is secure, and whether Tibetan translations of the text exist, was not established from the sources consulted.
