The *Mahāsaṃnipātaratnaketudhāraṇīsūtra*, also called the *Ratnaketuparivarta* (abbreviated RKP in the digital edition), is a Sanskrit Buddhist text that combines narrative sūtra material with dhāraṇī formulae. The edition that this text reproduces is a critical edition of the Sanskrit manuscript text published in Kyoto in 1978, and the transcription records that the first four folios of the manuscript are lost.[^gretil-rkp] A further digital text of the work is based on the Gilgit manuscripts as edited by Nalinaksha Dutt, in which the work is set out in eleven chapters (*parivarta*) and opens with a dhāraṇī and homage to buddhas.[^dsbc17]

In the portion preserved in the verse and prose of the Sanskrit text, the Buddha is at Rājagṛha when the wandering ascetics Upatiṣya and Kolita, who are about to take ordination, are approached by Māra, who tries to dissuade them with arguments that there is no result of good and bad action. The text presents Māra's verses and the ascetics' replies in the course of this encounter, with the elder Aśvajit also appearing in the narrative.[^gretil-rkp][^dsbc17]

Little else about the work's date, authorship, school or translations could be verified from the sources consulted for this introduction, so none is asserted here. Readers should consult the printed editions named above and specialist studies of the Ratnaketu texts.

### Manuscripts in the Schøyen Collection

Fragments of the *Ratnaketuparivarta* in the Schøyen Collection were published by Chanwit Tudkeao, who notes that the text must once have been of considerable importance, at least in Central Asia, because of its protective *dhāraṇīs*, and that it belongs to the *Mahāsannipāta* collection, of which only a few sūtras survive in Sanskrit, mostly in fragments.[^tudkeao2016]
