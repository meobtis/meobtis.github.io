The *Tarkabhāṣā* is a short Sanskrit handbook of Buddhist epistemology and logic (*pramāṇa*), attributed to the scholar Mokṣākaragupta. Its opening verse pays homage to the teacher and the Lord of the World (Lokeśa) and states that the work is composed so that the view of Dharmakīrti may be heard by beginners of modest intelligence.[^gretil-tarkabhasa] It is therefore a primer in the tradition of Dharmakīrti rather than an independent treatise.

The text begins with a general definition of a means of valid cognition: a cognition that is correct and reveals something not previously known, free of doubt and error and therefore non-deceptive. It then takes up objections concerning how such a cognition can be said to lead a person to its object, given that things are momentary.[^gretil-tarkabhasa] The editions divide the work into sections, the first of which deals with perception (*pratyakṣa*).[^dsbc322]

Little further is documented in the sources consulted here. Stanford's entry on Dharmakīrti lists Yuichi Kajiyama's annotated translation of the *Tarkabhāṣā*, published in 1966 in the Memoirs of the Faculty of Letters, Kyoto University, under the title *An Introduction to Buddhist Philosophy*, and notes that Mokṣākaragupta belongs to the later followers of Dharmakīrti's school.[^tillemans2026] The date of the author, the Tibetan and Chinese translations and the manuscript tradition were not verified for this introduction.
