The *Ādikarmāvatāra* ("Introduction to the First Practices") is a short Sanskrit work in prose with a few opening verses. In the digital edition it is headed as composed by the learned elder (*paṇḍitasthavira*) Mañjukīrti, and it opens with a salutation to the Buddha and two verses on the rarity of a Buddha's appearance and of a human birth free from the eight unfavourable conditions.[^dsbc930] No English title or translation was found, and little scholarship on the work could be located.

The text that follows is a ritual manual for a beginner. It prescribes that a faithful layman, on an auspicious day, should make offerings before an image of the Tathāgata, approach his teacher, and ask for the confession of wrongdoing, the three refuges, the lay (*upāsaka*) vow and the arousing of the thought of awakening (*bodhicitta*). It then supplies the formulae for the teacher and pupil to recite, including the confession, the refuge in Buddha, Dharma and Saṅgha, and the five precepts.[^dsbc930] The identity of the author, the date and the manuscripts on which the text rests are not stated in the edition, and the author's name should be treated as the edition's attribution rather than as a verified fact.
