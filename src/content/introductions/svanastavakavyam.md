The *Śvānastavakāvya* ("Poem in Praise of the Dog") is a short Sanskrit poem in verse, catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the miscellaneous (*vividha*) works of the treatise section. Its opening verses praise Gautama, the Bodhisattva, as protector of dogs, and describe how he freed his kin from death and granted them fearlessness; later verses turn to a dog born in his lineage, who is devoted and loyal to its master in hardship.[^dsbc747]

Little reliable information on the work could be found beyond the edition record. Its author, date and place of composition, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation could not be established from the sources consulted, and no established English title is known, so these matters are left open. The digital text is based on the 2002 edition *Śikhisamuccayaḥ* named in the record, a Vienna publication edited by Dragomir Dimitrov, Ulrike Roesler and Roland Steiner.[^dsbc747]
