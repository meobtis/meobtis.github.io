The *Śrīhevajrabhaṭṭārakasya Cakraviṃśatikāstotra* ("hymn of the twenty-verse circle of the Blessed Lord Hevajra", abbreviated *cvs* in the text) is a short Sanskrit stotra in praise of Hevajra, the central deity of the Hevajra cycle of Buddhist yoginī tantras. No established English title was found. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) files it under the yoginītantra commentarial literature (*yoginītantraṭīkā*) within its *śāstrapiṭaka* division.[^dsbc956]

The text opens with an homage to Śrī Hevajra and consists of twenty numbered verses followed by a concluding verse of dedication of merit. The opening verses praise Hevajra as the non-dual union of wisdom and means, embracing Nairātmyā, and describe his iconography: eight faces, sixteen arms and a posture of dance. The later verses venerate the goddesses of his retinue, each in turn, and the twentieth praises the lord who emerges from a pair of seed-syllables on a lunar and solar disc.[^dsbc956]

The colophon states that the work is by the *siddhācārya* Saroruhapāda; it is the only information about authorship, date or place in the text, and no independent source on the hymn, its Tibetan or Chinese translations, or its manuscripts could be consulted for this introduction, so little more can be said with confidence here.[^dsbc956]
