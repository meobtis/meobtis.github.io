The *Triśatikāyāḥ prajñāpāramitāyāḥ kārikāsaptatiḥ* ("seventy verses on the Triśatikā Prajñāpāramitā") is a short Sanskrit work in verse, numbered as kārikās, that comments on the *Triśatikā Prajñāpāramitā*, the "Perfection of Wisdom in Three Hundred Lines", better known as the *Vajracchedikā* or Diamond Sūtra. Its verses open with the benefits, aims and practice of the bodhisattva path as presented in that sūtra, and the digital text preserves a numbered verse sequence (TśPk_1 onwards).[^gretil-tspk] Little scholarship on its contents was available to the compiler of this note, so no detailed account of its argument is given here.

The work is attributed to Asaṅga, a Yogācāra author usually placed in the fourth century CE, although the date is uncertain and some references give the fifth.[^wiki-asanga] Modern scholars do not widely accept the attribution of this *Kārikāsaptati* to him.[^wiki-asanga] The Diamond Sūtra itself was already the subject of commentaries attributed to Asaṅga and Vasubandhu by the early fifth century, when it was first translated into Chinese, and it was also translated into Tibetan.[^wiki-vajra] The Sanskrit text was edited by Giuseppe Tucci in *Minor Buddhist Texts*; details of other translations, manuscripts and the work's reception could not be verified from the sources consulted and are therefore left out.[^gretil-tspk]
