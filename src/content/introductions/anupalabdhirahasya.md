The *Anupalabdhirahasya* ("The Secret of Non-apprehension") is a short Sanskrit work of Buddhist epistemology and logic. The text opens with a verse announcing that it will gather in one place what is scattered in the tradition concerning non-apprehension (*anupalabdhi*), and then proceeds in prose, taking up the claim that non-apprehension is the third kind of logical mark (*liṅga*) used to establish a negation.[^dsbc731]

The work is attributed to Jñānaśrīmitra, a Buddhist philosopher of the Dharmakīrti tradition who is generally placed around the turn of the first millennium; a recent encyclopaedic summary gives about 975 to 1025 CE and describes him as a gate-scholar (*dvāra-paṇḍita*) of the monastery of Vikramaśīla.[^wikipedia-jnanasrimitra] Dates vary between sources, so these should be taken as approximate. The same summary lists the *Anupalabdhirahasya* among his works, alongside the *Sarvaśabdābhāvacarcā* and a number of other treatises on logic, perception, exclusion (*apoha*) and idealism.[^wikipedia-jnanasrimitra]

The text is known mainly through Birgit Kellner's critical edition, which presents the *Anupalabdhirahasya* together with the *Sarvaśabdābhāvacarcā* and a survey of Jñānaśrīmitra's theory of non-apprehension; it was published in Vienna in 2007.[^dsbc731] I could not consult the edition's introduction directly, so details of the manuscript basis, the work's reception and any Tibetan translation are not stated here. No established English title for the work was found, and little further information about it was available in the sources consulted.
