The *Lalitavistara* (in English usually *The Play in Full*) is a Mahāyāna sūtra that tells how the Buddha manifested in the world and attained awakening. It is divided into 27 chapters and mixes prose with verse, and its narration shifts between the third and the first person.[^84000toh95]

The narrative covers the Buddha's birth, childhood and youth in the Śākya palace, his departure from home and austerities, his victory over Māra, his awakening beneath the Bodhi tree, his first teaching and the early saṅgha. It stops at awakening and does not describe the decades of teaching that followed. The title's "play" (*lalita*) reflects its presentation of these events as the effortless display of a bodhisattva. According to the introduction to the 84000 translation, the text is less a historical biography than an account of awakening itself, in which the events of the Buddha's life also mirror what earlier bodhisattvas experienced.[^84000toh95]

The sūtra has no single author. It is a compilation of several early, mostly unidentified sources, combined and elaborated by unnamed compilers from a Mahāyāna perspective. Parts of its framework go back to early Buddhist tradition, while the work as compiled was dated by Vaidya to the third century CE; some of its themes appear in writing only in the first centuries CE.[^84000toh95] Several manuscript strands circulated in India, differing in wording and content.[^84000toh95]

The Sanskrit text was edited by Rajendralal Mitra (from 1853), by Lefmann (1882), by Vaidya (1958) and, more recently, by Hokazono Kōichi. The work was translated into Chinese by Dharmarakṣa in the fourth century and by Divākara in the seventh, and into Tibetan in the ninth century, during the early translation period. The Tibetan version became the main source for the narrative of the Buddha's awakening in Tibet and remains widely read.[^84000toh95] The Kangyur version is catalogued as Toh 95.[^84000toh95]
