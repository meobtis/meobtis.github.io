The *Lokeśvara-pārājikā* is a short Sanskrit work preserved in a collection of texts from Nepal. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the Vinaya, as one item in a volume titled *Pārājikā (Texts from Nepal)*, edited by M. G. Dhadphale and published in Pune in 2013.[^dsbc776] The title combines the name of the bodhisattva Lokeśvara (Avalokiteśvara) with *pārājikā*, the term for the gravest class of monastic offences, but the catalogue record gives no further explanation of why the work is so named.

The opening lines of the digital text present a dialogue in verse. The goddess Tārā asks Lokeśvara about the signs of coming misfortune (*utpāta*) and how to judge whether they are auspicious or inauspicious, and Lokeśvara replies by describing ritual remedies for such omens. These include fire offerings (*homa*), a great offering (*mahābali*), recitation of the *Pañcarakṣā*, worship of a vase (*kalaśa*), gifts, and a meal for the monastic community.[^dsbc776]

Little else about the text is documented in the sources consulted. Its author, date and place of composition, any manuscript witnesses and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not stated in the edition records, and no reliable scholarly discussion of the work could be located. No established English title is known, so none is given here.
