The *Madhyāntavibhāgakārikābhāṣya* (more often *Madhyāntavibhāgabhāṣya*) is a prose commentary on the verses of the *Madhyāntavibhāga*, a Yogācāra work whose title is usually rendered "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes". It is attributed to Vasubandhu, who in the tradition is credited with commentaries on three treatises said to have been revealed to Asaṅga by Maitreya, this one among them.[^sep-vasubandhu] The edition on this site names the verses as attributed to Maitreyanātha.[^gretil-mvbh]

The commentary opens by stating that the treatise teaches seven topics: characteristics, obstructions, reality, the cultivation of the antidotes, the stages within that cultivation together with the attainment of its fruit, and the unsurpassed vehicle. It then begins with the first chapter on characteristics. The first verse states that false imagination (*abhūtaparikalpa*) exists, that the duality of grasped and grasper does not exist in it, and that emptiness exists in it and it in emptiness; the commentary glosses this as a middle path between "everything exists" and "nothing exists".[^gretil-mvbh]

Beyond what the edition itself shows, little could be verified in the sources consulted for this entry. Questions of date, the Tibetan and Chinese translations, the Sanskrit manuscript on which Nagao's edition rests, and modern translations are not documented here, and readers should consult specialist studies of the Madhyāntavibhāga.
