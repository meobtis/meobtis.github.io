The *Catuḥṣaṣṭisaṃvarastotram* ("Hymn to the Sixty-Four Saṃvaras") is a short Sanskrit stotra, a poem of praise, filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the stotras of the Cakrasaṃvara tradition.[^dsbc143] No established English title for it was found.

The verses, as printed in the edition, open with homage to Śrī Heruka and to Cakrasaṃvara, and then continue with a series of verses that each salute a form of Saṃvara named by its face or animal aspect, such as the sheep-faced, horse-faced, tiger-faced, tortoise-faced and fish-faced forms.[^dsbc143] The title apparently refers to sixty-four such forms, but this reading rests on the title and the opening verses only.

Little reliable information about the hymn's authorship, date, manuscripts or translations could be found in the sources consulted, so these questions are left open here. The text is known to this site only through the edition of Pandey (1994) from which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon took it.[^dsbc143]
