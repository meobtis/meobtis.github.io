This text is a commentary (*ṭīkā*) on a section of the *Guhyasamāja Tantra*, ascribed in its opening lines to Ācārya Candrakīrti. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon holds it as a single short item, and its first lines introduce the eleventh chapter (*ekādaśaḥ paṭalaḥ*) of the tantra, which they connect with the two kinds of deity forms taught in the first chapter.[^dsbc737] The title combines the name of Candrakīrti's well-known commentary, the *Pradīpoddyotana* ("Illuminating Lamp"), with *ṣaṭkoṭivyākhyā*, which may be read as an "explanation by the six alternatives" or "six limits"; the present sources do not explain this element further.

Candrakīrti, the author of the *Pradīpoddyotana*, is sometimes called the "tantric Candrakīrti" to distinguish him from the Madhyamaka philosopher of the same name. The *Pradīpoddyotana* is the principal commentary of the Ārya tradition of the *Guhyasamāja*, which is associated with the tantric figures Nāgārjuna, Āryadeva and Candrakīrti, who are generally dated to the ninth or tenth century and are not now regarded as identical with their Madhyamaka namesakes.[^wiki-candrakirti] A complete English translation of the first twelve chapters of the tantra together with this commentary has been published as *The Esoteric Community Tantra with The Illuminating Lamp*.[^wiki-guhyasamaja]

Reliable documentation of this particular text, as distinct from the *Pradīpoddyotana* as a whole, could not be found. It is not clear whether it is an excerpt, a separately transmitted section, or a modern edition of part of the commentary, and the DSBC record's title field ("Dhīh Journal") suggests that it was printed in an issue of that journal rather than as an independent book.[^dsbc737] Readers should compare it with the full *Pradīpoddyotana* before relying on it.
