The *Lokanāthastotram* ("hymn to Lokanātha") is a short poem of seven verses in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, where it is filed among the hymns to Avalokiteśvara. Each of its first six verses ends with the same refrain, "O glorious Lokanātha, I bow to your two feet" (*śrīlokanātha tava pādayugaṃ name'ham*), and the colophon calls it the hymn of the venerable noble lord Avalokiteśvara (*śrīmadāryāvalokiteśvarabhaṭṭārakasya stotram*).[^dsbc126]

The verses address the deity as a compassionate, many-formed being, "lotus-handed" (*padmapāṇi*), wide-eyed and a wish-fulfilling jewel for the world, and also identify him with other gods, such as Brahmā and Viṣṇu. The final verse asks forgiveness for any faulty wording and offers the hymn as a garland of flowers of devotion.[^dsbc126]

Little reliable information about the hymn could be found beyond its DSBC record. No author, date or place of composition is stated in the text, and no study, manuscript description or Tibetan or Chinese translation of it has been verified here, so none is claimed. The digital text is taken from the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and published by Motilal Banarsidass in 1994.[^dsbc126]
