The *Mṛtyuvañcanasitatārāsādhana* ("Sādhana of White Tārā for Cheating Death") is a short Sanskrit text in fifteen verses, set out as a sādhana, that is, a manual for a visualisation and recitation practice. Its legacy GRETIL identifier links it to number 103 of the *Sādhanamālā* collection.[^gretil-mvsts]

The verses open by stating the aim: the practitioner who meditates on the wheel at the heart of Tārā is said to remain untouched by the faults of death and to gain all accomplishments within six months. The text says that this visualisation of White Tārā was extracted from a "Collection on Cheating Death" (*mṛtyuvañcanasaṃgraha*) composed on the instruction of Vāgīśvara. It then describes taking refuge, a brief contemplation of emptiness, and the generation of Tārā, white and sixteen years old, seated on a lotus and moon, with a white wheel of eight compartments at her heart bearing a ten-syllable mantra. The closing verses list the benefits of the practice, including protection from death, old age, disease and poverty, and various accomplishments.[^gretil-mvsts]

Beyond this, little reliable information on the text could be gathered. The edition records state that it is taken from Johannes Schneider's 2010 edition of Vāgīśvarakīrti's *Mṛtyuvañcanopadeśa*, a Buddhist treatise on warding off death, but the date and authorship of this extract, and any Tibetan or Chinese translations, are not documented in the sources consulted.[^gretil-mvsts]
