The *Satyasūtra* ("Discourse on the Truths") is a very short Sanskrit text of nine numbered lines. It opens with the setting in Śrāvastī and states that all monks who go forth from home into homelessness out of faith do so for the sake of knowing the four noble truths: suffering, its origin, its cessation and the path leading to its cessation. It then urges intense effort, zeal and mindfulness for realising them, repeats the list of truths, and closes with a colophon naming it a *satyasūtra* and a short protective formula (*rakṣā*) ending in *svāhā*.[^gretil-satyasutra]

Little else is documented in the sources consulted. The only edition cited for the text is Waldschmidt's publication of a small scroll written in Brāhmī script, and the digital transcription preserves editorial marks showing that the damaged scroll is partly reconstructed (the closing lines are incomplete).[^gretil-satyasutra] No information on its date, provenance, parallels in Pāli, Chinese or Tibetan, or scholarly discussion was found, and no established English title is known.
