The *Bhramaharanāma Hevajrasādhana* is a short Sanskrit sādhana, a ritual and meditation manual, for the practice of the Buddhist tantric deity Hevajra. It is attributed to Ratnākaraśānti, and its Sanskrit text survives in manuscripts that were used for a critical edition by Harunaga Isaacson, published in 2002 as part of his series of studies on Ratnākaraśānti's tantric works.[^isaacson2002] The title is usually read as "the Hevajra sādhana called Dispeller of Delusion" (*bhrama-hara*), but no established English title was found.

The text opens with a verse in praise of Heruka and a statement that the author is composing a sādhana of the eight-faced, sixteen-armed, four-legged Hevajra. It then prescribes the practitioner's preliminary protection, the offering to eight goddesses beginning with Gaurī, the confession of sins, the taking of refuge and the generation of bodhicitta, the four immeasurables, and a contemplation that all phenomena are mind-only and empty of subject and object.[^gretil-bhrhe] No fuller scholarly summary of its contents was available to the compiler of this note.

Ratnākaraśānti (c. 970–1045 CE) was a scholar and tantric master associated with the monastery of Vikramaśīla and was a prolific author of commentaries, treatises and practice manuals, including the Hevajra commentary *Muktāvalī*.[^84000toh1189] Beyond this, little reliable information about the date, manuscripts or Tibetan translation of this particular work could be verified. A Sanskrit meditation on the generation of bodhicitta published elsewhere is said to take its wording from this sādhana, which suggests that it was used as a source for later practice texts.[^bhramahara-bodhicitta] A digital copy of the text is also listed by the Hamburg Centre for Tantric Studies.[^hamburg]
