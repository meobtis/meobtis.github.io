The Patna Dharmapada is a version of the *Dharmapada*, the anthology of short Buddhist verses best known in English through the Pali *Dhammapada*. It is written in Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit, and it is described as most likely belonging to the Sāmmatīya (Sammatiya) school.[^wiki-dharmapada] Its chapters, as edited by Margaret Cone, run from "Jama" (the Pairs) to "Sahasra" (the Thousands), with a further chapter, "Uraga", given in brackets.[^wiki-dharmapada]

The work belongs to a family of parallel collections. The Pali *Dhammapada* has 423 verses in 26 chapters.[^wiki-dharmapada] The Gāndhārī Dharmapada in Kharoṣṭhī script and the Sanskrit *Udānavarga* (with its Tibetan translation) are the other principal relatives. Brough's comparison of the Pali, Gāndhārī and Udānavarga versions found a common core of roughly 330–340 verses and sixteen shared chapter headings, but no evidence that any one of them is the "primitive" Dharmapada.[^wiki-dharmapada] In the digital text, many verses are followed by a line from the *Udānavarga*, which allows the two to be compared directly.[^gretil-patna]

Little further is documented here. The sources consulted for this introduction do not give the date or provenance of the manuscript, the Chinese or Tibetan relations of this particular version, or an established English title, and these are therefore left out rather than guessed.
