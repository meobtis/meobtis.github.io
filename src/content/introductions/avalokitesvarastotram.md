The title *Avalokiteśvarastotram* ("hymn to Avalokiteśvara") covers two separate short poems in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon. Both open with the salutation *oṃ namo'valokiteśvarāya* and praise the bodhisattva of compassion. The first is headed as composed by Vāsukināgarāja and consists of verses that each end with the refrain "I bow to the lord born from the jewel and lotus" (*namāmi nāthaṃ maṇipadmasaṃbhavam*), evoking the lotus-and-jewel imagery associated with Avalokiteśvara. The second is headed as composed by Carapatipāda and is a prayer for protection from rebirth, old age, sickness and death, addressing the deity as Lokeśa, "lord of the world".[^dsbc123][^dsbc124]

Both texts are taken from the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and published by Motilal Banarsidass in 1994. A library catalogue describes the book as a collection of one hundred and eight old Buddhist hymns, with a foreword by the Dalai Lama and an introduction in Hindi.[^nii1994] Little independent information about these two hymns could be found: no study of either text, no dated manuscript evidence, and no secure identification of the attributed authors were located, and any Tibetan or Chinese translations have not been verified here. The authorship and date given in the headings should therefore be treated as the attributions of the printed anthology, not as established facts.
