The *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* ("commentary on the interpretive rules") is a work of Sanskrit grammar included in the grammar (*vyākaraṇa*) section of the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon. The text opens with an invocation to Gaṇeśa and a verse, and is then arranged by chapter and quarter of Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī*, beginning with the first quarter of the first chapter.[^dsbc817] Each short rule (*paribhāṣā*) is stated and then explained at length, with discussion of how it applies to particular Pāṇinian sūtras, of examples such as *adhyetā* and *agīṣkaḥ*, and of earlier explanations, including those of the commentators referred to as Nyāsakāra and Anunyāsakāra.[^dsbc817]

Little reliable information about the work was found beyond the edition's own front matter, and its author, date and place of composition are not stated in the digital records. The digital text derives from an edition prepared by Harinatha Dube and published in Benares in 1887.[^dsbc817] No Tibetan or Chinese translation, manuscript history or other secondary scholarship could be verified, so none is described here.
