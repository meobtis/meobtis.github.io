The *Ekādaśamukhahṛdaya* ("Heart of the Eleven-Faced One") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist text of the dhāraṇī type, presented as a discourse of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. It opens in the usual sūtra manner, with the Buddha staying at Śrāvastī, and Avalokiteśvara approaches him with a great retinue of vidyādharas and announces that he will recite his *hṛdaya*, a "heart" or core mantra, said to have been taught over eleven aeons.[^gretil-ekadasa]

In the text, Avalokiteśvara states that the *hṛdaya* protects against illness, misfortune, bad dreams, untimely death and obstacles, and lists ten benefits visible in this life, among them freedom from disease, inexhaustible wealth, subjection of enemies and immunity to poison, weapons, drowning, fire and untimely death. It adds four further benefits, including seeing a tathāgata at the moment of death, avoiding the lower rebirths and rebirth in Sukhāvatī. The speaker recounts learning the text from earlier tathāgatas. The edition's apparatus notes that the numbering of the ten benefits appears incomplete.[^gretil-ekadasa] Little else about the work's date, authorship or transmission was found in the sources consulted, so these are left unstated here. The Gilgit manuscripts, in which it survives, were discovered in 1931 and are believed to date from roughly the fifth to sixth centuries, with some later material.[^wiki-gilgit]
