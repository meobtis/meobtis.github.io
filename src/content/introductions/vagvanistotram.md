The *Vāgvāṇīstotram* ("Hymn to Vāgvāṇī") is a short Sanskrit stotra of nine verses, with a closing mantra, addressed to the goddess Sarasvatī under the name Vāgvāṇī, "speech". It opens by paying homage to Sarasvatī as the consciousness dwelling in the heart, holding a vīṇā and granting wisdom, and one verse calls her "dear to the Jina". Later verses report that the goddess, pleased by the praise, appears and offers a boon. Bṛhaspati asks for divine knowledge, and she promises wisdom, poetic skill and fame to those who recite the hymn, and a permanent place in the throat of whoever recites it at the three junctures of the day. The colophon ascribes the hymn to Bṛhaspati.[^dsbc138]

The text is known here only through the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which files it among the stotras of Buddhist goddesses (Bhagavatī) and bases it on a modern printed collection of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc138] No scholarly discussion of its date, authorship, manuscripts or translations into Tibetan or Chinese could be consulted, and the attribution to Bṛhaspati should be read as part of the text's own framing rather than as established history. Little about the work appears to be documented, and no established English title is known.
