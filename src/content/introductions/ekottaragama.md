The Ekottarāgama (Ekottarika-āgama, "Numerical Discourses") is one of the early Buddhist collections of discourses, arranged by increasing numbers of items. It survives complete only in a Chinese translation (T 125 in the Taishō canon), and it broadly corresponds to the Pāli Aṅguttara-nikāya, although it differs from that collection more than the other Āgamas differ from their Pāli counterparts.[^wiki-ea] Several scholars associate the collection with the Mahāsāṃghika school, while others argue that later, mostly Mahāyāna-type additions were made when it was revised in China, so that its original affiliation is hard to recover.[^wiki-ea]

The Sanskrit text presented here is not a complete collection but a set of fragments from a Gilgit manuscript, edited by Chandra Bhal Tripathi in 1995.[^gretil-ea] The surviving passages are short sūtras on the mind (*citta*), for example that an untrained, ungoverned mind leads to suffering while a well-trained one leads to happiness, together with similes such as the image of a rightly or wrongly directed awn of grain. Gaps in the manuscript are marked in the text, and little else about these fragments is documented in the sources consulted, so details of their date, school affiliation and relation to the Chinese version are not given here.

### Manuscripts in the Schøyen Collection

A Gāndhārī counterpart is now known. Twenty-five (possibly twenty-six) small palm-leaf fragments in Gāndhārī and Kharoṣṭhī script from the Bamiyan region, in the Schøyen Collection, together with two fragments recorded in Pakistan in 1996, are the remains of what seems to have been a complete manuscript of a Gāndhārī version of the numerically arranged collection known in Sanskrit as the *Ekottarikāgama* and in Pali as the *Aṅguttara-nikāya*.[^jantrasrisalai2016]
