The *Tattvajñānasaṁsiddhi-mahāsukharakāśikā-ṭīkā* is a Sanskrit commentary (*ṭīkā*) catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the commentaries on Yoginī tantras.[^dsbc834] No established English title has been found. The opening of the text names its subject: after a salutation to the blessed Vajravārāhī, the commentator states that he will explain a sādhana (practice manual) of Vajravārāhī, and he describes the base work as a composition of Ācārya Śūnyatāsamādhivajra, a name given in the text itself.[^dsbc834]

The commentary glosses the base text verse by verse. It begins with two benedictory verses to Vajravārāhī and then a third verse in which the author says he will set out the "perfection of the knowledge of reality" (*tattvajñānasya saṁsiddhim*), followed by a section headed on external worship (*bāhyapūjāvidhiḥ*). Its comments explain the imagery of the goddess in terms of bodhicitta, the inner heat practice and non-apprehension, and quote Hevajra verses in support.[^dsbc834]

Little else could be established. The text's self-description names a monk Dhyānacandra in an introductory verse, and the title line carries an editorial attribution that appears to refer to a Banārasī Lāl, but the DSBC record gives no author, date, editor or printed source, and no independent scholarly source on this commentary was available when this introduction was prepared. Questions of authorship, date, Tibetan or Chinese translations and manuscripts therefore remain undocumented here.[^dsbc834]
