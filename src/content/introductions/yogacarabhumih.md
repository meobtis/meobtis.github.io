The *Yogācārabhūmi* (also known as the *Yogācārabhūmi-śāstra*, "Treatise on the Stages of Yogic Practice") is a very large Mahāyāna doctrinal compendium associated with the Yogācāra school. It draws on both Mainstream and Mahāyāna material. It is divided into a Basic Section of fourteen books, which treat seventeen *bhūmis* (levels or stages), and a Supplementary Section of four compendia.[^wiki-ybh] The text opens by listing the seventeen stages, beginning with the stage associated with the five sense-consciousnesses (*pañcavijñānakāyasamprayuktā bhūmiḥ*) and the stage of the mind (*manobhūmiḥ*), and continuing through the stages of meditative concentration, the three kinds of learning, and the paths of the hearers, solitary buddhas and bodhisattvas, as the opening lines of the digital text show.[^dsbc341]

Traditional sources attribute the work either to Asaṅga or to the bodhisattva Maitreya, whereas most modern scholars regard it as a composite work with several layers and authors, without excluding Asaṅga's involvement. Its final redaction is generally dated to the fourth century CE.[^wiki-ybh] At least half of the text is reported to survive in Sanskrit fragments, most of it from the Basic Section, while the Chinese and Tibetan translations are complete. Xuanzang introduced the full text to China, after a partial translation by Paramārtha in the sixth century, and the Tibetan translation was made in the ninth century at Samye.[^wiki-ybh]

Little is documented here about the digital edition itself. The records give only three sections, covering the first of the seventeen stages onward, and their bibliographic details are empty, so the text should be treated as an excerpt of the larger work rather than the whole treatise.[^dsbc341] The standard modern study of the work is the volume edited by Ulrich Timme Kragh.[^wiki-ybh]
