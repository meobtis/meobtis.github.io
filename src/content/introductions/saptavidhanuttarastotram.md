The *Saptavidhānuttarastotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) addressed to Avalokiteśvara under the name Lokanātha, "Lord of the World". It opens with a salutation to the venerable Lokanātha and consists of twelve verses, the first ten of which share a refrain in which the speaker bows and praises Lokanātha, and it closes with a colophon calling it the "*saptavidhānuttara*" hymn of the noble Avalokiteśvara.[^dsbc136] The title appears to refer to a sevenfold (*saptavidha*) supreme (*anuttara*) form of worship, and verse 11 mentions an offering made in seven ways (*saptadhā*); this reading is drawn from the text itself.[^dsbc136]

The verses describe the deity's appearance, such as lotus-petal eyes and a jewelled crown, and praise him as one who destroys sin and delusion and helps beings across the ocean of saṃsāra. The final verses dedicate the merit of the praise to rebirth in Sukhāvatī.[^dsbc136]

Little reliable information on the hymn's author, date or provenance could be found in the sources consulted, and it is not known to which tradition of Avalokiteśvara worship it belongs. Its Tibetan or Chinese translations, manuscripts and independent studies were not identified. The only documented edition is the one printed in the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, from which the digital texts derive.[^dsbc136]
