The *Catuḥśataka* ("Four Hundred [Verses]"), here titled *Catuḥśatikā*, is a Madhyamaka treatise traditionally attributed to Āryadeva, a pupil of Nāgārjuna. It is usually described as having sixteen chapters of about twenty-five verses each. According to the summary given in the Wikipedia article on Āryadeva, which draws on Ruegg's survey of Madhyamaka literature, the first eight chapters prepare the practitioner by countering the views that things are permanent, pleasant, pure or a self, and by treating the bodhisattva path and the defilements, while the last eight argue that phenomena lack intrinsic nature.[^wiki-aryadeva] I could not consult Ruegg's book or other scholarly studies directly, so this summary should be checked against them.

The complete Sanskrit text is not known to survive; only fragments are preserved, and the same article reports that a complete Tibetan translation exists, that Xuanzang translated only the second half into Chinese, and that Candrakīrti's commentary survives complete only in Tibetan.[^wiki-aryadeva] The digital text offered here is accordingly not the whole work but the verses available in Sanskrit, as collected in a printed edition by Bhagchandra Jain.[^gretil-catuhsatika] Little else about its transmission could be verified from the sources available.
