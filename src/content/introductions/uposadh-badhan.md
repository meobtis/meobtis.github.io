"Uposadh Badhan" is a Latin transcription of the title on the manuscript's first page, which reads approximately *śrī upoṣadhā badān*, that is, an *Upoṣadha-avadāna*, and gives a folio number. No established English title was found; the work can be described as a Newar account of the Upoṣadha fast.[^an-index]

The text, in Sanskrit verse, begins with a salutation to Āryāvalokiteśvara. King Aśoka asks the elder (Upagupta, to judge from the verses) about the merit and procedure of the observance, and the answer is set in Kapilavastu, in the Nyagrodha grove, where the Buddha is teaching. There the brahmin Vasiṣṭha appears, with a long beard and nails, because he has fasted for a hundred years. The Buddha tells him that his austerity has borne no fruit, and teaches instead the monthly rite: on the eighth day of each fortnight one should bathe, take refuge in the Three Jewels, make offerings and rejoice in merit, hear the Dharma and observe the ethical precepts, in honour of a Lokeśvara, a form of Avalokiteśvara whose epithet is partly illegible in the transcription. The transcription ends in the middle of the ritual instructions, so the rest, including any concluding story, cannot be read here.

This matches the Newar Buddhist *upoṣadha* (also *aṣṭamī*) *vrata*. Shakya describes it as the most popular of several Avalokiteśvara observances, performed on the full and half-moon days, with a priest guiding the recitation, and ending with the taking of refuge and the eight precepts.[^shakya1990] Brough noted in 1948 that the Newar *Aṣṭamī-vrata-māhātmya*, which consists mainly of *avadāna*-type tales illustrating the rite's merits, belongs to a rite honouring Amoghapāśa Lokeśvara and called it the lineal descendant of the old *Uposadha* ceremony.[^brough1948] Sakuma has studied the maṇḍala of Amoghapāśa Lokeśvara used in these ritual texts and concluded that it originated in Nepal.[^sakuma2017] Whether this manuscript is a copy of the *Aṣṭamī-vrata-māhātmya* or another recension was not established; that would require comparing it with those texts.
