The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") is a short Sanskrit stotra, a verse hymn of praise to the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha. The title carries the qualifier *viṣṇukṛtam*, "made by Viṣṇu", so the hymn presents itself as praise of the Buddha offered by the god Viṣṇu. The text opens with a salutation to the omniscient one (*om namaḥ sarvajñāya*) and has eleven numbered verses; its closing line calls it a *bhujaṅgaprayāta* hymn, naming the metre, and the tenth verse attributes it to Mādhava, another name of Viṣṇu.[^dsbc185]

Judging from the verses, most begin with "I bow" (*name*) and praise the Buddha as a bridge across the ocean of existence and a cause of liberation, as one who has gone beyond the perfections of giving, ethical conduct, patience, meditation and vigour, and as a teacher of the four truths. They recall his victory over Māra's army beneath the tree of awakening, his bodily marks (the thirty-two characteristics), and his birth into the Śākya lineage. The last verses state that whoever recites the hymn before an image of the Jina will find good fortune in the home and the protection of the buddhas, and that practitioners will finally reach the realm called Sukhāvatī.[^dsbc185]

Little else about the work appears to be documented in the sources available for this introduction. No reliable information was found on its date, on its authorship beyond the attribution in the title, or on any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The site's catalogue also lists similarly titled hymns with other qualifiers (for example *navagrahakṛtam* and *brahmaṇā kṛtam*), which are separate entries.
