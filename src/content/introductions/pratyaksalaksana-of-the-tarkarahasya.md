The *Pratyakṣalakṣaṇa* ("definition of perception") is a short Sanskrit passage taken from a work called the *Tarkarahasya*, presented in the digital edition as a self-contained section on the nature of perception (*pratyakṣa*). It opens with a Buddhist definition of perception as free from conceptual construction and non-erroneous, and then sets out, one after another, rival views of perception, quoting for instance a verse on the pervasion of all cognition by language attributed in the editor's notes to the *Vākyapadīya*.[^gretil-pratyaksalaksana] The editor's notes cross-refer to the *Nyāyabindu*, the *Pramāṇaviniścaya* and its commentary, the *Tattvasaṃgraha* and other works of the Buddhist epistemological tradition, as well as to Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā sources.[^gretil-pratyaksalaksana]

Little reliable information about the *Tarkarahasya* itself, such as its author, date or place of composition, could be established from the sources consulted for this introduction, and no established English title was found; the edition record gives only the printed study from which the text is taken, Yaita's first instalment of a series of studies on the work.[^gretil-pratyaksalaksana] Readers who need the work's authorship or context should consult that study directly.
