The *Anityatāsūtra* ("Sūtra on Impermanence") is a short discourse on the impermanence of conditioned states. In the Tibetan Kangyur two works with this title stand side by side in the General Sūtra section (Toh 309 and Toh 310), which share a theme and a similar narrative frame but differ in content; the English translation of Toh 310 is titled *The Sūtra on Impermanence (2)*.[^84000toh310]

In the Sanskrit text, the Buddha addresses the monks at Śrāvastī and declares that all conditioned states are impermanent, unstable and subject to change. Using a refrain that life ends in death, he works through a hierarchy of beings: wealthy householders, kings, forest ascetics, gods of the desire, form and formless realms, and finally arhats, pratyekabuddhas and tathāgatas, whose bodies too are said to be laid down. The simile of clay pots, fired or unfired, that must eventually break closes the prose, followed by verses urging the cessation of conditioned states.[^84000toh310]

According to the 84000 introduction, Sanskrit manuscripts fall into two transmissions: two undated manuscripts in Dhārikā script held at the Potala Palace, and seven Nepalese witnesses, each within a *Dhāraṇīsaṃgraha* collection and dated roughly to the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. The Tibetan version is dated to the tenth or eleventh century on the basis of colophons naming Kamalagupta and Rinchen Sangpo, and a Chinese translation by Fatian (Dharmadeva) was completed in 984 CE. There is no direct Pali equivalent, though similar passages occur in the Saṃyuttanikāya and Dīghanikāya, and the verses have parallels in the *Udānavarga*.[^84000toh310]

The text's affiliation is uncertain. Its editors tentatively suggest, without treating it as certain, that it may belong to a (Mūla-)Sarvāstivāda āgama that was not fully translated into Chinese.[^84000toh310] Its title is shared with another Kangyur work, and the Chinese canon likewise contains more than one sūtra with an equivalent title, whose contents differ substantially from Toh 309.[^84000toh309]
