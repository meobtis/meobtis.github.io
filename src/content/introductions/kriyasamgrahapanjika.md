The *Kriyāsaṁgrahapañjikā* is a Sanskrit commentary (*pañjikā*) whose title refers to a "compendium of rites" (*kriyāsaṁgraha*), and the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon classifies it among the commentaries on the Yoga and higher tantras (*yogottaratantraṭīkā*).[^dsbc828] The text opens by ascribing the work to "mahāpaṇḍita Niḥsaṁgācārya Kuladatta", so it is traditionally attributed to Kuladatta; its date and place of composition are not given in the sources consulted here.[^dsbc828]

The digitised text is arranged by numbered sections and begins with the examination of the ground (*bhūmiparīkṣā*), where it discusses which trees and signs make a plot unsuitable for ritual use. Later sections include the preparation of the vase (*kalaśa*) and the setting of the marking cords (*sūtrapātana*), which suggests a manual of preparatory and consecration procedures.[^dsbc828][^dsbc829] Little else about the work's history, translations or manuscripts could be documented from the sources available for this entry, and it is better studied through the printed edition named in the record.
