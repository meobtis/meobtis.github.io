The *Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā* ("Perfection of Wisdom in Twenty-Five Thousand Lines") is one of the long Mahāyāna Perfection of Wisdom sūtras. In the Tibetan Kangyur it fills three volumes and is divided into seventy-six chapters; it is the second longest of the three long Perfection of Wisdom sūtras, after the *One Hundred Thousand Lines* (Toh 8).[^84000toh9] The Sanskrit work opens in the usual manner of a sūtra, with the Buddha on Vulture Peak at Rājagṛha among a great assembly of monks and bodhisattvas, before he enters a samādhi and emits rays of light across the world systems.[^gretil-kimura]

The Tibetan translation belongs to the early translation period. The catalogues attribute it to the time of Yeshé Dé and others, and since it appears in the early imperial inventories it was complete by about 813 CE. A separate "eight-chapter" version, Toh 3790, is preserved in the Tengyur and is based largely on the Kangyur text.[^84000toh9] The English title above follows the 84000 translation by the Padmakara Translation Group, with an introduction by John Canti.[^84000toh9]

Little further is documented here about the composition of the work, its authorship or its Sanskrit manuscripts, and none of it has been verified in a source consulted for this introduction. The digital Sanskrit editions are partial and heterogeneous. They comprise a few gold leaves from Anurādhapura, Dutt's 1934 edition, and Kimura's multi-volume edition, which the GRETIL record describes as incomplete.[^gretil-gold][^gretil-dutt][^gretil-kimura]
