The *Buddhabhaṭṭārakastotram* ("Hymn to the Venerable Buddha") is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise in nineteen verses. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is classified among the hymns to Śākyamuni, and it is taken from a printed anthology of Buddhist hymns, the *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey.[^dsbc175] The text opens with "om namo buddhāya", and the closing line describes it as a hymn to the Buddha composed by Brahmā.[^dsbc175]

Each of the opening verses names the Buddha under a string of epithets (for example "all-knowing", "compassionate", "Śākyasiṃha" and "lord of the world") and ends with the refrain "I bow to him". The final verses promise that people who recite the hymn's epithets daily after rising will be freed from faults, gain the desired enjoyments, health and long life, and attain liberation.[^dsbc175] Little further is documented about the work: no source consulted for this entry discusses its date, authorship beyond the attribution to Brahmā in the colophon, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, so none is claimed here.
