The *(Ārya)tārāstutiḥ* ("Praise of the Noble Tārā") is a Sanskrit hymn addressed to Tārā, the Buddhist saviouress. In the digital edition it opens with a homage to Tārā and the attribution "ācārya candradāsakṛtā", that is, composed by the teacher Candradāsa, and then continues in numbered verses in long, heavily alliterative metres, the first of which describes the goddess's compassionate glance and her feet adorned by the light of crest-jewels.[^dsbc116]

The verses that follow praise Tārā as a protector from dangers such as wild beasts, rutting elephants and fire, a theme familiar from other Tārā hymns.[^dsbc116] Little reliable information about the work could be established beyond what the edition itself records: its date, the identity of Candradāsa, and the existence of Tibetan or Chinese translations or Sanskrit manuscripts have not been verified here, and the text should not be confused with the better-known *Twenty-One Praises of Tārā*. The printed source is a modern anthology of Buddhist hymns, the *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey.[^dsbc116]
