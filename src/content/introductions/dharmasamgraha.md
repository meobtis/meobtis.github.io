The *Dharmasaṃgraha* is a short Sanskrit compilation of Buddhist technical terms. In the form printed by P. L. Vaidya it consists of 140 numbered entries, each a list of items, introduced by a brief verse of homage to the Three Jewels, and it closes with a colophon stating that this *Dharmasaṃgraha* was composed by Nāgārjuna.[^gretil-dhs] It is known in English-language scholarship mainly through Kenjiu Kasawara's edition, prepared for the Anecdota Oxoniensia series and issued with F. Max Müller and H. Wenzel as editors, whose title describes it as an ancient collection of Buddhist technical terms.[^ia1981] A record of the first printing gives Clarendon Press, 1885.[^openlibrary]

The entries are enumerations in the manner of an abhidharmic or lexical handbook: it begins with the three jewels (Buddha, Dharma, Saṃgha), the three vehicles, five Buddhas and four goddesses, and it ends with the three roots of the wholesome, their opposites, and the three trainings.[^gretil-dhs] The ascription to Nāgārjuna is doubtful: the Wikipedia article on Nāgārjuna reports that Christian Lindtner counts the *Dharmasaṃgraha* among the attributed works that are questionable and probably not authentic.[^wiki-nagarjuna] Little else about its date, place of composition or transmission has been verified from the sources consulted, and no translation or commentary is documented here.
