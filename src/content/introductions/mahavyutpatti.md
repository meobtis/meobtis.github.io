The *Mahāvyutpatti* is a bilingual Sanskrit–Tibetan glossary of Buddhist terminology, included in the Tibetan Tengyur (Toh 4346). Its title is often rendered "The Great Volume of Precise Understanding" or similar, and it is commonly described as one of the earliest substantial bilingual dictionaries; the reference work consulted here lists about 9,500 entries arranged in many thematic chapters.[^wiki-mvy] The DSBC text follows the Bibliotheca Buddhica edition prepared by Minaev and Mironov.[^dsbc232]

According to the same source, the work was compiled in Tibet in the late eighth or early ninth century, under royal sponsorship, by a team of Tibetan translators working with Indian scholars. It is traditionally linked with the reign of Ralpacan (c. 838), though the source argues for an earlier date in the reign of Sadnalegs (c. 800–815); this dating rests on a tertiary source and should be checked against specialist scholarship.[^wiki-mvy] Little further reliable information on its manuscripts and the history of its modern editions could be found in the sources consulted, including the critical edition by Ishihama and Fukuda (1989) that is only mentioned there.[^wiki-mvy]
