The *Saṃtānāntarasiddhi* ("Proof of Other Minds"; also rendered "Proof of Others' Mindstreams") is a short treatise ascribed to the Buddhist epistemologist Dharmakīrti. It is counted among his two minor works, together with the *Sambandhaparīkṣā* ("Analysis of Relations"), and scholars find it harder to place in the sequence of his writings than the major ones.[^sep-dharmakirti] The Sanskrit text opens with a single verse followed by prose discussion.[^gretil-stas]

Its subject is the problem of other minds: how one can know that there are mindstreams besides one's own. The work argues that an idealist of the Yogācāra kind, who denies that external objects exist, can rely on the same argument from analogy as a realist Sautrāntika. Observing in oneself that bodily and verbal activity follows from an intention, one can infer from similar activity elsewhere that another mind is at work, and so avoid solipsism.[^sep-dharmakirti] The treatise is discussed as part of the Buddhist epistemological tradition's treatment of this problem.[^inami2001] The opening verse and prose of the Sanskrit text anticipate objections, for example that the other's mental precursors are never perceived, and answer that this is equally true of one's own case.[^gretil-stas]

The sources consulted for this introduction do not give a firm date for the work, and details of its Tibetan and Chinese translations, manuscripts and English translations could not be verified here, so little more can be reliably stated. The printed edition used for the digital texts is titled as an edition of the verses with a commentary (*kārikā ṭīkā*).[^gretil-stas]
