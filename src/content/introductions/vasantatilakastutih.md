The *Vasantatilakāstutiḥ* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) of four verses, ending with the colophon "śrīvasantatilakāstutiḥ samāptā". It is classified in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the stotras of the Cakrasaṃvara tradition, and its source edition is Pandey's anthology of Buddhist hymns, the *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc146]

Each verse opens with a description of the deity and closes with the formula "I bow to you", addressing in turn a Heruka in the form of "both mind" (*ubhayacitta*), an innate Heruka (*sahajātman*), and the goddess Herukī, called mother of the world. The imagery is that of Cakrasaṃvara yogic practice, including the bodily centres and the navel-wheel.[^dsbc146]

Little reliable information about the hymn's authorship, date, translations or manuscripts could be found beyond the edition record, so none is claimed here.
