The *Svayaṃbhupurāṇa* (*Svayambhū Purāṇa*) is a Sanskrit Buddhist work of the Nepalese tradition, associated with Vajrayāna Buddhism. It deals with the origin and development of the Kathmandu Valley and with the Buddhas said to have visited it, centred on the self-arisen (*svayambhū*) sacred site now known as Swayambhunath. In the form transmitted in the digital editions it has ten chapters, and it opens with a salutation to Dharmadhātuvāgīśvara and an announcement of the "origin of the Svayambhū Dharmadhātu".[^wiki-swayambhu][^dsbc7]

According to a summary of its contents, the chapters narrate the origin of the Svayambhū light, the draining of the lake that once filled the valley and the founding of a city, the establishment of pilgrimage sites, local legends of Nāgas and sacred images, the arrival of the figure Dharmaśrīmitra, the erection of a stupa over Dharmadhātuvāgīśvara, episodes of drought ended through the Nāga king Karkoṭaka, and a closing section on the merit of telling, hearing and copying the text.[^wiki-swayambhu] The same article reports that the oldest known manuscript is dated to 1558 CE (Nepal Sambat 678), citing Regmi and Malla; I could not consult those works directly.[^wiki-swayambhu]

Reliable scholarly information on the composition, authorship and recensions of the text could not be verified in the sources available for this entry, so these are left undescribed here. Little more is documented in the sources consulted.
