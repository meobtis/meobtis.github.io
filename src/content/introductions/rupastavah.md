The *Rūpastavaḥ* ("Hymn to the Form") is a short Sanskrit hymn of twenty-four verses, preserved in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the stotras associated with Avalokiteśvara. It is printed in Janardan Shastri Pandey's collection of Buddhist hymns, the *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc133]

Each verse praises one part of the body of the Buddha in turn, among them the hair, face, head, eyes, ears, teeth, tongue, voice, neck, body, garments, arms, hands, heart, navel and feet, and each ends with the refrain "I bow to the best of the Ten-Powered One" with the relevant bodily part named. The final verse promises that those who recite the hymn attain the path to liberation. The closing colophon of the text names the lord Avalokiteśvara (*śrīmadāryāvalokiteśvarabhaṭṭārakasya rūpastavaṃ samāptam*), which is why the edition files it under Avalokiteśvara, although the epithet "the ten-powered one" (*daśabala*) in the refrain is a standard title of a Buddha.[^dsbc133]

Little is documented about this hymn beyond the edition record. No author, date, or Tibetan or Chinese translation could be established from the sources consulted, and no established English title was found, so these questions are left open here.
