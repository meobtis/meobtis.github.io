The *Bodhisattvabhūmi* ("Stage of the Bodhisattva") is one section of the larger *Yogācārabhūmi* ("Stages of Spiritual Practice"), a foundational compendium of the Yogācāra school. Tibetan traditions treat it as a text in its own right, and count it among the six core texts of the Kadampa tradition. It is also known in English as "The Bodhisattva Levels"; Artemus B. Engle's complete English translation of 2016 is titled *The Bodhisattva Path to Unsurpassed Enlightenment*, and before it the work had been available in English only in partial versions.[^engle2016]

The text as edited here opens with a statement of ten topics (the foundation, the sign, the side, the aspiration, the abiding, the rebirth, the support, the stage, the practice and the culmination) that together make up the bodhisattva's path to the Mahāyāna, beginning with the "lineage" (*gotra*) chapter. The two digital editions are divided into 28 chapters.[^gretil-bbh][^dsbc328] The work as a whole explains how to awaken, develop and perfect the mind of enlightenment.[^engle2016]

The work is traditionally attributed to Asaṅga, a figure of roughly the fourth to fifth century CE, and is described in the publisher's account of Engle's translation as a part of the *Yogācārabhūmi*.[^engle2016] The question of its exact date, authorship and relation to the rest of the *Yogācārabhūmi* is not addressed in the sources consulted here and is left open.

The Sanskrit text survives in manuscripts. Unrai Wogihara's edition (1930 and 1936) was based mainly on a Cambridge manuscript, which he dated to the eighth or early ninth century, and was checked against the Tibetan and Xuanzang's Chinese translations. Dutt's edition of 1966, the basis of the digital texts, was made from a manuscript photographed by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana in Tibet in 1938; J. W. de Jong judged it to have been prepared with much less care than Wogihara's, since Dutt did not consult the Tibetan or Chinese versions.[^dejong] Little further is documented in the sources consulted for this page, including catalogue numbers of the Tibetan and Chinese translations.
