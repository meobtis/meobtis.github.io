This is a Sanskrit text in verse, headed in the GRETIL file as a "parivarta" (chapter) on the Buddhist teaching, ordination and observances of a woman named Sabhikā. According to the GRETIL record, it is Chapter XIV of the *Bhadrakalpāvadāna* and Chapter IX of the *Saṃbhadrāvadānamālā*, and the edited text includes a few additional verses found in the latter.[^gretil-sabhika] No established English title for it could be found.

The opening verses set the story as a discourse in which King Aśoka asks the monk Upagupta to speak again about the Buddha. The narrative then moves to Mathurā, where a wealthy merchant, Sādhubhoga, and his wife have a daughter born with a triply bent body; a soothsayer declares the child inauspicious, and the girl is handed over to a female wandering ascetic who raises and ordains her.[^gretil-sabhika] The rest of the story is not summarised here, because little reliable secondary literature on this chapter could be consulted.

Very little about the work's date, authorship or Tibetan and Chinese counterparts is documented in the sources available for this introduction. The text was edited from manuscripts for the first time by Chopra in 1990, as an appendix to a study of a folk belief concerning bodily deformity in Buddhist Sanskrit texts.[^gretil-sabhika]
