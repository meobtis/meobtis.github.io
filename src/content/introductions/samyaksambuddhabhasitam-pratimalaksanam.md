The *Samyaksaṃbuddhabhāṣitaṃ pratimālakṣaṇam* ("The characteristics of images, spoken by the perfectly awakened Buddha") is a short Sanskrit work on the making of Buddhist images. It belongs to the technical literature on image-making (*śilpa*) and is framed as a sūtra-style discourse, beginning with the usual "Thus have I heard" and the Buddha's residence at Jetavana.[^gretil-pratima]

In the text, Śāriputra asks the Buddha how devout sons and daughters of good family should act once he has departed or passed into parinirvāṇa. The Buddha replies that an image should be made for worship and honour, with a body proportioned like the banyan tree, and then sets out measurements in verse, counted in finger-widths (*aṅgula*): the opening lines give 120 finger-widths for bodhisattvas and 125 for buddhas, followed by dimensions of the head, forehead, nose, chin, cheeks, lips, teeth and eyes.[^gretil-pratima]

Little reliable information about the work's date, origin or transmission could be established from the sources consulted. The digital editions identify only the printed edition on which they are based, an edition by Ngawang Samten and Janardan Pandey in the journal *Dhīḥ* published at Sarnath in 2003, and no Tibetan or Chinese parallels, manuscript descriptions or secondary studies were verified here.[^dsbc317]
