The *Abhisamayālaṃkāravivaraṇa* is a short Sanskrit prose text that opens with the words "a brief explanation of the subjects contained in the *Abhisamayālaṅkāra*" and is headed *pariśiṣṭam* 1, that is, "appendix 1".[^gretil-vivarana] It appears to be an explanatory appendix in a modern edition rather than a freestanding classical commentary, and very little about it is documented independently. Its stated purpose is to set out concisely the eight subjects (*padārtha*) and seventy topics that the *Abhisamayālaṅkāra* teaches.[^gretil-vivarana]

The *Abhisamayālaṅkāra* is the verse treatise, traditionally ascribed to Maitreya, that organises the content of the Perfection of Wisdom (*Prajñāpāramitā*) sūtras. Its eight subjects are knowledge of all aspects, knowledge of the paths, knowledge of the basis, the complete application, the culminating application, the serial application, the single-instant application and the *dharmakāya*, which are elaborated into seventy points; in the Tibetan scholastic presentation these have 10, 11, 9, 11, 8, 13, 4 and 4 topics respectively.[^lotsawa] The text here follows that scheme, quoting the two opening verses that list the eight subjects and then giving, for each, its definition (*lakṣaṇa*), its divisions (*bheda*) and its scope (*avadhi*), for example for the knowledge of all aspects (*sarvākārajñatā*).[^gretil-vivarana]

The sources consulted give no author, date or manuscript tradition for this text, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation is known to the present author. The only edition identified is Tripathi's of 1977, whose title as recorded in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon is *Abhisamayalankarantah patinam*.[^dsbc277]
