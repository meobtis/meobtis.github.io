The *Grahamātṛkādhāraṇī*, whose text is headed *Āryagrahamātṛkā nāma dhāraṇī* ("the noble dhāraṇī called Grahamātṛkā"), is a short Sanskrit Buddhist protective text. It opens with a salutation to the goddess Āryagrahamātṛkā and the usual sūtra-style introduction ("Thus have I heard"), set in the great city of Aḍakavatī, where the Buddha is attended by a large assembly of bodhisattvas headed by Vajrapāṇi.[^gretil-grahamatrka]

In the text, Vajrapāṇi asks the Buddha for a teaching that will protect beings from the harm done by the *grahas*, the planetary and other seizing powers. The Buddha then prescribes offerings, incense and food for each of the planets, together with mantras addressed to the sun, moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, Saturn, Rāhu and Ketu, and describes the diagram (*maṇḍala*) on which they are to be worshipped. The planets, pacified, offer to protect the preacher of the Dharma.[^gretil-grahamatrka]

Little reliable information on the work's date, authorship, transmission or translations could be found in the sources consulted, so none is asserted here. The only printed edition identified is the one named in the digital record, published in *Dhīḥ* in 2005.[^gretil-grahamatrka]
