The *Jātinirākṛti* ("Refutation of Jāti") is a short Sanskrit work attributed to Jitāri (Tibetan Dgra las rnam rgyal), an Indian Buddhist philosopher of the later tenth century who followed Dharmakīrti's epistemological tradition and is also associated with Madhyamaka.[^wiki-jitari] The word *jāti* can mean both "universal" and "caste", and the work is listed in reference sources under the English title "Analysis of Caste", alongside separate works by Jitāri on universals and a critique of the twice-born castes.[^wiki-jitari] The digital texts, however, open with a salutation to Mañjuśrī and a statement that the author will set out some "topics of debate", and then begin by refuting the *jātivāda*, the theory of universals. The first argument holds that a universal can be described neither as different from nor as identical with the individuals in which it is supposed to reside, and so is not a real entity.[^sarit-jati]

Little else about this particular text could be established from the sources consulted. Jitāri is traditionally said to have been born in Varendra in Bengal, to have remained a lay follower, and to have been given the title of paṇḍita at Vikramaśīla; he is reported to have taught Atiśa and Ratnākaraśānti.[^wiki-jitari] Those details come from a tertiary source relaying later tradition and should be treated with caution. Its relation to Jitāri's other works on universals, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation, are not documented in the sources consulted here.
