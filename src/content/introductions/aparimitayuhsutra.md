The *Aparimitāyuḥsūtra* is a short Mahāyāna sūtra on longevity. In English it is generally known by the Sanskrit-derived title *The Aparimitāyurjñāna Sūtra*, and in Tibetan as the *Tsédo*, the "Sūtra of Longevity" or "Sūtra of Long Life".[^84000toh674] The name of its central buddha appears in three forms: Aparimitāyus, Aparimitāyurjñāna and Aparimitāyurjñānasuviniścitatejorāja.[^84000toh674] The Tibetan rendering of the shortest form, Tsépamé, is also the usual equivalent of Amitāyus, but the 84000 introduction argues that this buddha is presented as distinct from the Amitāyus or Amitābha of Sukhāvatī.[^84000toh675]

In the sūtra the Buddha Śākyamuni describes the buddha Aparimitāyurjñāna, who dwells in a realm above ours, Aparimitaguṇasaṃcaya. He promises that reciting, writing or honouring the text, and repeating its dhāraṇī, brings long life, protection and a good rebirth.[^84000toh674] The Sanskrit text in this edition follows that pattern: the Buddha addresses Mañjuśrī, announces the merit of hearing and copying the buddha's hundred and eight names, and gives a dhāraṇī, after which a series of paragraphs introduced by "*oṃ namo bhagavate*" states the benefits of copying the sūtra.[^gretil-aparimitayus]

The text survives in two closely related forms, distinguished by the number of times *oṃ* occurs in the dhāraṇī. The "three *oṃ*" version is the one best known in the Tibetan canon (Toh 674), while a "two *oṃ*" version is Toh 675.[^84000toh674] There are two Chinese translations, by Facheng (Taishō 936, early ninth century) and Fatian (Taishō 937, late tenth century), both with the "two *oṃ*" dhāraṇī. Early Tibetan manuscripts from Dunhuang, mostly of the "two *oṃ*" form, probably reflect an earlier translation.[^84000toh674] Well over a hundred Sanskrit manuscripts are known, mostly later Nepalese copies containing the "three *oṃ*" version; the oldest known witness is a Khotanese manuscript from Dunhuang, probably of the seventh or eighth century, with the "two *oṃ*" version. Early Sanskrit editions were produced by Sten Konow and Max Walleser in 1916.[^84000toh675] The 84000 introductions do not date the original composition, and it is not securely dated beyond the existence of these witnesses.[^84000toh674]
