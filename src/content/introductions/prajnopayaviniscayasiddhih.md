The *Prajñopāyaviniścayasiddhi* ("The Accomplishment of the Determination of Wisdom and Skilful Means") is a Sanskrit Buddhist tantric treatise. The digital text is a single section, divided into chapters (*paricchedas*), and is catalogued by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the commentarial works on the yoginī tantras.[^dsbc784] No established English title could be confirmed from the sources consulted.

The text opens with a salutation to Śrī Vajrasattva and an invocation of the Buddha's body of doctrine as constituted by wisdom (*prajñā*) and skilful means (*upāya*). The opening verses state that it is composed "briefly" for the benefit of beings who do not know the true nature of things, and that the cycle of existence arises from conceptual construction, which the wise say deludes the foolish.[^dsbc784] The title itself points to the central theme, the determination of the nature of wisdom and means as the two aspects of the path.

In the verse that follows the invocation, the author names himself as the venerable Anaṅgavajra.[^dsbc784] Anything further about his identity, date and school, and about the work's Tibetan translation, is not documented in the sources that could be consulted for this entry, and is left out rather than guessed.

The text is available in B. Bhattacharyya's 1929 edition, published by the Oriental Institute in Baroda, which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon digitised in 2018 in both romanized and Devanagari forms.[^dsbc784][^dsbc785] Little else about the work's manuscripts and reception is recorded here.
