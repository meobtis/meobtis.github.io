The *Vigrahavyāvartanīkārikā* is a Sanskrit text in verse (*kārikā*) that GRETIL attributes to Nāgārjuna and files among its Buddhist philosophical works.[^gretil-vvk] It belongs to the Madhyamaka tradition of Mahāyāna Buddhist philosophy and is a work of argument rather than of narrative or ritual.

The opening verses show its structure. In the first verse an objector states that if no entity anywhere possesses intrinsic nature (*svabhāva*), then the speaker's own statement must also lack it and so cannot negate intrinsic nature. The following verses examine this charge, take up the status of the speaker's own thesis, and discuss perception (*pratyakṣa*) and inference (*anumāna*) as means of knowledge, so that the text proceeds as a debate between an objector and a respondent.[^gretil-vvk] Little else about the work, such as its exact date, its translations into Tibetan and Chinese, or the printed editions of the Sanskrit, could be verified from sources consulted for this introduction, and readers should consult scholarly editions and translations for these matters.
