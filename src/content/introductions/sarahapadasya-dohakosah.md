The Sarahapādasya dohākoṣaḥ is a collection of *dohās*, couplets in an Apabhraṃśa vernacular, attributed to the Buddhist siddha Saraha. In this digital edition the text is accompanied by a Sanskrit commentary: it opens with a salutation to Vajrasattva and a statement that a "Sahajāmnāyapañjikā" on the Dohākoṣa is being written, then takes the verses one by one, quoting each in its vernacular form and explaining it in Sanskrit.[^dsbc350] The commentary begins by criticising the six philosophical positions (Brahmanical, Īśvara-centred, Jain, Buddhist, Lokāyata and Sāṃkhya) and attacks the claim that birth into a caste makes a person a brahmin.[^dsbc350]

Saraha is traditionally counted among the Buddhist mahāsiddhas. Wikipedia, which is used here only as a lead, notes that scholarly opinion on his date is not settled; one argument places him in the late eighth or early ninth century, and it is described there as disputed.[^wiki-saraha] A manuscript of the Dohākoṣa was reported in Nepal in 1907 by Haraprasad Shastri, and some of the dohās were first printed in 1916 with Sanskrit notes; the Tibetan canon also contains several works titled Dohākoṣa with different translators.[^wiki-saraha]

Little further reliable information about this specific Sanskrit commentary could be gathered. Its authorship, its date, and its exact relationship to the printed edition and to the Tibetan translations are not established in the sources consulted, so they are left unstated.
