The *Potalakāṣṭakam*, "Eight Verses on Potalaka", is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, filed by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among its hymns to that figure.[^dsbc130] The title refers to Mount Potalaka, the mythical dwelling of Avalokiteśvara, which first appears in the final chapter of the *Gaṇḍavyūha* section of the *Avataṃsaka Sūtra*.[^wikipotalaka] The colophon of the text calls it the *Potalakāṣṭaka* of the noble Avalokiteśvara.[^dsbc130]

The eight verses describe the bodhisattva seated on a lotus, holding a lotus and the perfections, bearing an image of the Tathāgata Amitābha in his crown, and adorned with jewelled earrings, bracelets and anklets. They also mention his dwelling in mountain caves, his banner and parasol, and his power to free beings from fever, disease and leprosy and from evil rebirths, and they end by calling him lord of the universe through compassion and suchness.[^dsbc130]

Little reliable information on the hymn's author, date or place of composition could be found, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation or manuscript tradition has been verified here, so none is asserted. The digital text records only that it derives from Pandey's printed collection of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc130]
