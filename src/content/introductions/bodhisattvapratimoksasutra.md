The *Bodhisattvaprātimokṣasūtra* is a short Sanskrit text of the Mahāyāna that opens with a procedure for taking the bodhisattva precepts: the candidate asks a qualified bodhisattva to administer the vows, confesses wrongdoing, takes the three refuges, and generates the aspiration to awakening, in front of an image of the Buddha.[^gretil-bps] No established English title is known to be in general use. The text is known through the edition by Nalinaksha Dutt, published in Calcutta in 1931.[^dsbc89]

Little is documented about its origin. The 84000 introduction to *Determining the Vinaya: Upāli's Questions* (Toh 68), in the Heap of Jewels (Ratnakūṭa) section of the Tibetan Kangyur, notes that a lengthy citation from that sūtra survives in this Sanskrit text, which Dutt studied and reproduced in 1931.[^84000toh68] Sangyop Lee reports that, since Dutt, the manuscript has often been called the Bodhisattvaprātimokṣa Sūtra because of a phrase partway through it, a practice he advises against; he also distinguishes it from the "bodhisattva prātimokṣa" of the *Bodhisattvabhūmi*, and from a work of the same name cited in the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.[^lee2021] Its date, authorship and relation to other works are therefore uncertain, and this introduction does not attempt to resolve them.
