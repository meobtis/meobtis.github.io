The *Vasudhārāstotram* is a brief Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) addressed to Vasudhārā, a Buddhist goddess of wealth and abundance whose name means "stream of gems".[^wiki-vasudhara] It consists of four verses in which the speaker bows to her. The verses describe her as bestowing great prosperity on those who recite her text correctly, as calming the suffering and misfortune of poverty for those who remember her, and as a mine of jewels who rains down wealth.[^dsbc219] No established English title was found; "Hymn to Vasudhārā" is a descriptive rendering.

The closing line of the text states that this hymn is excerpted from the *Śrīvasudhārākalpa*, a ritual work on Vasudhārā.[^dsbc219] Vasudhārā's cult is traditionally traced to the Vasudhārā dhāraṇī, and she is especially popular among the Newar Buddhists of the Kathmandu Valley, where she has been a household deity.[^wiki-vasudhara] The Tibetan canon contains a dhāraṇī of Vasudhārā (Toh 662).[^84000toh662] Little else about this particular hymn is documented in the sources consulted: no author, date or manuscript history could be established, and the digital record names only the printed anthology from which it was keyed.[^dsbc219]
