The *Aṣṭādaśasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā* is one of the Long Perfection of Wisdom sūtras, translated into English as *The Perfection of Wisdom in Eighteen Thousand Lines* (Toh 10). It is one of several versions of the Perfection of Wisdom that developed in South and South-Central Asia, probably in the first five centuries CE.[^84000toh10]

According to the introduction to the 84000 translation, the sūtra is arranged in five major divisions: an introduction, a brief exegesis, an intermediate exegesis, a detailed exegesis (chapters 22 to 82) and concluding summaries (chapters 84 to 86). Chapter 84 is a verse summary that also circulates separately in the Tibetan canon, and chapters 85 and 86 tell the story of Sadāprarudita and Dharmodgata before the Buddha entrusts the text to Ānanda.[^84000toh10] The text shares much of its structure and content with the Eight Thousand, Twenty-Five Thousand and One Hundred Thousand versions.[^84000toh10]

The principal Sanskrit witness is the Gilgit manuscript, a large birch-bark manuscript found in 1931 and dated by Zacchetti to between the sixth and early seventh centuries. Conze divided it into a Twenty-Five Thousand and an Eighteen Thousand portion, but Zacchetti argues that this division rests on a scribal error and that the manuscript reflects a single version.[^84000toh10] The text was known in Tibetan by about 820, when it is listed in the Denkarma catalogue.[^84000toh10] In Chinese, Bodhiruci in the early sixth century is the first to mention an Eighteen Thousand version explicitly; whether part of Xuanzang's large *Dabanruoboluomi jing* (completed around 659) corresponds to it is not confirmed.[^84000toh10]

Little further is documented here about the authorship of the sūtra, which like the other Perfection of Wisdom literature is not attributed to a named author.
