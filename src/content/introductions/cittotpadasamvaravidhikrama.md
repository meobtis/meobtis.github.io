The *Cittotpādasaṃvaravidhikrama* ("The Procedure for Generating the Aspiration and Taking the Vow") is a short Sanskrit prose work, with a few verses, that sets out a liturgy for giving rise to the aspiration to awakening (*bodhicitta*) and then receiving the bodhisattva vows. Both digital editions attribute it to Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, the Bengali master better known as Atīśa.[^gretil-citto][^dsbc264] The text opens with a salutation to Mañjuśrī and two introductory verses, and then moves through the stages of the ceremony.[^gretil-citto]

The text itself prescribes the sequence: homage and offerings, a request to the teacher, the threefold going for refuge, and the formal generation of the aspiration to free beings until awakening. It then lists four things that obstruct *bodhicitta* and four that preserve it, and gives a separate procedure for a person without a teacher, who performs the rite alone before the buddhas visualised in the ten directions. A second part covers taking the bodhisattva moral vows from a qualified teacher.[^gretil-citto] Atīśa (c. 982–1054) is generally said to have travelled from Vikramaśīla to Tibet in 1040.[^wiki-atisa]

Little reliable information on this particular work could be found in the sources consulted: its date of composition, its Tibetan and Chinese translations and its manuscript tradition are not documented in the records used here, and the attribution rests on the editions' own metadata. The DSBC text comes from Negi's printed collection titled *Ekādaśagrantha*, a collection of works attributed to Atīśa.[^dsbc264]
