The *Prajñāpāramitāpiṇḍārtha* ("Summary of the Perfection of Wisdom") is a short work in Sanskrit verse, attributed to the Buddhist scholar Dignāga. It is listed among his religious or scriptural writings as a summary of the *Aṣṭasāhasrikāprajñāpāramitā*, and is said to survive in both Sanskrit and Tibetan, with a Derge Kanjur number of 3809.[^wikipedia-dignaga] The English title used here is a gloss found in that source rather than an established translated title.

The opening verses of the Sanskrit text present the perfection of wisdom as non-dual knowledge, identified with the Tathāgata, and then list the topics through which the sūtra is to be read, among them its basis, its subject matter, practice, classification and benefits. Later verses speak of thirty-two principal topics and of a sixteenfold classification of emptiness, and describe the work as a condensation of the eight-thousand-line sūtra.[^gretil-pindartha]

Little reliable information about the work's date, transmission or printed editions could be established from the sources consulted. The attribution to Dignāga is accepted by the reference source cited above, which raises no doubt about it.[^wikipedia-dignaga] Details of the Tibetan translation and any modern critical edition remain to be checked against specialist scholarship.
