The *Bodhisattvāvadānakalpalatā* ("The Wish-Fulfilling Vine of Tales of the Bodhisattva") is a poetic epitome of Buddhist narrative literature by Kṣemendra, an eleventh-century Kashmiri poet.[^fiordalis2021] The work is often cited simply as the *Avadānakalpalatā*, but its Tibetan translation, found in the Tengyur as *Byang chub sems dpa'i rtogs pa brjod pa dpag bsam gyi 'khri shing*, was made by Shong ston rdo rje rgyal mtshan and Lakṣmīkara.[^fiordalis2021] A general study of the work with source materials is Mejor's *Kṣemendra's Bodhisattvāvadānakalpalatā: Studies and Materials* (1992).[^mejor1992]

The text offered here is not the whole collection but a single chapter, number 93, the Sumāgadhāvadāna. In the verses, the merchant Anāthapiṇḍada tells the Buddha at Jetavana in Śrāvastī that he wishes to give his daughter Sumāgadhā in marriage to Vṛṣabhadatta, son of a caravan leader at Puṇḍravardhana; the Buddha sees no objection, and she is married and travels far away, remembering the Buddha with tears.[^gretil-sumagadha] The same story is transmitted separately in the *Vratāvadānamālā* tradition, and a manuscript record notes that it also occurs in the *Avadānakalpalatā* as chapter 93.[^matsunami257]

For the whole work, Sanskrit editions include that of Das and Vidyabhusana (Calcutta, 1888), based on a Lhasa xylograph and Nepalese manuscripts, and Vaidya's of 1959 (Darbhanga). Two palm-leaf manuscripts at Drepung Monastery have more recently come to light, one preserving the complete work in Sanskrit.[^fiordalis2021] Beyond these points, little further detail about this individual chapter could be verified in the sources consulted.
