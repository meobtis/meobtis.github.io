The *Gurukriyākrama* ("The Sequence of the Teacher's Actions") is a very short Sanskrit prose text. Its colophon calls it the "abridged" sequence and names the great scholar-teacher Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, that is Atīśa, as its author.[^gretil-guru] The DSBC edition derives from a printed collection of works attributed to Atiśa.[^dsbc220] No English title or study of the work was found, and no independent scholarly discussion of its date, transmission or Tibetan translation could be consulted, so those matters are left open here.

From the text itself, it is a brief procedural guide for a teacher (*ācārya*). The teacher first awakens faith in the disciple, speaks of the benefits of the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*) and arouses enthusiasm; the offerings, praise, confession, rejoicing, requests and dedication are then carried out, and the disciple generates the two kinds of awakening mind. The teacher then explains the bodhisattva practices, namely the six perfections, the four means of gathering disciples and the four immeasurables, with their characteristics, causes, results and the merits and faults of keeping or losing them, and the threefold training, concluding with an aspiration prayer. The closing lines say that this is only a rough outline and that fuller instruction is to be learned elsewhere.[^gretil-guru]
