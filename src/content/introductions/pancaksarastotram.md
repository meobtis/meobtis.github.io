The *Pañcākṣarastotram* ("Hymn of the Five Syllables") is a short Sanskrit hymn of six verses. It opens with the salutation "namo buddhāya" and then takes up the syllables of the formula in turn: the first four verses each explain one syllable (the negation *na*, *mo*, *bu* and *dha*) and the fifth gives a further syllable, *ya*, so that each is read as an epithet or quality of the Buddha. The remaining verse states the merit of reciting the hymn by day and night, promising the highest destination to those who do so.[^dsbc148]

The verses play on sound: for instance, *na* is explained as pointing to one who is neither born nor dead and is found neither in saṃsāra nor in nirvāṇa, while *dha* is linked to the way of the dharma-realm (*dharmadhātu*) borne by bodhisattvas.[^dsbc148] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files the text among its *dharmadhātu* hymns.[^dsbc148]

Little reliable information about the hymn could be found in the sources available for this entry. Its author, date and place of composition are not given in the DSBC records, and no English title, Tibetan or Chinese translation, or scholarly study of it was located. The only printed edition identified is the one named in the DSBC record, Pandey's anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc148]
