The *Caryāsaṃgrahapradīpa* ("Lamp Summarising Conduct") is a very short Sanskrit verse work of twenty-two numbered stanzas, opening with a salutation to Lokeśvara (Avalokiteśvara). The author states that awakening is achieved by relying on both the perfection (*pāramitā*) approach and the secret mantra approach, but that he will here set out only, in brief, the conduct of a bodhisattva following the perfection method.[^gretil-csp] The verses then give practical advice: keeping the vows in body, speech and mind, moderation in food, guarding the sense doors, practising meditation in the early and late parts of the night, reciting aspirations, making offerings and gifts, and sleeping mindfully in the lion posture.[^gretil-csp]

The colophon ascribes the work to the great scholar and teacher Dīpaṅkara-śrījñāna, that is, the master usually known as Atiśa (c. 982–1054), and a general reference article on Atiśa likewise lists a *Caryāsaṃgrahapradīpa* among his works.[^wiki-atisa] No detailed scholarly study was found of the text, its manuscript tradition or its Tibetan and Chinese translations, and these matters are therefore left aside. Little about its date, transmission or reception is documented in the sources consulted.
