The *Catuṣpariṣat sūtra* ("Sūtra of the Four Assemblies") is a short Sanskrit text, listed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the Mahāyāna sūtras.[^dsbc38] The text is a single continuous piece of numbered passages, and it does not itself open with the usual introductory formula; it begins directly with the Bodhisattva-Bhagavān dwelling at Uruvilvā on the bank of the Nairañjanā river, at the foot of the tree of awakening.[^dsbc38]

As the digital text shows, the passages follow his night of meditation in turn: in the first watch he turns his mind to supernormal powers and the recollection of past lives, in the middle watch to the divine ear and divine eye (seeing beings pass away and be reborn according to their actions), and in the last watch to knowing the minds of others and the destruction of the taints. The text ends with his understanding of the four noble truths, the release of his mind from the taints, and the declaration that birth is exhausted, with the Buddha described as having done what was to be done.[^dsbc38]

The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon lists this text among the Mahāyāna sūtras, but it belongs with the early discourses. Anālayo identifies the *Catuṣpariṣat-sūtra* as the fourth discourse of the (Mūla-)Sarvāstivāda *Dīrgha-āgama*, whose first section, the *Ṣaṭsūtraka-nipāta*, also holds the *Mahāvadāna-sūtra* and the *Mahāparinirvāṇa-sūtra*; Bucknell's table lists it as the fourth discourse of the collection.[^analayo2017a][^bucknell2014] He describes it as an account of how the four assemblies of Buddhist disciples (monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen) came into being, with counterparts in the Vinayas of other schools, which suggests that material moved between the Vinayas and the discourse collections.[^analayo2017a] The Pali counterpart is not in the Nikāyas but in the Khandhaka section of the Pali Vinaya, so the Sarvāstivādins' placement of the text in their *Dīrgha-āgama* probably reflects the movement of a single large text from the Vinaya to the Sūtra-piṭaka.[^bucknell2014] Anālayo likewise notes that no parallel is known in the four Pali Nikāyas.[^analayo2017a] The digital text shown on this site covers only the part about the awakening. Waldschmidt's comparative study (1951/1967) and Kloppenborg's English translation (1973) are the standard studies of the sūtra.[^analayo2017a]

The edition history above rests on the bibliographic record of the digital edition, which credits a TITUS (Frankfurt, 2000) version.[^dsbc38] Little is documented there about the printed source, so none is asserted.
