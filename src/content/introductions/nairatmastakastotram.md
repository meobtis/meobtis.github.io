The *Nairātmāṣṭakastotram* ("Octet in praise of Nairātmā") is a short Sanskrit hymn of eight verses addressed to the goddess Nairātmā, whose name means roughly "she who is without self". The text opens with a salutation to her as a boon-giving goddess whose nature is free of support, form and conceptual construction, and goes on to describe her iconography: one face, three eyes, upward-standing tawny hair, a curved knife and skull-cup in her hands, and a corpse as her seat. Later verses salute the eight yoginīs (Gaurī, Caurī, Vetālī, Ghasmarī, Pukkasī, Śabarī, Cāṇḍālī and Ḍombī) and her victory over the four Māras, and the last verse addresses her as the mother of all mantras.[^dsbc163]

Nairātmā (also Nairātmyā; Tibetan *bdag med ma*) is known in Tibetan Buddhism as a ḍākinī and the consort of Hevajra in the Hevajra Tantra.[^wiki-nairatmya] Beyond the text itself, little reliable information on this particular hymn could be found: no author, date or place of composition, no translations into Tibetan or English, and no manuscript history were located, and it is not discussed here beyond what the edition records state. Its place in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon is under the heading of stotras to Nairātmā, within the śāstra section.[^dsbc163]
