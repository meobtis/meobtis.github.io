The *Vādanyāya* is a brief treatise on the conduct of debate by Dharmakīrti, the seventh-century Buddhist epistemologist. It is one of seven works attributed to him, and Frauwallner's proposed order of composition places it fifth among the five he could sequence (after the *Pramāṇavārttika*, *Pramāṇaviniścaya*, *Nyāyabindu* and *Hetubindu*).[^tillemans2026] The title is rendered in English in several ways; the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy gives "Logic of Argumentation", and "Reasoning for Debate" is also seen.[^tillemans2026][^wiki-dharmakirti]

The text begins by setting out the grounds on which a debater may be declared defeated (*nigrahasthāna*), a topic inherited from Nyāya debate theory. As its opening verse in the digital editions shows, Dharmakīrti allows only two: failing to state a part of a valid proof (*asādhanāṅgavacana*) and failing to point out a real fault in the opponent's position (*adoṣodbhāvana*).[^dsbc308] It then discusses the kinds of reasons (*liṅga*), namely essential property, effect and non-perception. According to Tillemans, it is here that Dharmakīrti's view of the "inference for others" is at its strictest: stating the thesis is itself treated as a point of defeat, since the conclusion can be grasped indirectly without being stated.[^tillemans2026]

Dharmakīrti's dates remain disputed: Frauwallner proposed about 600–660 CE, while later scholars have argued for an earlier floruit, from the mid-sixth century or 550–610 CE.[^tillemans2026] I found no reliable source for the work's Tibetan and Chinese translations, its manuscripts or its commentaries, and these are therefore not described here. The digital texts derive from Much's edition (Part I, 1991) and Gokhale's edition (1993).[^gretil-vadanyaya][^dsbc308]
