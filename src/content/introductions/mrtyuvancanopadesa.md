The *Mṛtyuvañcanopadeśa* ("instruction on cheating death") is a Sanskrit verse treatise that GRETIL attributes to Vāgīśvarakīrti. The title of Schneider's edition describes it as a Buddhist doctrinal work for warding off death.[^gretil-mrtyu] The text opens with an invocation, a salutation to the Buddha, and a statement of the subject: a method of "cheating" death that is hard for the dull-witted and the unsteady, yet is said to accord with the treatises.[^gretil-mrtyu]

In its opening verses the author defines death as the cessation of life-force, the sense faculties and mind, and says that life can be lengthened by various means, which is what "cheating death" denotes. He argues that, while one lives in saṃsāra, a person of disciplined conduct accumulates the merit that furthers awakening, so that prolonging life is worthwhile. He then says that death has external and internal signs by which its approach can be known, so that a suitable countermeasure can be applied, and he appeals to the recognised power of jewels, mantras and medicines.[^gretil-mrtyu]

Little else could be established from the sources available for this entry. Neither the date of the work, the identity of its author, nor its Tibetan or Chinese versions are documented in the GRETIL record, and no established English title was found, so none is given here.
