The *Yaśodharācarita* ("The Story of Yaśodharā") is a short Sanskrit poem in verse, classified as *kāvya*, attributed to Davuldeṇa Jñāneśvara Mahāsthavira, a Sri Lankan Buddhist monk. The digital editions derive from a 2003 publication titled "Contemporary Sri Lankan Sanskrit Poetry", which indicates that the poem is a modern composition rather than a work of the classical Indian Buddhist tradition.[^gretil-yasodhara] No established English title for the work was found.

The text begins with the Great Departure: the Bodhisattva Siddhārtha leaves Kapilavastu at midnight on the full-moon day of Āṣāḍha, giving up wife, child and royal fortune in search of liberation. The poem then follows Yaśodharā as she rises at dawn with her son, searches the palace for her husband and, not finding him, hurries to the minister's house. The digital text numbers its verses and ends at verse 55, where the narrative has turned to Siddhārtha's youth and his marriage in his sixteenth year; the editions give no indication whether this is the complete poem.[^gretil-yasodhara]

Very little reliable information about the poem, its author or its reception could be found; the details above come from the edition records and the text itself. The printed source is the presentation by Bhikkhu Pāsādika and Bhikkhu Tampalawela Dhammaratana in *Bulletin d'études indiennes* (Paris, 2003); the digital texts were prepared from it by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon project, whose records give no further description of the work.[^dsbc227]
