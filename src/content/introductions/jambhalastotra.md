The *Jambhalastotra* is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) addressed to Jambhala. The text consists of nine verses, opening with the salutation "oṁ namo jambhālāya" and closing with a colophon stating that it is the *Jambhalastotra* composed by Candragomin.[^dsbc948] In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed among the *stotra* texts under the heading *vasudhārā*.[^dsbc948]

In the verses the speaker, who describes himself as afflicted by suffering, appeals to Jambhala as a compassionate protector who is easily pleased and who looks after those fallen into bad rebirths. He compares the deity's generosity to the wish-fulfilling jewel and similar emblems of abundance, and asks for a share of the wealth the deity rains down for the benefit of beings. The final verses say that Lakṣmī dwells with whoever utters the deity's name even briefly, and dedicate the merit of the plea to the aim that people may become, like the deity, givers of what is desired.[^dsbc948] Jambhala is a Buddhist deity of fortune and wealth, sometimes equated with the Hindu Kubera, and is said in Tibetan tradition to be an emanation of Avalokiteśvara.[^wikipedia-jambhala]

Little is documented about this particular work. The attribution to Candragomin rests only on the colophon of the digital text, and no scholarly discussion confirming or dating it, nor information on manuscripts or Tibetan and Chinese translations, was located. The Wikipedia article on Jambhala is a tertiary source citing mainly non-academic web pages, so it is used here only for the general identification of the deity.
