Only four pages of this manuscript have been transcribed, taken from a scan of 107 pages that Commons dates to the seventeenth century, and the text of those pages is corrupt OCR that cannot be read reliably.[^commons102][^an102] The identity of the work therefore cannot be established from the transcription, and no source consulted describes a work with this title (*Guṇakāruṇyamaya* or similar, followed by *Biu*, a word that could not be explained).

What can be said is limited. The text is of mixed language: Sanskrit verses numbered with the usual double *daṇḍa* alternate with Newar passages.[^an102] It opens with homage ("*oṃ namaḥ*") and verses praising the Buddhas, mentions a goddess (*bhagavatī devī*) and lay followers (*upāsaka*, *upāsikā*), and the last transcribed page names Lokeśvara and the noble Avalokiteśvara and what appears to be Yamarāja.[^an102]

One line, on the page numbered 33 in the scan, reads like the colophon formula of the *Kāraṇḍavyūha*, "*iti … mahāyāna sūtra ratna[rāja] prathama …*" (the sūtra is catalogued in Nepalese manuscripts as *Ārya Kāraṇḍavyūha Mahāyāna Sūtra Ratnarāja*), and the later Nepalese verse version of that sūtra is known as the *Guṇakāraṇḍavyūha*.[^eap][^84000] That resemblance, and the similarity of the catalogue title to *Guṇakāraṇḍavyūha*, may or may not be significant; it is offered only as a tentative lead, and the manuscript might equally be a different work on Lokeśvara.
