The *Tārābhaṭṭārikānāmāṣṭottaraśatakastotra* ("Hymn of the one hundred and eight names of the Venerable Tārā") is a short Sanskrit stotra in verse, headed in the text as *Āryatārābhaṭṭārikānāmāṣṭottaraśatakastotra* and opening with a salutation to the noble Tārā. Its verses are numbered with the siglum "Tbh". The introductory verses set the scene on the beautiful Mount Potalaka, filled with trees, birds, kinnaras, siddhas, vidyādharas and bodhisattvas, where the lord Avalokita is described as teaching an assembly of deities.[^gretil-tarabhattarika] Potalaka is the traditional abode of Avalokiteśvara, and Tārā is widely associated with him in Buddhist tradition.[^wiki-tara]

The hymn belongs to the literature praising Tārā through lists of epithets, a form attested for her in several languages; a separate Tibetan-canon text on the same theme, translated by the 84000 project, should not be assumed to be the same work.[^wiki-tara] Little reliable scholarship specifically on this Sanskrit hymn could be found: its author, date and place of composition, and its relation to other Tārā name-lists are not documented in the sources consulted, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation or manuscript tradition could be verified here. The digital text is based on the printed edition in Godefroy de Blonay's 1895 *Matériaux pour servir à l'histoire de la déesse buddhique Tārā*, which presented Sanskrit materials on the goddess.[^gretil-tarabhattarika]
