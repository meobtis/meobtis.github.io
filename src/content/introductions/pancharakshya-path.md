The Pañcarakṣā, "Five Protectresses", is a group of five Buddhist protective texts of the dhāraṇī type, each presided over by a goddess. They are the Mahāsāhasrapramardanī, Mahāmāyūrī, Mahāpratisarā, Mahāśītavatī and Mahāmantrānusāriṇī (the order varies between lists); the Tibetan canon catalogues them together as the "Five Great Dhāraṇīs" in the Kriyātantra section.[^84000toh561][^wiki] The manuscript behind this edition is a Nepalese copy that the Commons record dates to the sixteenth century; it is catalogued as "Pancharakshya Path", a Latin rendering of *Pañcarakṣā pāṭha*, "recitation of the Pañcarakṣā".[^commons89]

Reading the transcription shows that it begins with the opening of the Mahāsāhasrapramardanī (set at Rājagṛha, with its protective spells), whose colophon *Āryamahāsāhasrapramardanī dhāraṇī samāptaṃ* follows on page 4. The Mahāmāyūrī then follows, ending with a colophon at the close of its second spell section, and a text beginning with homage to Āryā Mahāpratisarā appears from page 17. Later pages contain further dhāraṇī and homage-lists, and the final transcribed pages are unreadable OCR; the Mahāśītavatī and Mahāmantrānusāriṇī could not be identified in the transcribed pages.[^wsindex89] Much of the text consists of dhāraṇī syllables, whose spellings in Newar manuscripts vary considerably.

The Pañcarakṣā is still central to the Vajrayāna of the Newars of the Kathmandu Valley, who hold annual rites with the five texts, and numerous Nepalese manuscripts of the Mahāpratisarā survive from the ninth century onward.[^84000toh561] The collection is known to have existed by about 800 CE, from Tibetan records, and survives in many divergent versions in Tibet, Nepal and India.[^wiki] The Sanskrit text of individual members is available in other editions in this index, such as the GRETIL texts of the Mahāpratisarā, Mahāsāhasrapramardanī and Mahāmāyūrī. This manuscript has not been collated with them here.
