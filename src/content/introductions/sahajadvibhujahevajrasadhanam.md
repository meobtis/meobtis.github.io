The *Sahajadvibhujahevajrasādhanam* is a short Sanskrit verse text of 74 stanzas. Its opening homage is to Hevajra, and its title identifies it as a *sādhana* (a meditative practice text) concerned with the "innate" (*sahaja*), two-armed (*dvibhuja*) form of Hevajra. The colophon declares it the work of the master (*ācārya*) Alalavajra, and a closing verse states that it was written at the command of Nandipāda.[^dsbc925] Its verses open with reflections on the nature of mind as pure, free of the duality of grasped and grasper, and later include a list of the five factors to be cultivated and the five hindrances to be abandoned.[^dsbc925]

Little reliable information about this work could be gathered for this introduction. The digital edition supplies no bibliographic details for a printed edition, and no scholarly discussion of the text, of its date, or of its Tibetan or Chinese translations was available to the compiler, so none is asserted here. The attribution to Alalavajra rests on the colophon of the digital text alone.
