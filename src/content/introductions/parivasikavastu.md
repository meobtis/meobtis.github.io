The *Pārivāsikavastu* ("Section on Those Undergoing Probation") is one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, a major part of the monastic code (Vinaya) of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school. It is a short text, edited from a manuscript of the Gilgit collection, and the digital text marks the manuscript folios alongside the pages of the printed edition.[^gretil-parivasika] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya survives in a Tibetan translation of about the ninth century and a Chinese one of the eighth century, and only to some extent in Sanskrit.[^wiki-msv]

The section opens with mnemonic verses (*uddāna*) and then sets the scene at Śrāvastī, where the Buddha is staying at Jetavana. Monks who have incurred a *saṃghāvaśeṣa* offence, a serious offence requiring action by the community, feel remorse, abandon the training and leave the order, and the monastic community dwindles. The Buddha therefore prescribes that such monks be given a period of probation (*parivāsa*), followed by further disciplinary periods (*mānāpya*), and he sets out the formal procedure: the offender requests probation from the assembled community, and a motion and a formal act with repeated announcements follow.[^gretil-parivasika]

The remainder lists the duties and restrictions of a monk on probation. He may not accept the customary salutations and seats of other monks, may not give ordination or act as a preceptor, and may not take part in certain formal acts of the community. He must also perform menial services and announce his probation each day to the community and to monks who arrive as visitors.[^gretil-parivasika] The edition's front matter gives no date or author for the section, and I could not consult scholarship on it, so its date, its Tibetan and Chinese counterparts and its reception are not described here.
