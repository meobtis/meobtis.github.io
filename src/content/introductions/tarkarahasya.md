The *Tarkarahasya* ("The Secret of Reasoning") is a Sanskrit treatise in the Buddhist epistemological (*pramāṇa*) tradition. The digital texts open with an invocation to the Buddha and a verse announcing that the work examines the statements of the scriptures on doubtful points, and then begin with the definition of a means of knowledge (*pramāṇa*) as non-deceptive cognition, grounded in effective action (*arthakriyā*).[^gretil-tarkarahasya] The work mixes verse and prose and includes the editors' notes on variant readings and on the sources of its quotations.[^sarit-tarkarahasya]

The editions' own apparatus shows the milieu in which the work is placed: the reference list of the SARIT edition cites Dharmakīrti's *Hetubindu*, Arcaṭa's *Hetubinduṭīkā*, Durvekamiśra's *Dharmottarapradīpa*, Jñānaśrīmitra's treatises and the *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya*, besides grammatical and Mīmāṃsā sources.[^sarit-tarkarahasya] The text is also edited together with the *Dharmottaraṭippanaka* and the *Hetuvidyā* section of the *Yogācārabhūmi* in the same volume by Yaita.[^sarit-tarkarahasya] The sources consulted for this introduction did not establish the author, date or place of composition, nor a reliably documented English title or Tibetan or Chinese translation, so none is asserted here; the work appears to survive in a single manuscript that breaks off before the end of the text.[^gretil-tarkarahasya] A related excerpt on the definition of perception is available separately in the same digital corpus under the title *Pratyakṣalakṣaṇa of the Tarkarahasya*.
