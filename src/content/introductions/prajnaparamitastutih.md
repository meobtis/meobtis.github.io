The *Prajñāpāramitāstutiḥ* ("Hymn to the Perfection of Wisdom") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) classified in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the *prajñāpāramitā* section of the *śāstrapiṭaka*.[^dsbc171] The text consists of five verses, each ending with the refrain "prajñe devi namo 'stu te" ("homage to you, goddess Wisdom"), and opens with the salutation "om namaḥ śrīprajñāpāramitāyai". The wording is that of the digitized text itself.[^dsbc171]

The verses address Prajñāpāramitā as a goddess: free of conceptual construction and without support, she is called the mother of the buddhas and the grandmother of the bodhisattvas, the giver of wishes to those who invoke her as "mother", and she is identified with Lakṣmī, intelligence, faith, steadfastness and memory. The last verse presents her as both worldly and transcendent, taking the form of the ten perfections. The authorship, date and place of composition of the hymn are not stated in the edition's catalogue record, and no further reliable information on its history, manuscripts or translations could be consulted for this introduction, so these remain undocumented here.[^dsbc171]
