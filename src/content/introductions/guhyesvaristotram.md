The *Guhyeśvarīstotram* is a hymn of praise (*stotra*) in seven verses addressed to the goddess Guhyeśvarī, "the Secret Lady". Each of the first six verses ends with the same refrain, in which the speaker bows to the lotus feet of the goddess, and the seventh describes the benefits for one who recites it with devotion at the end of worship. The text is catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the stotras connected with Nairātmyā.[^dsbc162]

The verses praise the goddess as formless and beyond conceptual determination, and then describe her cosmic body, in which the worlds, mountains and oceans are identified with parts of her form. The colophon states that the hymn was taken from the *Svayambhū Purāṇa* and composed by Mañjunātha.[^dsbc162] Little else about the work could be established: the sources consulted do not give an author's dates, a Tibetan or Chinese translation, or manuscript information, and no scholarly study of this hymn was found. The identification of Guhyeśvarī, her relation to Nairātmyā and the date of the text therefore remain to be confirmed from further sources.
