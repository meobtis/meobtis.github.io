The *Caturdharmanirdeśasūtra* (*Āryacaturdharmanirdeśa*), rendered in English as "The Sūtra Teaching the Four Factors", is a very short Mahāyāna sūtra, only a few lines long in the Tibetan canon, where it is numbered Toh 249 in the General Sūtra section of the Degé Kangyur.[^84000toh249] The Sanskrit text has the subtitle *pāpaśodhanopāyabhūtaṃ catuḥpratipakṣabalanirdeśasūtram*, "the sūtra teaching the four powers of antidotes as a means of purifying wrongdoing".[^gretil-caturdharma]

The Buddha is staying in the Sudharmā assembly hall among the Thirty-Three gods and addresses Maitreya. He teaches that a bodhisattva who possesses four factors overcomes negative deeds already committed and accumulated: regret for the deed, the application of antidotes (devoting oneself to virtue), the power of restoration (taking vows of restraint so as not to repeat the act), and the power of support (taking refuge in the Buddha, Dharma and Saṅgha and not abandoning the awakening mind).[^84000toh249][^gretil-caturdharma] Bodhisattvas are advised to recite, study and reflect on the sūtra often, so that wrongdoings do not bear their results.[^gretil-caturdharma]

The sūtra is a Mahāyāna scripture of unknown authorship. According to the 84000 introduction, the Tibetan translation was made by the Indian preceptor Surendrabodhi and the translator-editor Bandé Yeshé Dé, who are placed in the late eighth to early ninth century; the page gives no more exact date.[^84000toh249] In many Kangyurs it is grouped with other sūtras on sets of four factors.[^84000toh249]

Two Sanskrit sources are known. One is a manuscript from the Potala library in Lhasa, now available in a critical edition by Tseng (2010), which contains a four-line verse attributed to the Buddha that is absent from the Tibetan canonical versions. The other is the text printed by Samten and Pandey (2003).[^84000toh249] The edition in the Sarnath journal is the one digitised here; its own text describes it as put back into Sanskrit from the Tibetan, and it names Surendrabodhi and Yeshé Dé as the translators.[^gretil-caturdharma] This account draws only on the sources listed below, and little further has been documented about the sūtra's history.
