The Āyuḥparyantasūtra ("Discourse on the Limit of Life"), known in English as *The Limits of Life*, is a short discourse on how long beings live in the various realms of existence. In the Sanskrit text the Buddha is staying at Śrāvastī, in Anāthapiṇḍada's park at Jetavana, and asks the monks whether they wish to hear about the limits of the lifespans of beings.[^gretil-ayuh]

According to the introduction to the English translation, the sūtra gives a systematic account of lifespans from hell beings and pretas up to the gods of the formless realm. Its main message is renunciation, since existence in any realm is suffering.[^84000toh307] The Sanskrit text proceeds by calculating the span in each realm and converting it into human years; for the pretas, for instance, a day is reckoned as thirty human days, giving a lifespan of five hundred such years, or fifteen thousand human years.[^gretil-ayuh] Verses are interspersed with the prose.[^gretil-ayuh]

The Sanskrit survives in a manuscript from Gilgit, in present-day northern Pakistan. It is about ninety percent complete, and was edited by Hisashi Matsumura in 1989. Its first editors had mistakenly labelled it *Kālasūtra*, and Oskar von Hinüber identified it with the text known in Tibetan.[^84000toh307][^matsumura1989] The sūtra is not attributed to a named author, as is usual for canonical discourses.

In Tibetan the work is catalogued as Toh 307. It was translated by the Indian preceptor Viśuddhasiṃha and the Tibetan monk Gewé Pal, and revised by Vidyākarasiṃha and Bandé Paltsek, in the late eighth and early ninth centuries; it is recorded in the early-ninth-century Denkarma catalogue (c. 812 CE). A Chinese version, the *Jiaoliang shouming jing*, was translated in 984 CE by Tianxi Zai, and appears to rest on a different version of the sūtra.[^84000toh307] The English translation of the Tibetan was made by Bruno Galasek-Hul for 84000.[^84000toh307]
