The *Narakoddhārastotram* ("Hymn for Deliverance from Hell") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist stotra of fifteen verses, each closing with the same refrain, "save me, O Tathāgata". The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among its hymns addressed to Avalokiteśvara, within the *śāstrapiṭaka* section, and takes it from a modern printed anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc129] The text itself ends with the colophon "the *Narakoddhārastotra* is complete".

The verses are a confession and appeal rather than a doctrinal exposition. The speaker describes himself as sunk in the mire of poverty in the ocean of saṃsāra, abandoned by his relatives, who are seen as like illusions, and as blind to right and wrong. He admits to grave misdeeds, including the five *ānantarya* (immediately retributive) acts, destroying a stūpa, harming the Saṃgha and killing living beings, and fears being cooked in hell. The closing verses ask who else could be a refuge and address the one invoked as the physician of physicians and lord of the world (*lokanātha*), a protector in the three worlds.

I found little reliable documentation of this hymn beyond the edition records cited here. Its author, date, school and any Tibetan or Chinese translation could not be established from the sources consulted, and no scholarly discussion was located; the attribution to Avalokiteśvara reflects the catalogue's classification and the epithet *lokanātha* in the last verse, not a verified tradition.
