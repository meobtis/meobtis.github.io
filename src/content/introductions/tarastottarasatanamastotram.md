The *Tārāṣṭottaraśatanāmastotram* ("Hymn of the Hundred and Eight Names of Tārā") is a short Sanskrit stotra in praise of the bodhisattva Tārā, the female deity of compassion and protection. In the digital edition it runs to some fifty-five verses, and its closing colophon calls it the "hundred and eight names of the glorious noble lady Tārā, spoken by the Buddha" (*buddhaparibhāṣita*).[^dsbc115] It belongs to the large body of devotional and ritual literature on Tārā; the tradition of giving her 108 names is also noted in general reference works, although those are tertiary sources.[^wikitara]

The text opens with a narrative frame. On the beautiful Mount Potalaka, Avalokiteśvara is surrounded by bodhisattvas, vidyā-queens and wrathful kings; the bodhisattva Vajrapāṇi asks how beings sunk in saṃsāra, bound by desire, hatred and delusion, may be freed. Avalokiteśvara introduces Tārā as a being born of the vow of Amitābha, and she in turn asks him to proclaim her names. The list itself (verses 28 to 40) strings together epithets, among them Sarasvatī, Prajñāpāramitā, Āryatārā, Mahāmāyā and Abhayā, and ends with the words "daughter of the glorious Lokeśvara". The remaining verses extol the benefits of reciting the names: protection from fear on the road, in battle and from untimely death, worldly prosperity, freedom from harmful spirits, good rebirth and constant companionship with buddhas.[^dsbc115]

Little reliable information on the work's author, date or translations was found in the sources consulted; the text attributes the names to Avalokiteśvara and the colophon to the Buddha, which should be read as the tradition's own framing rather than a historical attribution. The Sanskrit text is available through the Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha, an anthology of Buddhist hymns edited by Pandey.[^dsbc115]
