The *Ratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā* (in full, the *Āryaprajñāpāramitāratnaguṇasaṃcayagāthā*) is a verse text of the Perfection of Wisdom (*Prajñāpāramitā*) literature. The Sanskrit opens with a homage to the Prajñāpāramitā and to Mañjuśrī, and then presents the teaching in verse, introduced as the Buddha addressing the four assemblies to delight them.[^dsbc72] The Tibetan canon lists a text under the title "The Verses that Summarize the Perfection of Wisdom" as Toh 13,[^84000toh13] and a general survey of the Prajñāpāramitā describes it as the sixth of the "Six Mothers" of the Tibetan canon.[^wikipedia-pp]

The text is usually treated as the verse counterpart of the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* (Eight Thousand Line) Perfection of Wisdom, with which it runs in parallel. Some scholars have regarded the verses as slightly older than the prose because they are not in standard literary Sanskrit, although this reasoning depends on late-dated Indian texts in which verses often preserve archaic forms.[^wikipedia-pp] Edward Conze's English rendering, which combines strict translation with summary, appears together with the prose text in *The Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines and Its Verse Summary*.[^wikipedia-pp] Little else about the work could be verified in the sources consulted, so details of its date, place of composition, manuscripts and Chinese parallels are not given here.
