The *Varṣāvastu* ("Chapter on the Rains") is the fourth of the seventeen chapters (*vastu*) of the Vinaya of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school, known in Tibetan translation as *The Chapters on Monastic Discipline*. In English it is called *The Chapter on the Rains*.[^84000toh14]

The chapter describes the rite by which monastics bind themselves to remain at one site during the *varṣā*, the summer rains retreat. The narrative frames the institution of the retreat as a response to criticism from other ascetics, who objected that monks travelling during the rains harmed living creatures, whereupon the Buddha instituted the retreat. The chapter then lists circumstances in which a monk may leave early, such as a lack of alms, medicine or nursing, and circumstances in which leaving helps to resolve a schism in the community. Commentators discuss how the ritual language, framed for monks, was adapted for nuns.[^84000toh14] The rite of ending the retreat is treated in the preceding chapter, so that the two appear in reverse order in the collection.[^84000toh14] The Sanskrit text of the edition begins with the Buddha in Śrāvastī, where a monk is instructed to enter the rains retreat.[^gretil-varsavastu]

The chapter was translated into Chinese by Yijing in the late seventh to early eighth centuries, and into Tibetan by Palgyi Lhünpo with Indian and Kashmiri abbots, later revised by Vidyākaraprabha and Paltsek.[^84000toh14] Most of the Sanskrit survives in the Gilgit manuscript edited by Nalinaksha Dutt, with only one leaf missing, and Masanori Shono later produced a new edition.[^84000toh14][^gretil-varsavastu] Little further scholarship specific to this chapter was consulted for this introduction.
