The Saṃyuktāgama ("Connected Discourses", Chinese *Zá ahánjīng* 雜阿含經) is one of the collections of early Buddhist discourses known as Āgamas, and corresponds in genre to the Pāli *Saṃyutta-nikāya*. It survives almost complete only in Chinese translation (Taishō 99), made by Baoyun from a Sanskrit text recited by Guṇabhadra in 435 CE; a shorter, partial translation (T 100) also exists. Scholars generally connect T 99 with the Sarvāstivāda tradition, though a Mūlasarvāstivāda attribution is also found, and the school of T 100 is disputed.[^wiki-sa] The Sanskrit original is not preserved as a whole, but fragments of it, together with Gāndhārī and Tibetan parallels, have survived, and these have been surveyed by Jin-il Chung.[^wiki-sa]

The digital text offered here is not the whole collection but a small group of Sanskrit fragments that correspond to sūtras 966–972 of the Chinese canon, from the section on the wanderers (*parivrājaka*). The passage that opens the text records the monk Pūrṇika reporting to the Buddha a conversation he had held with a group of non-Buddhist wanderers, and asking whether his account of the Buddha's teaching was faithful.[^gretil-sa966] Little further has been documented about these particular fragments beyond the scholarly editions named in the edition's own front matter, so nothing more is claimed here.
