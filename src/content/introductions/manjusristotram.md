The *Mañjuśrīstotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) addressed to Mañjuśrī, the bodhisattva of wisdom, who is also invoked here under the name Mañjughoṣa. In the text as digitised it runs to nine verses and closes with a colophon calling it a "Mañjuśrī praise-hymn". The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the *śāstrapiṭaka*, in the category of hymns to Mañjuśrī.[^dsbc161]

The verses themselves describe the bodhisattva in devotional terms: a youthful figure adorned with jewelled ornaments, holding a sword and a book, seated on a moon disc, and praised as the sun that dispels the darkness of ignorance and as an ocean of wisdom and knowledge. These are the stock attributes of Mañjuśrī in Buddhist hymnody, though this description comes from reading the verses of the edition itself.[^dsbc161]

Very little is documented about the hymn's author, date or place of composition, and no study or translation of it was found in the sources consulted. It is known here only from the printed anthology of Buddhist hymns on which the digital text is based, so it should not be assumed to be identical to other Mañjuśrī hymns of similar title.[^dsbc161]
