The *Sudhanakinnaryavadāna* is a Sanskrit verse narrative by the Kashmirian poet Kṣemendra. In the GRETIL file it is labelled as an extract from his *Avadānakalpalatā*, where it is Avadāna 64, and its verses are numbered accordingly (KAvk_64).[^gretil-sudhana] No established English title for this extract was found; it can be described as the story of Prince Sudhana and the kinnarī.

The tale concerns Sudhana, a prince and a past life of the Buddha, who falls in love with a kinnarī, a half-human, half-bird being, and rescues her. It is one of the most popular *avadānas* of northern Buddhism, and it survives in two main early versions: a Mahāsāṅghika one in the *Mahāvastu*, where it is the *Kinnarījātaka*, and a Mūla-Sarvāstivāda one in the Vinaya's *Bhaiṣajyavastu*, which is close to the version in the *Divyāvadāna*.[^jaini1966] Jaini's comparison of the texts includes Kṣemendra's poem, which he describes as a Sanskrit poem of 337 verses, of which 331 are Kṣemendra's own; it adds an intervention by Śakra that is absent from the Pali versions.[^jaini1966] The story also appears in the reliefs at Borobudur.[^jaini1966]

Little else about this particular text was found in the sources consulted. The GRETIL text derives from Martin Straube's critical edition and German translation, and a fuller account of the poem's date and relation to other versions would have to be sought in that study, which was not consulted here.[^gretil-sudhana]
