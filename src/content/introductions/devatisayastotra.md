The *Devātiśayastotra* ("Hymn on the Superiority over the Gods") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in verse, which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files among the hymns to Śākyamuni.[^dsbc944] It opens with homage to the Buddha and then compares him with the gods of the Hindu tradition. The opening verses observe that neither the Buddha nor Viṣṇu, Hara (Śiva) or Brahmā (Hiraṇyagarbha) is seen directly, so one must judge them by what is heard of their forms and conduct. The hymn then contrasts Viṣṇu, with his raised mace, and Śambhu (Śiva), with his garland of skulls, against the Buddha, whose conduct is described as wholly peaceful and who is said to be solely concerned with the welfare of the world.[^dsbc944]

The edition front matter gives no printed source, editor, date or author, and no scholarly discussion of this work could be located, so its authorship, date and place of composition, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation, are not documented here. The digital text is held as a romanised version and a Devanāgarī version in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon.[^dsbc944][^dsbc943]
