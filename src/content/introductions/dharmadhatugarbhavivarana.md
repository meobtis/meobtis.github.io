The *Dharmadhātugarbhavivaraṇa* ("Commentary on the Womb of the Dharma-Realm") is a very short Sanskrit prose work of only a few hundred words. Its opening names it the *Āryadharmadhātugarbhavivaraṇa* by the teacher Nāgārjuna and gives a Tibetan title in transliteration, which shows that it is known through a Tibetan translation as well.[^gretil-dgv] No established English title was found for it, and little scholarship on the work could be consulted in preparing this note.

The text comments on the well-known verse "ye dharmā hetuprabhavāḥ", which says that the Tathāgata has taught the cause of phenomena and their cessation. The commentary analyses the verse by listing the seven resultant factors (consciousness, name and form, the six sense bases, contact, feeling, birth, and old age and death) and the five causes (ignorance, craving, grasping, formations and becoming), and glosses the terms "tathāgata" and "mahāśramaṇa". It then connects the verse with the four truths and with the forward (*anuloma*) and reverse (*pratiloma*) sequences of dependent origination.[^gretil-dgv] A closing colophon names the Indian preceptor Jñānagarbha and a translator given as Palacek; its exact sense could not be verified from other sources.[^gretil-dgv] The attribution to Nāgārjuna is that of the text itself and of the edition's front matter; no independent discussion of its authenticity or date was found.[^gretil-dgv]
