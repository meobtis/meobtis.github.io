The *Garbhāvakrāntyavadāna* is a Sanskrit poem in verse that the GRETIL record attributes to Kṣemendra.[^gretil-garbha] Its opening stanzas present the Buddha, staying beside a lotus pond, as asked by a monk named Ānanda (the text's own wording) about human birth, and as describing the course of life from conception onward.[^gretil-garbha] The following verses use similes to describe how semen and blood unite under karmic conditions, how the being enters the womb, how the embryo develops over the nine months, and the suffering of birth.[^gretil-garbha] This subject matches that of the *Garbhāvakrānti-sūtra* ("Entry into the Womb"), which survives in Tibetan in a long and a short version translated from Chinese, in which the Buddha explains conception, week-by-week gestation driven by karmic winds, and the sufferings of birth and life.[^84000toh58] The relationship of Kṣemendra's poem to that sūtra is inferred here from the shared subject, not from a source that discusses the two together.

Kṣemendra was a Kashmiri poet whose dated works fall between 1037 and 1066, and he is known for an *Avadānakalpalatā* on the former lives and good deeds of the Buddha.[^wikipedia-ksemendra] The verse numbering "KAvk" in the edition suggests a connection to that collection, but little reliable documentation on this particular text was found: its date, its exact place among Kṣemendra's works, and any English translation remain unverified here. The Sanskrit text is available in Hahn's 1997 edition, which was prepared from material in the Tibetan Tanjur.[^gretil-garbha]
