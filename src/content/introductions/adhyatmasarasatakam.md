The *Adhyātmasāraśataka* ("Hundred [Verses] on the Essence of the Inner Reality") is a Sanskrit verse work whose title indicates a text of about one hundred stanzas. The digital edition presents it together with a prose commentary (*ṭīkā*), and is filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among commentaries on Yoginī tantras.[^dsbc803] The text opens with a salutation to Vajrasattva, and its first verse argues that all things, from the elements up to consciousness, lack intrinsic nature (*niḥsvabhāva*). The commentary then glosses the verse word by word and takes up the question of what might be a cause of the world, whether a creator god or emptiness.[^dsbc803]

The edition's header attributes the work to "mahāpaṇḍita Śrīprabhākaragupta" (given in the editor field), but I found no scholarly study confirming this attribution, the date, or the identity of the commentator. A Sanskrit manuscript of an *Adhyātmasāraṭīkā* in the Takakusu Junjirō collection of the University of Tokyo General Library is catalogued as Buddhist tantra on the *Adhyātmasāraśataka*. That record states that the manuscript names neither the author of the verses nor the commentator, and that it contains only up to 74 verses, the rest being missing.[^samhita] Little else about the work, including any Tibetan or Chinese translation or printed edition, could be established from the sources consulted.
