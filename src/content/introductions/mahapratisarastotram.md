The *Mahāpratisarāstotram* is a Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of Mahāpratisarā ("Great Protectress"), a Buddhist goddess. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is classified under the stotras, in the group of texts on the *pañcarakṣā* goddesses, and it was edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey in the collection *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*.[^dsbc167] The text opens with a salutation to the venerable Mahāpratisarā and continues in verses that each end with the refrain "homage to her, again and again". The verses recall, one by one, occasions on which she is said to have saved people from danger, among them a merchant's son threatened by a snake, merchants in a storm at sea, and a king who won victory over his enemy.[^dsbc167]

Mahāpratisarā is counted among the five protector goddesses (*Pañcarakṣā*), a group associated with five dhāraṇīs and with protection from sin, disease and other misfortune. The Pañcarakṣā is described as an early work of the dhāraṇī genre, preserved in Sanskrit manuscripts from Tibet, Nepal and India in many divergent versions, and mentioned in Tibetan records by about 800 CE.[^wiki-pancaraksa] Little reliable information about this particular hymn (its author, date, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation) could be found in the sources consulted, so these are left undetermined here.
