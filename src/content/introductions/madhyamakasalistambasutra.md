The work edited here as the *Madhyamakaśālistambasūtra* is the Śālistamba Sūtra, known in English as "The Rice Seedling". It is a short Mahāyāna sūtra in which, at the request of Śāriputra, Maitreya explains a brief teaching that the Buddha gave while looking at a rice seedling. The sūtra treats outer dependent arising, illustrated by the growth of a plant from a seed, and inner dependent arising, the twelvefold chain from ignorance to ageing and death, and presents understanding of this process as the way of release from it.[^84000toh210] The Sanskrit text printed by Vaidya opens with verses on dependent arising as the "pacification of conceptual proliferation" and then explains the outer and inner sequences through the six elements (earth, water, fire, wind, space, and either season or consciousness).[^gretil-salistamba]

No complete Sanskrit manuscript of the sūtra is known; Sanskrit passages survive as quotations in works by Yaśomitra, Candrakīrti, Prajñākaramati, Śāntideva and Vācaspatimiśra, and modern Sanskrit reconstructions have been made by La Vallée Poussin (1913), Ayaswami Sastri (1950), Gokhale (1961) and Reat (1993).[^84000toh210] The relation of the Sanskrit printed in Vaidya's volume to these reconstructions has not been checked here.

The sūtra is preserved in Tibetan (Toh 210) and in four Chinese translations (Taishō 709–712), the earliest dated to the Eastern Jin period; a closely similar sūtra (Taishō 708) is attributed to Zhi Qian of the Wu dynasty. Three Indian commentaries survive in Tibetan, including one by Kamalaśīla and two attributed to Nāgārjuna.[^84000toh210] Little else is documented about this particular edition's title, the prefix "Madhyamaka" being the one given in the edition front matter.
