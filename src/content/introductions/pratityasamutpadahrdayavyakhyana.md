The *Pratītyasamutpādahṛdayavyākhyāna* is a brief Sanskrit commentary in prose on a set of verses, the *Pratītyasamutpādahṛdaya*, which are attributed to Nāgārjuna. The text opens with a student asking where the twelve "limbs" of dependent origination taught by the sage are to be gathered, and the first verse answers that they are comprised, without remainder, in three: the afflictions, action and suffering.[^gretil-phv] The commentary then explains each term in turn, and argues that these factors arise from one another in mutual dependence, like a bundle of reeds leaning together, and not from a single cause such as nature, a creator, time or chance.[^gretil-phv]

Both the verses and the commentary are traditionally assigned to Nāgārjuna. Modern opinion is not uniform: according to a general survey of his attributed works, Christian Lindtner counts this text among those he regards as authentically Nāgārjuna's, and T. R. V. Murti is said to accept it as well because Candrakīrti quotes it extensively.[^wikinagarjuna] That survey is a tertiary source and these scholarly judgements have not been independently verified. The date, the Tibetan and Chinese translations and the manuscript history of the work are not documented in the sources consulted, and so are not described here.
