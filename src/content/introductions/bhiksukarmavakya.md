The *Bhikṣukarmavākya* ("formulas for the monks' acts") is a short Sanskrit text of the Buddhist monastic code (Vinaya). It sets out in order the spoken formulas by which a candidate is admitted to the monastic community: the request to the community, the going for refuge, the ten precepts of a novice, the questions put to the candidate about obstructing conditions, and the motions (*jñapti*) and announcements (*karmavācanā*) by which the assembly grants full ordination. The text also contains the four "supports" (*niḥśraya*) of a monk's life, such as rag-robes and almsfood.[^gretil-bkv] It begins with the salutation *namaḥ sarvajñāya*.[^gretil-bkv]

Little reliable information on this specific work could be found in the sources consulted. The edition on which both digital texts rest is that of A. C. Banerjee, first published in 1949 and reprinted in 1977 together with a *Prātimokṣa Sūtra*.[^gretil-bkv][^dsbc87] A Japanese article on a Sanskrit *Karmavācanā* manuscript from the Naupur finds near Gilgit, discovered in 1931 and 1938, notes that Banerjee was the first to publish such a Gilgit ordination text.[^shono2019] How far the present text corresponds to that manuscript, and its precise date and school affiliation, are not established here.
