"Dramatic Fragments (buddhist)" is the GRETIL title for a set of fragmentary Sanskrit play manuscripts of Buddhist content, recovered from the Turfan finds and edited by Heinrich Lüders in his *Bruchstücke buddhistischer Dramen* (1911). The text reproduces the fragments in transliteration with Lüders's editorial symbols; the remains are small and broken, so lines are often incomplete.[^gretil-drama]

According to the GRETIL record, the material comes from two entries in the catalogue of Sanskrit manuscripts from the Turfan finds (SHT 16 and SHT 57). It is divided into three dramas: a dialogue between the allegorical figures Buddhi, Dhṛti and Kīrti (the opening fragment, in which the Buddha appears at the end, is on this site); the *Śāriputraprakaraṇa* attributed to Aśvaghoṣa; and a third play that presumably involves a courtesan. Many further small SHT 16 fragments are not assigned to any drama, and the catalogue notes that some were never edited.[^gretil-drama] Aśvaghoṣa is believed to have lived around the 1st or 2nd century CE, and of his *Śāriputraprakaraṇa* only a fragment survives in Sanskrit.[^wiki-ashvaghosha] Beyond what the edition's own header states, little further information about these fragments could be verified for this introduction.
