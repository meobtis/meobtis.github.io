The *Śūnyatāsaptati*, commonly rendered the *Seventy Verses on Emptiness* or *Seventy Stanzas on Emptiness*, is a short treatise attributed to Nāgārjuna. The digital texts contain 73 numbered verses, beginning with a statement that the Buddha spoke of origination, abiding and destruction, and of existence and non-existence, from the standpoint of worldly convention and not of ultimate reality.[^gretil-ss] The editions are headed as accompanied by an auto-commentary (*svopajñavṛtti*), although the digitised text consists of the verses alone.[^gretil-ss]

Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy lists the work among Nāgārjuna's argumentative treatises, whose attribution to him is largely uncontested, and notes that, besides emptiness and dependent origination, it deals with agency and the two truths.[^westerhoff2010] The same entry cautions that several authors may have borne the name Nāgārjuna and that works were often attributed to earlier authorities, so questions of authorship are not fully settled.[^westerhoff2010]

Little further is documented in the sources consulted for this introduction: details of the work's Tibetan and Chinese translations, its manuscript witnesses and its commentaries were not verified, and are therefore not stated here. Readers who need them should consult specialist studies of Nāgārjuna's works.
