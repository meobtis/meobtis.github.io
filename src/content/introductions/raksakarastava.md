The *Rakṣākarastava* is a short Sanskrit hymn addressed to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, here invoked as Lokanātha and Lokanāyaka ("Lord of the World"). It opens with a salutation ("om namo lokanāthāya") and runs to fifteen verses, the first thirteen of which close with the petition "rakṣa māṃ lokanāyaka" ("protect me, Lord of the World"). The colophon calls it the hymn of the venerable noble lord Avalokiteśvara; the title is written *rakṣākāla(kara)stavaḥ* in the text itself, and the catalogue records give it variously.[^dsbc131][^dsbc616]

The verses combine a confession of wrongdoing of body, speech and mind with prayers for protection from the sufferings of saṃsāra. One verse mentions a twelve-year drought and great fear in Nepal and asks that the king be established, which suggests a Nepalese setting for the hymn, although this is an inference from the text rather than a documented fact. Others praise the bodhisattva as made of all gods, all buddhas and all accomplishments, as compassion and wisdom, as a remover of the suffering of the afflicted, and as one who will hold beings in saṃsāra until all have reached Sukhāvatī. The last verses ask for release from distress and for unwavering devotion.[^dsbc131]

Little is documented about the work. The sources consulted give no author, date, or place of composition, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation or manuscript was identified; these matters should be regarded as unknown here. Further web research on the text could not be completed, so the account above rests on the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon records and the text itself.
