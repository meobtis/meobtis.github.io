The *Kalpanāmaṇḍitikā* is a collection of illustrative stories (*dṛṣṭānta*) attributed to the elder Kumāralāta, who is traditionally dated to about the third century CE. It is also known as the *Dṛṣṭāntapaṅkti*, and is sometimes rendered in English as the "Garland of Examples".[^wiki-kumaralata]

Kumāralāta is described in Chinese sources as a native of Taxila and a founder of the Sautrāntika school, whose members were sometimes called his disciples.[^wiki-kumaralata] Modern scholarship has read the stories as reflecting the concerns and values of urban Buddhist merchants, such as piety, the pursuit of wealth and social respectability.[^wiki-kumaralata] The work is not dated independently of its attributed author, and the sources consulted here say little more about its contents, transmission or translations, so those matters are left out rather than guessed.

The Sanskrit text survives only in fragments. The digital edition reproduces manuscript fragments of the Berlin Turfan collection (SHT 21), published by Heinrich Lüders in 1926, together with further fragments that the record notes are not yet edited.[^gretil-kalp]
