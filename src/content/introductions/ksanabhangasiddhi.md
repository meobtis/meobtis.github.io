The *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi* ("Proof of Momentariness") is a brief Sanskrit treatise of Buddhist epistemology and logic that defends the doctrine of momentariness (*kṣaṇikatva*): the claim that everything which exists lasts only for an instant and is replaced by a new instance at every moment. The text opens with a verse and then states its thesis as an inference, "whatever exists is momentary, like a pot", and goes on to defend the reason ("existence", *sattva*) against the charge that it is unestablished, contradictory or inconclusive.[^gretil-ksb] Neither the GRETIL nor the DSBC record names an author. The title is, however, conventionally associated with Ratnakīrti, an eleventh-century Buddhist logician of the Yogācāra and epistemological tradition who is said to have studied at Vikramaśīla and whose works are collected in the *Ratnakīrtinibandhāvalī*; I could consult only a tertiary summary for this attribution, and the point should be checked against a scholarly source.[^wiki-ratnakirti] Two works with this title exist in the Sanskrit corpus, this one and the *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi vyatirekātmikā*, which is catalogued separately.

The argument belongs to a long line of Buddhist reasoning about impermanence. Dharmakīrti's "inference from existence" (*sattvānumāna*), largely his own invention according to the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, holds that something exists only by virtue of its causal efficacy (*arthakriyā*), and that nothing can produce new effects while remaining the same; a permanent thing could produce effects neither all at once nor in succession.[^tillemans2026] The present text develops this line of thought by considering whether a pot could produce its effect in the present moment but not at other times, and by testing the supposed reason with the usual positive and negative concomitance (*anvaya* and *vyatireka*) requirements.[^gretil-ksb] Whether this edition corresponds to the positive-concomitance counterpart of the *vyatirekātmikā* is a question I could not settle from the sources available.

The edition that both digital texts follow was published by the Asiatic Society in Calcutta as Haraprasad Shastri's *Six Buddhist Nyāya Tracts in Sanskrit*; the Internet Archive holds a 1910 printing of this collection.[^archive-sbnt] Little else about the manuscript tradition or the Tibetan and Chinese reception of this specific text could be verified, and a fuller study should consult a critical edition and the specialist literature on Ratnakīrti.
