The *Dvādaśakāranayastotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of the Buddha Śākyamuni. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed among the stotras under the heading "Ādibuddha", and it opens with a homage to the Buddha Śākyamuni followed by numbered groups of verses.[^dsbc760] The title can be read as "hymn on the method of the twelve acts", and the verses are in fact arranged in numbered sections that follow the deeds of the Buddha's life.[^dsbc760]

The text praises the Buddha for descent from the Tuṣita heaven and entry into the womb of Māyādevī, his birth at Lumbinī, his skill in the crafts and in overcoming rivals as a prince, his life in the palace, his departure from home on seeing the emptiness of worldly life, his six years of austerities by the river Nairañjanā, and his awakening beneath the tree at Magadha. It continues with the first teaching at Vārāṇasī, the defeat of opponents, the great miracle at Śrāvastī, and his final passing at Kuśinagara, ending with the sharing of his relics.[^dsbc760] These stages correspond to the traditional "twelve deeds" of a Buddha, although this introduction relies only on the text itself for the list.

Little is documented about the work's author, date or manuscript history in the sources consulted, and no English title or Tibetan or Chinese counterpart could be verified. The only known source for the digital text is the printed edition named in the DSBC record, a 2017 issue of the journal *Dhīḥ* published at the Central University of Tibetan Studies in Sarnath.[^dsbc760]
