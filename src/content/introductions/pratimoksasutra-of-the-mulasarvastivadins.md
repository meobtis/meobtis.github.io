The *Prātimokṣasūtra* is the core disciplinary text of the Buddhist monastic order: a list of the training rules that fully ordained monks recite together at the fortnightly *poṣadha* (observance) ceremony. This version belongs to the Mūlasarvāstivāda school, whose Vinaya survives in Sanskrit only in part, in Tibetan (the Kangyur) and in Chinese (Taishō 1442 is the Chinese Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya).[^wiki-pratimoksa] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya was the one to which Tibetan ordination was restricted, and it is a major Vinaya lineage still followed in the Tibetan tradition.[^wiki-msv] Charles Prebish's *Buddhist Monastic Discipline* presents the Sanskrit Prātimokṣa texts of the Mahāsāṃghikas and the Mūlasarvāstivādins side by side.[^wiki-pratimoksa]

The text opens with invocatory verses and an introductory formula for the recitation. It then proceeds through the classes of rules: four *pārājika* (defeat), thirteen *saṃghāvaśeṣa*, two *aniyata*, thirty *naiḥsargika-pāyantika*, ninety *pāyantika*, four *pratideśanīya*, 108 *śaikṣa* (training rules) and seven procedures for settling disputes (*adhikaraṇaśamatha*), and ends with closing verses.[^gretil-pratimoksa] Little else about its date, redaction or relation to the Tibetan and Chinese recensions could be verified in the sources available for this entry, so none is asserted here.
