The *Ṣaḍabhijñastotram* ("Hymn of the Six Superknowledges") is a brief Sanskrit hymn to the Buddha. In the digital edition it consists of seven verses in the *anuṣṭubh* metre, and its colophon describes it as the hymn of the six superknowledges belonging to Śrī Mahābuddhabhaṭṭāraka.[^dsbc193] Little independent scholarship on this particular hymn could be located, so the account here rests on the text and the edition records themselves.

Verses 1 to 6 each address the Buddha as "perfect in the mark" of one of the six sense-fields (form, sound, smell, taste, touch and mental object), and each ends with a salutation to him as one who has gone beyond one of the six perfections: giving, ethical conduct, patience, vigour, meditation and wisdom, in that order. Each verse also asks him to bring about the corresponding virtue. The seventh verse promises that a devoted person who recites the hymn at the three junctures of the day will leave the bonds of saṃsāra and reach the highest state.[^dsbc193] The title's reference to six superknowledges is not explained in the text itself, and no information on its author, date or any Tibetan or Chinese translation was found.
