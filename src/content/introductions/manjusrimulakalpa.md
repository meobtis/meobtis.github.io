The *Mañjuśrīmūlakalpa* ("Root Manual of the Rites of Mañjuśrī", also known as the *Āryamañjuśrīmūlakalpa*) is a Mahāyāna sūtra that functions as a ritual manual associated with the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. Tibetan tradition classes it among the Kriyā (action) tantras, and it is often cited as the earliest extant Indian Buddhist tantra.[^wiki-mmk][^84000-mmk] The surviving Sanskrit text has 55 chapters.[^84000-mmk]

Most of the work consists of mantras, mudrās, maṇḍalas and fire rites (*homa*), together with an account of their soteriological purpose. It is also encyclopaedic in scope, containing material on astrology, geography, languages, prophetic history, medicine and dreams, and its opening scene lists more than 1,300 named beings among the audience.[^84000-mmk] Other sources note that the mantras presented include some drawn from Śaiva, Garuḍa and Vaiṣṇava traditions, and that the teachings are attributed to Mañjuśrī.[^wiki-mmk]

The date of the work is not settled. The 84000 introduction states that parts of it were circulating by at least the early eighth century, as shown by Chinese translations, and that the Sanskrit text as we have it was probably assembled in stages over the eighth century and later.[^84000-mmk] A summary of scholarship reports a date of about 775 CE proposed by Sanderson and Matsunaga, and a view that a core verse text may go back to about the sixth century with later additions.[^wiki-mmk] The Tibetan translation, by Kumārakalaśa and the monk Śākya Lodrö, was probably made in the mid-eleventh century and is shorter than the Sanskrit: it omits chapters 18–23 and 39–49 and combines chapters 53 and 54.[^84000-mmk]

The standard Sanskrit text is Ganapati Śāstrī's edition in three volumes (Trivandrum, 1920–25), based on a transcript of a Trivandrum manuscript that may date to the eleventh century and probably came from Nepal; a few other incomplete manuscripts exist.[^84000-mmk] Rahul Sankrityayan published a further edition in 1934, and P. L. Vaidya reprinted Śāstrī's text with some modifications in 1964.[^wiki-mmk] An English translation of the Tibetan-attested chapters, made from the Sanskrit by Wiesiek Mical for the Dharmachakra Translation Committee, is available from 84000 as Toh 543.[^84000-mmk]
