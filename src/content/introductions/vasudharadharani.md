The *Vasudhārādhāraṇī* is a short Mahāyāna text in the dhāraṇī genre, devoted to the goddess Vasudhārā, a Buddhist deity of wealth and abundance. In Tibetan it is known as "The Dhāraṇī of Vasudhārā" and is catalogued in the Action Tantras section of the Kangyur as Toh 662.[^84000toh662] The Sanskrit editions available here present it as a sūtra-like narrative that frames a longer sequence of invocations and mantras.

The text opens in the usual sūtra manner: the Buddha is staying at Kauśāmbī, in the Ghoṣitārāma grove, when a householder named Sucandra approaches him. Sucandra describes himself as poor, burdened with a large family and dependants, and asks for a teaching by which poor and sick beings may become prosperous and healthy. The Buddha answers by recounting a teaching given in a distant past age by a tathāgata named Vajradharasāgaranirghoṣa, and then gives the dhāraṇī and mantras of Vasudhārā, beginning with homage to the Three Jewels and to the goddess.[^gretil-vdh]

The manuscript record is wide. The Sanskrit text is preserved in the Gilgit manuscripts, which one of the digital editions transcribes from the published facsimiles.[^gretil-vdh-gilgit] A later recension, copied and recited among the Jainas of Gujarat, was edited by Padmanabh S. Jaini in 1968; its title already describes it as a Buddhist work in use among the Jainas, and the version opens with a Jaina invocation ("śrī jinaśāsanāya") before the Buddhist narrative.[^dsbc21][^gretil-vdh-alt] A further edition, by Ngawang Samten and S. S. Bahulkar, appeared in 2007.[^gretil-vdh]

Little else could be verified in the sources consulted for this note. The date and authorship of the work, the details of its Chinese and Tibetan translations, and its reception in Nepal are not documented in the sources read here, and the reader should consult the introduction to the 84000 translation and the editions named above for further detail.
