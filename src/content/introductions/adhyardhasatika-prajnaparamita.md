The *Adhyardhaśatikā prajñāpāramitā*, "the perfection of wisdom in a hundred and fifty (lines)", is a short Prajñāpāramitā scripture of tantric character. In East Asia it is best known through Amoghavajra's Chinese version, called *Rishukyō* in Japanese, which has long been central to Shingon liturgy.[^oeaw2009] Ian Astley-Kristensen's English translation of that version renders the title as "the Perfection of Wisdom in 150 verses".[^astley1991]

The Sanskrit text opens in the usual sūtra manner, with the Buddha Mahāvairocana (in the Tomabechi edition) addressing a great assembly of bodhisattvas headed by Vajrapāṇi, Avalokiteśvara and Mañjuśrī, and goes on to present a series of "methods" (*naya*) of the perfection of wisdom. The opening teaching enumerates "stages of purity" (*viśuddhipada*) in which states ordinarily regarded as afflictions, such as desire, anger and pride, are said to be bodhisattva stages.[^gretil-adhsatpu] In the shorter, differently arranged text in Vaidya's edition, further methods are taught by other Buddhas, including Śākyamuni and Vairocana.[^gretil-adhs]

Until recently the Sanskrit was known only from a fragmentary manuscript from Central Asia studied by Ernst Leumann in the early twentieth century. Tomabechi's 2009 edition is based on a newly available manuscript from Tibet, consulted through a photocopy in the China Tibetology Research Center, Beijing, and also edits the Tibetan translation (*Śes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa'i tshul brgya lṅa bcu pa*) from fourteen Kanjur exemplars and a Dunhuang manuscript.[^oeaw2009] Vaidya's earlier edition, which gives the text in a shorter form with gaps marked, was published in 1961.[^dsbc66] The Chinese versions are not treated in detail here; for the *Rishukyō* the reader is referred to Astley-Kristensen's study.[^astley1991]
