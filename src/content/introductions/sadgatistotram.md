The *Ṣaḍgatistotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) of six verses, followed by a colophon that calls it the "Ṣaḍgatistotra" of the venerable Āryāvalokiteśvara.[^dsbc134] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the *stotra* category in its list of hymns to Avalokiteśvara, and it is taken from the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha* edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey (Varanasi, 1994).[^dsbc134] The title can be read as "hymn of the six destinies", but the verses themselves do not discuss the six realms of rebirth in any detail.

The first five verses are salutations, each ending with the same refrain, "homage to him, the one made of compassion". They praise a compassionate figure who stands on a lotus, holds a lotus in the hand, wears matted hair, a crown jewel and a golden necklace, and who is called a Tathāgata and a Jina-lord who carries beings across the ocean of saṃsāric suffering.[^dsbc134] The sixth verse is a concluding statement: it names the author of the hymn as a wise man called Yacchata (or Yaccha-) and promises that those who recite it diligently will reach a splendid heavenly abode.[^dsbc134] The reading of this name and of the place name in the first verses is uncertain in the digital text.

Little reliable information on the hymn's author, date or place of composition could be found in the sources consulted, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation or manuscript was identified. Statements about its origin should therefore be treated as unknown; only the features of the text as printed in the anthology are documented here.
