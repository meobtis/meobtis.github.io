The *Sūtrārthasamuccayopadeśa* ("Instruction that Gathers the Meaning of the Sūtras") is a short Sanskrit prose work with a few opening verses, attributed in the digital editions to Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, better known as Atiśa.[^gretil-sutrartha] Atiśa was born around 982 and died around 1054 in Tibet; he is best known for the *Bodhipathapradīpa*.[^wikipedia-atisa] The Sanskrit text opens with homage to the Three Jewels and a verse in which the author says he is setting down matters that came from his revered teacher, after contemplating the profound and vast sūtras.[^gretil-sutrartha] The DSBC prints it in a collection of Atiśa's works titled *Ekādaśagrantha*, which suggests that it belongs to a group of eleven works by him.[^dsbc253]

The text announces fifty dharmas of the bodhisattva and explains them through a long series of comparisons, in which qualities such as the awakening mind, emptiness and compassion, or the ten virtuous actions are likened to life, parents, a house, wealth or a friend. It then discusses obstacles to awakening, citing passages attributed to scriptures such as the *Āryabodhisattvapiṭaka* and the *Āryamahāyānopadeśasūtra* (a list of thirty-two hindrances), and ends with practical advice to seek a good teacher and to read the sūtras constantly.[^gretil-sutrartha] Little reliable scholarship on this work could be located, so its date, its Tibetan and Chinese versions and its reception are left undescribed here.
