The *Nagaropamasūtra* ("Discourse on the Simile of the City") is a short Sanskrit sūtra preserved in manuscripts from Central Asia. The GRETIL text reconstructs it from the Pelliot/Stein manuscript, supplemented by fragments from other manuscripts and with a further manuscript, noted in the editorial comments, used in an appendix.[^gretil-nag] The edition from which it derives describes the work as an apotropaic text from the *Saṃyuktāgama*.[^gretil-nag]

The sūtra opens at Śrāvastī, in the Jeta Grove. The Buddha tells the monks how, before his awakening, he reflected on ageing and death and asked in turn what conditions each member of the chain: ageing and death depend on birth, birth on becoming, then in order grasping, craving, feeling, contact, the six sense bases and name-and-form.[^gretil-nag] This is the sequence of dependent origination. The Pāli parallel, the *Nagara Sutta* (Saṃyutta Nikāya 12.65, "The City" in the translations of Bhikkhu Sujato and Bhikkhu Bodhi), is summarised by SuttaCentral as the Buddha's account of how his awakening came about through investigating dependent origination.[^suttacentral-sn1265] Little further reliable documentation of the Sanskrit text was available for this introduction, so its transmission history and its relation to other Chinese and Tibetan versions are not discussed here.
