The *Saptajinastava* ("Hymn to the Seven Jinas") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) of nine verses. It is preserved in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which files it among the hymns to Buddhas, in the group on the seven Buddhas.[^dsbc190]

The first seven verses each praise one Buddha in turn: Vipaśyin, Śikhin, Viśvabhū, Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, Kāśyapa and Śākyamuni, that is, the six Buddhas of the past and the historical Buddha. Verse 8 honours Maitreya, who is said to be waiting in the Tuṣita heaven, and verse 9 dedicates the merit of praising the seven Jinas and the eighth, future, bodhisattva to the welfare of beings.[^dsbc190]

Little is documented about the work itself. The edition's front matter names no author and no date, and the sources consulted for this introduction did not allow its authorship, date or place of composition, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, to be established. It is therefore not discussed further here. The text is known to these editions through Pandey's printed anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc190]
