The *Saddharma-pārājikā* is a short Sanskrit work of verse, edited in a printed collection of *pārājikā* texts from Nepal, and known to the site only through the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) edition.[^dsbc766] It opens with a salutation to Vajrasattva and to all the buddhas, and is then framed as a dialogue in which Mañjuśrī asks the Blessed One how, in the degenerate Kali age, beings can be cleansed of the suffering that results from evil deeds.[^dsbc766]

The Buddha's reply, as it appears in the text, sets out graded periods of penance for different grave offences (for example, ten or twelve years for certain killings) and the means by which they are said to be purified: keeping moral precepts, giving, recitation of mantras, circumambulating a stūpa, taking refuge, fasting, bathing at sacred places and making pious donations.[^dsbc766] Despite the word *pārājikā* in its title, which in Vinaya literature names the gravest class of monastic offence, the work appears to belong to a Nepalese ritual and expiatory genre rather than to a monastic code. Little reliable scholarly information about its date, authorship or manuscripts could be found for this introduction, and none is asserted here.
