The *Vidyāsthānopamasūtra* (roughly "Discourse on the Simile of the Fields of Knowledge") is a short sūtra preserved in Sanskrit fragments. In the digital edition it opens in the usual way: the Buddha is at Śrāvastī, in Anāthapiṇḍada's park in the Jeta Grove, and addresses the monks.[^gretil-vidya]

The surviving text begins with the world's cycles of dissolution and renewal, whether or not Tathāgatas arise. It mentions beings reborn among the Ābhāsvara gods, and the later appearance in the world of separate crafts, occupations and arts such as spells, medicines and magical skills.[^gretil-vidya] The edition records an alternative opening in a British Library manuscript, which announces a discourse named "Vidyāsthānopama".[^gretil-vidya]

Little reliable information about the work's date, authorship, school or translations could be consulted for this entry, so none is given here. Readers should consult the printed editions named in the record, namely Waldschmidt's 1959 publication and the British Library volume of Karashima and Wille.[^gretil-vidya]
