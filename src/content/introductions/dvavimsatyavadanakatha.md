The *Dvāviṃśatyavadānakathā* ("Account of the twenty-two avadānas") is a Sanskrit Buddhist narrative text in verse, with short prose connecting passages. In its opening verses King Aśoka asks the elder Upagupta, who is staying at the Kukkuṭārāma, to relate the "twenty-two" stories on the merit of caring for caityas (shrines), and Upagupta agrees; the stories that follow are presented as teachings of the Buddha to Ānanda and others.[^gretil-dvavimsati]

The digital text is divided into chapters (the GRETIL header counts twenty-four, the last bracketed), whose subjects include encouraging merit-making, hearing the Dharma, the rarity of a human birth, giving, restoring stupas and images, bathing images, and offering parasols and banners.[^gretil-dvavimsati] The modern edition on which the text is based describes the work in its subtitle as a medieval Buddhist text on the piety of giving (*Spendenfrömmigkeit*).[^gretil-dvavimsati] Little else about its author, date, manuscripts or translations could be verified from the sources consulted, and it is best left undescribed beyond what the edition and its record state.
