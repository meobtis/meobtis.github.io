The *Śrīsahajasiddhi* is a short Sanskrit work in verse on the Buddhist tantric theme of the "innate" or "co-emergent" (*sahaja*) accomplishment. It opens with a salutation to Vajranātha and a declaration that its author will expound the *sahajasiddhi* for the benefit of beings, setting it apart from practices of fire offering and the like.[^dsbc786] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under commentaries on the Yoginī tantras, although the text itself does not announce itself as a commentary.

The opening verses define the *sahaja* in terms typical of Yoginī-tantra practice: the female principle is wisdom (*prajñā*), the male is skilful means (*upāya*), and the innate is described as fourfold, a teaching said to have been taught by the fully awakened Buddha.[^dsbc786] The Sanskrit text was edited by Malati J. Shendge and published in the *Indo-Iranian Journal* in 1967.[^shendge1967] Little reliable information on its authorship, date, Tibetan or Chinese translations and manuscript sources could be consulted for this introduction, and none is asserted here; readers should consult Shendge's edition and its introduction.
