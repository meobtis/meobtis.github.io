The *Vajrasatva-pārājikā* is a short Sanskrit work preserved in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the Vinaya texts. The DSBC record places it in a volume titled *Pārājikā (Texts from Nepal)*, published in Pune in 2013, which indicates that it belongs to the Sanskrit manuscript tradition of Nepal.[^dsbc764] No established English title for the work was found.

The text opens with salutations to Vajrasattva and to the omniscient one, and then announces an account of the rules of expiation (*prāyaścitta*) for taking life (*prāṇivadha*). It addresses in particular the *vajrācārya* and other observers of vows (*vratin*) and members of the social classes beginning with the brāhmaṇas, distinguishes killing done by oneself from killing through another, and discusses what counts as the occasion (*nimitta*) of a death, including whether killing an assailant incurs fault.[^dsbc764] Little reliable secondary literature on this specific work could be located, so its date, authorship and place in the Nepalese Vinaya-related literature remain undocumented here.
