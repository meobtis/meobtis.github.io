The *Pramāṇavārtikam* (Commentary on Valid Cognition) is a Sanskrit verse treatise by the Buddhist philosopher Dharmakīrti. It is presented as a commentary on Dignāga's *Pramāṇasamuccaya*, but it is unfinished and in practice an independent work on epistemology, logic and related questions. The text has an autocommentary, the *Svavṛtti*, and later commentators include Devendrabuddhi, Śākyabuddhi, Prajñākaragupta, Manorathanandin and Karṇakagomin.[^sep]

The work is divided into four chapters. In the order of the Sanskrit tradition, the first chapter treats inference, including Dharmakīrti's views on universals; the second addresses the authority of the Buddha, karma and rebirth; the third contains the discussion of perception, including yogic perception, and the move towards an idealist position; and the fourth deals with logical reasons and the triple criterion of a valid reason.[^sep] The digital editions here preserve the four-chapter division, opening with the chapter on the establishment of valid cognition (*pramāṇasiddhi*).[^dsbc298]

Dharmakīrti's dates are disputed: Frauwallner proposed 600–660 CE, while Krasser and Balcerowicz argue for earlier dates. Frauwallner also placed the *Pramāṇavārtika* first among Dharmakīrti's works in order of composition.[^sep] The verses are available in a Sanskrit and Tibetan edition by Miyasaka, and the Sanskrit text of the autocommentary and the first chapter was edited by Gnoli; an earlier edition of the work was prepared by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana from manuscripts and published in 1938.[^sep] Little else has been verified for this introduction, which relies chiefly on a single reference work and the edition records.
