The *Buddhagaṇḍīstavaḥ* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of the Buddha. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed under the *stotra* section, in a group of hymns associated with the *gaṇḍī* (the wooden or metal gong struck in monasteries), and the text opens with a heading attributing it to Ācārya Aśvaghoṣa.[^dsbc149] That attribution is the edition's own; no independent scholarly discussion of the work could be consulted for this entry, so its authorship and date should be treated as unverified.

The verses are written in long, ornate metres and dwell on the episode of the Buddha's victory over Māra beneath the Bodhi tree. The opening stanzas describe Māra's army and the seductive women sent against the Buddha, with onomatopoeic sounds imitating the din of the assault, and pray that the "lion of the Śākyas" protect the reader, since he was not shaken by them.[^dsbc149] Beyond what the text itself shows, little is documented here about its Tibetan or Chinese translations, manuscripts or reception.
