The *Kāraṇḍavyūha* is a Mahāyāna sūtra devoted to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara; the manuscript headings call it the *Avalokiteśvaraguṇa-Kāraṇḍavyūha*. Its title is usually rendered in English as "The Basket's Display", since *karaṇḍa* normally means a reed basket and *vyūha* a display or presentation.[^84000toh116] In the Tibetan Kangyur it is Toh 116.[^84000toh116] The Sanskrit text edited by Vaidya is divided into 24 chapters.[^dsbc42]

The sūtra is set at Jetavana in Śrāvastī, where a great assembly of monks, bodhisattvas, gods and other beings gathers; the opening chapter, as in the Sanskrit text, describes the miraculous adornment of the monastery on that occasion.[^gretil-karandavyuha] The work presents Avalokiteśvara as the most important figure of the Buddhist pantheon, even superior to the buddhas, although he also appears as a messenger of Amitābha. It is the source of the mantra *oṃ maṇipadme hūṃ*, which is central to Tibetan Buddhism.[^84000toh116]

The text is generally regarded as an early Mantrayāna sūtra. According to the introduction to the 84000 translation, it was probably written down around the fifth century CE, and Adelheid Mette places its composition in the fourth or fifth century. The earliest surviving manuscript evidence is a fragment in Buddhist Hybrid Sanskrit among the Gilgit finds of the 1940s.[^84000toh116]

The Tibetan translation dates from the ninth century, probably about 820 CE or earlier, and most Kangyur editions attribute it to Yeshé Dé, Dānaśīla and Jinamitra, though the Narthang Kangyur names Śākyaprabha and Ratnarakṣita. Earlier Chinese translations attributed to Dharmarakṣa and Guṇabhadra in fact render a different work, the *Ratnakaraṇḍavyūha*; the *Kāraṇḍavyūha* itself was first translated into Chinese in 983 CE. A longer, fully versified Nepalese version of the fifteenth century is not found in Tibetan. Among Sanskrit sources, the same introduction names a Cambridge University Library palm-leaf manuscript, the Sāmaśrami edition of 1872 and Vaidya's edition, which closely follows Sāmaśrami's.[^84000toh116]
