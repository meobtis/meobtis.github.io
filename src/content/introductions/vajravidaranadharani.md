The *Vajravidāraṇādhāraṇī* (*Āryavajravidāraṇā nāma dhāraṇī*), rendered in English as *Vajra Conqueror*, is a short Mahāyāna dhāraṇī text. It opens with the usual "Thus have I heard", set among the vajras, where Vajrapāṇi enters a vajra-concentration through the Buddha's power, and then presents mantras, verses and a short ritual.[^84000toh750] The Sanskrit text is only a few pages long in print.[^gretil-vvdh]

According to the 84000 introduction, Vajrapāṇi proclaims three dhāraṇī-mantras by the power of the Buddha and the blessing of all buddhas and bodhisattvas. These are followed by verses on the benefits of the dhāraṇī and a concise purification ritual involving ablutions. Indic commentarial tradition, as reported there, links its origin to the healing of King Ajātaśatru of Magadha.[^84000toh750] The Sanskrit text itself closes the mantras with a "heart and sub-heart root mantra" and verses on the removal of misfortune.[^dsbc12]

The dhāraṇī is popular among Newar Buddhists in Nepal, where it belongs to the daily practices known as the *Saptavāra*; the National Archives in Kathmandu holds more than two hundred Sanskrit witnesses.[^84000toh750] The 84000 translators also compared a late Devanāgarī manuscript (NGMCP E 1414/8), which is titled *Vajravidāraṇahṛdayamantradhāraṇī*, and printed Sanskrit editions of 1937 and 2005.[^84000toh750]

In Tibetan the text appears twice in the Degé Kangyur, as Toh 750 in the Tantra section and Toh 949 in the Dhāraṇī section, with the same colophon and very few variant readings. The colophon credits Jinamitra and Dānaśīla with the translator Yeshé Dé, and the early ninth-century *Denkarma* catalogue already lists it. Tradition counts it among the royal sūtras said to have been recommended to King Trisong Detsen to prolong his life.[^84000toh750] Commentaries are attributed to Padmasambhava, Vimalamitra, Buddhaguhya and Smṛtijñānakīrti, and later scholars of all four Tibetan schools have written on the text.[^84000toh750] Little further independent scholarship on the Sanskrit text could be consulted here, and parallels in Chinese are not documented in the sources read.
