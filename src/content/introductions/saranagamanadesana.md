The *Śaraṇagamanadeśanā* is a short Sanskrit prose treatise on taking refuge in the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṃgha. The text opens with a salutation to the Three Jewels and a verse of homage, and then announces that it will set out the topic under fifteen headings, which are listed in a verse: basis, abiding, intention, time, nature, measure, method, training, etymology, function, simile, divisions, disadvantages, purpose and benefits. The colophon names its author as the "guru-bodhisattva" Dīpaṅkaraśrījñāna, and the text closes by saying that it is only a summary and that fuller treatment is to be learned from teachers and treatises.[^gretil-sarana]

Within these headings the work distinguishes those of the Mahāyāna lineage from those of the Hīnayāna lineage, and gives for each a different account of the Three Jewels and of the motivation and duration of refuge, citing verses to support the Hīnayāna account. It closes with the benefits of refuge, in this life and the next, along the path and at its fruition.[^gretil-sarana] The author is the Indian master better known as Atīśa (c. 982–1054), who left Vikramaśīla in 1040, travelled to Tibet through Nepal and wrote the *Bodhipathapradīpa* there.[^wiki-atisa] Beyond what the Sanskrit text and its edition records say, little about this treatise's date, transmission, translations or reception could be established from the sources consulted, so the attribution rests on the colophon and the printed edition.[^dsbc250]
