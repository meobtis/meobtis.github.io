The *Sarvajñasiddhi* ("Proof of Omniscience") is a short Sanskrit treatise attributed to Jitāri, a Buddhist scholar of the later Pāla period. Jitāri is dated to roughly the second half of the tenth century and is associated with the monastic university of Vikramaśilā in eastern India; his surviving works include several brief "proofs" (*siddhi*) on topics of Buddhist epistemology and philosophy, of which the *Sarvajñasiddhi* is one.[^wiki-jitari]

The opening of the text, as preserved in the edition, begins with a salutation to the omniscient one and announces a proof of omniscience. It then builds its argument on the notion of a statement whose content is established by valid cognition: such a statement, it holds, must ultimately rest, directly or at one remove, on knowledge that perceives its object, and it uses the doctrine that all conditioned things are momentary as its example.[^sarit-sarvajnasiddhi] Little else about the work's reception, date of composition or translation history could be verified in the sources consulted, so these are left open here.
