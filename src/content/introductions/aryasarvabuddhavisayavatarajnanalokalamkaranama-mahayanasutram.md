The *Āryasarvabuddhaviṣayāvatārajñānālokālaṃkāranāma mahāyānasūtra*, commonly shortened to *Jñānālokālaṃkāra*, is a Mahāyāna sūtra preserved in Sanskrit and Tibetan. In the Tibetan Kangyur it is Toh 100 in the General Sūtra section, and the 84000 project translates its title as *The Ornament of the Light of Awareness That Enters the Domain of All Buddhas*.[^84000toh100] The Sanskrit text opens in the usual way, with the Buddha at Vulture Peak near Rājagṛha in a palace called the Womb of the Dharmadhātu, surrounded by a vast assembly of monks and bodhisattvas.[^dsbc32]

According to the 84000 summary, the sūtra explains how the Buddha and all phenomena share the same empty nature. Through a series of similes it shows how the illusion-like Buddha teaches in ways suited to the dispositions of beings; his activity is effortless because his realisation is free of concepts, and his nonconceptual awareness gives rise to a compassion that is not tied to any reference point.[^84000toh100] The introduction to that translation proposes an eleventh- to thirteenth-century date for the Sanskrit manuscript on which the modern edition rests and notes that the Tibetan translation was made centuries earlier. It also refers to Kimura's transliteration of the manuscript. I found no source giving the date or author of the original composition or the Chinese translations, so these are left out here.[^84000toh100]
