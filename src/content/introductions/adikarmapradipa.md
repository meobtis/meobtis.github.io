The *Ādikarmapradīpa* ("Lamp on the Initial Rites") is a Sanskrit manual of Buddhist tantric ritual practice. Its opening verses present it as a compilation for those who want it, gathering together the mantras taught for worship (*pūjā*) and other rites, and describe a daily routine that begins on waking with purification, meditation, recitation and the chanting of divine names.[^ras69] A catalogue record of a manuscript in the Kawaguchi Ekai collection describes it as a Kriyātantra work consisting of a root text (*mūlasūtra*) with a running commentary, dealing with initiation rituals and the duties of the day, and notes that the author says he wrote it to help his pupil understand.[^matsunami57] No standard English title appears to have become established.

The work is attributed in its colophon to the teacher (*ācārya*) Anupamavajra; the colophon also contains a dating by the reign of a king Devapāla, which the Royal Asiatic Society catalogue does not interpret, and I found no further reliable biographical information on the author.[^ras69] Sanskrit manuscripts survive on palm leaf, among them Hodgson's manuscript from Nepal, now in the Royal Asiatic Society, and the Tokyo manuscript in old Devanāgarī script.[^ras69][^matsunami57] Poussin edited the text in 1898,[^hathi1898] and I could not confirm from the sources consulted any Tibetan or Chinese translation of it, nor its place in a canon; little else about its history or reception is documented in the sources I could consult.
