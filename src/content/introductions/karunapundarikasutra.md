The *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīkasūtra* ("Sūtra of the White Lotus of Compassion") is a Mahāyāna sūtra preserved in Sanskrit. The digital editions divide it into six chapters, the first of which is titled "Dharmacakrapravartana" (the turning of the wheel of the Dharma). The text opens with a verse of homage to the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha announcing a "bodhisūtra" called the *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka*, and then follows the usual pattern of a sūtra: the Buddha is at Rājagṛha on Gṛdhrakūṭa (Vulture Peak) with a very large assembly of monks, nuns, bodhisattvas such as Mañjuśrī, Avalokiteśvara and Maitreya, and gods and other beings.[^gretil-kp][^dsbc43]

Little reliable scholarly information about the work could be gathered for this introduction, so its date, authorship, Tibetan and Chinese translations and reception are not described here rather than guessed. The only printed edition identified by both digital sources is Isshi Yamada's 1968 edition for the School of Oriental and African Studies.[^gretil-kp][^dsbc43]
