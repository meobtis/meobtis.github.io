The *Ālambanaparīkṣā* ("Examination of the Object of Cognition") is a short epistemological treatise by the Buddhist logician Dignāga, who is dated to roughly 470/480 to 530/540 CE. It is accompanied by an auto-commentary, the *Ālambanaparīkṣāvṛtti*, and is counted among his surviving works besides the major *Pramāṇasamuccaya*.[^wiki-dignaga] The digital text described here combines the verses with the prose commentary in a single file, and the verses are marked in it as Āv_1, Āv_2 and so on.[^gretil-alambana]

The work aims to show that external objects cannot be the ālambana, the supporting object, of cognition, and that only consciousness is real.[^wiki-dignaga] In the opening lines of the text the two candidates put forward by realists, atoms and aggregates of atoms, are examined in turn: atoms may cause a cognition but do not appear in it, while an aggregate may appear in it but, lacking a substantial existence of its own, cannot be its cause, like the double moon seen by a person with defective vision.[^gretil-alambana]

Chinese translations of the vṛtti by Paramārtha and Xuanzang exist, and their divergent renderings of controversial passages have been studied by Lin.[^lin2008] Little else about the transmission of the text could be verified from the sources available for this introduction.
