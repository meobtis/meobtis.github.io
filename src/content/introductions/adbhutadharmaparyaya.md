The *Adbhutadharmaparyāya* ("Marvelous Dharma Discourse") is a short Mahāyāna sūtra concerned with the merit of making stūpas. In the Tibetan Kangyur it is the General Sūtra Section text Toh 319, and 84000 has published an English translation under the title *The Marvelous Dharma Discourse*.[^84000toh319]

In the sūtra, Ānanda asks the Buddha whether more merit comes from huge offerings or from a miniature stūpa, the size of a gooseberry, made for a buddha who has passed into parinirvāṇa. The Buddha answers that the small stūpa brings far greater merit than offering the entire universe to the noble ones and the saṅgha.[^84000toh319]

The Tibetan translation was made by Bandé Yeshé Dé with the Indian preceptors Jinamitra and Surendrabodhi, who worked together in the late eighth and early ninth centuries, and the text is listed in the Denkarma catalogue of 812 CE. Three Sanskrit manuscripts of the work survive in the Gilgit collection and were published in facsimile in *Gilgit Buddhist Manuscripts*. There are two Chinese parallels, Taishō 688 (by an unknown translator of the late Han period) and Taishō 689 (by Xuanzang, 649 CE); according to Bentor, both stand closer to the Sanskrit of the *Great Rumble* sūtra (Toh 208) than to this text.[^84000toh319] Little else about the sūtra's authorship and date appears to be documented in the sources consulted.
