The *Pratītyasamutpādastutisubhāṣitahṛdaya* ("The Heart of Fine Sayings: Praise for the Teaching of Dependent Origination") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stuti*) in verse. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) it is filed among the Madhyamaka works under the heading *ācārya*, and its text is divided into numbered verses up to sixty.[^dsbc896] No established English title was found, and very little independent scholarship on the work could be located in the sources consulted, so what follows rests on the digital edition itself.

The opening verses pay homage to the Buddha as the teacher who saw and taught dependent origination, and describe it as the root cause for the removal of the sufferings of the world. The verses that follow praise this teaching, say that whatever arises dependently is empty of own-nature (*svabhāva*), and present it as the means of cutting through the net of conceptual elaboration (*prapañca*). Later verses refer to Nāgārjuna and to Candra (probably Candrakīrti, though the text only says "Śrīcandra"), and the author speaks of his own doubts after reading many works. The colophon states that the hymn was composed by a learned monk (*bahuśruta-bhikṣu*) named Sumatikīrtiśrī at a place in the Himalayas that it renders as Vijayadvīpa, with Tibetan forms of the names also given, and that the scribe was Gaganaśrī.[^dsbc896] The identification of these persons and the date of the work are not given in the sources consulted and are left open here.
