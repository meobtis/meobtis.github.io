The *Advayaparamārthā nāmasaṅgīti* is the text more widely known as the *Mañjuśrīnāmasaṃgīti* ("Chanting the Names of Mañjuśrī"). The text here opens with the salutation "om namaḥ śrīmahāmañjunāthāya" and then begins the narrative frame, in which Vajradhara introduces the recitation.[^dsbc110] The work is a short verse text, roughly 160 verses with some prose and mantra passages, divided into chapters, and its length is counted slightly differently in different descriptions.[^wiki-ms]

Its subject is Mañjuśrī understood as the embodiment of non-dual awakened gnosis, and the names it recites are used in meditative practice. In Vilāsavajra's commentary, the *Nāmamantrārthāvalokinī*, the text is read through a visualisation framework based on the Vajradhātu-maṇḍala.[^tribe2016] Other Indian commentators, including Smṛti, Candrabhadrakīrti and Līlāvajra, are mentioned in later summaries of its exegetical tradition, as are Kālacakra-related readings.[^wiki-ms]

A composition date is not firmly documented in the sources consulted: Vilāsavajra is dated to the 8th or 9th century, which gives a latest limit for the text's existence.[^tribe2016] The Tibetan translation was made by Rin-chen-bzaṅ-po with the Indian paṇḍita Kamalagupta and later revised by Śoṅ Blo-gros brtan-pa; the text stands at the head of the tantra section of the Kangyur.[^wiki-ms] The Sanskrit text has been edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and by Ronald Davidson, and translated into English by Alex Wayman as *Chanting the Names of Mañjuśrī*.[^wiki-ms][^wayman1999] Little more about this particular title form, with its added *Advayaparamārthā* epithet, could be established from the sources consulted.
