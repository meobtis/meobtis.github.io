The *Madhyāntavibhāgakārikā* is the set of root verses of the *Madhyāntavibhāga*, usually rendered "Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes"; the Tibetan canon's title is translated by the 84000 project as "The Verses on Distinguishing the Middle from the Extremes" (Toh 4021).[^84000toh4021] In the editions indexed here the verses are divided into five chapters: on characteristics (*lakṣaṇa*), on obstructions (*āvaraṇa*), on reality (*tattva*), on the cultivation of the antidotes together with the stages and fruit, and on the unsurpassed vehicle.[^dsbc571] The opening verses state that "unreal imagination" (*abhūtaparikalpa*) exists while duality does not, and that emptiness exists within it, a formulation that frames the whole work as a middle path between existence and non-existence.[^gretil-mvk]

The text is presented in the tradition as one of the teachings of the future Buddha Maitreya, revealed to Asaṅga; Vasubandhu, Asaṅga's half-brother, is said to have written a commentary on it, the *Madhyāntavibhāgabhāṣya*.[^sep-vasubandhu] The editions' front matter attributes the verses to Maitreya, which should be read as the traditional attribution rather than an established historical fact. Little else about its date, translations or manuscripts could be verified from the sources consulted for this introduction, so these are left undocumented here.
