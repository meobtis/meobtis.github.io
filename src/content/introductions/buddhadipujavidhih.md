The *Buddhādipūjāvidhi* ("Procedure for the worship of the Buddha and others") is a short Sanskrit ritual manual. It is catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the heading *sveṣṭadevatā* ("one's chosen deity") within the *śāstrapiṭaka*, and the text is a single undivided unit.[^dsbc923] It opens with an invocation of the Buddha and a verse announcing that the order of worship of the Buddha, the lord of the world (Avalokiteśvara), the lord of speech (Mañjuśrī) and Tārā will be set out briefly, in accordance with tradition.

The prose and verse that follow, as read in the text itself, describe the practitioner as a lay follower who has taken the three refuges and is devoted to the guru, and lay out a sequence beginning with meditation, mantra recitation and recollection of the Buddha at the end of the night, then washing and rinsing the mouth, bathing, and the preparation and purification of the ground and the maṇḍala, each with its own mantra.[^dsbc923] Little else about the work is documented in the sources consulted: the catalogue record gives no author, date, editor or printed source, and no reliable scholarly discussion, translation or manuscript information could be found, so its authorship, date and relation to similar Nepalese Buddhist worship manuals remain unestablished.
