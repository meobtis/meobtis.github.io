**MEOBTIS** stands for **M**arkdown **E**ditions **O**f **B**uddhist **T**exts **I**n
**S**anskrit. It brings together Buddhist texts in Sanskrit from several digital
collections. Each collection is converted to Markdown and kept in its own repository in
the [meobtis organisation on GitHub](https://github.com/meobtis). This website is a single
index to all of them, and a reader for every text in it.

The index currently lists **{catalog.works.length} texts** with **{editions} Markdown editions**
from {catalog.sources.length} sources.

## Why Markdown?

Markdown is plain text with light formatting for headings, verses and notes. A Markdown
edition:

- can be read in any text editor or directly on GitHub;
- shows exactly what changed between versions, line by line, under Git;
- is easy to search, quote, process with scripts, and turn into websites, e-books or
  print.

The converted texts keep the bibliographic details of their source, such as edition,
editors and the people who keyed them in, in a metadata header (YAML front matter) at
the top of each file.

## Introductions

Many texts have an introduction, shown above the text. It describes the work (its
contents, date, authorship, translations and manuscripts) and the history of the edition
you are reading: the printed edition it is based on and who prepared the digital text.
Text lists show its one-sentence summary.

:::caution[Based on internet research]
Introductions, summaries and edition histories are compiled from research on the internet,
with the help of AI. They draw on the sources listed with each introduction, but they **may
be incomplete or inaccurate**. They are not peer-reviewed scholarship. Please check
anything important against the sources cited and the scholarly literature, and cite those
rather than the introductions.
:::

If you find an error, use the *Suggest a correction* link at the end of the introduction,
or [open an issue](https://github.com/meobtis/meobtis.github.io/issues).

## How the index is organised

Each text is listed **once**, however many sources provide it. Its entry links to every
available edition, and each link names its source. For example, a text in both
collections might link to `GRETIL`, `DSBC` and `DSBC देवनागरी` (the Devanāgarī edition
of the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon). The ↗ link next to each edition opens the text
on the source's own website.

## Scripts and transliteration

Texts are available in IAST (the Roman transliteration used by scholars) and in Devanāgarī. Where a source has a text in
Devanāgarī but no IAST edition of it, MEOBTIS has an IAST version made by machine, with
[Aksharamukha](https://www.aksharamukha.com/python). These are labelled **transliterated**, as in `DSBC (transliterated)`
or `SARIT (transliterated)`, and each says on its page which Devanāgarī edition it comes from. The Devanāgarī edition is
the authoritative text, so please check the transliteration against it for anything important. To test the tool, we
compared it with the IAST editions that DSBC keyed in separately: on 338 works that DSBC has in both scripts, the
transliterated Devanāgarī agrees with the IAST edition word for word in the median case.

## Reading a text

Each edition link opens a reader page on this site. The page loads the edition's Markdown
file directly from its source repository on GitHub and displays it. The page also shows:

- the edition details from the file: source edition, contributors and licence;
- a table of contents for texts with chapters;
- links to the file on GitHub, to the original website, and to the other editions of the
  same text.

Many texts also have an [introduction](#introductions) above the text.

The texts themselves are not copied into this website. The reader always shows the
current version in the source repository, so a correction made there appears here
straight away.

Texts are arranged in groups, broadly following the divisions of the Sanskrit canon used
by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon:

- **Sūtra:** Āgama, Mahāyāna sūtras, Prajñāpāramitā, Dhāraṇī, Avadāna and Jātaka
- **Vinaya:** Prātimokṣa and karmavācanā, the Vinayavastu, and other Vinaya texts
- **Śāstra:** Abhidharma, Madhyamaka, Yogācāra, Pramāṇa, Prajñāpāramitā commentaries and
  epistles
- **Tantra:** the tantras by class, their commentaries, sādhana and ritual texts, and
  songs of realisation
- **Stotra:** hymns to the Buddha, to bodhisattvas, and to goddesses and other deities
- **Literature** and **Sciences:** kāvya, drama, gnomic verse, grammar, poetics,
  astronomy, medicine and the arts

Where a source assigns a text to a category, that classification is used. Texts that the
source does not classify are assigned to a category by the MEOBTIS editors. A
classification is a finding aid, not a scholarly judgement on a text's date, school or
authenticity.

## How the index is built

The index is generated, not written by hand. A build script reads the metadata of every
Markdown file in each source repository and merges editions of the same work. It matches
titles after normalising transliteration, so that `Abhisamayālaṅkāra` and
`Abhisamayālaṃkāraḥ` are recognised as the same title, and Devanāgarī titles are
transliterated so that they can be matched too. A small file of manual corrections
handles the cases the rules miss. The details are in the
[README](https://github.com/meobtis/meobtis.github.io#readme).

New sources will be added over time. Each new collection gets its own repository and is
merged into the same index.

## Using the texts

The texts belong to their sources and are subject to the sources' terms:

- **GRETIL** texts carry the licence given in each file. Most are distributed under a
  [Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/)
  licence.
- **SARIT** texts are licensed
  [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/) or
  [3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). You may share and adapt them with attribution, and must
  distribute adaptations under the same licence.
- **DSBC** texts are made available for non-commercial educational and research use. See
  the DSBC [usage policy](https://www.dsbcproject.org/pages/usage-policy).

When you quote or reuse a text, please cite the original edition named in its metadata
as well as the source collection.
