The *Advayasiddhi* ("Accomplishment of Non-duality") is a short Sanskrit work of Buddhist tantra attributed to the yoginī Lakṣmīṅkarā. It opens with a salutation to Vajrasattva and announces a concise supreme practice (*sādhana*) of that deity that dispenses with the usual apparatus of place, time, lunar day, asterism and maṇḍala.[^gretil-advayasiddhi] A recent Bengali translation describes it as a brief tantric manual of thirty-six verses.[^chakraborty2025]

The work belongs to the Yogottara or Anuttarayoga-type tantric literature, and the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the *yogottara tantra* heading.[^dsbc85] The opening verses reject vows, fasts and the mere recitation of syllables as sufficient for success, and go on to prescribe worship through contemplation of reality (*tattva*) and through the union of insight (*prajñā*) and method (*upāya*).[^gretil-advayasiddhi] The Bengali translation's abstract describes the text as antinomian, mentioning taboo substances and discussing the status of women and other groups.[^chakraborty2025]

Lakṣmīṅkarā is counted among the eighty-four *mahāsiddhas* in the list attributed to Abhayakaradatta and is said by the same source to have been a princess of Oḍḍiyāna and the sister of King Indrabhūti, author of the *Jñānasiddhi*; this is a tradition rather than a documented biography.[^chakraborty2025] Dating is uncertain: a library catalogue record of the 1995 edition places her in the eighth century, while the Bengali translation proposes about the ninth century CE.[^nii1995][^chakraborty2025] The same article notes a close relationship with Padmavajra's *Guhyasiddhi*, with fourteen of the thirty-six verses having parallels there, and single verses also appearing in the *Hevajra Tantra* and in Āryadeva's *Cittaviśuddhiprakaraṇa*, which raises questions of borrowing and priority.[^chakraborty2025]

Ram Prasad Mishra published the Sanskrit text with an English translation, an introduction and a bibliography (Delhi: Kant Publications, 1995; 118 pages).[^nii1995] Little else about the manuscript transmission, or the Tibetan and Chinese translations, could be verified in the sources consulted, so these are not described here.
