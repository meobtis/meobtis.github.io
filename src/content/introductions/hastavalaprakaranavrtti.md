The *Hastavālaprakaraṇavṛtti* is the prose commentary (*vṛtti*) on the *Hastavālaprakaraṇa*, a brief Madhyamaka verse treatise. The Sanskrit text opens with homage to Mañjuśrī and states that the work was composed so that beings, who cling to the ultimate reality of what is merely conventional, may reach non-erroneous knowledge by examining the nature of things.[^dsbc239] Its first verse compares mistaking a rope for a snake with the way even the parts of the rope are, on examination, found to be only erroneous cognition; the commentary extends the analysis to jars, cloths and other everyday objects, which dissolve into their parts and so cannot be ultimately established.[^dsbc239]

Authorship is disputed. The Chinese tradition attributes the *Hastavālaprakaraṇa* to Dignāga, whereas the Tibetan tradition ascribes it to Āryadeva; Frauwallner, Hattori and Ruegg are reported to consider Dignāga the more likely author.[^wiki-aryadeva] A further account lists it among Dignāga's more scriptural works, with Tibetan canon entries (Derge nos. 3844 and 3848), and calls the ascription to Āryadeva mistaken.[^wiki-dignaga] These are tertiary reports, and little else about the date, the commentary's own authorship or its manuscript history could be verified; the Sanskrit text is available in a modern printed edition rather than from a surviving manuscript recorded in the sources consulted.[^gretil-hvpv]
