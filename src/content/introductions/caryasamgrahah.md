The *Caryāsaṃgraha* ("Collection of Caryā [songs]") is known to this index only through the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which files it among the commentaries on yoginī tantras (*yoginītantraṭīkā*) in its śāstrapiṭaka section.[^dsbc856] The text opens with salutations to Śrī Cakrasaṃvara and Vajravārāhī. It then gives a series of numbered pieces, each headed by a rāga and a tāla and written in a vernacular mixed with Sanskrit forms. The first is marked as song 23 of a numbering labelled "NC", and its verses depict Saṃvara in cremation-ground imagery with a refrain addressed to the consort Vajravārāhī.[^dsbc856] This description rests only on the opening of the digital text; its contents have not been checked against a scholarly study.

Little reliable information about the work could be found: no author, date or place of composition, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation could be verified. The title may recall the better-known *Caryāgīti* or Charyapada, a collection of Vajrayāna songs of realisation attributed to a group of mahāsiddhas and written in an early eastern Indo-Aryan idiom, with a Sanskrit commentary by Munidatta.[^wiki-charyapada] Whether the *Caryāsaṃgraha* is related to that collection, or to the ritual song-and-dance repertoire of Newar Buddhism, is not established by the sources consulted and should not be assumed.
