The *Cakrasaṃvarastuti* ("Praise of Cakrasaṃvara") is a short Sanskrit devotional poem of eighteen verses. The text gives an alternative title in a parenthesis after its heading, *Herukaviśuddhistotra*, and ends with the colophon "the praise of Śrī Cakrasaṃvara is completed".[^dsbc142] No established English title was found. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon lists it among the stotras (hymns) of the Cakrasaṃvara group, and gives no description, author or date.[^dsbc142]

Judging from the text itself, it opens with homage to Śrī Heruka, the "great hero" and lord of the circle of ḍākinīs, and to Vajravārāhī. It then salutes a series of goddesses, each paired with a named place and a part of the body (for example the crown of the head, the ears, the navel, the feet), before venerating further goddesses stationed in the directions. It closes with a dedication of merit that the world may become Vajraḍāka, the teacher of the world.[^dsbc142] This pairing of deities with places and bodily locations belongs to the "body mandala" practice associated with the Cakrasaṃvara tradition, in which the practitioner visualises the deities within his or her own body.[^wikicakra]

The Cakrasaṃvara Tantra itself is an Anuttarayoga (Yoginī) tantra that David B. Gray dates to roughly the late eighth or early ninth century, with the late tenth century as the latest likely date; its central deity is Heruka (Saṃvara), whose consort is Vajravārāhī.[^wikicakra] This hymn's own author and date are not documented in the sources consulted, and its relation to the tantra or to its commentarial literature has not been established here. Little further is known about the text from the reliable sources available.
