The *Śayanāsanavastu* is the fifteenth chapter of the *Vinayavastu*, the first of the four divisions of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya, whose seventeen chapters set out the procedures and rules of monastic life. According to the 84000 introduction to the *Vinayavastu*, this chapter concerns the kinds of shelter suitable for monastics, such as temples, multi-storey buildings, huts and caves.[^84000toh1] Its title can be rendered "lodgings and seats" or similar; no established English title was found in the sources consulted.

The Sanskrit text opens with a summary verse (*uddāna*) that lists its topics, and then moves through short narratives in which the Buddha, staying at Śrāvastī, answers questions raised by the monks. The first of these concerns whom the monks should honour, and the Buddha replies that seniority is the criterion, so that the elder monk is to be shown reverence and given the first seat, water and alms-food.[^gretil-sayan] Beyond this, little documentation specific to this chapter was found.

For centuries the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya was known mainly through its translations. The 84000 introduction reports that manuscripts found near Gilgit in 1931, dated to about the fifth or sixth century CE, include many vinaya manuscripts, most of them from the *Vinayavastu*. The only complete translation is the ninth-century Tibetan one, and Yijing made a partial Chinese translation, covering at least seven chapters, in the late seventh to early eighth century.[^84000toh1]

The Sanskrit text of this chapter was edited by Raniero Gnoli from the Gilgit manuscript, together with the *Adhikaraṇavastu* (the sixteenth section), in a single volume published in Rome in 1978. The digital editions in the index are both based on that volume.[^gretil-sayan][^dsbc725]
