The *Pāramitāsamāsa*, known in English as the *Compendium of the Perfections*, is a Sanskrit verse work attributed to Āryaśūra. The digital text is divided into six chapters, one for each of the six perfections: giving (*dāna*), ethical conduct (*śīla*), patience (*kṣānti*), vigour (*vīrya*), meditation (*dhyāna*) and wisdom (*prajñā*).[^dsbc280] It opens with a homage to the Buddha and verses on taking refuge in the Three Jewels and fixing the mind on awakening, and then turns to the practice of giving.[^gretil-ps]

Little reliable information on its date, author and transmission could be gathered in the sources consulted here. A catalogue record lists an edition and translation of the work by Carol Jean Meadows, *Ārya-śūra's Compendium of the Perfections* (1986), which is the source of the English title, and an earlier dissertation of 1976 on the text.[^googlebooks] The relationship of this Āryaśūra to other authors of the same name, and the work's Tibetan and Chinese versions, are not documented in the records consulted.
