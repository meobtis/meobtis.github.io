The *Udayananirākaraṇam* (literally "the refutation of Udayana") is a short Sanskrit treatise in three chapters. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon lists it as the *Udayananirākaraṇam* of Ratnakīrti and gives the printed source as an edition by R. N. Pandey (Delhi, 1984).[^dsbc306] The text opens with an invocation to Tārā and proceeds as a polemical argument about whether things can be said to have the capacity (*sāmarthya*) to produce effects, in terms close to the debates on momentariness and causation in Buddhist epistemology.[^dsbc306]

Ratnakīrti is usually placed in the eleventh century CE, and a list of his works that includes this title is given in a general reference article, which in turn relies on McDermott's study of his *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi* and on Thakur's edition of his collected treatises (*Ratnakīrtinibandhāvali*, 1957).[^wiki-ratnakirti] The name suggests that the work answers the Nyāya philosopher Udayana, but little detailed information about its date, manuscripts, or Tibetan or Chinese translations was found in sources that could be verified, and no established English title was identified.
