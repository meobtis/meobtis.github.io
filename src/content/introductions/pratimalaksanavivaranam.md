The *Pratimālakṣaṇavivaraṇa* ("Explanation of the Characteristics of Images") is a short Sanskrit text on the proportions and features of sacred images. It is catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the technical sciences (*śilpaśāstra*), and the edition it records is the work of Dr. Banarsi Lal, published at Sarnath in 2003.[^dsbc316] The text opens with a verse promising to set down the characteristics of Buddha images and then gives measurements in finger-widths (*aṅgula*) and in *tāla* units, the latter a system of proportion based on the height of the figure; it distinguishes, for example, ten-*tāla* figures for buddhas and bodhisattvas from nine-*tāla* figures for goddesses such as Locanā and eight-*tāla* figures for certain wrathful deities.[^dsbc316] It then proceeds feature by feature through the face, eyes, ears, limbs, hands and feet, describes the facial shapes proper to different classes of figure, and refers to the iconography of Śrīsaṃvara and other deities.[^dsbc316]

Little reliable information about the work's author, date, place of composition or transmission could be found, and no scholarly study was available to the research behind this note; the account above rests on the text itself and on the edition record. The text concludes by calling itself a teaching of the perfectly awakened one (*samyaksambuddhabhāṣita*), which should be read as a claim made by the text rather than a historical attribution.[^dsbc316]
