The *Balimālikā* ("Garland of Offerings") is a short Sanskrit text on the *bali*, the food offering that Buddhist tantric practice directs to gods, spirits and other beings. Its opening verses present it as written for practitioners, say that the offering gratifies all beings, and credit it with serving rites of pacification, increase, subjugation and wrathful action; the same verses name its compiler as Sumatisiṃha.[^dsbc780] The text then gives verses, said to be drawn from a *Subāhu* tantra, for presenting the offering to a long list of beings who live in the various realms and places of the world.[^dsbc780]

Little reliable information about the work is documented in sources accessible here. The digital edition records Sumatisimha as editor of a 1967 printing in Japan, but it does not identify the manuscript or the printed edition behind it, and I could not verify the author's date, the tradition within which the text was composed, or any translation of it into English.[^dsbc780] No established English title was found.
