The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") is a short Sanskrit stotra, a verse hymn of praise addressed to the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha. The title carries the qualifier *navagrahakṛtam*, "made by the nine planets", so the hymn presents itself as the praise of the Buddha offered by the navagraha. The text consists of ten numbered verses, nine of which begin with the words "I bow" (*praṇamāmi*).[^dsbc182]

Judging from the verses, the hymn follows the Buddha's life in outline: his golden-coloured body, his leaving of the household life accompanied by his charioteer Chandaka and his horse, his austerities among sages in the forest, his victory over Māra's army beneath the great tree, the offering of milk-rice by merchants, and the teaching of the Dharma at the deer park. The closing verse states that a person who recites this hymn, described as composed by a teacher, is free from fear of planetary affliction and illness and will reach the city of liberation.[^dsbc182]

Little else about the work appears to be documented in the sources available for this introduction. No reliable information was found on its date, its authorship beyond the attribution in the title, or on any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The site's catalogue also lists similarly titled hymns with other qualifiers (such as *brahmaṇā kṛtam*), which are separate entries.
