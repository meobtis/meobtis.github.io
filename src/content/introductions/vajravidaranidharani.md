The *Vajravidāraṇīdhāraṇī* (also given as *Āryavajravidāraṇānāmadhāraṇī*) is a short Mahāyāna dhāraṇī text, known in English as *Vajra Conqueror*. In it Vajrapāṇi, empowered by the Buddha and by all bodhisattvas, proclaims a series of powerful dhāraṇī-mantras, and the text closes with verses on the benefits of the dhāraṇī and a simple ablution ritual.[^84000toh750]

According to the Indic commentarial tradition summarised in the introduction to the English translation, the dhāraṇī was revealed to cure King Ajātaśatru of Magadha. It is popular among Newar Buddhists in Nepal, where it belongs to the daily *Saptavāra* practices.[^84000toh750] The Sanskrit text opens with the homage "namo bhagavatya āryamahāvajravidāraṇāyai" and sets the scene "in the vajras", before the mantras themselves begin.[^gretil-vajravidarani]

In Tibet the work is known as *rdo rje rnam par 'joms pa'i gzungs* and appears in the Degé Kangyur both among the tantras (Toh 750) and among the dhāraṇīs (Toh 949). It was translated by the Indian preceptors Jinamitra and Dānaśīla with Yeshé Dé, and is already listed in the Denkarma catalogue of 812 CE. Commentaries are attributed to Padmasambhava, Vimalamitra, Buddhaguhya and Smṛtijñānakīrti, and it remains important in all Tibetan traditions, chiefly for healing and purification.[^84000toh750] The same source relies on Iwamoto's 1937 edition and a 2005 *Dhīḥ* edition for the Sanskrit. Little further documentation of the Sanskrit transmission was found, and no Chinese translation is mentioned there.[^84000toh750]
