The *Madhyamakaśāstrastuti* ("Praise of the Madhyamaka Treatise") is a short Sanskrit stotra in verse, written in the elaborate metres of classical Sanskrit poetry. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) it is filed among the Madhyamaka stotras and is headed as the work of the teacher (*ācārya*) Candrakīrti.[^dsbc154] No English title for it is established, and very little independent scholarship on the work could be found in the sources consulted; the attribution to Candrakīrti rests on this heading and should be treated with caution.

From the text itself, the hymn opens by describing the Buddha's teaching as arranged in nine kinds of scripture, such as *sūtra*, and addressed to the dispositions of beings and founded on the two truths. It then argues that the teaching which removes delusion (*moha*) is the one that removes all the afflictions, and identifies the middle way (*madhyamā*) with emptiness (*śūnyatā*). It goes on to praise Nāgārjuna as a son of the Buddhas, who composed his Madhyamaka treatise out of compassion to refute wrong views, and it mentions Rāhulabhadra and Deva as associates of Nāgārjuna and speaks of his disciples. It also laments that the works of Nāgārjuna and his disciples were largely lost over time.[^dsbc154]
