The *Sarvajñatākāradhāraṇī* ("dhāraṇī of the form of omniscience") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist text in the form of a sūtra framing a dhāraṇī. It opens with the usual "Thus have I heard" formula and sets the scene at Rājagṛha, on Vulture Peak, where the Buddha is surrounded by his assembly and begins to teach a discourse on the bodhisattva's range of vision. The bodhisattva Ratnavairocana asks about the rays of light that fill the world-system, and the Buddha answers by describing a distant world named Padmā, where the Tathāgata Padmottara teaches.[^gretil-sarvajnatakara]

In the narrative that follows, an earlier Tathāgata, Candrottama, is about to pass into nirvāṇa. He prophesies that the bodhisattva Gaganamudra will become the Buddha Padmottara, and he hands Gaganamudra a dhāraṇī gateway said to have been taught by the Buddhas of all three times. The dhāraṇī itself follows in a sequence of numbered passages, each ending with a formula that ties it to a set of the factors of awakening, such as the four foundations of mindfulness, the four right efforts and the seven limbs of awakening.[^gretil-sarvajnatakara]

Little reliable information on the text's date, authorship, manuscripts or any Tibetan and Chinese counterparts was found in the sources consulted, and none is claimed here. The Sanskrit text is known to this index from Isshi Yamada's edition, printed in the appendix volume of his critical edition of the *Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka*, published in London by Luzac for the School of Oriental and African Studies in 1968.[^yamada1968] How the appendix text relates to that sūtra is not documented in the records consulted.
