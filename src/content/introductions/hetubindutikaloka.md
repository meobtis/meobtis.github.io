The *Hetubinduṭīkāloka* ("Light on the Commentary on the Hetubindu") is a Sanskrit subcommentary by Durvekamiśra (Durveka Miśra). It explains the *Hetubinduṭīkā* of Bhaṭṭa Arcaṭa, which in turn comments on Dharmakīrti's *Hetubindu*.[^gretil-hba] Dharmakīrti is the Buddhist logician and epistemologist whose works on inference and perception shaped later Indian Buddhist philosophy.[^sep-dharmakirti] The *Āloka* follows the structure of Arcaṭa's commentary, glossing it passage by passage.[^gretil-hba]

Little reliable information about the work itself could be found in the sources consulted; further web searches were not possible when this note was prepared, so its date, the author's biography and its translations are not described here. Durvekamiśra is also named as the author of the *Dharmottarapradīpa*, a subcommentary on Dharmottara's *Nyāyabinduṭīkā*.[^gretil-dp]

The text is known in print from the 1949 Baroda edition by Sanghavi and Jinavijaya (Gaekwad's Oriental Series 113), which presents Arcaṭa's *Ṭīkā* together with Durvekamiśra's *Āloka*. The digital text reproduces the *Āloka* part (pp. 233–411), and notes in the text point to torn or missing passages in the *Ṭīkā* where the editors reconstructed readings.[^gretil-hba]
