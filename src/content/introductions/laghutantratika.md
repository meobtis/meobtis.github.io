The *Laghutantraṭīkā* ("Commentary on the Short Tantra") is a Sanskrit commentary attributed in the edition's title to Vajrapāṇi. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) text is taken from Claudio Cicuzza's 2001 critical edition, published in Rome by the Istituto Italiano per l'Africa e l'Oriente.[^dsbc866] The text opens with homage to Śrī Cakrasaṃvara and a verse in which the author names himself Vajrapāṇi, says he wrote at the urging of a "lord of heroes" (*vīreśa*), and states that the work explains the "Laghutantra", an essence drawn from a tantra of one hundred thousand (*lakṣa*) verses and identified with the Heruka and Ḍākinī Cakrasaṃvara.[^dsbc866]

The Cakrasaṃvara tradition has several Sanskrit commentaries on the abridged root tantra, known as the *Laghusaṃvara*; one modern study notes at least eleven surviving Sanskrit commentaries on it, though the source consulted does not list this work.[^wiki-cakrasamvara] Beyond what the edition's own opening verses state, no further reliable information about this commentary's date, the author's identity, or its Tibetan or Chinese translations was found in the sources available for this introduction, so none is given here.
