The *Vajrayoginyāḥ stutipraṇidhānam* ("Praise and Aspiration of Vajrayoginī") is a short Sanskrit work in verse. It opens with a salutation to Śrī Vajrayoginī, the Buddha, Dharma and Saṅgha, the teachers and the wrathful heroes, and its heading ascribes it to the *siddhācārya* Virūpa.[^dsbc217] The opening verses describe the goddess as a red figure seated in the lotus at the navel, within a triangular, three-pointed and four-seated structure, with a yellow *hrīṃ* seed-syllable at its centre. They also place her in relation to the central, left and right channels of the subtle body, and picture her naked in a dancing stance, holding a curved knife and skull cup.[^dsbc217] The text is therefore a stotra that combines praise with the visualisation of a tantric deity.

Virūpa is generally described as an Indian *mahāsiddha* of roughly the eighth to ninth century, regarded in the Sakya tradition as the source of the Lamdré teachings, and many works are attributed to him.[^wiki-virupa] I did not find a scholarly discussion of this particular text, so its authorship, date and any Tibetan or Chinese translation remain undocumented here, and the ascription should be treated as the tradition of the edition rather than as established fact. The digital text is known only from the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon, which gives the printed source as a modern anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc217]
