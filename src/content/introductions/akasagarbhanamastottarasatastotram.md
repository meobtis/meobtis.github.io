The *Ākāśagarbhanāmāṣṭottaraśatastotram* is a brief Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of the bodhisattva Ākāśagarbha. The title means roughly "hymn of the one hundred and eight names of Ākāśagarbha". The text opens with the invocation *om namo buddhāya* and consists of sixteen numbered verses: fifteen verses of homage, in which strings of epithets are addressed to Ākāśagarbha and each verse closes with *namo 'stu te* ("homage to you"), and a final verse promising that whoever bears these one hundred and eight names will quickly obtain the consecration (*abhiṣeka*) of all the buddhas. A closing colophon calls it a hymn of invitation or entreaty (*adhyeṣaṇā*) addressed to Ākāśagarbha.[^dsbc112] No established English title was found in the sources consulted; it may be rendered "Hymn of the One Hundred and Eight Names of Ākāśagarbha".

Ākāśagarbha ("Essence of Space") is one of the eight great bodhisattvas, or "close sons", of the Buddha, and is associated with purifying the results of negative actions and with removing obstacles on the path.[^84000toh260] The epithets of the hymn reflect this profile: many play on *ākāśa* (space) and *ratna* (jewel), together with consecration and generosity (*dāna*), and the hymn also refers to the perfection of giving.[^dsbc112]

Little is documented about this particular hymn. The sources consulted gave no reliable information about its author, date, place of composition, manuscripts or translations, and the edition's front matter does not say. The longer Ākāśagarbha Sūtra (Toh 260) is a different work, which is preserved in Tibetan, and an earlier Chinese version is attributed to Buddhayaśas (403–413 CE); the Tibetan text was probably prepared in the ninth century.[^84000toh260] Whether the hymn is related to that literature is not established here.
