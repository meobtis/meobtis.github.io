The *Abhisamayālaṅkārāloka* ("Illumination of the *Abhisamayālaṅkāra*") is a Sanskrit commentary by Haribhadra on the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā*, the Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines. It is dated to about 750 CE and is among the works most studied in Tibetan monasteries, where commentaries on it are still written.[^wiki-aal] Its title in English is not fixed; "Illumination of the Ornament of Clear Realization" is a literal rendering of the Sanskrit.

The text takes the verses of Maitreya's *Abhisamayālaṅkāra* as its framework and relates them to the sūtra, so that the sūtra is read through the eight topics of that treatise. The Sanskrit text opens with verses of homage that name Maitreya, Asaṅga and Vasubandhu among the earlier interpreters of the Prajñāpāramitā.[^dsbc276] The digital edition is divided into 32 sections, following the chapters of the sūtra.[^dsbc276]

According to Wikipedia, the work was translated into Tibetan around the tenth or eleventh century.[^wiki-aal] Giuseppe Tucci first edited the Sanskrit in 1932, and Unrai Wogihara later edited it together with the text of the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* (1932 and 1935).[^wiki-aal] The Japanese national catalogue lists Haribhadra as a ninth-century author, so the dating varies between sources.[^nii-tucci1932] Little else about the text is documented in the sources consulted here.
