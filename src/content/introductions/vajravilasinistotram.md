The *Vajravilāsinīstotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn of twenty-two verses addressed to the goddess Vajrayoginī, who is also invoked in the text as Vajravārāhī. It opens with the salutation "om namaḥ śrīvajrayoginyai" and consists of rhymed verses in which the poet praises the goddess's body and attributes, appeals to her for protection from suffering, and refers to the Vajrayāna themes of the purity of subject and object, emptiness and innate bliss.[^dsbc214] The colophon calls it the "*Śrīvajravilāsinīstotra* in the *Guhyasamayasādhana* tantra" and ascribes the work to the *mahāpaṇḍita* Vibhūticandra.[^dsbc214]

Little reliable information about this particular hymn could be gathered beyond what the edition itself records. The attribution is that of the colophon, and no independent evidence for the date or circumstances of composition was consulted. The digital text is drawn from a modern printed collection of Buddhist hymns, and the editors' notes in the text show that variant readings were recorded in brackets.[^dsbc214]
