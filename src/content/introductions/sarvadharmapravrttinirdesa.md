The *Sarvadharmāpravṛttinirdeśa* is a Mahāyāna sūtra. Its Sanskrit is known from 29 fragments in the Schøyen Collection, several of which belong to the same folios: they come from 13 folios in all, two of them complete but for a few syllables. The first folio of the sūtra is folio 397 of the manuscript, and the following folios survive up to 402; folio 403 is lost, and 404 to 408 survive in fragments. Where two further fragments stood cannot be decided. There are thus numerous Sanskrit fragments from the first third of the text, and from the remaining two thirds only a short one in the middle of the sūtra.[^braarvig2000]

Four quotations of the sūtra in Śāntideva's *Śikṣāsamuccaya* preserve further Sanskrit, two of them from its final parts, and the edition inserts them into the reconstruction.[^braarvig2000] The reconstruction divides the sūtra into paragraphs according to its contents, a division made by the editor. The verses that survive in whole or in part are in Triṣṭubh metre, although some lines have ten, twelve or thirteen syllables instead of the standard eleven.[^braarvig2000]

The whole sūtra is preserved in a Tibetan translation, whose colophons name Rinchen mTsho among those who translated and revised it, and in three Chinese translations: by Kumārajīva, by Jñānagupta, and a very late one by Shaode. Neither the Tibetan nor the Chinese translations were made from the Sanskrit manuscript known today, which in many respects differs from them, especially in the verses; Braarvig finds Jñānagupta's version generally closest to it.[^braarvig2000]
