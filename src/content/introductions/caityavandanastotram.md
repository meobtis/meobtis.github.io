The *Caityavandanāstotram* ("hymn in homage to shrines") is a very short devotional poem in Sanskrit, consisting of five verses followed by a closing colophon. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is classed under the hymns (*stotra*) of the treatises (*śāstrapiṭaka*), in a group labelled *caityabhaṭṭāraka*, and its source is the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha* edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey.[^dsbc140]

Each verse ends in a refrain of homage to the Buddhas. The first verse honours the birth, awakening, first sermon and miracles of the Buddha, and the second names sites such as Vaiśālī, Gṛdhrakūṭa, Śrāvastī, Lumbinī and Kuśinagara. The later verses extend the veneration to relic shrines (*dhātucaitya*) said to stand in places ranging from Mount Kailāsa and the Himalaya to Kashmir, China, Nepal and Sri Lanka, and finally to relic-filled stūpas and images of the Buddha in every place.[^dsbc140] Little reliable information about the hymn's author, date or place of composition, or about any translation or commentary, could be found in the sources consulted, so these are left undetermined here.
