The *Suhṛllekha* ("Letter to a Friend") is a Sanskrit epistle in verse, in the genre of Buddhist letters of advice, and is traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna. The Sanskrit text opens by presenting its verses as ārya-metre songs gathered from the Buddha's words, and goes on to urge the recipient to keep in mind the six recollections (the Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha, moral conduct, giving and the deities), to practise the ten courses of wholesome action, to avoid intoxicants and to give generously.[^gretil-suhrllekha] The Tibetan canonical title is *Letter to a Friend* (Toh 4182), where the work is classed among the epistles.[^84000toh4182]

Modern scholars differ on the attribution. One standard overview lists the *Suhṛllekha* among the religious works attributed to Nāgārjuna that deal with the bodhisattva path, ethics and practice, and notes that Christian Lindtner counts it among the works he regards as definitely authentic, while other scholars do not include it in their core lists.[^wikinagarjuna] Little else about its date, the identity of its recipient, or its translation history could be confirmed from the sources consulted here, so this introduction does not go beyond the editions' own front matter.
