The *Dharmadhātunāmastava* ("Praise Called the Dharmadhātu") is a very short Sanskrit hymn of seven verses, each addressed with the refrain "namo 'stu te" ("homage to you"). Its verses salute, in turn, Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi, the Dharmadhātu and Siddhivajra, and connect each to a seed syllable (such as *vaṃ*, *raṃ* and *dha*), to an element or colour, and to a quality.[^dsbc147] No established English title was found.

Little reliable information about this text could be found. The digital record does not name an author, and the sources consulted do not give a date, place or translations into Tibetan or Chinese. Its content suggests a place in the tantric Buddhist stotra literature, but that is an inference from the verses themselves, not a documented classification. The only printed source identified is the anthology edited by Pandey, from which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon took its text.[^dsbc147]
