The *Caṇḍikādaṇḍakastotram* ("hymn to Caṇḍikā in the daṇḍaka style") is a brief stotra, or hymn of praise, in nine verses. It opens with a homage to the goddess Caṇḍikā (*om namaḥ śrīcaṇḍikāyai*) and closes with a colophon marking the end of the hymn. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed under the stotra section, in a group of hymns to Ugratārā.[^dsbc200]

Read directly, the text describes a fearsome goddess, addressed variously as Caṇḍī, Ugracaṇḍī and Kālarātri, who is invoked with strings of seed syllables and onomatopoeic sounds. She holds a sword, a noose, a trident and a skull-cup, and wears a garland of skulls. Most verses end with the refrain *siddhicaṇḍī namaste* ("homage to Siddhicaṇḍī"), and the final verse is a short salutation to Ugracaṇḍī.[^dsbc200] Little is documented about the hymn's author, date, place of composition or transmission, and no scholarly study or translation could be consulted in preparing this note. The digital edition is drawn from Janardan Shastri Pandey's printed collection *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha* (Motilal Banarsidass, 1994), which is the only edition recorded here.[^dsbc200]
