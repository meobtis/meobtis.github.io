The *Ahorātravratacaityasevānuśaṃsāvadāna* ("Avadāna on the Benefits of Serving Caityas in the Day-and-Night Vow") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist text in verse. It is also known by the shorter title *Ahorātravratakathā*, the title under which a library catalogue lists the edition in which it appears.[^cinii] No established English title was found.

The text opens with an invocation of the Three Jewels and a statement that the author will recount the story of the day-and-night vow (*ahorātravrata*) on the basis of a teacher's instruction. King Aśoka, seated before the monk Upagupta at the Kukkuṭārāma, asks to hear the rules of the caitya vow and of the day-and-night vow, and Upagupta replies by relating what the Buddha once taught at Jetavana.[^gretil-ahoratra] A manuscript catalogue entry describes it as a ritual text on caitya worship, written in verses in a style resembling avadāna literature, built on the figures of Aśoka and Upagupta.[^samhita]

Little appears to be documented about its authorship, date or place of composition. The same manuscript record, citing Matsunami's catalogue, notes that the text is regarded there as the tenth chapter of an *Aśokāvadānamāla*; this is a catalogue statement and has not been verified elsewhere.[^samhita] The manuscript, in Nepālākṣara script, is held in the Kawaguchi Ekai Collection of the General Library of the University of Tokyo.[^samhita]

The text was edited, together with two other Sanskrit texts on caitya worship and English synopses, by Ratna Handurukande in 2000.[^handurukande2000][^cinii]
