The *Sāgaranāgarājaparipṛcchā* ("The Questions of the Nāga King Sāgara") is the title of several distinct Mahāyāna sūtras preserved in the Tibetan Kangyur. In the Degé Kangyur they are catalogued as Toh 153, a long version, Toh 154, a medium-length version that appears to be an excerpt of the long one, and Toh 155, a very short text.[^84000toh154] The text in these editions is of the last, shortest kind: it consists of a brief narrative frame (the Buddha is dwelling at the abode of the nāga king Sāgara with an assembly of 1,250 monks and many bodhisattvas) followed by a single short teaching.[^gretil-sagara]

In that teaching the Buddha tells Sāgara that when four "Dharma summaries" (*dharmoddāna*) are taught, the whole of the 84,000 aggregates of the Dharma is taught. The four are that all conditioned things are impermanent, that all contaminated phenomena are suffering, that all phenomena are without self, and that nirvāṇa is peace; the assembly rejoices at the end.[^gretil-sagara][^84000toh155] These are the four statements often called the four "seals" of the Dharma.[^84000toh155]

According to the 84000 introduction to its translation of Toh 155, the Sanskrit original of this short sūtra is no longer extant. The four statements themselves survive in Sanskrit in Vasubandhu's commentary on the *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra* (XVIII.80) and in Asaṅga's *Bodhisattvabhūmi*. The Tibetan translation, by Surendrabodhi and Yeshé Dé, was probably made in the late eighth or early ninth century, and there is a Chinese parallel (Taishō 599) by Yijing, as well as a twelfth-century Tangut fragment from Khara-khoto.[^84000toh155] The Sanskrit text in these digital editions presents itself as "punaruddhṛta", a restoration into Sanskrit rather than a surviving manuscript, and the DSBC version gives the Tibetan title alongside it.[^dsbc30] Further detail on the history of this short version is not documented in the sources consulted.
