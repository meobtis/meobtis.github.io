The *Vinayaviniścaya* (also called *Upāliparipṛcchā*, "Upāli's Questions") is a short Mahāyāna sūtra belonging to the Heap of Jewels (*Ratnakūṭa*) collection. In English it is known as *Determining the Vinaya: Upāli's Questions* (Toh 68).[^84000toh68] The Sanskrit text opens with the monk Upāli, foremost of the Buddha's disciples in the discipline, asking how the *prātimokṣa* vows should be taught to followers of the śrāvaka, pratyekabuddha and bodhisattva vehicles.[^gretil-vvu]

According to the 84000 introduction, the sūtra examines how monastic vows relate to a bodhisattva's conduct, explaining how the different vehicles differ in orientation and in how they apply their vows. It also contains a distinctive form of the confessional "Three Sections" rite with a set of thirty-five buddhas.[^84000toh68] In the Sanskrit passage the Buddha contrasts the strictness appropriate to a śrāvaka with a more forgiving, compassion-oriented training for bodhisattvas, and discusses which transgressions are weightier.[^gretil-vvu]

Sanskrit witnesses include a long citation in the *Bodhisattvaprātimokṣa Sūtra* edited by Nalinaksha Dutt in 1931, as well as verses cited by Śāntideva and Candrakīrti; the Pāli *Upāliparipucchāsutta* is a different text. Chinese versions are Taishō 310, 325 and 326.[^84000toh68] Little further about its authorship, date or the manuscript tradition was found in the sources consulted, so these are left unstated.
