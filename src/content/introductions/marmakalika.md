The *Marmakālikā* is a Sanskrit work of the Buddhist tantric tradition, classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the commentaries on Yoginī tantras (*yoginītantraṭīkā*). In the digital text it is divided into nine sections, headed respectively the statement of benediction (*āśīrvādābhidhānam*), outer worship (*bāhyārcanavidhiḥ*), meditation (*bhāvanāvidhiḥ*), favouring the disciple (*śiṣyānugrahavidhiḥ*), the extraction of the mantra (*mantroddhāravidhiḥ*), worship (*pūjāvidhiḥ*), offerings (*balividhiḥ*), recitation (*japavidhiḥ*) and the keeping of the pledges (*samayaparipālanavidhiḥ*).[^dsbc348]

The opening verses of the text invoke the goddess Vajravārāhī, and the commentary introduces itself as a *pañjikā* (a word-by-word gloss) that speaks of a teacher named Samādhivajra and of the practice (*sādhana*) of Vajravārāhī.[^dsbc348] The arrangement of the sections suggests a practice manual, but the sources consulted do not describe the work's contents, date, author or place of composition, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation or manuscript tradition could be documented here. Little reliable information about the text was found beyond the edition's own front matter, so these matters are left open.
