The *Yogaśataka* ("Hundred Remedies") is a short Sanskrit work on medicine, classed as *cikitsā* (therapeutics). In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed under the "science" section (*śāstrapiṭaka*, *cikitsavidyā*) as a single undivided text.[^dsbc917] Its opening lines pay homage to the Buddha Bhaiṣajyaguru-vaiḍūryaprabhā-rāja, the "Medicine Master", and to Āryanāgārjuna, which signals the Buddhist transmission of the work and its attribution to Nāgārjuna.[^dsbc917]

The attribution is not settled. The Nāgārjuna article on Wikipedia reports that Christian Lindtner placed a "Yogaśataka", described as a medical text, among the works attributed to Nāgārjuna that he regarded as perhaps authentic, citing Lindtner's study of the writings attributed to the master.[^wiki-nagarjuna][^lindtner1982] Beyond this, little reliable information about its date, textual history, translations or printed editions could be established from the sources consulted, and none of them is given here as fact.
