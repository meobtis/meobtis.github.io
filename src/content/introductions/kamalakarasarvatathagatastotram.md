The *Kamalākarasarvatathāgatastotram* ("hymn to all the tathāgatas, [spoken in] the lotus grove") is a short Sanskrit stotra in verse. It begins with a prose introduction stating that, in a lotus grove, the speaker praised the buddhas of the past, future and present with a hymn to all the tathāgatas, and then continues in numbered verses. The opening verses pay homage to the victors of the ten directions, and the following ones describe the physical marks of a buddha in conventional imagery, such as a golden body, a resonant voice and eyes like blue lotuses, before turning to the relief of beings in the hells and other unfortunate rebirths.[^dsbc192]

The text is taken from the anthology *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey and published by Motilal Banarsidass in 1994, according to the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon record.[^dsbc192] Little reliable information about the hymn itself could be found: no study of it, no author or date, and no Tibetan or Chinese translation or manuscript tradition were located, and none is claimed here.
