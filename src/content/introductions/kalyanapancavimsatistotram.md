The *Kalyāṇapañcaviṃśatistotram* (the title can be read as "hymn of twenty-five [verses] for welfare") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist hymn. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is classified among the stotra texts connected with Svayambhū, and its romanized version is drawn from Pandey's anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc194] Very little documentation of the work could be found: no English title, translation, manuscript description or scholarly study was located, so what follows is limited to what the digital edition itself shows.

The text is composed in long verses, each closing with the same refrain asking that the figures praised may bring "welfare" (*kalyāṇa*) to the listeners. The opening verses name the primordial Buddha Svayambhū, the five Tathāgatas, Vajrasattva, Tārā, the Pañcarakṣā and other goddesses, followed by the earlier Buddhas down to Śākyamuni and a series of bodhisattvas. Later verses refer to named sacred places and their founding legends, including the Vāgmatī river, Gokarṇa and Oḍiyāna.[^dsbc194] The edition marks several lacunae with ellipses. Its author, date and place of composition are not stated in the edition, and none could be established from the sources available.
