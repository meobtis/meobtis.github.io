The *Bodhicaryāvatāra* ("Entering the Conduct of Awakening", commonly rendered in English as *The Way of the Bodhisattva*) is a Sanskrit poem in ten chapters, which leads the reader through the path of becoming a bodhisattva. In its most widely known form it has just over 900 verses.[^iep] It is sometimes also cited under the variant title *Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra*.

The chapters are organised around the bodhisattva's practice. According to the Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy, chapter III concerns the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*), chapter V good conduct, and chapters VI to IX the last four perfections: patient endurance, heroic strength, meditation (including the equalisation and exchange of self and other) and the metaphysical insight of the Madhyamaka tradition.[^iep]

The work is attributed to Śāntideva, who is also credited with the *Śikṣāsamuccaya*. Tibetan hagiographies place him as a monk at Nālandā, a tradition that most modern historians doubt in its details, although there is no good reason to deny that he was a monk there. Because the Chinese pilgrim Yijing does not mention him, the works are thought to have been composed, or to have become famous, after Yijing left India in 685; they are dated approximately to the eighth century.[^iep]

The Sanskrit text is not the only early form of the work. The Dunhuang manuscripts preserve a shorter Tibetan version of about 700 verses in nine chapters, whose colophon names Akṣayamati rather than Śāntideva as author; Saito concluded that the longer ten-chapter text is an enlarged version of it.[^vanschaik2014] The *Bodhicaryāvatāra* was translated into Tibetan more than once, and a less influential Chinese translation exists.[^iep]

The most important Sanskrit commentary is Prajñākaramati's *Bodhicaryāvatārapañjikā*, and the poem has attracted many Tibetan commentaries, and Tibetan authors such as Tsong kha pa and sGam po pa quote it at length.[^iep] P. L. Vaidya's 1960 edition of the root text together with the *Pañjikā* is the printed source of several of the digital texts here.[^oeaw-vaidya]
