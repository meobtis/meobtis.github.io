The *Pramāṇāntarbhāva* (roughly, "the inclusion of the other means of valid knowledge") is a Sanskrit treatise on epistemology (*pramāṇa*) that survives in a manuscript and was published by Satīndra Candra Nyāyācārya in 1969. No established English title is in use. The edition is divided into four chapters (*paricchedas*), the later three of which are supplied by the editor in angle brackets, and its apparatus records readings from a manuscript.[^sarit-pramanantarbhava]

The opening verses state the thesis summed up in the title: perception (*pratyakṣa*) and inference (*anumāna*) are the only two means of valid knowledge, because the objects of knowledge are of two kinds, and the further means accepted by other schools, such as verbal testimony (*śabda*) or presumption (*arthāpatti*), are not independent. The text goes on to argue that what is directly apprehended is what exists, and develops this in debate with opposing views.[^sarit-pramanantarbhava]

The electronic record names no author and no date, and the sources consulted for this introduction do not settle the question; little else about the work is documented there.[^sarit-pramanantarbhava]
