The *Pramāṇavārttikasvavṛttiṭīkā* is a Sanskrit commentary attributed to Karṇakagomin on the *Pramāṇavārttikasvavṛtti*, the prose autocommentary that Dharmakīrti wrote on his own *Pramāṇavārttika*, a verse treatise on epistemology and logic.[^tillemans2026] According to the front matter of the digital text, it was edited by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana together with the autocommentary and the verses of the first chapter (*svārthānumānaparicchedaḥ*), under the title *Ācāryadharmakīrteḥ Pramāṇavārttikam* (Allahabad: Kitāb Mahal, 1943).[^sarit-pvsvt] The text opens with verses of homage to Mañjunātha and an explanation of why a work begins with a verse of salutation, and then proceeds through the autocommentary phrase by phrase.[^sarit-pvsvt]

Karṇakagomin is named in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy only in passing, as one of the commentators, along with Śākyabuddhi, who explicitly connected the discussions of universals and caste in Dharmakīrti's work.[^tillemans2026] The same entry notes that the Sanskrit of Dharmakīrti's autocommentary was edited by Raniero Gnoli in 1960.[^tillemans2026] Little else about the author's dates, the manuscript on which the 1943 edition rests, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation could be verified from the sources consulted, and none is asserted here.
