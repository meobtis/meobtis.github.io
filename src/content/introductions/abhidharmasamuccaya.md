The *Abhidharmasamuccaya* ("Compendium of Abhidharma", Tibetan *Chos mngon pa kun las btus pa*) is a Mahāyāna treatise on Abhidharma, the scholastic analysis of experience, attributed to Asaṅga and associated with the Yogācāra tradition.[^martin2002] The Sanskrit original is only partly preserved: the Sanskrit text in circulation today was reconstructed and edited by Pralhad Pradhan in 1950, using the surviving Sanskrit together with the complete Chinese and Tibetan translations.[^martin2002]

The work opens with verses listing its topics and then takes up the basic categories of Abhidharma, beginning with the aggregates (*skandha*), elements (*dhātu*) and sense-bases (*āyatana*), in a first chapter on the "three dharmas".[^gretil-abhsam] The Chinese translation divides the work into seven chapters, whereas the Tibetan divides it into five.[^gokhale-rare]

Part of a palm-leaf Sanskrit manuscript was reported by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana in the Tibetan monastery of Ṣa-lu near Shigatse. According to Gokhale it comprises seventeen leaves, about 40 per cent of the text, and he dated it on palaeographic grounds to the first half of the eleventh century.[^gokhale-rare] The Chinese translation was made by Xuanzang in 652 CE (Taishō 1605), and the Tibetan by Jinamitra and Śīlendrabodhi with Ye-shes-sde, dated by Gokhale to 814 CE; he found that both follow the Sanskrit closely.[^gokhale-rare] The dating of the Tibetan translators' activity is itself discussed in the scholarship.[^martin2002] Achim Bayer's monograph on the theory of karman in the work includes a critical edition and translation.[^bayer2010] Little else is established in the sources consulted here regarding the date and place of composition.
