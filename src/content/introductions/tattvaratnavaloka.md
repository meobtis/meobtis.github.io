The *Tattvaratnāvaloka* ("Looking at the Jewel of Reality") is a short Sanskrit work in verse. The digital texts present it as a single sequence of twenty numbered stanzas, and the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among its commentarial literature on the Yogottara class of tantras.[^dsbc358] Its closing colophon states that the work is by Paṇḍita Vāgīśvarakīrti; the GRETIL header does not record an author, so the attribution rests on that colophon as printed in the edition.[^gretil-tra][^dsbc358]

After an opening salutation to the teacher, the verses refer to the "fourth initiation" and to the need to understand it through clear wording. They then compare the views of non-Buddhist schools, including the Vedāntins, and of the Śrāvakas and Pratyekabuddhas about the goal to be realized, and contrast these with the Mahāyāna account of a goal gained through the six perfections and the ten stages. The closing stanzas stress that the ultimate cannot be captured by words or by similes such as dream, illusion or mirage.[^dsbc358] This summary is drawn from the text itself. No scholarly study, translation, English title or manuscript description was found, and little reliable information is documented on its date, any Tibetan or Chinese versions, or its reception.
