The *Tārāsragdharāstotra*, also called the *Sragdharāstotra* ("hymn in the sragdharā metre"), is a hymn of thirty-seven verses in praise of the Buddhist goddess Tārā. The GRETIL edition numbers its verses 1 to 37, and the colophon in one of the manuscripts used by Blonay calls it the "Sragdharā hymn of the noble lady Tārā".[^gretil-sragdhara][^blonay1895] No established English title was found, and the work is usually cited by its Sanskrit name.

The poem is written as the personal plea of a suffering devotee who takes refuge in Tārā as the saviour of those in distress. It is composed throughout in the long and demanding sragdharā metre, in an ornate courtly style; Blonay, its first editor, praised it as a skilful poem that places learned diction at the service of fervent devotion.[^blonay1895]

The hymn is attributed to the monk Sarvajñamitra, whom the DSBC edition names as its author.[^dsbc114] According to the account of the Tibetan historian Tāranātha, as summarised by Blonay, Sarvajñamitra was a pupil of Guṇaprabha who lived in Kashmir and studied at Nālandā, and who gave away all his possessions after seeing Tārā; Blonay places him in the seventh to eighth century.[^blonay1895] These are later traditional reports and not independent evidence, so the author's dates are uncertain.

Blonay knew the hymn from Nepalese-tradition manuscripts, one of which also contained a commentary by Jinarakṣita with an introductory life of Sarvajñamitra, and from an earlier, unreliable Calcutta print. He published the text in 1895 as part of his study of the goddess Tārā.[^blonay1895] Tibetan or Chinese translations and modern English translations were not verified in the sources consulted, and little further documentation was found.
