The *Ṣaṭpāramitāstotram* ("Hymn of the Six Perfections") is a very short Sanskrit stotra of six verses. Each verse is devoted to one of the six perfections in turn: giving (*dāna*), ethics (*śīla*), patience (*kṣānti*), effort (*vīrya*), meditation (*dhyāna*) and wisdom (*prajñā*). The verses share a single pattern, in which a Buddha is said to have arisen through the power of the respective perfection and a voice is heard in the city.[^dsbc172]

No established English title was found, and no scholarly discussion of the hymn, its author, date or place of composition, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation could be located in the sources consulted. The text is therefore best described only by what its edition records say. The DSBC lists it among the hymns associated with the Prajñāpāramitā literature, and its romanized version is taken from a modern printed anthology of Buddhist hymns edited by Pandey (1994).[^dsbc172] Nothing further about its transmission or reception is documented here.
