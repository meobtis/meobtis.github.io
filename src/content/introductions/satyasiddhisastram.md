The *Satyasiddhiśāstra* (also known as the *Tattvasiddhi*, and in Chinese as the *Chengshi lun*) is a treatise attributed to Harivarman. The only complete surviving version is the Chinese translation made by Kumārajīva in 411 (Taishō no. 1646), in sixteen fascicles and 202 chapters arranged under the framework of the Four Noble Truths.[^wiki-tattva]

The work criticises the Sarvāstivāda claim that "all exists" and the Pudgalavāda theory of a person, and teaches the emptiness of phenomena, with discussions of the two truths, of mind and mental factors, and of meditative concentration.[^wiki-tattva] Harivarman is dated by Chinese sources to roughly 250–350 CE, and his sectarian affiliation is disputed: he has been called a Dharmaguptaka, Sautrāntika, Dārṣṭāntika, Bahuśrutīya or Mahāyānist, and modern scholars differ.[^wiki-tattva] In China the text gave rise to the Chengshi school, later absorbed into Madhyamaka. An English translation by N. Aiyaswami Sastri appeared in the Gaekwad's Oriental Series in 1978.[^wiki-tattva]

Little further reliable information could be verified for this introduction. Because the Chinese version is the only extant one, the Sanskrit text in the digital editions below should not be assumed to descend from a surviving Sanskrit manuscript; the front matter of the editions does not say how the Sanskrit was produced, and this point should be checked in the printed edition.
