The *Tattvasaṃgraha* (here rendered "The Compendium of Principles"; the Sanskrit title is also glossed "Summary of Reality") is a work by the Indian Buddhist scholar Śāntarakṣita. It is an encyclopaedic verse text of 3,645 stanzas arranged in 26 chapters, each called an "investigation" (*parīkṣā*), and it belongs to the "tenets" (*siddhānta*) genre of surveying and assessing rival philosophical systems.[^wiki-tattvasamgraha][^sep-madhyamaka]

Rather than expounding Buddhist doctrine in isolation, the text is organised chiefly around the examination and refutation of non-Buddhist positions. Early chapters treat the proposed sources of the world, such as the primordial matter of Sāṃkhya and the creator God of Nyāya. Later chapters turn to substance, quality, action, universals and inherence, the meaning of words, perception and inference, Jain epistemology, time, materialism, and scriptural authority. The work also gives space to Buddhist positions such as the Pudgalavāda and to the Yogācāra view that things arise from consciousness.[^wiki-tattvasamgraha][^wiki-shantarakshita][^sep-madhyamaka] Its opening six verses are read by scholars as a kind of table of contents.[^wiki-tattvasamgraha]

Śāntarakṣita is conventionally dated to 725–788 CE, and Tibetan tradition associates him with the founding of Samyé monastery in Tibet.[^wiki-shantarakshita] The Madhyamaka-Yogācāra synthesis for which he is known is set out most clearly in another of his works, the *Madhyamakālaṃkāra*, rather than in the present text.[^sep-madhyamaka] His pupil Kamalaśīla wrote a commentary, the *Tattvasaṃgrahapañjikā*.[^wiki-tattvasamgraha] A Sanskrit manuscript of the root text with this commentary is reported to have been found in 1873 by G. Bühler in a Jain temple at Jaisalmer.[^wiki-shantarakshita] Little else about the work's transmission, including its Tibetan and Chinese translation history, could be verified from the sources consulted here.

Ganganatha Jha's English translation, *The Tattvasangraha of Shantaraksita with the Commentary of Kamalashila*, appeared in two volumes in the Gaekwad's Oriental Series (no. 83) at Baroda, with the date given as 1937 or 1939 in different places on the sources consulted, and was reprinted by Motilal Banarsidass in 1986.[^wiki-shantarakshita][^wiki-tattvasamgraha]
