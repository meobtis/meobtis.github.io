The *Saptākṣarastotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) of eight verses addressed to Avalokiteśvara under the epithet Lokanātha, "protector of the world". It opens with a salutation to Lokanātha, here also called Matsyendra and Viṣṇu, and closes with a colophon calling it the "Saptākṣarastotra" of the venerable noble Avalokiteśvara.[^dsbc135] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon classifies it among the stotras devoted to Avalokiteśvara.[^dsbc135]

The verses praise the deity as a giver of liberation and enlightenment and as a protector from yakṣas, rākṣasas, vetālas and serpents. They also mention Amitābha in the deity's crown and pay homage to the four consorts Locanā, Māmakī, Tārā and Pāṇḍarā.[^dsbc135] Little else about the work is documented in the sources consulted: its author, date, place of composition and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not stated in the edition record, and no scholarly discussion or English title was found, so none is asserted here. The reason for the title (literally "hymn of seven syllables") is likewise not explained in the available material.
