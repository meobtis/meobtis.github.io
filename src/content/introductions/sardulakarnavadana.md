The Śārdūlakarṇāvadāna is an *avadāna*, a narrative of the past and present deeds of Buddhist figures, and it is transmitted as the thirty-third of the thirty-eight stories in the Sanskrit collection known as the *Divyāvadāna*.[^wiki-divy] The GRETIL record presents it as a separate text, "Avadāna 33 of the Divyāvadāna".[^gretil-sardula] Like the other avadānas of the collection, it opens with the usual formula, placing the Buddha at Śrāvastī in Anāthapiṇḍada's park, the Jetavana.[^gretil-sardula]

The opening episode concerns the monk Ānanda, the Buddha's attendant. After his alms round he asks for water at a well from a girl named Prakṛti, who belongs to the Mātaṅga (outcaste) community. She tells him her status and he replies that he is not asking about family or birth, only for water. She falls in love with him and asks her mother, a skilled practitioner of spells, to bring him to her. The mother performs a rite with fire and flowers and recites a charm, and Ānanda is drawn to their house, where he weeps and the Buddha turns his attention to him and counters the spells with his own.[^gretil-sardula] The same story is known in other Buddhist sources, where the girl is usually said to have been led to ordain; the Ānanda article on Wikipedia summarises this tradition, though it does not identify the Śārdūlakarṇāvadāna.[^wiki-ananda] The later part of the Sanskrit text was not read in preparing this note, and little else about the work (its date, authorship, or Tibetan and Chinese versions) could be verified from the sources consulted, so it is not described here.

The Divyāvadāna as a whole has been edited by Cowell and Neil (1886) and by P. L. Vaidya (1959).[^wiki-divy] The edition on which the digital text is based is a separate printing of this single avadāna by Sujitkumar Mukhopadhyaya, published in 1954.[^gretil-sardula]
