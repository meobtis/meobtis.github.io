The *Hetubindu* ("Drop of Logical Reasons") is a short work of Buddhist epistemology and logic by Dharmakīrti. It is counted among the seven treatises usually attributed to him, together with the *Pramāṇavārttika*, *Pramāṇaviniścaya*, *Nyāyabindu*, *Vādanyāya*, *Sambandhaparīkṣā* and *Saṃtānāntarasiddhi*.[^sep-dharmakirti] In the order of composition reconstructed by Frauwallner, it stands after the *Nyāyabindu* and before the *Vādanyāya*.[^sep-dharmakirti] The Sanskrit text opens with a verse stating that the logical reason (*hetu*) is of three kinds only, and the prose that follows takes up the relation of the reason to the property to be proved and the fallacious reasons (*hetvābhāsa*); the Sanskrit text is a reconstruction from the Tibetan, as the edition used for the GRETIL version indicates.[^gretil-hetubindu][^dsbc288]

Dharmakīrti's dates remain debated: scholars have placed him in the sixth or seventh century, with proposals including 600–660 (Frauwallner), the mid-sixth century (Krasser) and 550–610 (Balcerowicz).[^sep-dharmakirti] Little further information about the transmission, translations and commentarial tradition of this particular work could be verified in the sources consulted here, so it is not described further.
