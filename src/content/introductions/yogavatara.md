The *Yogāvatāra* ("Entry into Yoga") is a very short Sanskrit work in nine verses, preceded by an homage to Tārā. Its colophon states that it is the work of the noble Dignāga ("kṛtiriyamāryadignāgapādānām"), and verse 8 is incomplete in the digital text (a gap is marked in the lines). The verses sketch a course of meditation: the practitioner hears the teaching, settles on the ultimate reality, and takes up yoga with faith; recognises that the duality of grasped and grasper is a net of conceptual construction; and comes to see mind as having only its own aspect, culminating in non-apprehension, the knowledge of suchness (*tathatā*), and the cessation realised by insight. The yogin is then said to work for the benefit of beings in many ways, and the text mentions the perfection of wisdom and other practices such as concentration and generosity.[^dsbc486]

Dignāga is the Buddhist logician and epistemologist usually dated to about 470/480 to 530/540 CE, best known for the *Pramāṇasamuccaya*. A general reference work lists a *Yogāvatāra* among his works of a scriptural or religious character, giving a Tibetan title and Derge number (Derge 4074) and describing it as a work on yoga from the mind-only point of view; that page is a tertiary source whose list rests on several scholarly footnotes, which have not been checked individually here.[^wikipedia-dignaga] Little further information about the text, its Tibetan or Chinese translations, manuscripts or printed editions could be established, and the authorship of such a short work is best treated as traditional attribution rather than settled fact.
