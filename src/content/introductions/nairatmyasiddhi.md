The *Nairātmyasiddhi* ("Proof of Selflessness") is a very short Sanskrit prose treatise attributed to Jitāri, a Buddhist philosopher who followed the epistemological school of Dharmakīrti and is generally placed in the later tenth century.[^wiki-jitari] It appears in a list of Jitāri's philosophical works, alongside other short "proofs" (*siddhi*) such as the *Apohasiddhi*, *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi* and *Sarvajñasiddhi*.[^wiki-jitari] Little else about its transmission or reception could be established from the sources consulted.

The text in the SARIT edition occupies roughly a single page. It argues by the logical reason of "non-apprehension of the pervading property": a living body has no connection with a permanent self, because there is no relation, whether identity or causal origination, that could link them. The author then considers and rejects the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika suggestions that the relation might be conjunction or inherence (*samavāya*), arguing that these lead to a regress, and concludes that the reason is neither unestablished, contradictory nor inconclusive.[^sarit-nairatmya] The edition is based on a manuscript, and its footnotes record manuscript readings.[^sarit-nairatmya]
