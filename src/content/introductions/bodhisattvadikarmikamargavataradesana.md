The *Bodhisattvādikarmikamārgāvatāradeśanā* is a short Sanskrit prose text attributed to Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, better known as Atīśa. Its title can be read as an instruction on entering the path for a bodhisattva who is a beginner (*ādikarmika*). The text opens with a salutation to the teacher and a verse of homage, and then proceeds largely in prose.[^gretil-bodhisattvadi]

The work lays out a daily routine for such a practitioner. It describes the person who is disenchanted with saṃsāra and seeks a good spiritual friend, takes refuge, and receives the bodhisattva trainings. It then prescribes rising before dawn, making offerings and prostrations to the Three Jewels, confessing and rejoicing, requesting the teaching, dedicating merit, and generating the awakening mind with a verse of refuge. Further instructions cover meditation on calm and insight, reading Mahāyāna sūtras, and moderation in eating, including dividing a meal into portions for the Three Jewels and teacher, oneself, the destitute and animals.[^gretil-bodhisattvadi]

Little reliable information on this particular text could be found: no study, translation record or catalogue entry that I could read describes its date, its Tibetan translation or its manuscript basis. Its attribution to Atīśa rests on the editions' own attribution. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon identifies its source as a modern printed collection of Atīśa's works edited by Negi,[^dsbc261] and a publisher's listing describes that title as a critical edition of eleven treatises of Atīśa, with the Tibetan text and Sanskrit, edited by Ramesh Chandra Negi at the Central Institute of Higher Tibetan Studies.[^bagchee] The listing does not name the treatises, so it is not confirmed here which of them this is.
