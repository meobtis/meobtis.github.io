*Prasādapratibhodbhava* is a Sanskrit stotra, a hymn of praise, which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files among the hymns addressed to Śākyamuni and marks as a fragment.[^dsbc942] The surviving text is a short run of verses in the anuṣṭubh metre, beginning mid-poem with verse 52 and continuing a few verses further, so the opening and the title's own context are missing.

The verses that survive praise the Buddha's bodily beauty and his qualities. They say that the sight of his form delights the eye of anyone who looks at it, whether for the hundredth time or for the first, and that it gives fresh pleasure at each viewing. They then pass to his virtues (*guṇa*), which the verses present as inseparable from the form that supports them.[^dsbc942]

The available records give no author, date, school or place of composition, and the sources consulted for this introduction do not discuss the work. No English title, Tibetan or Chinese translation, or printed edition could be identified, so none is asserted here. The title, which can be read as "arising from the clarity of faith and inspiration", is a literal reading only and not an established English name.

The two digital records, in romanised and Devanagari script, state no editor, publisher, place or year for the source of the text; little else is documented about it.[^dsbc942][^dsbc941]
