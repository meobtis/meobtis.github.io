The *Mahāpratisarāvidyādhāraṇī* is a Sanskrit dhāraṇī, a protective incantation, addressed to the goddess Mahāpratisarā, whose name means "Great Amulet" or "Great Protectress". The text in this index opens with a homage to the Blessed Lady, the noble Mahāpratisarā, followed by a long run of invocations and mantras ending in repeated *svāhā* formulae.[^gretil-mpv]

The name is best known through the longer sūtra, translated into English as *The Noble Queen of Incantations: The Great Amulet* (Toh 561). That work is one of five protective scriptures known as the Pañcarakṣā, and it prescribes writing its incantation on amulets worn on the body, attached to banners, or placed in stūpas. It promises protection from illness, help in conceiving sons, power over nonhuman spirits, and protection from the results of harmful actions.[^84000toh561] In East Asian and Vajrayāna Buddhism Mahāpratisarā is also venerated as a female deity, sometimes described as an emanation of Ratnasambhava and as the principal figure of the Pañcarakṣā.[^wiki-mahapratisara]

According to the 84000 introduction, the earliest Sanskrit manuscript fragments of the sūtra, from Gilgit, date to about the early seventh century, and Sanskrit incantations on amulets and plates survive from the eighth to eleventh centuries. The Tibetan translation was made in the early ninth century. The Chinese canon contains translations by Ratnacinta (693; Taishō 1154) and by Amoghavajra (Taishō 1153), and a third by Vajrabodhi is reported but lost. Many Sanskrit manuscripts survive, including many copied in Nepal, and the text may have passed through two main recensions before being grouped with the other four Protectresses in the early eighth century.[^84000toh561]

The digital edition is much shorter than the full sūtra: it presents only the dhāraṇī, with its invocations of Mahāpratisarā, homage to the guardians of the directions and to classes of beings such as *nāgas*, *yakṣas* and *gandharvas*, and a closing set of further mantras. How this printed text relates to the manuscript recensions is not documented in the sources consulted, and little further is recorded about it.
