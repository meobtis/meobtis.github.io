The *Gururatnatrayastotram* ("hymn to the guru and the Three Jewels") is a short Sanskrit stotra preserved in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the heading of hymns to the Three Jewels (*triratna*).[^dsbc199] The text consists of seven verses. Verses 1–2 are closed by the colophon-line *iti buddharatnastotram*, verses 3–4 by *iti dharmaratnastotram*, and verses 5–6 by *iti saṃgharatnastotram*, so the hymn is arranged as one section for each of the Jewels. A final verse declares that the guru is the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha alike, and pays homage to the "glorious Vajradhara" guru; the text then ends with a colophon naming it the *Gururatnatrayastotra*.[^dsbc199]

Very little about the work is documented in the sources available for this entry. No author, date, place of composition or Tibetan or Chinese translation could be established, although the final verse's mention of Vajradhara may point to a Vajrayāna setting. The hymn is known here only from the edition in Pandey's anthology of Buddhist hymns as digitised by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon.[^dsbc199]
