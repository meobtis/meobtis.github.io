The *Sāratamākhyā pañjikā*, usually called the *Sāratamā*, is a commentary (*pañjikā*) on the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā Prajñāpāramitā*, the "Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines". The opening of the digital text attributes it to Ratnākaraśānti.[^dsbc281] It is organised by the chapters of the sūtra it explains, beginning with the first chapter, on the practice of omniscience (*Sarvākārajñatācaryāparivarta*).

Ratnākaraśānti is dated to the late tenth and mid-eleventh century, and is associated with several commentaries on Perfection of Wisdom literature.[^wiki-ratna] Jaini describes the *Sāratamā* as an eleventh-century work, and, together with Haribhadra's ninth-century *Ālokā*, as one of only two commentaries on the *Aṣṭasāhasrikā* that survive in the original Sanskrit.[^jaini1972] Jaini's article compares the two commentaries; the details of that comparison are not reproduced here, since only a short extract of the article was available.

The Sanskrit text rests on two incomplete manuscripts photographed by Rāhula Sāṃkṛtyāyana in 1937 and held in the library of the Bihar Research Society.[^jaini1972] Jaini's edition, published in Patna in 1979, is the printed source of the digital text.[^dsbc281] Little further about its reception or its Tibetan and Chinese transmission could be verified in the sources consulted.
