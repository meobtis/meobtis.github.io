The *Karmavibhaṅgopadeśa* ("Instruction on the Analysis of Karma") is a short Buddhist Sanskrit work in prose and verse. It is known from the edition of P. L. Vaidya in the *Mahāyāna-sūtra-saṃgraha*, Part 1, where it is numbered as item 212 of the volume, and it opens with a verse of homage to the Buddha.[^gretil-karmavibhanga] The digital texts do not name an author, and the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the Yogācāra section of its śāstra division.[^dsbc330]

The text takes up questions of karma and its results. It treats the ten benefits of ordaining, living in the forest and going on alms rounds, and the ten benefits of honouring the Tathāgata's shrine with salutations, incense, flowers and parasols. It cites a sūtra from the *Ekottarika* collection and a verse on the inconceivability of the Buddha and of the result of faith in him, and it argues against those who deny that offerings made at the stūpa of a Buddha who has passed into nirvāṇa bear fruit.[^gretil-karmavibhanga]

Little reliable secondary literature on this particular work could be consulted for this introduction, so its date, authorship, relation to other texts on the analysis of karma, and any Tibetan or Chinese counterparts are not stated here. The details above come from the front matter of the digital editions and the opening of the text itself.
