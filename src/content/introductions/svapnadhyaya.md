The *Svapnādhyāya* ("Chapter on Dreams") is a short text in Sanskrit verse on the interpretation of dreams, classified in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under medical science (*cikitsāvidyā*). It begins with a salutation, "namo buddhāya", and then announces in its first verse that the author will expound a teaching on dreams "composed by Bṛhaspati", by which a person may know the good and bad indicated in dreams.[^dsbc770]

The opening verses go on to relate the time of a dream to when its result will appear: a dream in the first watch of the night is said to bear fruit after a year, one in the second watch after eight months, and so on, while a dream at dawn is said to bear fruit within ten days.[^dsbc770] The text thus belongs to the wider Indian literature of dream omens, though the opening dedication to the Buddha shows that this version was transmitted in a Buddhist setting.

Little reliable information about the work could be found in the sources consulted. Its date, its relation to other Sanskrit dream-omen texts and the origin of the attribution to Bṛhaspati are not documented in the edition record, and no English title or translation was identified. The digital text is recorded as taken from Ernst Steinkellner's *Sanskrit Manuscripts in China*, a 2009 publication of the China Tibetology Publishing House in Beijing, which suggests that it comes from a manuscript preserved in China.[^dsbc770]
