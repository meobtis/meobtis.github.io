The *Ālokamālā* ("Garland of Light") is a Sanskrit treatise in verse which opens with a salutation to the Buddha, "who taught mind-only" (*cittamātratā*), and then sets out a short, systematic account of how bondage and liberation arise from the activity of mind. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the Yogācāra division of its treatise (*śāstra*) section.[^dsbc910]

The opening verses summarise the approach: defilement of the mind by desire and similar afflictions is saṃsāra, and freedom from it is liberation; desire, hatred and ignorance, and the phenomena imagined by the foolish, are said to be only constructions of one's own mind.[^dsbc910] An English translation, *Garland of Light: Kambala's Alokamala* by Christian Lindtner, was published by Asian Humanities Press; its publisher's description presents the work as holding that the perfected and dependent natures are real but cannot be expressed positively, while aspects belong only to the constructed nature.[^namse]

The colophon is reported to name Kambala as author, and Lindtner assigned him to a period he placed between about 450 and 525 CE, a dating that should be treated as his own proposal.[^tola] Lindtner's critical edition of the Sanskrit, together with the Tibetan translation (*Snang ba'i phreng ba zhes bya ba'i rab tu byed pa*), appeared in the volume *Miscellanea Buddhica* (Indiske Studier V, 1985).[^tola] A later review essay judged that, despite philological shortcomings, the edition remains an important contribution to the study of Mahāyāna philosophy.[^scherer2006] Little further about the work's transmission is documented in the sources consulted.
