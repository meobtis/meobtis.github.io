The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") with the qualifier *yaśodharākṛtam*, "made by Yaśodharā", is a short Sanskrit stotra in praise of the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha. It consists of eighteen numbered verses and closes with a colophon calling it the "Śrī Śākyasiṃhastotra composed by Yaśodharā".[^dsbc186]

Judging from the verses, the hymn is framed as the words of the Buddha's wife Yaśodharā to their son Rāhula, whom she addresses as "son" (*suta*) and urges to bow to, and be shown, his father. The verses recall episodes of the Buddha's life in sequence: his renunciation of a large harem, the seven treasures and his kingdom; his birth at Lumbinī; his schooling under Viśvāmitra; the five ascetics; his departure on a horse with Chandaka and the sending back of the horse; six years of austerities by the Nairañjanā; his victory over Māra beneath the bodhi tree; and the turning of the wheel of the Dharma at the deer park near Kāśī (Vārāṇasī). The final verse says that Rāhula, having heard his mother's words, was astonished, bowed to his father and rejoiced.[^dsbc186]

Little else about the work appears to be documented in the sources available for this introduction. No reliable information was found on its date, on its authorship beyond the attribution in the title (which is best read as a literary convention rather than a historical claim), or on any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The catalogue also lists similarly titled hymns with other qualifiers, which are separate entries.
