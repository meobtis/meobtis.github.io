The Mahāsaṃnipātaratnaketudhāraṇīsūtra, known in English as the Ratnaketu Dhāraṇī (Tibetan Kangyur Toh 138), is a Mahāyāna sūtra that mixes genres, above all dhāraṇī (incantation) and vyākaraṇa (prophecy).[^84000toh138] It is also transmitted as the *Ratnaketuparivarta*, a chapter or section of the larger Mahāsaṃnipāta, the "Great Collection" of Mahāyāna sūtras.[^84000toh138]

Its core narrative concerns Māra's attempt to destroy the Buddha, break up the Saṅgha and annihilate the Dharma, an attempt the Buddha defeats. The first chapter, "The Intimidation of Māra", is set in Rājagṛha and includes the conversion of Upatiṣya (Śāriputra) and Kolita (Maudgalyāyana), Māra's efforts to stop them, the conversion of his daughters and the failure of his armies. The sūtra also contains past-life stories.[^84000toh138]

The 84000 introduction describes the work as probably combining at least two or three originally independent texts, among them the Ratnaketu material, a section on bringing an end to rebirth, and the story of the yakṣa Āṭavaka, before a final chapter presents the whole as a single discourse. It situates the text within the early core of the Mahāsaṃnipāta, which Jens Braarvig dates to the early centuries CE.[^84000toh138] The Sanskrit text survives incompletely; the English translation draws on the surviving Sanskrit and fills the gaps from the Tibetan.[^84000toh138]

The Tibetan translation names the Indian preceptor Śīlendrabodhi and the translator Yeshé Dé in its colophon, and the text is listed in the Denkarma catalogue (dated 812 CE), so it was translated no later than the early ninth century. Manuscript copies of the Tibetan also survive from Dunhuang, and the introduction notes two Chinese translations.[^84000toh138] The digital text here covers only the chapters printed in Dutt's Gilgit edition that were keyed, namely chapters 1–6 and 10–11.[^gretil-rkp]
