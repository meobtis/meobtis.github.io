The *Nyāyabinduprakaraṇa* ("Drop of Reasoning", also rendered "Drop of Logic") is one of the seven works traditionally attributed to the Buddhist philosopher Dharmakīrti. Frauwallner's proposed order of composition places it third, after the *Pramāṇavārttika* and the *Pramāṇaviniścaya*.[^sep-dharmakirti] Its perception chapter, like that of the *Pramāṇaviniścaya*, is among the places where Dharmakīrti explains yogic perception.[^sep-dharmakirti] Stcherbatsky's *Buddhist Logic* (1930–1932) is a study and translation of the work.[^sep-dharmakirti]

The text presented here is not the root treatise alone. It is an edition that combines the *Nyāyabindu* with two Sanskrit commentaries: the *Nyāyabinduvistaraṭīkā* (also called *Śiṣyahitā*), ascribed in the text to Ārya Vinītadeva, and the *Nyāyabinduṭīkā* of Dharmottara. The edition's text is divided into chapters, beginning with the first chapter on perception (*pratyakṣa*).[^gretil-nyayabindu] Dharmottara is also named in the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy among the later commentators on Dharmakīrti's thought.[^sep-dharmakirti]

Little further reliable information on the dating, translations and manuscript history of these two commentaries could be consulted in preparing this note, so none is given here.
