The *Mahāmaṇivipulavimānaviśvasupratiṣṭhitaguhyaparamarahasyakalparājadhāraṇī* is a short ritual dhāraṇī text known from the Gilgit manuscripts, a collection of Buddhist Sanskrit birch-bark manuscripts found in Gilgit in 1931 and generally dated to about the fifth to sixth centuries CE.[^wiki-gilgit] The GRETIL text transcribes folios 53r to 54r of the manuscript (marked GBM 1724 to 1726), so it is a fragment of a larger work rather than a complete text.[^gretil-mmv]

In the surviving lines the text prescribes a ritual: many vessels and offerings (oil, ghee, sweets and cakes) are to be prepared, a maṇḍala is laid out, and the practitioner is led in through the entrance and consecrated with a mantra beginning "oṃ maṇi vipula supratiṣṭhita siddha". The text then lists the benefits of the empowerment, including the purification of obstructions of past lives and progress towards non-regression on the path to awakening, and it closes with a colophon calling the passage the third chapter (*paṭala*) on the maṇḍala rite.[^gretil-mmv] The edition on which the digital text rests was published under a title describing the manuscript text as one on esoteric iconography.[^gretil-mmv] Little else about the work's date, authorship, translations or reception could be found in sources consulted, and none is claimed here.
