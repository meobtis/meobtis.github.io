The *Tathāgatabimbakārāpaṇasūtra* is a short Buddhist sūtra in Sanskrit prose interspersed with verses, known only from fragmentary leaves among the Gilgit manuscripts. The digital edition is based on an edition by Adelheid Mette of two small fragments from Gilgit, and the surviving text begins and ends mid-passage, with several damaged or uncertain readings.[^gretil-tbk] The Gilgit manuscripts are a collection of Buddhist texts on birch bark, written in Śāradā script and found near Gilgit in 1931; the core manuscripts are generally dated to about the fifth to sixth centuries CE.[^wiki-gilgit]

In the passages that survive, the Buddha tells his monks of the merit of making Buddha images. The prince Suvarṇaprabha is shown to have commissioned an image of the past Buddha Vipaśyin, and the sūtra traces the fruits of that act through many rebirths, as a universal monarch, as lord of the gods and as a Brahmā. It then encourages the making of images in many materials, such as clay, stone, wood, gold or crystal, and of any size, and lists the benefits of bathing and honouring them. Verses on the bathing of a Buddha image close the surviving portion.[^gretil-tbk] Little else about the work, such as its author, date of composition or any Tibetan or Chinese counterpart, is documented in the sources consulted here, so none is asserted.
