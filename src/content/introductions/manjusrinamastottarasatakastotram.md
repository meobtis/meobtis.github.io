The *(Ārya)mañjuśrīnāmāṣṭottaraśatakastotram* is a brief Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī. The title means roughly "hymn of the one hundred and eight names of (the noble) Mañjuśrī". The text in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon opens with a verse of homage to the sage (*muni*) and a promise to recite the names proclaimed by the fully awakened ones, and it runs to nineteen numbered verses, ending with a colophon that gives the full title.[^dsbc160] No established English title was found in the sources consulted; it may be rendered "Hymn of the One Hundred and Eight Names of Mañjuśrī".

The verses are mostly strings of epithets addressed to Mañjuśrī in the second or third person. They praise him as beautiful in form, as inconceivable, as one whose nature is imbued with emptiness (*śūnyatā*), as omniscient, lotus-eyed, a "child of the victors", a guide and teacher of the four truths, and a wish-fulfilling jewel for beings. The hymn also identifies him with the sun, moon and planets and with deities such as Viṣṇu and Maheśvara, and the last verse is an homage to the "great knowledge" (*mahāvidya*) honoured by all beings.[^dsbc160] Mañjuśrī is a bodhisattva of Mahāyāna Buddhism who embodies *prajñā*, transcendent wisdom, and is commonly depicted as a youth holding a sword of wisdom and a Prajñāpāramitā scripture.[^wiki-manjusri]

Little is documented about this particular hymn. The sources consulted gave no reliable information about its author, date, place of composition, manuscripts or translations, and the edition's front matter does not say. It should not be confused with the better-known *Mañjuśrīnāmasaṃgīti*, a different and much longer work; whether the two are related is not established here.[^wiki-manjusri]
