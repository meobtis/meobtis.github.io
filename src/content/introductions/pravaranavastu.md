The *Pravāraṇāvastu* is a section (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, the part of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya that deals with monastic procedure. It concerns the *pravāraṇā*, the ceremony at the end of the rains retreat in which monks invite one another to point out offences they have seen, heard or suspected.[^gretil-prava] The digital text is edited from Sanskrit manuscripts, and several of its opening sections are marked in the edition as lost in Sanskrit.[^gretil-prava]

The preserved text gives formal procedures. It describes how a monk is appointed as the one who conducts the ceremony (*pravārakaḥ*), using a formal motion (*jñapti*) put to the community, and it gives the wording of the invitation on the fifteenth day of the fortnight. It also distinguishes four kinds of ceremony according to whether the act is done lawfully or unlawfully and by a united or divided community, and it records the Buddha's rulings for a monk who gives his *pravāraṇā* by proxy.[^gretil-prava]

Little else about this chapter could be verified from the sources consulted, so its date, its Tibetan and Chinese counterparts and the scholarly discussion of it are not described here. The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya as a whole survives in a Tibetan translation of the ninth century and a Chinese translation of the eighth century, and in part in the original Sanskrit.[^wiki-msv] The edition record identifies the Gilgit manuscript (Dutt 1950) and fragments from the Turfan finds as the Sanskrit sources, and cites Klaus Wille's study of the manuscript transmission of the Vinayavastu.[^gretil-prava]
