The *Lokātītastava* ("Hymn to the One Who Has Transcended the World") is a short Sanskrit hymn in verse addressed to the Buddha, whom the opening verse salutes as "lokātīta", the one beyond the world. It is attributed to Nāgārjuna, and is listed as the first of the four hymns that make up the *Catuḥstava* ("Four Hymns"), the others being the *Niraupamya-stava*, the *Acintya-stava* and the *Paramārtha-stava*.[^wiki-nagarjuna] Tola and Dragonetti published a study of the *Catuḥstava* in the *Journal of Indian Philosophy* in 1985.[^tola1985]

From the verses themselves, the hymn praises the Buddha's compassion in labouring for the welfare of beings, while holding that no self or being exists apart from the aggregates (*skandha*). It presents the aggregates as like illusions, mirages, dream-cities of the gandharvas and dreams, and goes on to take up the aggregates of form, feeling and perception in turn, in the manner of a Madhyamaka argument on dependent origination put into praise.[^gretil-lokatita]

Little further is documented in the sources consulted here. The *Catuḥstava* is included in some modern lists of Nāgārjuna's authentic works but not in others, so the attribution is debated, and the sources consulted give no date for the hymn itself beyond the conventional dating of Nāgārjuna to about 150 to 250 CE.[^wiki-nagarjuna] Tibetan and Chinese translations and manuscript details were not checked, and the account above should be read with that limit in mind.
