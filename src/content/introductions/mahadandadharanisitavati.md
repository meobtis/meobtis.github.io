The *Mahādaṇḍadhāraṇīśītavatī* is a short Buddhist scripture of the protective (apotropaic) type, centred on a dhāraṇī, or spell, called the *Mahādaṇḍadhāraṇī* ("great staff dhāraṇī"). The text opens in the usual way ("Thus have I heard") with the Buddha staying at Rājagṛha, in the great charnel ground of Śītavana. There the monk Rāhula is tormented by a long list of malevolent beings, including *graha* spirits of gods, nāgas, yakṣas, rākṣasas, pretas and piśācas, as well as animals such as crows, owls and insects. Rāhula weeps before the Buddha, who then teaches him the dhāraṇī for the protection and safety of the four assemblies of monks, nuns, laymen and laywomen; the dhāraṇī itself follows as a series of mantric words.[^gretil-mdd]

Little reliable information on the work could be gathered beyond its edition. The GRETIL record names no author, gives no date, and describes the text as a Buddhist apotropaic scripture edited by Gergely Hidas from the manuscript tradition; its header does not describe the manuscripts used.[^gretil-mdd] Its Tibetan and Chinese translations, if any, and its place within the wider *dhāraṇī* literature are not documented in the sources consulted here, and the reader should consult Hidas's edition for the textual history.
