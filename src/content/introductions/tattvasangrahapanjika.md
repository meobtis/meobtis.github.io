The *Tattvasaṅgrahapañjikā* is the commentary of Kamalaśīla on the *Tattvasaṅgraha* ("Compendium of Principles") of his teacher Śāntarakṣita. The root text is a large work of over 3,600 verses in 26 chapters, which surveys the main Indian philosophical views of its time and is organised chiefly around the refutation of non-Buddhist positions, including those of Sāṅkhya, Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, Jainism, the Cārvākas and Mīmāṃsā.[^wiki-shantarakshita] I found no established English title for the commentary itself; Ganganath Jha's English translation of the root text bears the title *The Tattvasangraha of Shantaraksita with the Commentary of Kamalashila* (Baroda, 1939).[^wiki-shantarakshita]

Śāntarakṣita is dated to about 725–788 CE and Kamalaśīla, his pupil, to about 740–795 CE.[^wiki-shantarakshita][^wiki-kamalasila] A Sanskrit manuscript of the work together with Kamalaśīla's commentary is reported to have been found at Jaisalmer in 1873, but the source I consulted gives no reference for this, and little else about the commentary's textual history could be verified from the sources available.[^wiki-shantarakshita] The edition on which this digital text is based is the Baroda edition prepared by Embar Krishnamacharya in 1926.[^sarit-panjika]
