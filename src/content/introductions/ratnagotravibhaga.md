The *Ratnagotravibhāga*, also known as the *Mahāyānottaratantraśāstra* ("Treatise on the Highest Continuum of the Great Vehicle"), is a Mahāyāna treatise on the buddha-nature (*tathāgatagarbha*) doctrine. It is transmitted in Sanskrit as a combination of verses and a prose commentary (*vyākhyā*), and the opening of the text presents seven "vajra topics": the Buddha, the Dharma, the Saṅgha, the element (*dhātu*), awakening, the qualities and the buddha-activity.[^gretil-rgv][^wiki-rgv]

Modern scholars generally regard the work as a compilation of basic verses, further verses and prose commentary rather than a single composition. One analysis, by Takasaki, separates a small core of verses from a larger body of supplementary verses, although this view has been disputed.[^wiki-rgv]

Its authorship is uncertain. Tibetan tradition holds that Maitreya composed the verses and Asaṅga the commentary, whereas Chinese sources attribute the work to Sāramati, an attribution that modern scholarship has often favoured. The Sanskrit text is thought to have been composed between the mid-third century and 433 CE at the latest.[^wiki-rgv]

The Sanskrit text survives in manuscripts found in Tibet by Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana, on which E. H. Johnston based his critical edition of 1950. In Chinese, Ratnamati translated it at Luoyang in 511 CE (Taishō 1611), and in Tibetan the root text and commentary (Tohoku 4024 and 4025) were translated in Kashmir in the late eleventh century. Takasaki's study of 1966 and Brunnhölzl's translation of 2015 are the complete English renderings that include the commentary.[^wiki-rgv]
