The *Viśvaprakāśa* is a Sanskrit lexicon (*kośa*) in verse, attributed in its opening lines to a learned author called Śrī Maheśvara. The digital text opens with an obeisance to the perfectly awakened Buddha, followed by verses in praise of Sarasvatī and of the author's teacher, and then mentions King Sāhasāṅka and the physician Haricandra's commentary on the *Caraka*, which situates the author in a courtly, literary and medical milieu rather than in a specifically Buddhist one.[^dsbc815] After the invocations the text proceeds through verses that list words with their several meanings, grouped by their final syllables.[^dsbc815]

Little reliable information on the work could be gathered for this introduction: no scholarly study, translation or established English title was consulted, and the date and exact identity of the author are not documented in the sources read. The Internet Archive record of a printed edition (Chowkhamba Sanskrit Series, Benares, 1911) lists the keywords Sāhasāṅka, Amarakośa and Medinīkośa, which suggests the work belongs to the same lexicographical tradition, but this is only a cataloguer's indication.[^ia-visva] The DSBC file is a single-chapter text of modest length that breaks off within the first few hundred verses, so it should be treated as a partial witness rather than a complete edition.[^dsbc815]
