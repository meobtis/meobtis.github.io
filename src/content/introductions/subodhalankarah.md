The *Subodhālaṅkāra* ("Easy Ornament" or "Ornament of Easy Understanding") is a Pali work on poetics, the study of literary ornaments (*alaṅkāra*). Surveys of Pali literature attribute it to Saṅgharakkhita, a grammarian placed in the twelfth century, who is also credited with a work on Pali metre, the *Vuttodaya*, and one on syntax, the *Sambandhacintā*.[^wiki-pali] The same survey notes that the Sixth Buddhist Council classification lists the *Subodhālaṅkārapāṭha* and a commentary on it among the grammatical treatises (*byākaraṇa*).[^wiki-pali] That is the extent of what could be verified here, and the dating and attribution rest on a tertiary source.

The digital text opens with a Pali invocation and verses, each followed by a Sanskrit rendering (*chāyā*). The opening verses name earlier ornament-theorists and say that the author composes the work for Pali (Māgadhī) readers, who were not served by those earlier treatises. The text is headed "the first part" of a Buddhist treatise on rhetoric and names Saṃgharakṣita as author.[^dsbc103] Little further documentation, such as translations, manuscripts or critical editions, was located, so the work's wider reception is not described here.
