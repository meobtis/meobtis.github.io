The *Jātakastava* ("Hymn on the Birth Stories") is a short Sanskrit devotional poem (*stotra*) attributed to an author named Jñānayaśas. In the digital edition it consists of twenty stanzas of four verse-quarters each, with the first stanza marked as being in the Śārdūlavikrīḍita metre.[^gretil-jatakastava] The title indicates that the praise of the Buddha is organised around his deeds as a bodhisattva in earlier lives. The opening stanzas, for example, contrast the poet's limited speech with the Buddha's great qualities, and one stanza alludes to the well-known episode in which the bodhisattva gives up his body to a starving tigress.[^gretil-jatakastava]

Very little about the hymn is documented in the sources consulted for this introduction. No English title, date, or place of composition could be established, and nothing is stated here about the identity of Jñānayaśas beyond the attribution recorded in the edition's front matter. Information on any Tibetan or Chinese translation, on manuscripts, or on commentaries was not found, and no claims about them are made. The text is known to the digital corpus through Shackleton Bailey's 1954 edition, which remains the printed basis of the available digital text.[^gretil-jatakastava]
