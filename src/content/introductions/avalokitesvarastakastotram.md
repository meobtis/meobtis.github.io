The *Avalokiteśvarāṣṭakastotram* ("Hymn of Eight Verses to Avalokiteśvara") is a brief Sanskrit praise-poem addressed to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. In the text as digitised it opens with a salutation to Avalokiteśvara, followed by eight verses of praise and a ninth verse promising that those who recite the eight verses daily will obtain wealth, sons and liberation and be freed from diseases such as leprosy; a colophon calls it the "set of eight verses" (*ślokāṣṭaka*) of the noble Avalokiteśvara.[^dsbc121] The verses address the bodhisattva as Lokeśvara and Lokanātha, praise his compassion and his body marked with the thirty-two signs, describe him adorned with ornaments and a crescent moon in his matted hair, surrounded by gods, spirits and other beings, and declare that his qualities are as inexhaustible as the ocean.[^dsbc121]

Avalokiteśvara, also known as Lokeśvara and, in Tibetan, Chenrezig, is the bodhisattva associated with great compassion (*mahākaruṇā*) and is depicted in a great many forms across Buddhist traditions.[^wiki-avalokitesvara] Little is documented about this particular hymn: the edition's record names no author, date or place of composition, and no study or translation of the work was found in the research for this introduction, so its authorship, age and relation to other Avalokiteśvara stotras remain unknown.
