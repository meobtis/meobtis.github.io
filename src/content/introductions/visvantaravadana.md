The *Viśvantarāvadāna* is a Sanskrit Buddhist legend (*avadāna*) about Prince Viśvantara, who in Pali tradition is called Vessantara. The story belongs to the best-known account of the Buddha's former lives: the prince gives away a rain-bringing elephant, is banished, then gives away his horses and chariot, and finally his children and wife, and the family is eventually reunited and restored.[^wiki-vessantara] In Tibetan and Chinese tradition the story is known under other titles and with the prince under other names (Arthasiddhi in Tibetan, Sudana in Chinese).[^wiki-vessantara] Wikipedia is used here only as a general lead for the outline of the tale.

Little reliable information on this particular Sanskrit text could be found beyond its edition record. It is preserved in a damaged manuscript: the edition's concordance lists leaves with gaps, such as a lost folio 9, and gives the corresponding pages of the Gilgit Buddhist Manuscripts facsimile.[^gretil-visvantara] Its date, authorship and relation to other versions of the tale were not established from the sources consulted, and are left open here.
