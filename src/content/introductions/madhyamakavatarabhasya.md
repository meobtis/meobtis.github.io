The *Madhyamakāvatārabhāṣya* is the autocommentary that Candrakīrti wrote on his own *Madhyamakāvatāra* ("Introduction to the Middle Way"), a work presenting the Madhyamaka philosophy of Nāgārjuna.[^wiki-candrakirti] Candrakīrti is placed in roughly the seventh century and is associated with the monastery of Nālandā; the *Madhyamakāvatāra* and its commentary became the principal Madhyamaka sourcebook in most Tibetan monastic colleges.[^wiki-candrakirti] The text opens with homage and a discussion of the verses on compassion, the non-dual understanding and the awakening mind as the causes of the bodhisattvas.[^dsbc887]

Little detailed information beyond this could be verified. The first Tibetan translation of the root text and the commentary is reported to have been made by Naktso Lotsawa, a student of Atiśa, and a commentary on the root text by the Kashmiri scholar Jayānanda is also known.[^wiki-candrakirti] An English translation of the *Madhyamakāvatāra* with the *bhāṣya* and extracts from Jayānanda's commentary by Fredrik Liland (2020) is listed in the literature.[^wiki-mav] The digital edition here covers chapters 1 to 5 of the Sanskrit text only, as recorded in the edition's bibliographic details.[^dsbc887]
