The *Amoghapāśahṛdaya* ("Heart of Amoghapāśa") is a short Sanskrit Mahāyāna sūtra of the dhāraṇī type. In the opening lines of the GRETIL text the Buddha is staying on Mount Potalaka, in the dwelling of Avalokiteśvara, when the bodhisattva rises and announces a "heart" (*hṛdaya*) named Amoghapāśarāja, which he says he received in a distant past age from a buddha, and which he offers for the benefit of beings.[^gretil-amogha]

The text then describes what recitation of this heart-mantra is said to achieve: the purification of grave misdeeds, relief from a long list of fevers, pains and afflictions, protection by deities where the text circulates, and a set of twenty benefits for those who fast on the bright eighth day of the lunar month and recite it seven times.[^gretil-amogha] Its subject is the bodhisattva form of Avalokiteśvara known as Amoghapāśa, "Unfailing Noose".

The work is closely tied to a much larger compendium, the *Amoghapāśakalparāja* ("Sovereign Ritual of Amoghapāśa"; Toh 686), which has been translated into English by Wiesiek Mical for 84000. Its first part also exists as a separate, slightly different text in the Tibetan canon (Toh 682, *Āryāmoghapāśahṛdaya-nāma-mahāyāna-sūtra*), which suggests that it may once have circulated independently. The same introduction reports that Chinese translations place part 1 in the second half of the sixth century CE, and the rest of the compendium about a century later; only one complete Sanskrit manuscript of the larger work is known.[^84000toh686] Little is documented in accessible sources about the short sūtra as an independent work.
