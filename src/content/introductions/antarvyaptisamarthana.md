The *Antarvyāptisamarthana* is a short Sanskrit treatise on Buddhist logic and epistemology (*pramāṇa*). The title can be rendered roughly as a "justification" or "defence" of internal pervasion (*antarvyāpti*); no established English title is in use. The work is attributed to the Buddhist scholar Ratnākaraśānti, and a reference work describes it as a technical treatise on formal logic within the theory of valid cognition.[^academia-ratnakarasanti]

Internal pervasion is a theory about how the invariable connection between a reason and the property to be proved is established. According to one modern summary, it does not replace the more usual external pervasion (*bahirvyāpti*), which relies on a familiar example, but covers a class of theses to which external pervasion does not apply.[^wayman1996] The opening of the Sanskrit text applies the idea to the Buddhist argument for momentariness: whatever is existent is able to produce effects, and this is pervaded by succession or simultaneity, which are in turn excluded from what is not momentary. The text then asks whether the pervasion is to be grasped in the example or in the subject of the thesis, and discusses the objection that an inference whose pervasion is already known would be pointless.[^gretil-antarvyapti]

Ratnākaraśānti is described as an eleventh-century Buddhist author in one library record, while another source places him in the tenth century, at Vikramaśīla, noting that the evidence for his dates is not firm.[^appliedbuddhism][^academia-ratnakarasanti] Little else about the composition of this particular treatise is documented in the sources consulted.

The text survives in a Sanskrit manuscript held in the National Archives in Kathmandu, which was published in colour facsimile by Yūichi Kajiyama in 1999.[^appliedbuddhism] A catalogue record of Kajiyama's volume lists, besides the Sanskrit, a Tibetan text and an English translation.[^kajiyama1999] An earlier Sanskrit edition was printed by Haraprasad Shastri in *Six Buddhist Nyaya tracts in Sanskrit*, on which the digital texts here rest.[^dsbc285] Alex Wayman also wrote an article on the work in the *Journal of the Asiatic Society* (Calcutta).[^wayman1996]
