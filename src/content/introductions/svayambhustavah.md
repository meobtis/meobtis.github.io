The *Svayaṃbhūstava* ("Praise of Svayaṃbhū") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) of seven verses. It opens with homage to Svayaṃbhū as the form of light and as the cause of the world's deliverance, and then addresses him as the Buddha, the Dharma, the bodhisattva, the monk and the hearer, and also as the Vedic and Hindu deities (Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Maheśa, Indra, Yama and others). The final verse promises that those who recite the hymn will attain the rank of a universal monarch (*cakravartin*) and then liberation. The colophon attributes the hymn to the "four great kings" (*caturmahārāja*).[^dsbc197]

Svayaṃbhū means "self-arisen" or "self-existent", and is the name under which the great stūpa on the hill west of Kathmandu, known as Swayambhunath, is venerated.[^wiki-swayambhunath] The hymn's syncretic address to the deity as the ground of all gods and beings is consistent with such a cult, but little reliable scholarship specifically on this hymn could be found, and its authorship, date and place of composition, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation remain undocumented here. It is known to this index through the printed collection of Buddhist hymns edited by Pandey, from which the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon took its text.[^dsbc197]
