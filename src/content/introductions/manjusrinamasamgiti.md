The *Mañjuśrīnāmasaṃgīti* ("Chanting the Names of Mañjuśrī", also rendered as a litany or concert of names) is a Buddhist tantric text consisting of a litany of epithets of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, the embodiment of wisdom. It is presented as a teaching given by the Buddha to Vajrapāṇi and his wrathful retinue, and is regarded in the tradition as a concise summary of the Buddha's teaching. It is organised in about thirteen chapters of roughly 160 verses, with some prose, and the standard modern study and translation is Alex Wayman's *Chanting the Names of Mañjuśrī*.[^wiki-mns]

Commentaries on the work in the Indian and Tibetan traditions include those of Vilāsavajra and Mañjuśrīmitra, and it is also cited in Kālacakra and Hevajra literature.[^wiki-mns] The Tibetan translation is said to have been made by Rin-chen-bzaṅ-po with the Indian paṇḍita Kamalagupta.[^wiki-mns] I could not consult a scholarly reference work for this introduction, so these details come from a tertiary source and should be checked. The date and place of composition, and its Chinese translation, are not documented in the sources I was able to read.
