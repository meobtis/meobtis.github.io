The *Ajitasenavyākaraṇa* ("Prophecy of Ajitasena"; its full title is given as *Ajitasenavyākaraṇanirdeśa*) is a short sūtra usually classed as Mahāyāna, preserved in Sanskrit in a manuscript found near Gilgit in 1931, which is dated to roughly the sixth or seventh century.[^wiki-ajitasena] The English name "Ajitasena Sūtra" is the one used in encyclopaedic descriptions of the work; no widely used translated title appears to exist.

The sūtra is set in Śrāvastī. In outline, the elder Nandimitra is sent by the Buddha to King Ajitasena of Magadha and instructs him on giving alms to the monastic community. The prince ordains, becomes an arhat and is able to see the buddha-fields, and a poor woman who gives gifts to the monks is prophesied to attain Buddhahood. The text also commends reciting the Buddha's name as a protection from suffering and hell, and ends with promises for those who teach or hear it and warnings for those who condemn it.[^wiki-ajitasena]

Scholars have described it as "proto-Mahāyāna", since it does not clearly separate the Mahāyāna from the Śrāvaka teaching and the word *mahāyāna* does not occur in it, so that it may belong to a period before Mahāyāna had become a distinct self-identifying movement.[^wiki-ajitasena] The date and place of composition are not settled. A Japanese study has examined the lifetime and region of activity of Nandimitra in order to bear on the sūtra's formation.[^hirabayashi2016]

The Sanskrit text was edited from the Gilgit manuscript by Nalinaksha Dutt in the first volume of *Gilgit Manuscripts* (1939; reprinted Delhi 1984), on which all the digital editions here are based.[^gretil-asv][^dsbc23] The sources I could consult do not document translations or later reception in detail, so little more can be said with confidence.
