The *Trisvabhāvanirdeśa* (Treatise on the Three Natures, also rendered "Exposition on the Three Natures") is a short Sanskrit work in verse belonging to the Yogācāra tradition. It is traditionally listed among the works of Vasubandhu, and in the digital editions it opens with a homage to Mañjuśrī and is ascribed to the master Vasubandhu.[^gretil-tsn][^sep-vasubandhu] The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy groups it with the *Thirty Verses* as one of Vasubandhu's concise Yogācāra works.[^sep-vasubandhu]

The verses open by naming the three natures (*svabhāva*): the fabricated (*parikalpita*), the dependent (*paratantra*) and the perfected (*pariniṣpanna*).[^gretil-tsn] Gold's account presents them as three aspects of one reality: how a thing appears, the causal process that produces the appearance, and the absence of the thing as it appears. The text is poetic and does not argue against objections; it illustrates the natures with the magician's illusory elephant, where the elephant stands for the fabricated nature and its appearance for the dependent nature.[^sep-vasubandhu]

Attribution to Vasubandhu is contested. Some scholars doubt it, and others regard the work as late and different from his other writings; Kapstein's article on the question is a principal discussion.[^wiki-vasubandhu][^kapstein2017] Little else about its date and transmission could be verified here, and Tibetan and Chinese translations, commentaries and manuscripts are not discussed in the sources consulted. An English translation with commentary by Jay Garfield is reported to exist.[^wiki-vasubandhu]
