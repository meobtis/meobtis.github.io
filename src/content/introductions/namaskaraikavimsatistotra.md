The *Namaskāraikaviṃśatistotra* is a hymn in which each of twenty-one verses opens with the word *namaḥ* ("homage") addressed to the female bodhisattva Tārā; the Sanskrit text begins "namas tāre ture vīre".[^gretil-nevs] In Tibetan it is known as the Praise to Tārā with Twenty-One Verses of Homage (Toh 438), and in English also as the Twenty-One Praises of Tārā.[^84000toh438]

The praise survives in the Tibetan Kangyur in two forms: as an independent Tibetan translation, and as transliterated Sanskrit within a tantra (Toh 726) in which the Buddha teaches it as a *dhāraṇī*. The relationship between the two is unclear. Some Kangyur colophons say the Tibetan was prepared from the transliterated Sanskrit, whereas the Tibetan master Jetsün Drakpa Gyaltsen (1147–1216) is reported to have held that it was transmitted from India and translated as an independent text.[^84000toh438] No author or date of composition is given in the 84000 introduction, and little else is firmly documented here. The reconstructed Sanskrit title in the Tibetan catalogue entry is longer than the one used for this edition.[^84000toh438]

The 84000 translation was compared with Sanskrit editions, among them de Blonay's of 1895, which is also the printed basis of the digital text described below.[^84000toh438][^gretil-nevs] It is among the most widely used prayers to Tārā in Tibetan Buddhism.[^wiki-praise]
