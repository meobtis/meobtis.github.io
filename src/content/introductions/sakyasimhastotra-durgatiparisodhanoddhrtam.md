The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") with the qualifier *durgatipariśodhanoddhṛtam*, "extracted from the Durgatipariśodhana", is a short Sanskrit stotra in fourteen numbered verses. It begins with the salutation *om namaḥ śākyasiṃhāya* and closes with a colophon calling it the "venerable Śākyasiṃha hymn extracted from the Durgatipariśodhana".[^dsbc181] The qualifier points to the Durgatipariśodhana literature, but the sources consulted for this introduction do not say from which work, or which section of it, the hymn was taken, so that link is not discussed further here.

The verses salute the Buddha as Śākyasiṃha, the turner of the wheel of the teaching, and then pay homage in turn to a series of "uṣṇīṣa" forms (vajra, ratna, padma, and others), before turning to offering goddesses, door guardians, bodhisattvas and the guardians of the directions. The penultimate verse says that a mantrin holding a vajra and bell, standing before the maṇḍala, should recite this "king of hymns", which indicates a ritual use.[^dsbc181] No reliable information was found on its date, authorship, manuscripts, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The catalogue also contains other hymns of the same name with different qualifiers, which are separate entries.
