The *Nairātmyāyā Bhagavatyā Āśīḥstutiḥ* ("Benedictory praise of the Blessed Lady Nairātmyā") is a short Sanskrit hymn of seven verses in long, elaborate metres. It opens with a salutation to the venerable Nairātmā and closes with the colophon stating that the benedictory praise of the Blessed Nairātmyā is complete.[^dsbc929]

Each verse ends with a petition that the goddess protect or bless the audience. The verses describe her wrathful and dancing form, her ornaments and the skull-cup, curved knife and *khaṭvāṅga* she carries, her victory over Māra, and the white lotus and blood-filled vessel in her hands. The seventh verse identifies the three channels of the subtle body with her: the left channel (*lalanā*) with emptiness (*śūnyatā*) and wisdom (*prajñā*), the right (*rasanā*) with compassion (*karuṇā*), and the central (*avadhūtī*) with their non-dual union.[^dsbc929]

Nairātmyā (Tibetan *bdag med ma*), "she who is without self", is a ḍākinī who is paired with Hevajra in the Hevajra Tantra.[^wiki-nairatmya] Beyond the text itself, little reliable information on this particular hymn could be found: no author, date or place of composition, no translations into Tibetan or English, and no manuscript history were located. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the section of commentaries on yoginī tantras (*yoginītantraṭīkā*) within the śāstra division, but the record gives no source edition, so its origin cannot be stated here.[^dsbc929]
