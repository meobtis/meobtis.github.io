The *Abhidharmakośavyākhyā*, also called the *Sphuṭārthā* ("Clear Meaning"), is a Sanskrit commentary by Yaśomitra on Vasubandhu's *Abhidharmakośabhāṣya*, itself a prose commentary on the verses of the *Abhidharmakośa*. The text in this edition is the second volume of a two-volume edition and opens with the fourth chapter (*kośasthāna*), on karma, quoting each verse and then glossing the autocommentary phrase by phrase.[^dsbc872] The opening passages, for example, take up the nature of intention and of bodily and verbal action, and the commentary reports and answers objections such as the Vaiśeṣika view of how mental events and sounds perish.[^dsbc872]

Little reliable biographical information about Yaśomitra appears to be documented. In 2018 Dominik Wujastyk noted that a date of 580 is given in a reference work but that he could not trace its source, and that the standard introductions by La Vallée Poussin, Stcherbatsky and Law say nothing of the author's background.[^wujastyk2018] The commentary matters for another reason: as the editor of one edition explained, the Sanskrit original of Vasubandhu's *Bhāṣya* is lost, so the Sanskrit of the *Vyākhyā* has had to be edited with the help of the Tibetan translation of the *Bhāṣya*.[^law1949]

The Sanskrit text has been edited several times. Law's edition (Calcutta, 1949) was prepared from a manuscript of the Asiatic Society and a rotograph of a Cambridge manuscript, and its editor records that an earlier attempt by Lévi, La Vallée Poussin and Stcherbatsky had published only the first two chapters.[^law1949] Unrai Wogihara's Roman-script edition, published in Tokyo in two parts (chapters 1–3 and 4–8) in 1936 and reprinted since, covers the whole work.[^wogihara1971]
