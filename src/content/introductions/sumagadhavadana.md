The *Sumāgadhāvadāna* is a short Sanskrit prose narrative of the avadāna type, which tells a story of past and present actions and their results, spoken by the Buddha to his monks. The text itself opens with a homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas, sets the scene at Śrāvastī, and closes with the colophon "sumāgadhāvadānaṃ samāptam", which fixes its title.[^gretil-sumagadha] The digital text runs to roughly five thousand words.

In the narrative, Sumāgadhā is the daughter of the wealthy householder Anāthapiṇḍada of Śrāvastī. Vṛṣabhadatta, son of a householder of the city of Puṇḍravardhana and an adherent of the Nirgranthas (Jains), is told of her as a suitable bride. Later parts of the text bring the Buddha, with his monks, to Puṇḍravardhana, where his display of miraculous power wins over the assembled crowd, and he then explains the earlier actions behind the events. The story ends with a general lesson on dark, bright and mixed actions and their corresponding results, and with the monks rejoicing in the Buddha's words.[^gretil-sumagadha]

Little reliable information on the date, provenance, manuscript tradition or translations of this particular Sanskrit version was found in the sources consulted, and none is asserted here. The only edition identified is the printed one on which the digital text is based, and the account above rests on the text and its front matter alone.
