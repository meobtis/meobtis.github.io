The *Vāgīśvaravarṇanāstotra* ("Hymn describing Vāgīśvara") is a very short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*) of six verses, each ending with the same refrain, "vāgīśvaraṃ suraguruṃ satataṃ namāmi" ("I always bow to Vāgīśvara, teacher of the gods"), followed by a colophon. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the *stotra* devoted to Vāgīśvara, "Lord of Speech", a name associated with Mañjuśrī.[^dsbc204]

The verses describe the deity as honoured by Brahmā and other gods, humans and asuras, as a master of knowledge and of the Vedic and other treatises, and as lord of speech and wisdom. The last verses add an iconographic note: four arms holding an arrow and bow, a book and a sword, and bestowing every wish.[^dsbc204] The hymn is thus a devotional description of the deity's attributes rather than a doctrinal exposition.

I could not find reliable published information on the authorship, date or place of composition of the hymn, on any manuscript, or on translations into Tibetan or Chinese, and none is claimed here. The only source located is the DSBC record, which gives the modern anthology from which the text was keyed.[^dsbc204]
