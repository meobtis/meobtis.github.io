The *Kāśyapaparivartasūtra*, "the chapter (or discourse) of Kāśyapa", is a Mahāyāna sūtra preserved in Sanskrit manuscripts and in Tibetan, where the 84000 project renders its title as "The Discourse for Kāśyapa" and places it as Toh 87 in the *Heap of Jewels* (Ratnakūṭa) collection.[^84000toh87] The Sanskrit text opens in the usual way: the Buddha is at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak with eight thousand monks and sixteen thousand bodhisattvas, and he addresses the elder Mahākāśyapa, beginning with four things that cause a bodhisattva's wisdom to decline, such as disrespect for the Dharma and for those who teach it. The teaching is given in alternating prose and verse.[^gretil-kp][^dsbc44]

Little further information could be verified from the sources consulted for this introduction, so the date, the translation history in Tibetan and Chinese, and the contents of the later sections are not described here. The GRETIL record lists the manuscripts underlying the 2002 edition (SI P/2, SI P/85A, Hoernle 143 and Mannerheim 3, and SHT I 374), and an earlier printed edition was published by von Staël-Holstein in 1926.[^gretil-kp][^gretil-kp-alt]
