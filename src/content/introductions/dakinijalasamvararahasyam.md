The *Ḍākinījālasaṃvararahasya* ("the secret of the Ḍākinījālasaṃvara") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist tantric text. Its heading names Anaṅgayogin as its author and it opens with homage to the glorious Vajrayoginī; the opening verse says that the secret is written down by Anaṅgayogin.[^dsbc104] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among commentarial works on the *advayatantra* (non-dual tantra) literature, in a single chapter.[^dsbc104]

From the opening of the text, it presents verses with prose glosses. It distinguishes worldly and supramundane accomplishment (*siddhi*), speaks of four consecrations (*abhiṣeka*), and refers to teachings that are to be learnt from a qualified teacher, often in enigmatic "twilight" language.[^dsbc104] Little reliable scholarship on the work could be consulted when this introduction was written, so its date, the identity of Anaṅgayogin, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not discussed here; no established English title is known to me.

The digital text rests on the 1990 Sarnath edition by Samdong Rinpoche and Vajravallabhi Dwivedi.[^dsbc104]
