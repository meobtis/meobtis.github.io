The *Nālandādahanam* ("The Burning of Nālandā") is a short Sanskrit stage work whose opening lines describe it as a dance-drama (*nṛtyanāṭaka*) composed by a certain Śrī Subrahmaṇya, in which the heroic, furious, fearful and pathetic sentiments predominate, and as one to be performed at the gate of the Nālandā university.[^dsbc272] The text is divided into scenes (*dṛśya*) and is made up largely of sung verses with named melodic frameworks (*rāga*, such as Pūrvakalyāṇī and Ābhogi) and a preliminary benedictory song (*nāndī*) addressed to the goddess Durgā.[^dsbc272]

The first scene shows students and senior monks at the gate of the university, anxious about an approaching army of Turkic soldiers, while the head monk tries to calm them. In the second scene the invaders arrive on horseback, cut down monks and students, and set fire to the library, and the head monk and the librarian-nun lament and plead for the books.[^dsbc272] The subject evidently refers to the destruction of Nālandā; its date and relation to the Buddhist canon are not documented in the sources consulted. Beyond what the text says of itself, no reliable information on its author, date, performance history or printed edition could be found, and no established English title is known.
