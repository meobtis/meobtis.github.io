The *Caryāmelāpakapradīpa* ("Lamp that Integrates the Practices") is a Sanskrit Buddhist tantric work attributed to Āryadeva. It is known in English through Christian K. Wedemeyer's book *Aryadeva's Lamp that Integrates the Practices*, whose subtitle describes the text as an account of the gradual path of Vajrayāna Buddhism according to the "Esoteric Community" (Guhyasamāja) Noble tradition, published in 2007 with a critical edition of the Sanskrit as its third part.[^wikiaryadeva][^sarit-carya] The GRETIL file gives the title in the variant form *Caryāmelāvaṇapradīpa*, and its text is divided into chapters (*paricchedas*) beginning with an invocation of Vajrasattva.[^gretil-carya]

Modern scholarship does not identify the author with the third-century Madhyamaka philosopher Āryadeva. Works of this kind, among them the *Caryāmelāpakapradīpa*, are dated to roughly the late ninth or early tenth century and are placed in a Vajrayāna Madhyamaka tradition that included a later tantric author of the same name; traditional historians such as Tāranātha explained the gap in time by other means, including mystical revelation.[^wikiaryadeva] Beyond this, little detail about the work's contents, its Tibetan translation or its manuscript witnesses could be verified from the sources consulted, and these are best sought in Wedemeyer's study.
