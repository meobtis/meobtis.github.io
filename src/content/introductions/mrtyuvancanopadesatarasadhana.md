The *Mṛtyuvañcanopadeśatārāsādhana* ("Sādhana of Tārā from the instruction on cheating death") is a short Sanskrit text in verse. The digital text consists of sixteen verses, numbered in the file as Mvupts_1 to Mvupts_16, and ends with a colophon naming it a sādhana of Tārā.[^gretil-mvupts] The GRETIL record gives the printed source as Johannes Schneider's edition of Vāgīśvarakīrti's *Mṛtyuvañcanopadeśa*, subtitled "a Buddhist treatise for warding off death", where the sādhana is printed on p. 235.[^gretil-mvupts] The sādhana therefore appears to belong with that larger work, though the sources consulted here do not say how.

According to the text itself, the practitioner rises at dawn and settles in a pleasant place. He visualises a moon disc in his heart and draws down the white, two-armed goddess Tārā, who holds a lotus and makes the gesture of granting boons. He then makes offerings, confesses misdeeds, rejoices in merit, dedicates it and takes refuge. He next meditates on emptiness and generates Tārā in the sky and in his heart, where a white wheel (*cakra*) with eight compartments bears the eight syllables of a mantra. He is to recite a ten-syllable Tārā mantra for six months. The closing verses promise victory over death and also relief from old age, illness and poverty, the dangers of lions and other fears, and worldly attainments (*siddhi*) such as eloquence and wisdom.[^gretil-mvupts]

Little further information was found for this text. Details such as the date and attribution of the sādhana, its place within Vāgīśvarakīrti's work, and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not established by the sources consulted and are left out here.
