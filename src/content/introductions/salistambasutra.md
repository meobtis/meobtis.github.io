The *Śālistambasūtra* is a short Mahāyāna sūtra known in English as "The Rice Seedling". It is framed as an explanation by the bodhisattva Maitreya, at the request of Śāriputra, of a brief teaching the Buddha gave while looking at a rice seedling: that one who sees dependent arising sees the Dharma, and one who sees the Dharma sees the Buddha. It is regarded as one of the most important sūtras on dependent arising (*pratītyasamutpāda*).[^84000toh210]

The text explains the Dharma as the noble eightfold path and the Buddha as the one who awakens to all phenomena, and then treats dependent arising in two parts, outer and inner. The outer kind is illustrated by the growth of a plant from a seed through the conjunction of several elements, the inner kind by the twelve links from ignorance to ageing and death.[^84000toh210] The Sanskrit text printed by Vaidya begins with a Sanskrit passage in which Maitreya answers Śāriputra and goes on to describe the outer sequence as arising from the coming together of the elements earth, water, fire, wind, space and season.[^gretil-salistamba]

The introduction to the 84000 translation gives no date of composition. No complete Sanskrit text is known to survive, but the sūtra is extensively quoted in Sanskrit works such as Yaśomitra's *Abhidharmakośavyākhyā*, Candrakīrti's *Prasannapadā* and Śāntideva's *Śikṣāsamuccaya*, and reconstructions of the Sanskrit have been made by de La Vallée Poussin (1913), Sastri (1950), Gokhale (1961) and Reat (1993), the last with an English translation.[^84000toh210] How the text printed by Vaidya relates to these reconstructions has not been checked here.

A Tibetan translation existed before the late eighth century, and four Chinese translations are recorded (Taishō 709–712), the earliest dating to the Eastern Jin period (317–420 CE); a closely similar sūtra (Taishō 708) was translated earlier by Zhi Qian. Three Indian commentaries survive in Tibetan and Mongolian, one by Kamalaśīla and two attributed to Nāgārjuna.[^84000toh210] The Tibetan version is catalogued as Toh 210, and its English translation is the work of the Dharmasāgara Translation Group.[^84000toh210]
