The *Suvarṇavarṇāvadāna* ("The Story of Suvarṇavarṇa", that is, of one of golden complexion) is a Sanskrit Buddhist narrative of the avadāna type, in which the deeds of a person in past and present lives are told and linked by the Buddha. The Sanskrit text is known from a palm-leaf manuscript from Tibet, which Sitaram Roy deciphered and published in 1971 as an apparently unknown Mahāyāna avadāna text; a later edition and translation, together with the Tibetan version, formed Tissa Rajapatirana's 1974 doctoral thesis.[^gretil-suvarna] No established English title was found in the sources consulted.

The Sanskrit opens after the death of Mahākāśyapa, with Ānanda teaching at Vaiśālī, and then moves to Rājagṛha, where the wealthy merchant Divākara has no child. The god Śakra persuades a deity about to fall from the Trāyastriṃśa heaven to be reborn as his son on the condition that the merchant and his wife take refuge in the Three Jewels, and the child is born with a golden complexion and golden garments, amid showers of cloth and flowers.[^gretil-suvarna] The text is also transmitted in a Tibetan translation, *gSer mdog gi rtogs pa brjod pa*, in the Vinaya section of the Kangyur, which the GRETIL record names as having been consulted for the edition.[^gretil-suvarna]

Little else about the work's date, authorship, school affiliation or reception could be verified from the sources available for this introduction, and none is asserted here.
