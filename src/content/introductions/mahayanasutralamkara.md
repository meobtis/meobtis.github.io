The *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra* ("The Ornament of the Mahāyāna Sūtras") is a Yogācāra treatise in verse that sets out the Mahāyāna path to awakening and introduces the foundational doctrines of the school. The Tibetan tradition counts it among the "Five Treatises of Maitreya", a group of works traditionally attributed to Maitreya.[^szanyi2024] The identity of Maitreya is itself uncertain: traditional sources identify him with the future Buddha, while modern scholarship treats him as a historical figure.[^szanyi2024] The editions on this site divide the work into twenty chapters, each called an *adhikāra*, and the first chapter begins with a salutation to all buddhas and bodhisattvas.[^dsbc333]

The Sanskrit text is transmitted together with a prose commentary (*bhāṣya*). Its authorship is debated: some Buddhist tradition ascribes the verses to Maitreya, who is said to have taught them to Asaṅga, and modern scholars have questioned whether the five "Maitreya" works share a single author. One scholar, Mario D'Amato, is reported to place the verses and commentary after the *Bodhisattvabhūmi* and before Asaṅga's *Mahāyānasaṃgraha*, which quotes the work as authoritative.[^wiki-asanga] The Tibetan canon contains the verses as "The Verses on the Ornament for the Mahāyāna Sūtras" (Toh 4020).[^84000toh4020] Little else about its date, translations and manuscripts could be confirmed from the sources consulted, and further research is needed to describe them reliably.
