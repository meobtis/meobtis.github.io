The *Kaṭhināvadāna* is a short Sanskrit Buddhist text that combines a manual for the offering of the *kaṭhina* with an *avadāna*, an edifying story that links deeds to their karmic results.[^wiki-avadana] Its opening, as it appears in the digital editions, begins with a salutation to the Three Jewels and verses in praise of the Buddha, and then introduces the narrative's occasion. It presents a dialogue in which the monk Mahākāśyapa asks the Buddha when the *kaṭhina* arises, and the answer concerns a thirty-day period beginning in the dark half of the month of Kārttika.[^gretil-kathina] The word *kaṭhina* refers to the cloth given to a monastic community for making a robe; in the Theravāda tradition the associated festival comes at the end of the rains retreat.[^wiki-kathina]

The text goes on to describe how a lay donor should announce the gift to the community through an intermediary, how the cloth is prepared and consecrated, and how the donor may add further offerings. It also gives an explanation, put in the Buddha's mouth in reply to the monk Sumanas, of why the gift is called *kaṭhina*: the word is glossed as "firm", because of the donor's resolve, the purity of the robe, the unbroken community and the lasting fruit of the gift.[^gretil-kathina] The narrative section is set at Śrāvastī in the Jetavana. The text is known to modern scholarship through Almuth Degener's 1990 edition, but the authorship, date and place of composition are not documented in the sources consulted, nor are any Tibetan or Chinese translations, so none are claimed here.[^gretil-kathina]
