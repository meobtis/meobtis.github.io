The *Vajramahākālastotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of Mahākāla, here invoked under the title Kṣetrapāla, "protector of the field". The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon classifies it among the stotras devoted to Mahākāla and records its source as Janardan Shastri Pandey's *Bauddha Stotra Saṃgraha*, published at Varanasi in 1994.[^dsbc159] In the digital text it consists of nine verses and closes with the colophon "śrīvajramahākālastotraṃ samāptam"; the heading ascribes it to Ācārya Nāgārjuna (*ācāryanāgārjunakṛtam*).[^dsbc159]

The verses describe the deity's terrifying form and cries: each opens with strings of seed-syllables and onomatopoeic sounds, and the protector is pictured surrounded by ghosts and *vetāla*s, carrying a skull-staff and a garland, with tawny eyes and hair. Each verse asks him to protect, or to quell obstacles and enemies, and the final verse promises that an *ācārya* or practitioner who recites it will gain long life, fame, strength, peace and omniscience, with obstacles destroyed.[^dsbc159] Beyond what the edition itself states, little reliable information was found on the date, the authorship (the attribution to Nāgārjuna is only the text's own claim and should be treated with caution), or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, so none is asserted here.
