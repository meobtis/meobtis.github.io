The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") is a short Sanskrit stotra, a verse hymn of praise addressed to the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha. The title carries the qualifier *śaṅkarakṛtam*, "made by Śaṅkara", and the closing colophon likewise calls it the hymn made by Śaṅkara. The text consists of seven numbered verses in long, ornate metre.[^dsbc183]

The verses praise the Buddha as a "conqueror" (*jina*) and "lord of the world" whose body shines like a thousand suns, who removes sorrow, disease, old age and calamity and carries beings across the ocean of existence. They allude to his victory over Māra's army at the seat of awakening, his birth in the Śākya clan, and his presence beneath a wish-fulfilling tree on a jewelled throne. The seventh verse says that those who recite the hymn before a Buddha image, described as composed by Hara, prosper and go easily to Sukhāvatī.[^dsbc183]

Little else about the work appears to be documented in the sources available for this introduction. No reliable information was found on the identity of the Śaṅkara named in the title, on its date, or on any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The site's catalogue also lists similarly titled hymns with other qualifiers (such as *navagrahakṛtam* and *brahmaṇā kṛtam*), which are separate entries.
