The *Bhavasaṅkrāntiparikathā* is a very short Sanskrit work in verse attributed to Nāgārjuna. It opens with a salutation to Mañjuśrī as a youth (*kumārabhūta*), and its first stanzas argue that existence does not arise from non-existence or from existence, and that what appears as existence is an illusion comparable to a flower in the sky.[^gretil-bhavasankranti] The surviving Sanskrit text is a reconstruction, and the editions in this index preserve a number of irregular spellings that reflect that state of the text.[^gretil-bhavasankranti]

Little reliable information on the work is documented beyond its catalogue records. The introduction to the 84000 translation of the *Bhavasaṅkrāntisūtra* lists it, as Toh 4162, as a treatise attributed to Nāgārjuna that is distinct from the *Bhavasaṅkrānti* (Toh 3840), and notes that the authorship of these related treatises is uncertain.[^84000toh226] The same introduction records that the sūtra itself has three Chinese translations and a Sanskrit manuscript from the Potala Palace; no source I could verify reports a Chinese version of the *Parikathā* itself.[^84000toh226]
