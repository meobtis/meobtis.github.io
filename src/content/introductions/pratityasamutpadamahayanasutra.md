The *Pratītyasamutpādamahāyānasūtra*, titled in the text itself the *Āryapratītyasamutpādo nāma mahāyānasūtram*, is a very brief Sanskrit sūtra. The Sanskrit text opens with the usual "Thus have I heard" and sets the scene on the Pāṇḍukambala rock among the gods of the Thirty-three, with a large assembly of śrāvakas, bodhisattvas such as Maitreya, Avalokiteśvara and Vajrapāṇi, and deities.[^gretil-pratitya]

Avalokiteśvara asks the Buddha to teach the gods, who wish to hear the Dharma. The Buddha answers by speaking the well-known verse beginning *ye dharmā hetuprabhavā*, which says that the Tathāgata has taught the cause of things that arise from causes, and also their cessation. He then declares dependent origination to be the Dharma body (*dharmakāya*) of the Tathāgatas, and says that whoever sees it sees the Tathāgata. The sūtra also promises merit and a good rebirth to a faithful person who builds a tiny caitya, plants a tiny bodhi tree or raises a tiny parasol and recites the verse.[^gretil-pratitya]

The verse itself, also called the *Pratītyasamutpāda-gāthā*, is known in Sanskrit and Pali and was widely treated as a protective formula. It was inscribed on caityas, stupas and images and placed inside them.[^wiki-gatha] The sūtra thus presents a Mahāyāna framing for this widespread devotional verse. Little reliable scholarship specifically on this short sūtra, including its date, Tibetan or Chinese translations, or manuscripts, could be consulted for this introduction, so these matters are left unstated.
