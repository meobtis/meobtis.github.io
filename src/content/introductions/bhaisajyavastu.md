The *Bhaiṣajyavastu* ("Chapter of Medicines") is one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinaya of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school, the body of rules governing monastic life. The Vinaya survives in Chinese and Tibetan translations, but only part of its Sanskrit original is preserved.[^yao2025] Its English name is not fixed; the description "Chapter of Medicines" is used in the title of a recent edition.[^yao2025]

For several decades the only Sanskrit source for this chapter was a manuscript excavated at Gilgit in the 1930s, which has lost about half of the chapter's text.[^yao2025] Nalinaksha Dutt edited it in the series *Gilgit Manuscripts* (volume 3, part 1), published at Srinagar in the Kashmir Sanskrit Series (no. 71); the language of the text is described there as Prakrit-influenced Sanskrit, with some Tibetan equivalents supplied.[^cinii-gilgit] The text opens with a summary verse (*piṇḍoddāna*) listing the chapter's topics, and then begins its narratives at Śrāvastī, where monks suffer from an autumn illness.[^gretil-bhaisajya]

A second Sanskrit manuscript, identified toward the end of the twentieth century, is written in Gilgit/Bamiyan Type II script and was copied somewhere between present-day Pakistan and eastern Afghanistan in the late seventh or eighth century. It once comprised about 205 birch-bark folios but survives only in fragments; it nevertheless fills gaps in the Gilgit manuscript and helps show which source texts the Chinese and Tibetan translators used.[^yao2025] Fumi Yao's 2025 volume in the Schøyen Collection series edits it, reconstructing 92 incomplete folios from 509 fragments, and presents a reconstructed text alongside the Chinese and Tibetan versions.[^yao2025]

Little more could be verified here about the work's contents, date and authorship; the sources consulted document chiefly its manuscripts and editions.
