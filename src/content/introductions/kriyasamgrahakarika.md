The *Kriyāsaṃgrahakārikā* ("Verses summarising the rites") is a short Sanskrit śāstra on Buddhist ritual procedure. In the digital editions it consists of ten verses followed by a colophon that names the work and attributes it to Nāgārjuna.[^gretil-kriya] The verses are a list rather than an exposition: each line names a stage of a ritual sequence without explaining it.

The stages run from the examination of teacher and pupil, through the testing and purification of the building site, to the setting up of images and the performance of fire offerings. They continue with the entry of the vajra master, meditation, painting, offerings, the consecration of the finished works, ordination, the raising of banners, and the restoration of old structures, and they end with worship of the assembly maṇḍala.[^gretil-kriya] The sequence reads like a table of contents for the building and consecration of a temple or shrine, though no commentary or translation confirming this reading has been located. The attribution to Nāgārjuna rests only on the colophon, and little else about the work's date, origin or transmission appears to be documented in the sources available. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon lists the text under architecture and craft (*śilpaśāstra*), whereas GRETIL files it with Buddhist philosophy.[^dsbc315]
