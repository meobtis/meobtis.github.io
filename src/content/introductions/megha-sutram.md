The *Megha sūtra* in this edition is a brief Mahāyāna discourse of the dhāraṇī-sūtra type. It opens with the Buddha staying in the palace of the nāga kings Nanda and Upananda, where a great assembly of nāga kings gathers and makes vows, and one nāga sovereign asks how all nāgas might be freed from suffering so that rain falls in season and crops grow in Jambudvīpa. The Buddha answers that loving-kindness (*maitrī*) relieves that suffering, and goes on to give a dhāraṇī, lists of buddha names and mantra phrases to be recited in times of drought.[^dsbc50]

The text appears to be closely related to the short rainmaking sūtra preserved in the Tibetan Kangyur as *The Great Cloud (2)* (Toh 235). According to 84000, that text presents itself as a chapter of the longer *Great Cloud* (Toh 232), was used for rainmaking rituals, was translated into Chinese four times between the sixth and eighth centuries, survives in several Dunhuang manuscripts and is preserved in Sanskrit only in part.[^84000toh235] This identification rests on matching content and has not been confirmed in a scholarly source consulted here. The title is also close to that of the *Mahāmegha sūtra*, which is a separate entry in this index. Little else about the date, authorship or manuscript basis of this Sanskrit text was found, beyond the 1880 printed edition named in the digital edition's record.
