The *Kośāmbakavastu* ("Section on the Kauśāmbī Monks") is one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, a major part of the monastic code (Vinaya) of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school. It is edited from a manuscript of the Gilgit collection, whose folio numbers are marked in the digital text.[^gretil-kosambaka] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya survives in a Tibetan translation of about the ninth century and a Chinese one of the eighth century, and only to some extent in Sanskrit.[^wiki-msv]

The section opens with a mnemonic verse (*uddāna*) and then tells how a dispute arose at Kauśāmbī, where the Buddha was staying at the Ghoṣilārāma. A monk of Kauśāmbī, learned in the sūtras, the Vinaya and the lists (*mātṛkā*), and a visiting monk from Vaiśālī, equally learned, disagree about how a sūtra is to be read and then about a water pot left empty in a latrine, an offence under a rule laid down by the community. The Kauśāmbī monk has the Vaiśālī monk suspended (*utkṣepaṇīya*), the community divides into two parties, and the Buddha repeatedly summons both sides and urges them not to quarrel. He also lays down rules of conduct for the suspended monk and for those who suspended him, and explains that rites such as the *poṣadha* and *pravāraṇā* performed by a divided community are invalid.[^gretil-kosambaka] The same episode is known from the Pali tradition, where the Buddha is said to have withdrawn from the quarrelling monks and the dispute was settled only after the lay community withheld support and the monks repented.[^wiki-kosambi] Beyond the text and the edition's front matter, little specific scholarship on this section could be consulted, so its date, Tibetan and Chinese parallels, and reception are not described here.
