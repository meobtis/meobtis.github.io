The *Vinayavastu* ("Sections on Discipline") belongs to the Vinaya of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school, the monastic code that regulates the life of ordained monks. The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is preserved in full in Tibetan, in a translation dated to the ninth century, and in Chinese, in an eighth-century translation, and only in part in the original Sanskrit.[^wiki-msv] The Sanskrit text offered here is arranged as a series of "sections" (*vastu*), each of which opens with summary verses (*uddāna*) listing the topics it covers; the Bhaiṣajyavastu, for example, begins with such a verse and then treats the permitted medicines, with the narrative framing familiar from Vinaya literature, in which the Buddha lays down each rule in response to a particular incident.[^dsbc91]

Little further information could be verified for this introduction. The scholarly question of the school's origins is debated: some regard the Mūlasarvāstivādins as an offshoot of the Sarvāstivādins, others as an independent community, and Schopen has argued that the school developed in the second century CE.[^wiki-msv] The edition described by the digital record is a Sanskrit text published in a volume titled *Gilgit Manuscripts*, which suggests that it derives from the Gilgit manuscript find, but the record itself does not say so, and the details of the manuscripts and of the printed edition should be checked against Dutt's volume.[^dsbc91]
