# Prajñāpāramitāhṛdayasūtram [saṃkṣiptamātṛkā] (The Discourse of the Heart of the Perfection of Wisdom the concise text)

The Discourse of the Heart of the Perfection of Wisdom  
the concise text

Homage to the `sarvajña` (All Knowing).

The bodhisattva ārya Avalokiteśvara, while carrying out the practice in the profound `prajñāpāramitā` (perfection of wisdom), looked closely. There are five `skandha` (collections), and he saw them as `śūnya` (empty) of `svabhāva` (intrinsic nature).

Here, Śāriputra, `rūpa` (form) is `śūnyatā` (emptiness); emptiness indeed is rūpa. śūnyatā is not separate from rūpa; rūpa is not separate from śūnyatā. Whatever is rūpa, that is śūnyatā; whatever is śūnyatā, that is rūpa.

So indeed are `vedanā` (feeling), `saṃjñā` (apperception), `saṃskāra` (mental construction), and `vijñāna` (consciousness).

Here, Śāriputra, all phenomena have the `lakṣaṇa` (characteristic) of emptiness; they are `anutpanna` (unarisen), `aniruddha` (unceased), `amala` (unstained), not `vimala` (pure), not `ūna` (deficient), not `paripūrṇa` (complete). Therefore, Śāriputra, in śūnyatā there is no rūpa, no vedanā, no saṃjñā, no mental constructions, no vijñāna. There is no eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, or mind; no form, sound, smell, taste, tangible, or phenomenon. There is no `dhātu` (component) of the eye, and so forth up to no component of the mind.

There is no knowledge, no `avidyā` (ignorance), no exhaustion of knowledge, no exhaustion of ignorance, and so forth up to no `jarāmaraṇa` (ageing and death), no exhaustion of ageing and death; no `duḥkha` (dissatisfaction), `samudaya` (origin), `nirodha` (cessation), or `mārga` (path); no knowledge, no attainment.

And a bodhisattva, relying on the prajñāpāramitā, dwells without obstruction of the `citta` (mind). Because there is no obstruction of the mind, he is unafraid, has passed beyond `viparyāsa` (distortion), and has `niṣṭhanirvāṇa` (final extinguishment). All the Buddhas who abide in the three times, relying on the prajñāpāramitā, have fully awakened to unsurpassed `samyaksaṃbodhi` (perfect awakening).

Therefore the great `mantra` (incantation) of the prajñāpāramitā should be known: the incantation of great `vidyā` (knowledge), the unsurpassed incantation, the incantation equal to the unequalled, the allayer of all dissatisfaction, the `satya` (truth) because it is not false, the incantation spoken in the prajñāpāramitā. It is as follows: `gate` (gone one), `gate` (gone one), `pāragate` (one gone beyond), `pārasaṃgate` (one gone completely beyond), `bodhi` (awakening), hail!

Thus the Discourse of the Heart of the Perfection of Wisdom is completed.
