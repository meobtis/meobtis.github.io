The *Vajrayoginīpraṇāmaikaviśikā* ("Twenty-one Verses of Homage to Vajrayoginī") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) addressed to the tantric goddess Vajrayoginī. The text opens with the salutation "om namaḥ śrīvajrayoginyai" and consists of twenty-one verses, the last of which gives the closing wish of the composer; the colophon calls it the *śrīvajrayoginīpraṇāmaikaviṃśikā*. Verses 1 to 20 each end with the refrain "namaste vajrayogini" ("homage to you, Vajrayoginī").[^dsbc215]

The verses praise the goddess in terms of tantric Buddhist ideas: she is described as embodying innate bliss and the union of wisdom and awareness, as free of existence and non-existence and of conceptual construction, as holding a skull-bowl, a khaṭvāṅga staff and a chopper, and, in one verse, as the mother of the perfection of wisdom (*prajñāpāramitā*) who is also addressed as Vajravārāhī.[^dsbc215] No author, date or translation could be verified in the sources consulted, and little about the work appears to be documented; further research on its attribution and on any Tibetan version is needed.
