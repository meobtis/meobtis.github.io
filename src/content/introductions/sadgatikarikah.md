The *Ṣaḍgatikārikāḥ* ("Verses on the Six Destinies") is a Sanskrit work in verse, catalogued by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under kāvya.[^dsbc858] The text is divided into six sections, each ending with a colophon-like closing line, which deal in turn with hell (*naraka*), animals (*tiryak*), hungry ghosts (*preta*), humans (*manuṣya*), asuras and gods (*deva*), that is, the six destinies of rebirth.[^dsbc858]

The opening verses state that beings experience the fruit of the good and bad actions of body, speech and mind that they themselves have performed, and that the Victors taught which action brings which result; the author then announces a concise account of what should be done and avoided.[^dsbc858] The sections that follow thus connect each destiny with the conduct leading to it.

Little reliable information about the work could be found: the digital edition names no author, and no date, place or tradition is given in its front matter or on the catalogue page.[^dsbc858] Information on Tibetan or Chinese translations and on manuscripts was not found, so none is claimed here. The edition used is by Kiyoshi Okano (Kyushu University, 2018).[^dsbc858]
