The *Pramāṇaviniścaya* ("Ascertainment of Valid Cognition") is a work on epistemology and logic by the Buddhist philosopher Dharmakīrti.[^sep2026] Modern scholars, following Frauwallner's proposed sequence, place it after Dharmakīrti's *Pramāṇavārttika* and before the *Nyāyabindu*, *Hetubindu* and *Vādanyāya*, although the dates of Dharmakīrti himself remain debated.[^sep2026] Among the topics it treats are perception, the proof of momentariness by the argument from existence, and the "wheel of reasons" (*hetucakra*) together with the triple criterion of a valid logical reason.[^sep2026]

The text offered here is not the work itself but a short Sanskrit fragment of it: a single manuscript folio from the National Archives collection in Kathmandu, reported by Matsuda and Steinkellner. In the passage, the argument turns on whether a reason can be shown to be absent from dissimilar cases and on a proof that depends on the self, which the author denies.[^gretil-pvin] Little else is documented about this fragment beyond that report. For the complete work, readers are usually pointed to Steinkellner's edition of the second chapter, which combines the Tibetan text with Sanskrit passages and a translation.[^sep2026]
