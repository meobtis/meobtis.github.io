The *Avadānaśataka* (often rendered "The Hundred Legends" or "A Century of Edifying Tales") is a Sanskrit collection of one hundred *avadāna*, narrative stories of the deeds of the Buddha, his disciples and others, and of the results of those deeds. It is arranged in ten sections of ten stories each, and each section has its own theme.[^nariman1919] Many stories combine a frame set in the Buddha's lifetime with an account of past lives, and the collection is closely related to the Jātaka literature.[^bagchi2011]

According to Nariman's survey, the first four sections show the kinds of deeds that lead to buddhahood or to the state of a pratyekabuddha, the fifth gathers stories of hungry ghosts (*preta*), the sixth tells of humans and animals reborn among the gods, and the last four describe how disciples attained arhatship.[^nariman1919]

The date is not fixed. Nariman assigns the work to the second century CE, partly on the strength of a mention of the *dīnāra* coin, and notes that it was already translated into Chinese in the first half of the third century.[^nariman1919] The Chinese version, the *Zhuanji baiyuan jing* (Chuan tsi pai yuan king), is attributed to a lay Indo-Scythian believer from the Yuezhi country who worked in the Han and Wu period.[^bagchi2011] Dating of the Sanskrit work thus rests on indirect evidence and scholars differ on details.

The standard critical edition of the Sanskrit text is J. S. Speyer's, published in St. Petersburg in the Bibliotheca Buddhica series (volume 3) with an English introduction and notes.[^speyer] P. L. Vaidya's later edition appeared at Darbhanga in 1958.[^bnf-vaidya] Little further documentation of the digital editions was found beyond their own front matter.

### Manuscripts in the Schøyen Collection

Thirty-eight fragments in the Schøyen Collection have been identified as parts of the *Avadānaśataka*, all but one from a single manuscript. Both manuscripts are written in the Gilgit/Bamiyan Type I script, in use from the sixth century CE, and Mitsuyo Demoto considers them most probably the oldest known manuscripts of the work.[^demoto2006]
