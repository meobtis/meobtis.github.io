The *Mañjuvajrastotra* is a short Sanskrit hymn of nine verses praising Mañjughoṣa, a form of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī, to whom the opening line pays homage as "Śrī-Mañjuvajra". The verses share a refrain, "I bow to Mañjughoṣa", and describe his appearance and attributes: a moon-white youth with five locks of hair, bearing a sword and a book, with lotus-petal eyes. The final verses promise that whoever recites the poem will become a master of poetry and a lion on the debater's throne.[^dsbc196]

The text's own closing lines call it "drawn from the Svayambhū Purāṇa" and "composed by Mañjugarbha", and the introductory line also names Mañjugarbha as author.[^dsbc196] Little else about its authorship, date or transmission could be documented here: no translations, manuscripts or scholarly studies were located in the sources consulted, so these questions are left open. The digital text is catalogued by the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon in its Svayambhū section of stotra literature.[^dsbc196]
