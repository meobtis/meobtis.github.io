The *Suprabhātastotram* ("Hymn of the Good Dawn") is a short Sanskrit stotra in praise of the Buddha. In the digital text, the opening verse states that the hymn is composed by one who, though of modest ability, wishes to praise the fully awakened one, and the following verses each close with the refrain "daśabala tava nityaṃ suprabhātaṃ prabhātam", addressed to the Buddha as the one possessing the ten powers.[^dsbc188] The verses describe the Buddha as the conqueror of Māra's forces and the destroyer of ignorance, using the image of a golden-hued figure with lotus-like eyes.[^dsbc188]

The heading of the text attributes it to Śrīharṣadeva, called a *bhūpati* (king).[^dsbc188] The sources I could consult do not establish who this king was or when the hymn was composed. The better-known King Harṣa of Kanauj is credited with the Buddhist-themed play *Nāgānanda*, and Buddhist accounts treat him as a patron, but nothing in the sources read links him to this hymn, so the identification should not be assumed.[^wikiharsha] Little further is documented here about translations, manuscripts or commentaries on the text, and none could be verified.
