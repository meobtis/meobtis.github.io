The *Māravijayastotram* ("Hymn on the Victory over Māra") is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise to the Buddha, composed in verse. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) files it in its section of stotra (hymns) under the heading *vajrāsana*, and presents it as a single chapter.[^dsbc208]

The opening verses, as they appear in the DSBC text, address the Buddha as one who has defeated the evil one (Māra), as the sole teacher of the world, to whom gods, *yakṣa*s, great serpents and *daitya*s bow, and as the one who taught the path of the four truths. Later verses praise his physical beauty and his compassion towards beings caught in the dangerous ocean of existence.[^dsbc208] The title evidently refers to the Buddha's victory over Māra at the seat of awakening, which fits the DSBC's *vajrāsana* classification.

Little reliable information about the hymn could be gathered. The edition front matter names no author, and no date, place of composition or school is given there. No scholarly discussion, Tibetan or Chinese translation, or manuscript description of this particular work was found, so none is asserted here; it may be related to other works in the collections of Buddhist stotras, but that is not established. The only documented history is that of the digital text, which derives from Pandey's 1994 anthology of Buddhist stotras.[^dsbc208]
