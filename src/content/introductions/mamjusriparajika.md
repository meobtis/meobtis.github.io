The *Maṃjuśrīpārājikā* is a Sanskrit work preserved in a collection of texts from Nepal. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the Vinaya, as one item in a volume titled *Pārājikā (Texts from Nepal)*, published in Pune in 2013.[^dsbc772] The title joins the name of the bodhisattva Mañjuśrī with *pārājikā*, the term for the gravest class of monastic offences, but the catalogue record does not explain the choice of title.

The text opens with a homage to the Three Jewels and a verse dialogue in which Mañjuśrī asks the Buddha how beings sunk in saṃsāra may be saved and how a *pārājikā* offence arises; the Buddha then replies.[^dsbc772] The text that follows is a compilation of ritual and ethical instruction rather than a monastic rulebook in the usual sense. Colophon-style closing lines in the digital text name sections on the rites of birth and cutting the umbilical cord (*jātakarma-nāḍī-chedana-vidhi*), the taking of the going-forth (*pravrajyā*), purity (*śuci*), daily conduct (*nityācāra*), the offering of funeral rice-balls (*piṇḍadāna*), and offences concerning killing, including a *hiṃsā-pārājikā* section.[^dsbc772] Its content thus appears to combine life-cycle ritual of the Newar Buddhist type with regulations on conduct.

Little else about the work is documented in the sources consulted. Its author, date and place of composition, any manuscript witnesses and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not stated in the edition records, and no reliable scholarly discussion could be located. The description above rests solely on the digital text and its catalogue record. No established English title is known, so none is given here.
