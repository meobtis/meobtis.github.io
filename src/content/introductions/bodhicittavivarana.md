The *Bodhicittavivaraṇa* ("Exposition of the Awakening Mind") is a verse text traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna. One modern publication describes it as rooted in the *Guhyasamāja Tantra*: it surveys the philosophical positions of the four Buddhist tenet systems and ends with the Madhyamaka-Prāsaṅgika view of emptiness.[^jamgon] The fragments given here include verses on the five aggregates compared to foam, bubbles, mirage, plantain stem and illusion, on the teaching of "mind only" as a device to relieve the fear of the naive, and on emptiness as the nature of all phenomena.[^gretil-bhc]

Only a selection of verses of the Sanskrit original is available in this digital text, which is described as a set of fragments; the surrounding verses are marked as omitted.[^gretil-bhc] Little else could be verified from the sources consulted here: questions of authorship, dating, the Tibetan translations and modern editions and translations are discussed in the scholarly literature but are not documented in this note.
