The *Kāvyādarśa* ("Mirror of Poetry") is a Sanskrit treatise on poetics (*alaṃkāraśāstra*) attributed to Daṇḍin; the 1957 edition used here calls it the *Kāvyalakṣaṇa* ("Characteristics of Poetry"). Most printed editions divide it into three chapters (*paricchedas*) with about 660 verses, the first of which is titled *Mārgavibhāga* ("Division of the styles").[^wiki-kavyadarsha][^sarit-ratnasri]

It is a general work on the theory of poetic composition, not a Buddhist treatise as such. Its Buddhist connection in this edition lies in the commentary, the *Ratnaśrī*, composed by the Buddhist monk Ratnaśrījñāna, whom the edition's heading describes as a Sinhalese teacher (*siṃhalācārya*).[^sarit-ratnasri] The *Kāvyādarśa* was also translated into Tibetan, where it is known as *Sñan ṅag me loṅ*.[^wiki-kavyadarsha]

Little reliable detail about the dating of the commentary and its author could be verified for this introduction. The root text is generally placed before the eleventh century, since King Bhoja drew on it extensively; its precise date is not settled in the sources consulted.[^wiki-kavyadarsha] Other Sanskrit commentaries on the work exist, and the *Ratnaśrī* has also been printed in a later multi-volume edition.[^wiki-kavyadarsha]
