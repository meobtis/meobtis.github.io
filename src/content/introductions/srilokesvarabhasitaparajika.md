The *Śrīlokeśvarabhāṣitaparājikā* ("the *pārājikā* spoken by the glorious Lokeśvara") is a short Sanskrit work in verse, preserved in a collection of texts from Nepal. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the Vinaya, as one item in a volume titled *Pārājikā (Texts from Nepal)*, published in Pune in 2013.[^dsbc768] The title pairs the name of the bodhisattva Lokeśvara with *pārājikā*, the term for the gravest class of monastic offences, but the catalogue record does not explain why the work is so named.

The opening verses of the digital text present a dialogue: the goddess Tārā asks Lokeśvara how to judge the signs of coming misfortune (*utpāta*) as auspicious or inauspicious, and Lokeśvara replies by listing remedies for various omens, including recitation of the *Pañcarakṣā*, a great offering (*mahābali*), gifts of land and gold, fire offerings (*homa*) and feeding the monastic community.[^dsbc768] The same dialogue appears in a text catalogued separately in the same collection as *Lokeśvara-pārājikā*, and the two may be versions of one work, though this was not checked against the printed volume.

Little else is documented in the sources consulted. The author, date and place of composition, manuscript witnesses and any Tibetan or Chinese translation are not stated in the edition records, and no scholarly discussion of the work could be located. No established English title is known, so none is given here.
