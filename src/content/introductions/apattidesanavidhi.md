The *Āpattideśanāvidhi* (Ritual for the Confession of Downfalls) is a very brief Sanskrit liturgical text. The edition front matter and colophon ascribe it to the master Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, that is Atiśa.[^gretil-apatti][^dsbc256] The Tibetan canon catalogue entry used by the 84000 project lists a work of this name in the Middle Way section of the Tengyur as Toh 3973, under the English title "Ritual for the Confession of Downfalls".[^84000toh3973]

The surviving Sanskrit consists of a spoken formula. The penitent asks the teachers, the buddhas and bodhisattvas of the ten directions, and the presiding monastic to take notice of them, and then confesses, without concealing, the accumulated faults of body, speech and mind committed since beginningless saṃsāra. These include the ten unwholesome actions, the five heinous deeds and their near equivalents, and breaches of the prātimokṣa vows, the bodhisattva training and the esoteric (*guhyamantra*) pledges. The formula is to be spoken three times, and is followed by brief instructions for the officiant's responses.[^dsbc256]

The colophon states that the work was composed by the ācārya Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna and that it was then rendered and settled by the Indian preceptor together with the Tibetan monk Jayaśīla (Tshul khrims rgyal ba).[^dsbc256] I found no scholarly discussion of its date, place of composition or relation to other confession rites, so these remain unaddressed here.

No English translation or study appears to be available. The 84000 catalogue records the Tibetan version only by title and collection.[^84000toh3973] Both digital editions derive from the Sarnath volume of eleven works attributed to Atiśa edited by Negi (1992), whose full bibliographic details are given in the DSBC record.[^dsbc256]
