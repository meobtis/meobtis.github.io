The *Mahācakravartināmāṣṭottaraśatastotram* is a short Sanskrit hymn of praise (*stotra*). Its title, which may be read as a "hymn of the one hundred and eight names of the great universal monarch", points to the format of a litany of epithets, and its colophon calls it a *mahācakravartināmāṣṭottaraśatādhyeṣaṇāstotra*, that is, a hymn of request (*adhyeṣaṇā*) built on those names. In the digital text each verse strings together vocative epithets and closes with the refrain *namo'stu te* ("homage to you"); the verses in the text run to about twenty, the last of which asks the addressed buddha to bring forth his own family for the benefit of all beings.[^dsbc157]

The epithets are largely Vajrayāna in flavour (Vajrasattva, Mahāvajra, Vajranātha, Vairocana, Samantabhadra, Vajrapāṇi), and verse 17 declares that the addressee is called Vajrasattva because his mind is firm like a vajra, so the hymn appears to be addressed to Vajrasattva.[^dsbc157] Little is documented about its authorship, date, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and no reliable scholarly discussion could be consulted in preparing this note, so none is claimed here. The only edition information available is that of the digital text, which was keyed from Pandey's printed collection of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc157]
