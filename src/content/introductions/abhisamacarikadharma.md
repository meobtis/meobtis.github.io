The *Abhisamācārikadharma* (also *Abhisamācārikā Dharmāḥ*) is a Sanskrit text of rules of proper conduct for Buddhist monks. It is part of the monastic code (Vinaya) of the Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravāda school.[^kieffer2015] It survives in one heavily corrupted Sanskrit manuscript, and the review literature notes that its many obscure terms, largely names of material objects and practices, needed fresh study; an earlier edition of 1969 had misjudged the overall structure of the text.[^kieffer2015] The digital text opens with an invocation and a scene at Śrāvastī in which the Buddha is residing and the community's observance day (*poṣadha*) is being arranged.[^gretil-abhisamacarika]

The standard modern edition is Seishi Karashima's *Die Abhisamācārikā Dharmāḥ: Verhaltensregeln für buddhistische Mönche der Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravādins* (Bibliotheca philologica et philosophica Buddhica 13), published in three volumes in Tokyo in 2012 with the collaboration of Oskar von Hinüber. It compares the Sanskrit with the Chinese parallel version and adds a German translation, commentary, grammar and glossary.[^karashima2012] Little else about the work's date, authorship or Tibetan transmission could be established from the sources consulted, so these are left unstated here.
