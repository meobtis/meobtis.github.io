The *Cīvaravastu* ("the chapter on robes") is, by its title and its place in the edition, one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, the part of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya that deals with the rules of monastic discipline. The Sanskrit text begins with mnemonic verses (*uddāna*) listing the stories it contains, which name figures such as Jīvaka, and it carries manuscript folio references from the Gilgit manuscript, so it belongs to the Sanskrit material recovered at Gilgit.[^gretil-civara] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is one of the three Vinaya traditions that survive in full, and it is preserved chiefly in Tibetan and Chinese translations, with the original Sanskrit extant only in part.[^wiki-msv]

Little detailed, reliable scholarship on this particular chapter could be consulted in preparing this note, so its contents, date and translations are not described further here. The Sanskrit of other chapters of the same Vinaya was found among the Gilgit manuscripts in the 1930s and was edited by Nalinaksha Dutt, whose edition is the basis of the digital text.[^84000leather][^gretil-civara]
