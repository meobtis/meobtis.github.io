The *Suprabhātastava* ("Praise of the Auspicious Dawn") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) in praise of the Buddha, classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the stotra works devoted to Śākyamuni.[^dsbc938] The text opens with a salutation to the Buddha and runs to a few dozen verses, which are numbered in the edition (the numbering is given twice in places, apparently reflecting two verse orders). Each of the central verses ends with the same refrain addressed to the Buddha as the "one with the ten powers" (*daśabala*), declaring that for him it is always a good dawn.[^dsbc938]

The hymn works by contrast. Verses describe the Buddha as awake in the night of ignorance, and then present a series of gods and sages, among them Indra, Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva, Gaṇapati, Skanda, the world-guardians and the great ṛṣis, as asleep, entangled in desire. Later verses reflect on the repeating cycle of dawns, birth, ageing and death, and close with homage to the Buddha, Dharma and Saṃgha each day.[^dsbc938] Little reliable external documentation about the work, its author, date, manuscripts or any Tibetan or Chinese translation could be consulted for this introduction, so none is claimed here. The DSBC record gives no printed edition, editor or year.[^dsbc938]
