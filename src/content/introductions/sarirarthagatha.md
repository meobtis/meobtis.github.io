The *Śarīrārthagāthā* is a collection of Sanskrit verses that GRETIL presents as a chapter of the *Yogācārabhūmi* attributed to Asaṅga; the legacy GRETIL header places it in the *Cintāmayī bhūmi*.[^gretil-sarira] The electronic text numbers about forty-one verse units (Śag 1 to 41). No established English title for the work was found.

The verses are short canonical-style pieces: they speak of restraint of body, speech and mind, mindfulness, and the cessation of suffering, and include exchanges between a deity and a monk and between a young woman and a sage, question-and-answer verses in the manner of the *Pārāyaṇa*, images of crossing a flood, and a closing verse of the *Bhadraikarātri* type. Several verses are noted in the text as parallel to the *Udānavarga*.[^gretil-sarira]

Little reliable information about the work's date, transmission and reception could be gathered beyond what the edition front matter and the GRETIL record state, so these are left undiscussed here. The only printed edition identified is Fumio Enomoto's, published in Göttingen in 1989 as part of a series of newly discovered and newly edited Sanskrit texts from the Buddhist canon.[^gretil-sarira]
