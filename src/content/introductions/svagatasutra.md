The *Svāgatasūtra* is a very short Sanskrit sūtra of seven numbered sections. It is set at Śrāvastī, and in the text as edited the Buddha greets the monks with "welcome" (*svāgata*) and tells them that they have gone forth well and gained a good human birth. He lists the conditions that make this birth fortunate: birth in a noble region, intact faculties, and the ability to understand well-spoken and ill-spoken teachings.[^gretil-svagata]

The Buddha then says that he has appeared in the world as teacher and that the teaching he gives, which leads to peace, nirvāṇa and awakening, is dependent arising, stated in its standard formulation. He concludes that such a combination of conditions is extremely rare and that the monks should therefore train with an eye to their own good, the good of others and both.[^gretil-svagata] The edition notes parallels with a Turfan fragment (Sanskrit Handschriften aus den Turfanfunden, vol. 4, additions, no. 881) and a passage in the *Saṅghabhedavastu* of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya (cited there as SBhV II).[^gretil-svagata] Little else about the text's date, school, or translations could be established from the sources consulted; the title of Waldschmidt's article suggests it was edited as a second text of the *Daśabalasūtra* type, but no English title is established.
