The *Saptabuddhastotra* ("Hymn to the Seven Buddhas") is a short Sanskrit hymn of nine verses in a long metre. According to the text itself, each of the first seven verses praises one of the seven Buddhas of the past in turn (Vipaśyin, Śikhin, Viśvabhū, Krakucchanda, Kanakamuni, Kāśyapa and Śākyamuni), the eighth praises the future Buddha Maitreya, and the ninth is a concluding wish that the merit of the praise may lead to liberation.[^dsbc189]

Each verse names the Buddha's birthplace and family, the length of his life and the tree under which he awakened. For example, the verse on Śākyamuni places his birth at Kapilavastu and his awakening at the foot of an *aśvattha* tree.[^dsbc189] The hymn does not name its author, and no reliable information about its date, authorship or translations was found in the sources consulted; it is known here only from the printed anthology named in the edition record.[^dsbc189]
