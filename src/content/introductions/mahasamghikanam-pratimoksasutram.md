The *Mahāsāṃghika Prātimokṣa Sūtra* is the code of monastic rules (*prātimokṣa*) belonging to the Mahāsāṃghika order. Its Sanskrit is given here in the text of the Lokottaravāda branch edited by N. Tatia and in that edited by W. Pachow and R. Mishra.[^gretil-prmo][^gretil-pratimoksa-ma] In Buddhist monastic practice a prātimokṣa is the list of training rules recited by the assembled community of monks, and several such codes, belonging to different ordination lineages, survive.[^wiki-pratimoksa] The Pachow and Mishra text opens with a short verse section in praise of the Buddha and the recitation of the rules, followed by the rules themselves.[^dsbc90]

The Lokottaravāda text sets out the categories of offence in the usual sequence: *pārājika* (4 rules), *saṃghātiśeṣa* (13), *aniyata* (2), *niḥsargika-pācattika* (30), *śuddha-pācattika* (92), *pratideśanīya* (4), the *śaikṣa* training rules and the seven procedures for settling disputes (*adhikaraṇaśamatha*). It closes with verses attributed to successive Buddhas and a colophon naming the recitation as that of the Lokottaravādin Mahāsāṃghikas of the Madhyoddeśika school.[^gretil-prmo] The Lokottaravāda is described as a sub-school of the Mahāsāṃghika whose best-known work is the *Mahāvastu*, which serves as a preface to its Vinaya, and manuscripts of a *Prātimokṣa-vibhaṅga* from a Mahāsāṃghika-Lokottaravāda monastery at Bamiyan are held in the Schøyen Collection.[^wiki-mahasamghika]

The Mahāsāṃghika school's complete Vinaya survives in Chinese translation as the *Mohesengzhi lü* (Taishō no. 1425), which, according to the account summarised in a general reference article, was brought to China by Faxian and translated in 416 CE.[^wiki-mahasamghika] Charles S. Prebish's monograph on the Sanskrit prātimokṣa sūtras of the Mahāsāṃghikas and Mūlasarvāstivādins is the standard study listed for this literature.[^wiki-pratimoksa] Little else about the manuscript basis and the date of the Pachow and Mishra text could be verified from the sources consulted here, and readers should consult the introduction to the 1956 edition and Prebish's work for detail.
