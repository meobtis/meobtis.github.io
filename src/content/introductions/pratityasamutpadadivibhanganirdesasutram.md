The *Pratītyasamutpādādivibhaṅganirdeśasūtra* is a very short discourse on dependent arising (*pratītyasamutpāda*). The 84000 translation renders its title as "Teaching the Fundamental Exposition and Detailed Analysis of Dependent Arising"; the Tibetan title is *rten cing 'brel par 'byung ba dang po dang rnam par dbye ba bstan pa*, and the work is Toh 211 in the General Sūtra section of the Degé Kangyur.[^84000toh211]

The sūtra is set at Jeta's Grove in Śrāvastī. The Buddha announces that he will teach both the "beginning" (*ādi*), that is the basic formula of dependent arising, and the "analysis" (*vibhaṅga*). He first states the principle that when this exists, that comes to be, and then lists the twelve links from ignorance and formations through to birth, and old age and death. He then takes each link in turn and defines it: for example, consciousness as the six groups of consciousness, name-and-form as the four non-material aggregates together with the material one, and feeling as pleasant, painful or neutral. The Sanskrit text confirms this structure.[^dsbc65][^84000toh211]

According to the 84000 introduction, the Sanskrit original is preserved as part of the *Nidānasaṃyukta*, a collection belonging to the Sarvāstivāda *Saṃyuktāgama*. The same teaching is the subject of Chinese parallels: two translations (Taishō 123 and 124, attributed to Kumārajīva and Xuanzang respectively) and a version within the *Saṃyuktāgama* (Taishō 99).[^84000toh211] The Tibetan translation has no colophon and does not name its translators, but the work appears in the early ninth-century Denkarma inventory, so it was translated by then.[^84000toh211]

Sanskrit editions cited by 84000 include Tripathi's study of twenty-five sūtras of the *Nidānasaṃyukta* (1962) and Vaidya's edition, available with an English translation by Ānandajoti Bhikkhu.[^84000toh211] The DSBC text follows Vaidya's 1961 printing.[^dsbc65] Little else is documented about the sūtra's individual transmission history.
