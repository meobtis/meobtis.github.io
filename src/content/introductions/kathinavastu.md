The *Kaṭhinavastu* ("Section on the Kaṭhina") is one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, a major part of the monastic code (Vinaya) of the Mūlasarvāstivāda school. It deals with the *kaṭhina*, a ceremony in which a community of monks that has spent the rainy-season retreat together receives cloth, makes it into a robe and gains a set of relaxations of the usual rules for a limited period. The Sanskrit text is edited from a manuscript of the Gilgit collection, whose folio numbers are marked in the digital text.[^gretil-kathinavastu] The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is preserved in Tibetan and Chinese translations and only in part in Sanskrit, and the origins of the school remain debated.[^wiki-msv]

The text opens with a mnemonic verse (*uddāna*) and then narrates, in the usual Vinaya manner, an occasion for a ruling. A group of monks who have kept the retreat at Sāketa travel in heat and mud to Śrāvastī, where the Buddha, seeing their fatigue, allows the *kaṭhina* to be spread. He lists five advantages that follow from it, including freedom from the limits on eating in groups and on keeping extra robes, and permission to travel without all of one's robes, for the period from the month of Kārttika to Phālguna.[^gretil-kathinavastu] The procedure is then set out as formal acts of the Saṅgha: the assembly announces that cloth has been received, appoints a monk as the one who spreads the *kaṭhina* (with lists of those who may and may not be chosen), and gives him the cloth through a motion and a vote by silence.[^gretil-kathinavastu]

The *kaṭhina* is also known in the Theravāda tradition, where it survives as an annual festival of offering robes to monks at the end of the rains retreat, over a period of about a month after the full moon of the eleventh lunar month.[^wiki-kathina] Beyond what the text and the edition's front matter show, little specific scholarship on this section could be consulted, so its date, Tibetan and Chinese parallels, and reception are not described here.
