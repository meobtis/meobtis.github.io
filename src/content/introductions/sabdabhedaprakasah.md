The *Śabdabhedaprakāśa* ("Illumination of the Varieties of Words") is a short Sanskrit work in verse, about fifty stanzas long in the digital edition. Its opening verses explain that differences between forms of a word may arise from vowel length, from a different letter, or from a different meaning, and the verses that follow list pairs and groups of words that are found in two or more spellings, such as *agāra* and *āgāra* or *añkura* and *aṅkūra*.[^dsbc807] In its closing verses the author says that the work serves poets and readers in settling doubts about such forms, and about their use in metre, alliteration, and wordplay.[^dsbc807]

The concluding colophon states that the piece is the *Śabdabhedaprakāśa* in the *Viśvaprakāśa* composed by Maheśvara, whom it describes as a master of verse, prose and learning.[^dsbc807] The *Viśvaprakāśa* is a Sanskrit lexicon, and the colophon therefore suggests that this text is a section or appendix of that lexicon, which is consistent with the editor field of the Devanāgarī record naming Maheśvara.[^dsbc808] The text itself is philological rather than doctrinal. Its presence in a collection of Buddhist texts, under the category of kośa (treatises and lexica), probably reflects the cataloguing of the collection and not any Buddhist content, but the records do not say why it was included.

Little else about the work could be verified in the sources consulted for this introduction. The date and background of Maheśvara, the printed editions of the *Viśvaprakāśa*, and any translations or commentaries are not documented in the edition records, and no further reliable sources were found, so they are left out here rather than guessed.
