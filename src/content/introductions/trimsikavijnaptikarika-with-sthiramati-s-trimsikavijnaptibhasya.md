This file presents Vasubandhu's *Triṃśikāvijñaptikārikā*, a Yogācāra treatise in thirty verses, interleaved with the commentary on it by Sthiramati, the *Triṃśikāvijñaptibhāṣya*. The digital text opens with the invocation and an explanation of why the treatise is composed, namely to teach the absence of self in persons and in phenomena (*pudgala-* and *dharma-nairātmya*), which the commentary links to removing the afflictive and cognitive obscurations; the text also marks the pagination of the printed edition.[^gretil-tvbh]

Sthiramati is described in reference works as a sixth-century Indian Buddhist scholar-monk associated with Valābhī and Nālandā, and as the author of this commentary, which survives in Sanskrit and Tibetan.[^wiki-sthiramati] Dating and authorship are debated: it has been argued that several commentators may have been merged into the single figure of Sthiramati.[^wiki-sthiramati] Beyond what the edition itself records, little further documentation of this particular work could be verified for this introduction.
