The *Pratītyasamutpādāvadāna* ("The Tale of Dependent Origination") is a short Sanskrit verse text attributed to the Kashmiri poet Kṣemendra. The GRETIL record identifies it as Avadāna 75 of his *Bodhisattvāvadānakalpalatā*, a collection of Buddhist narratives in verse, and its verses are numbered accordingly (KAvk_75.1 onwards).[^gretil-pratityasamutpada] No English title for this single chapter appears to be established, and I found no separate scholarly treatment of it.

The opening verses set the scene at Śrāvastī, where the Buddha is staying in the Jetavana and addresses the assembly of monks. He announces that he will explain dependent origination (*pratītyasamutpāda*) in sequence, beginning with ignorance (*avidyā*), described as the root of the tree of saṃsāra, and then proceeding through the formations (*saṃskāra*), consciousness (*vijñāna*) and name-and-form (*nāmarūpa*).[^gretil-pratityasamutpada] The text is thus an exposition of the twelve-link chain in the metrical style of the collection.

Kṣemendra (c. 990–c. 1070 CE) was a Kashmiri polymath whose dated works span 1037 to 1066, and the *Avadānakalpalata* is listed among his devotional works on the former lives and good deeds of the Buddha.[^wiki-kshemendra] Wikipedia is used here only as a lead; details of the collection's composition, transmission and Tibetan translation are not covered in the sources I could consult, and are therefore left out.
