The *Guṇakāraṇḍavyūha* is a Sanskrit Mahāyāna sūtra devoted to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, here called Lokeśvara. It opens with verses of homage to the Three Jewels and a promise to recount the "good account" of Lokeśa, and it is divided into twenty sections, the last of which describes the benefits of hearing the text (*phalaśruti*). Its section titles concern the liberation of beings from the hells (Avīci), the realms of hungry ghosts, yakṣas and rākṣasas, as well as the bodhisattva's conduct, the path to awakening, the Pure Abodes and Sukhāvatī.[^dsbc41]

According to the 84000 introduction to the *Kāraṇḍavyūha* (the source of the mantra *oṃ maṇipadme hūṃ*), the *Guṇakāraṇḍavyūha* is a later and longer Nepalese version of that sūtra, composed entirely in verse, dated to the fifteenth century, and not translated into Tibetan.[^84000toh116] No established English title for the verse version was found, and little else about its authorship, manuscripts or reception could be verified from the sources consulted, so these are left undiscussed here.
