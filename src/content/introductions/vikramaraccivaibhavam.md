The *Vikramāraccivaibhavam* is a Sanskrit poem of about seventy-five verses, many in ornate metres, that the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files among the Buddhist hymns (*stotra*) of its "paṇḍita" section. Its opening verses praise the island of Sri Lanka (*siṃhala*), recalling the Buddha's teaching of the *Laṅkāvatāra*, the Sinhalese kings, and the poets Kālidāsa and Kumāradāsa. It then describes the villages, orchards, rice fields and Buddhist shrines of the island and the devotion of its people, and closes with verses of blessing on a named person.[^dsbc111]

Little is documented about the work beyond the text itself. Its closing verse and colophon call it the *Vikramāraccipadyam* and attribute it to Paṇḍita Śāntibhikṣu Śāstrin, a pupil of Bhadanta Dharmānanda who is described as a resident of Sri Lanka and a professor of Sanskrit at Vidyālaṅkāra University, and give the date as Buddhist year 2510.[^dsbc111] No English title, translation, printed edition or scholarly discussion could be found for it, so its occasion, the meaning of its title and its publication history remain uncertain.
