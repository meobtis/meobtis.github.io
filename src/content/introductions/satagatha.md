The Śatagāthā ("Hundred Verses") is a short Sanskrit collection of didactic verses of the nīti (worldly wisdom) kind, attributed in the digital editions to Vararuci. The verses open with maxims that urge learning in youth, the cultivation of virtue over display, and the value of the company of good people; for example, they contrast the cow that gives milk with one that is merely sold for the bell it wears. The text is made up of numbered verses, mostly in the common śloka metre with some longer stanzas.[^gretil-satagatha]

Very little reliable information on the work's date, the identity of its author, its place in the Buddhist tradition or any Tibetan or Chinese translation could be found in the sources consulted, so none is claimed here. The attribution to Vararuci is that given in the GRETIL record, and the text is filed under Buddhist philosophy there and under nīti in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon.[^gretil-satagatha][^dsbc275] Both digital editions descend from a printed edition by Losang Norbu Shastri published at Sarnath in 2001, as described in the records cited below.
