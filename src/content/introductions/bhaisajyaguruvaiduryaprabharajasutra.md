The Medicine Buddha Sūtra (*Bhaiṣajyaguruvaidūryaprabharājasūtra*) is a Mahāyāna sūtra set in Vaiśālī, in which Śākyamuni tells Mañjuśrī about the buddha Bhaiṣajyaguruvaidūryaprabha. The Tibetan version, classed as an Action tantra in the Kangyur (Toh 504), is rendered in English as *The Detailed Account of the Previous Aspirations of the Blessed Bhaiṣajyaguruvaiḍūryaprabha*.[^84000toh504]

The sūtra describes the twelve great vows the buddha made as a bodhisattva, the pure buddhafield of Vaidūryanirbhāsā in the east, and the benefits of hearing and retaining his name. It also contains a forty-nine-day offering ritual taught by Trāṇamukta for restoring a dying person, a list of nine kinds of untimely death, and the pledges of twelve yakṣa generals to protect practitioners.[^84000toh504]

The text survives in Sanskrit in manuscripts from Gilgit, dated to the fifth and sixth centuries CE, which show at least two recensions; Śāntideva quoted substantial passages in the eighth century.[^84000toh504] Chinese versions include an early fourth-century translation by Śrīmitra as part of a longer work, and standalone translations by Dharmagupta (616 CE) and Xuanzang (650 CE). The Tibetan translation was made in the early ninth century by Jinamitra, Dānaśīla and Bandé Yeshé Dé.[^84000toh504] The printed Sanskrit text used for the digital editions here is that of Vaidya (1961).[^dsbc36]
