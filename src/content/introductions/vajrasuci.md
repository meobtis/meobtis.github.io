The *Vajrasūcī* ("Diamond-pointed Needle") is a short Sanskrit work attributed to Aśvaghoṣa. It is a polemic against the Brahmanical caste system, asking what actually makes a person a brahmin (the soul, the body, birth, knowledge, deeds or ritual) and rejecting birth as the basis of social class.[^wiki-vs] The opening verses of the text, as edited by Dvivedi, name Aśvaghoṣa as the author and bow to Mañjughoṣa.[^gretil-vs] A Hindu work of the same name, the *Vajrasūcī Upaniṣad*, is a separate text, although the two share a theme.[^wiki-vs]

The attribution to the Buddhist poet Aśvaghoṣa is considered seriously doubtful by many scholars, who sometimes call the work "pseudo-Aśvaghoṣa".[^wiki-vs] The text became known in the West when Brian Houghton Hodgson received a manuscript in Nepal and published a translation in 1835; a Sanskrit edition with the title *The Wujra Soochi* appeared in Bombay in 1839.[^wiki-vs] Later editions and translations include Albrecht Weber's German edition (1860), and Takao Kagawa compared several textual versions in a short 1958 paper.[^kagawa1958][^wiki-vs] Little else about its date, place of composition and sources is firmly documented in the sources consulted, and the information given here should be checked against specialist scholarship.
