The *Adhikaraṇavastu* is the sixteenth chapter of the *Vinayavastu*, the first of the four Scriptural Divisions of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya. The *Vinayavastu* deals with the communal rites, procedures and disciplinary measures of monastic life, and has seventeen chapters.[^84000toh1] The Tibetan title of the chapter is rendered in English as "The Chapter on Disputes".[^84000toh1]

According to the 84000 introduction to the first chapter of the *Vinayavastu*, the *Adhikaraṇavastu* discusses seven means of resolving disputes that arise from disagreements over the Buddha's teachings, reproaches about another monk's conduct, offences, or acts of the saṅgha.[^84000toh1] The opening verses (*uddāna*) of the Sanskrit text itself list quarrel, dispute and the legal case (*adhikaraṇa*), ask what their root is, and name procedures such as resolution in the presence of the parties, the casting of lots, and the "covering with grass" procedure.[^gretil-adhik] Beyond this, little documentation specific to this chapter was found in the sources consulted.

For centuries the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya was known only through Tibetan and Chinese translations and excerpts such as those in the *Divyāvadāna*. Manuscripts found near Gilgit in 1931, dated to the fifth or sixth century CE, include many vinaya manuscripts, most of them from the *Vinayavastu*. The Tibetan translation of the ninth century is the only complete version known, and Yijing made a partial Chinese translation in the late seventh to early eighth centuries.[^84000toh1]

The Sanskrit text of this chapter is edited by Raniero Gnoli from the Gilgit manuscript, together with the *Śayanāsanavastu* (the fifteenth section), in a single volume published in Rome in 1978.[^hathi-gnoli]
