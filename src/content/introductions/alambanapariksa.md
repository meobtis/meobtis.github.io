The *Ālambanaparīkṣā* ("Examination of the Objective Support") is a short work by the Buddhist logician Dignāga, consisting of eight verses with a brief prose autocommentary, the *Ālambanaparīkṣāvṛtti*. It deals with perception and epistemology, asking what can properly be called the object (*ālambana*) that supports a cognition.[^eckel2016]

The Sanskrit original is no longer extant. The work is preserved in Tibetan (*dMigs pa brtag pa*) and in Chinese (*Guan suoyuan yuan lun*), and the autocommentary exists in several Chinese translations, the earliest attributed to Paramārtha.[^eob-alambana] The Sanskrit text on this site is therefore a reconstruction, made by the editor from the Tibetan and Chinese versions. Another such reconstruction, with the Tibetan text, was published by Yamaguchi Susumu in 1953.[^eob-alambana]

Vinītadeva is recorded as having written a subcommentary on the root text, and the work was later studied in Tibet, including by Dge lugs scholars.[^eob-alambana][^eckel2016] An English translation of the work and its commentarial tradition appeared in 2016.[^eckel2016] Little further detail on its date and transmission could be verified from the sources consulted here.
