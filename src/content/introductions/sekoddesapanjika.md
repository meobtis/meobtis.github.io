The *Sekoddeśapañjikā* is a short Sanskrit commentary in the Buddhist tantric tradition, classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the commentaries on non-dual tantras.[^dsbc106] Its title indicates a *pañjikā* (a word-by-word commentary) on the *Sekoddeśa*, the "summary of initiation". The edition's front matter gives no editor, printed source, author or date, so these particulars are not documented here.[^dsbc106]

The text opens with a salutation to the Primordial Buddha (*ādibuddha*) and works through the root text phrase by phrase, glossing terms such as the threefold and sevenfold initiations, the "great seal" (*mahāmudrā*) knowledge, the subtle channels (*nāḍī*) and the breath-control practice connected with them. It also refers to the Kālacakra tradition and to Āryapuṇḍarīkapāda.[^dsbc106] A work of similar title, the *Sekoddeśaṭīkā*, is associated with Naḍapāda, a Sanskrit form of the name of Naropa;[^wiki-naropa] whether the present text is the same as, or related to, that work has not been verified from the sources consulted. Little else about the commentary's authorship, date, translations or manuscripts could be established.
