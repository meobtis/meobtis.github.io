The *Mahāsudarśanāvadāna* is a Sanskrit *avadāna*, an edifying narrative, about King Mahāsudarśana, a wheel-turning monarch (*cakravartin*) endowed with the seven treasures and four human accomplishments. The text is divided into numbered sections and includes verse, ending with a verse on the end of rebirth for the Tathāgata.[^gretil-msuav]

The text opens with a speaker addressing a "great king" (*mahārāja*) and recalling what he did for the benefit of beings while aspiring to supreme awakening, namely having once been King Mahāsudarśana, and ends with a verse on the end of rebirth for the Tathāgata.[^gretil-msuav]

The story corresponds to the Pali *Mahāsudassana Sutta* (DN 17), whose Chinese counterpart in the *Madhyama-āgama* is MĀ 68 (大善見王).[^bucknell2014] Anālayo notes that in the Dīrgha-āgama collections the same story is not a separate discourse but part of the *Mahāparinirvāṇa* discourse, DĀ 2 in Chinese and DĀ (Skt) 6 in the Sanskrit collection.[^analayo2017a] Authorship, date and school are not stated in the edition's header, and the sources consulted here do not discuss them.[^gretil-msuav]

The Sanskrit text survives in the Gilgit manuscripts, as shown by the GBM line references embedded in the edition. These refer to the facsimile edition of the Gilgit manuscripts by Raghu Vira and Lokesh Chandra (New Delhi, 1959–1974).[^gretil-msuav] Little further is documented in the sources consulted; Tibetan translations and the text's reception were not verified.
