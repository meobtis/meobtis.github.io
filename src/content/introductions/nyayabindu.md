The *Nyāyabindu* ("Drop of Reasoning") is a work of the Buddhist epistemologist Dharmakīrti. The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy counts it among his seven works and, following Frauwallner's proposed ordering, places it after the *Pramāṇavārttika* and *Pramāṇaviniścaya* and before the *Hetubindu* and *Vādanyāya*.[^tillemans2011] The entry also notes that its chapter on perception, like that of the *Pramāṇaviniścaya*, explains yogic perception, and that Stcherbatsky's translation and study *Buddhist Logic* (1930/32), which drew on this work, helped to popularise the label "logic" for the Buddhist theory of reasons.[^tillemans2011]

Little more could be verified from the sources consulted for this entry, and details such as the work's chapter structure, its Tibetan and Chinese translations and its manuscript history are therefore not described here. The digital text is accompanied by a commentary tradition: the edition on which it is based pairs the root text with Dharmottara's commentary in Durvekamiśra's abridged form, and a separate GRETIL file combines the work with the commentaries of Vinītadeva and Dharmottara.[^gretil-nyayabindu]
