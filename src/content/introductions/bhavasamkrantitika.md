The *Bhavasaṅkrāntiṭīkā* is a brief Sanskrit commentary attributed to a "Paṇḍita Maitreyanātha". In the Tibetan Tengyur it is catalogued as Toh 3841 and is described as a word-by-word commentary on the *Bhavasaṅkrānti* treatise (Toh 3840) attributed to Nāgārjuna.[^84000toh226] That treatise is one of several similarly titled works ascribed to Nāgārjuna, each of which contains verses that loosely match the verse section of the *Bhavasaṅkrāntisūtra* ("Transmigration Through Existences"); the relationship between the sūtra and these treatises is ambiguous, and some scholars have suggested that the treatises may have influenced the sūtra's verses rather than the reverse.[^84000toh226] No established English title for the commentary was found.

The opening of the Sanskrit text shows its concerns. It discusses how a being can arise if existence does not arise from an existent or from a non-existent thing, using the images of seed and sprout, and it supports its argument with quotations that include the *Laṅkāvatāra* and the *Śālistamba* sūtras and verses ascribed to Śūra (Āryaśūra).[^gretil-bhavasamkranti] These are the themes of dependent origination and emptiness applied to the question of rebirth, and they fit the subject of the sūtra and treatises it comments on, although this introduction has not examined the whole text.

Little is documented about the commentator. The attribution to Maitreyanātha, and the identification of the commentary's author with any other figure of that name, should be treated with caution; the sources consulted do not discuss the matter, and the date of the work is not established in them. The Sanskrit source texts of the sūtra were not known until a manuscript was found in the Potala Palace in Lhasa, and the 84000 introduction notes that Sanskrit fragments of the sūtra also survive in quotation in Śāntideva's *Śikṣāsamuccaya*.[^84000toh226]

The commentary is known in Sanskrit through N. Aiyaswami Sastri's edition of the sūtra and of Nāgārjuna's treatise "with the commentary of Maitreyanātha", published by the Adyar Library in Madras in 1938 and reprinted by Jain Publishing in 2006. The catalogue record states that the Sanskrit was restored from the Tibetan and Chinese versions and edited with the Tibetan versions and English translations.[^cinii-sastri] The digital texts here derive from that printed edition.
