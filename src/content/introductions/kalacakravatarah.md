The *Kālacakrāvatāra* ("Introduction to the Wheel of Time") is a short Sanskrit work classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under *jyotiḥśāstra*, the science of the heavenly bodies. The text is headed "paṇḍita abhayākaraguptapādānām", that is, it is presented as a work of the paṇḍita Abhayākaragupta.[^dsbc838] The record itself lists no author, editor, publisher or year.[^dsbc838]

The Sanskrit consists of a short invocation of Mañjuśrī followed by technical prose and verse on calculation. Its opening says that sectarian (*tīrthika*) astronomers had hidden the *siddhānta* behind elaborate procedures, and that a simpler computational method is therefore set out. It goes on to give rules for deriving the weekday, the lunar day, the lunar mansion (*nakṣatra*), the yoga and the *karaṇa*, and then for the positions of the sun, moon and other planets, with lists of the twenty-seven mansions, the twelve signs and the yogas.[^dsbc838]

An Abhayākaragupta who was abbot of Vikramaśīla and a prolific author, active around the turn of the twelfth century, is listed in a general reference article as the author of a work of this title, but that article gives only the title and says nothing of its contents.[^wiki-abhaya] This introduction has not found independent scholarly discussion of the text, so its attribution, date and relationship to the astronomical system of the Kālacakra tradition are not established here, and the identification of the author named in the heading with the Vikramaśīla abbot should be treated as unconfirmed. No translations or printed editions have been traced.
