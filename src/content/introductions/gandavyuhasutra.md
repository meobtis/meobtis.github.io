The *Gaṇḍavyūhasūtra* is a Mahāyāna sūtra that forms the final and longest chapter of the *Buddhāvataṃsaka* (Avataṃsaka) collection. In the Tibetan Kangyur it is catalogued as Toh 44–45, and Peter Alan Roberts's English translation for 84000 is titled "The Stem Array"; it occupies 72 of the 115 fascicles of the Tibetan *Buddhāvataṃsaka*.[^84000gv] The translators divide it into 56 chapters, following the later Sanskrit division, and the Sanskrit editions digitised here follow the same division.[^84000gv] The meaning of the Sanskrit title is uncertain, and other renderings such as "Supreme Array" are also found; the work is also widely known in English as part of the *Flower Ornament Scripture*.[^wiki-gv]

The narrative follows the youth Sudhana, who travels in search of awakening and consults a succession of spiritual friends (*kalyāṇamitra*), from whom he learns different aspects of the bodhisattva path; it ends with his meeting with Samantabhadra.[^wiki-gv] The number of teachers differs between versions: the shorter early Chinese translation has 25, whereas Maitreya, in chapter 54 of the Sanskrit-based Tibetan text, gives the number as 110, and 84000's summary counts fifty.[^84000gv]

The Tibetan translation is credited in the Degé Kangyur to Yeshé Dé, Jinamitra and Surendrabodhi and is dated to about 800–836.[^84000gv] Chinese versions include one by Shengjian (between 388 and 408), those within the full *Buddhāvataṃsaka* by Buddhabhadra (420) and Śikṣānanda (695–699), and a longer independent version by Prajñā (798), the first to include the "Prayer for Completely Good Conduct".[^84000gv] The earliest extant Sanskrit manuscript is Hodgson 2, a Nepalese palm-leaf manuscript dated 1166 CE; 84000's translation is based on Vaidya's Roman-script edition, checked against Suzuki's Sanskrit edition.[^84000gv] Little else about the date and authorship of the original Indian text could be verified from the sources consulted here, and the dating of c. 200–300 CE sometimes given in tertiary sources should be treated cautiously.[^wiki-gv]
