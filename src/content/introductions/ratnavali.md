The *Ratnāvalī* ("Precious Garland", in Sanskrit also called a *rājaparikathā*, a discourse to a king) is a Mahāyāna treatise in verse attributed to Nāgārjuna. It is addressed to an Indian king, who some scholars have suggested was a Sātavāhana ruler.[^wikipedia-nagarjuna] The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy counts it among Nāgārjuna's argumentative works, though it is sometimes grouped with his epistolary writings because emptiness plays a comparatively minor part in it. It contains an extensive discussion of ethical questions.[^westerhoff-sep]

The work distinguishes the Dharma of worldly well-being and good rebirth (*abhyudaya*) from the Dharma of definitive goodness, or liberation (*naiḥśreyasa*), the first leading in due course to the second; faith is associated with the former and wisdom with the latter. This is the framework the opening verses of the text itself announce. Much of it is aimed at lay practice and the virtues of a ruler, such as generosity, non-harming and the perfections.[^wikipedia-nagarjuna]

Nāgārjuna's authorship is largely uncontested in modern scholarship,[^westerhoff-sep] although the tradition-internal lists of his authentic works are not uniform, and a date around the second or third century CE is commonly proposed.[^wikipedia-nagarjuna] Only the Sanskrit text as printed by Vaidya is available in the editions listed here; little further detail about its Sanskrit manuscripts, Tibetan and Chinese translations could be verified for this introduction, and it is therefore left out. A recent English translation is McClintock and Dunne's *Nāgārjuna's Precious Garland* (2024).[^westerhoff-sep]
