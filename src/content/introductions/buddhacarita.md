The Buddhacarita (*Buddhacarita*, "Acts of the Buddha"; also rendered "Life of the Buddha") is a Sanskrit poem on the life of the Buddha attributed to the Buddhist monk Aśvaghoṣa. Its publisher's description calls it the earliest surviving text of the Sanskrit literary genre of kāvya, and places its composition in the first or second century CE, probably in Ayodhya.[^olivelle2008] The Sanskrit editions available here run to fourteen cantos, beginning with the king Śuddhodana, the queen Māyā and the birth of the future Buddha.[^dsbc222][^gretil-bc-alt]

According to the same description, the poem presents the Buddha's teaching as the culmination of the Brahmanical tradition, and gives particular attention to Prince Siddhārtha's confrontation with sickness, old age and death.[^olivelle2008] Little else about the work could be verified from the sources consulted for this page. Fuller information on the lost Sanskrit portion, the Tibetan and Chinese translations, and the manuscript history was not confirmed in a source that could be cited here, and is therefore omitted.

Johnston's 1935 edition and Cowell's 1893 edition are the printed sources named for the digital texts, while the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon text derives from a 1995 Sarnath edition.[^gretil-bc][^gretil-bc-alt][^dsbc222]
