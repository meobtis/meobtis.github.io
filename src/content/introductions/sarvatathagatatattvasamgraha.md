The *Sarvatathāgatatattvasaṃgraha*, often called the *Tattvasaṃgraha Tantra* and rendered into English as the "Compendium of Principles" (also "Compendium of the Reality of All Tathāgatas"), is an Indian Buddhist tantric scripture. Although it calls itself a Mahāyāna sūtra, its contents are mainly tantric, and it is usually dated to the seventh century.[^wiki-tattva] It is classed among the Yoga Tantras, and the Tibetan canon has it under Toh 479, where 84000 titles it "The Compendium of Realities".[^84000toh479]

The work is important chiefly as a source for the esoteric Buddhism of China and Japan, where its teaching underlies the Shingon tradition, and for the development of Yoga Tantra in India, Tibet and Sumatra.[^wiki-tattva] It survives in Sanskrit, Tibetan and Chinese. The opening of the Sanskrit text, as edited in the editions below, places the Buddha Vairocana in the palace of the Akaniṣṭha gods with a host of bodhisattvas, among them Vajrapāṇi, Avalokiteśvara, Ākāśagarbha and Mañjuśrī, and introduces the Vajradhātu maṇḍala.[^gretil-sarvatathagata]

Modern study in Western languages began with Giuseppe Tucci's work on the myth of the subjugation of Maheśvara contained in the text; David Snellgrove later published a facsimile of one of the surviving Sanskrit manuscripts with an introduction, and Dale Todaro has translated the first section.[^wiki-tattva] Beyond these points, little more could be verified from the sources consulted here. Details such as the exact structure of the text, the Tibetan and Chinese translators and the manuscript witnesses should be checked against specialist studies, such as those of Weinberger, Snellgrove and Lokesh Chandra's introduction.
