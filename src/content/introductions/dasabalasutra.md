The *Daśabalasūtra* ("Discourse on the Ten Powers") is a short sūtra on the ten powers (*daśabala*) that a Tathāgata, a perfectly awakened Buddha, possesses. The text opens in the usual form, with the Buddha addressing the monks at Śrāvastī, in the Jetavana, the park of Anāthapiṇḍada, and announcing the ten powers by which a Tathāgata claims his lofty position.[^gretil-dasabala]

The version indexed here is not a single text but four related versions, numbered 1 to 4 in the digital edition. The source information records that versions 1 and 2 rest on editions by Ernst Waldschmidt, published in 1932 and 1958 (the latter under the title "Ein zweites Daśabalasūtra"), and that versions 3 and 4 rest on Jin-il Chung's 2009 article "Ein drittes und ein viertes Daśabala-Sūtra".[^gretil-dasabala] Waldschmidt's 1932 volume belongs to his series of fragments of Buddhist sūtras from the Central Asian Sanskrit canon, which suggests that the material derives from manuscript fragments from that region, although this page does not describe the manuscripts themselves. Anālayo identifies a Chinese *Saṃyukta-āgama* discourse, SĀ 348, as a parallel to the Pali *Dasabalasutta* (SN 12.21); like the Sanskrit fragment edited by Waldschmidt in 1958, it says that the Tathāgata, endowed with the ten powers and four intrepidities, "knows the place of former Buddhas", which he takes to reflect an early exegetical reading of the "bull's place" formula in the Pali text.[^analayo2015] Date and school affiliation of the Sanskrit versions are not stated in the sources consulted.
