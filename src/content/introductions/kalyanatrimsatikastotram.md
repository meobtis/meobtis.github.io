The *Kalyāṇatriṃśatikāstotra* ("Hymn of Thirty Verses on Auspiciousness") is a short Sanskrit Buddhist hymn (*stotra*) that the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files under the hymns to Vajrayoginī.[^dsbc211] The title suggests thirty verses, and the text as keyed has thirty numbered verses followed by a colophon. The first verse is an invocation, and verses 2 to 29 each begin with "yā devī" ("the goddess who …") and share a refrain of homage to her. Verse 30 states the fruit of reciting the hymn, and the colophon names the goddess as Vajradevī dwelling in the three bodies (*trikāya*).[^dsbc211]

The praises address the goddess as Vajrayoginī (verse 17) and describe her as protector of beings in the various realms of rebirth, destroyer of fears such as kings, thieves, fire and lions, and mother of beings, and as present in the sun, moon and pilgrimage sites (*pīṭha*).[^dsbc211] No scholarly study, translation, authorship, date, or Tibetan or Chinese counterpart for this hymn could be found in the sources consulted, so little is documented beyond the edition information given here. It should not be assumed to be identical to any better-known Vajrayoginī hymn.
