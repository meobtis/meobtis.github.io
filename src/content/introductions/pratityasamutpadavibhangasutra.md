The *Pratītyasamutpādavibhaṅgasūtra* is a short discourse of the Buddha on dependent arising (*pratītyasamutpāda*), the chain of twelve links from ignorance to ageing and death. The Sanskrit text opens in the usual way, with the Buddha at Śrāvastī in Anāthapiṇḍada's park, and announces that he will teach both the "beginning" (*ādi*) and the detailed analysis (*vibhaṅga*) of dependent arising, which explains the title.[^gretil-pvs] In English the sūtra is known from the 84000 translation as *Teaching the Fundamental Exposition and Detailed Analysis of Dependent Arising* (Toh 211), whose Sanskrit title is given as *Pratītyasamutpādādivibhaṅganirdeśa*.[^84000toh211]

After stating the formula in its standard form ("when this exists, that comes to be"), the text takes each link in turn and defines it. The Sanskrit passage on ignorance, for example, lists the kinds of not-knowing involved: of the past and the future, of what is internal and external, of action and its result, and of the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha.[^gretil-pvs] The sūtra is therefore a compact doctrinal exposition rather than a narrative.

The 84000 introduction records that the Sanskrit survives as part of the *Nidānasaṃyukta* collection. It was translated into Chinese several times: by Kumārajīva in the early fifth century (Taishō 123), by Xuanzang in 661 CE (Taishō 124), and, within the *Saṃyuktāgama*, by Guṇabhadra in the early fifth century (Taishō 99). The Tibetan translation has no colophon, so its translator and date are unknown, but it is listed in the Denkarma inventory, which is thought to date from 812 CE.[^84000toh211] The Sanskrit text edited here comes from a different kind of witness, a version inscribed on bricks found at Nālandā, which Chung edited in 2017.[^gretil-pvs]

Little more about this particular Sanskrit witness could be verified from the sources consulted, and the date of the Nālandā inscription is not discussed here.
