The *Mahāmāyāsādhana* ("Practice of Mahāmāyā") is a short Sanskrit liturgy for meditation on the deity Mahāmāyā. It opens by describing the deity as four-faced and four-armed, embracing a consort, with four further goddesses in the cardinal directions, and then presents the practice in prose with verses. The text as transmitted instructs the practitioner to purify himself, take refuge, generate the aspiration to awakening, visualise a vajra enclosure and a palace with a lotus at its centre, and generate the four ḍākinīs (Vajra, Ratna, Padma and Viśva) before the deity Heruka appears, accompanied by Buddhaḍākinī.[^dsbc345] The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the commentarial works on yoginī tantras.[^dsbc345]

Mahāmāyā is the central deity of the *Mahāmāyātantra*, a yoginī tantra of the Unexcelled Yoga class that probably emerged in Buddhist tantric circles in the late ninth or early tenth century. Its Sanskrit original does not survive, but the commentary by Ratnākaraśānti preserves much of its Sanskrit terminology, and the tantra was translated into Tibetan by Jinavara and Gö Lhetsé (Toh 425). Several sādhanas for Mahāmāyā are known, attributed to figures such as Kukkuripa, Kṛṣṇācārya, Ratnākaraśānti and Ratnavajra.[^84000toh425] The sources consulted do not say which of these, if any, composed the present text, nor give its date or manuscript source, so little more can be said about its authorship and history here.
