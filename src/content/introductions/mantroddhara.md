The *Mantroddhāra* ("extraction of the mantras") is a short Sanskrit tantric text transmitted in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the heading of commentaries on Yoginītantras (*yoginītantraṭīkā*).[^dsbc778] Its bibliographic record is sparse: the "editor" field repeats the title, and the publisher, place and year are left blank, so no printed edition or manuscript is identified.[^dsbc778]

The opening lines of the text show that it concerns the Guhyasamāja tradition. It introduces itself as setting out the order of the practice-method (*sādhanopāyikā*) of the Guhyasamāja great yoga tantra, and cites a verse listing four stages: first the determination of the mantras, second the teaching of their placement (*nyāsa*), third the arrangement of the circle of deities, and fourth the vision of reality (*tattva*). The text then explains that the mantra syllables are drawn out from the components of the practice, fixed in the mind, and placed on the practitioner's own body in the aggregates, elements and sense-bases in the forms of Vairocana and the other deities.[^dsbc778]

Beyond what the edition itself shows, little reliable information about the work was found: no author, date, Tibetan or Chinese translation, or manuscript could be verified from the sources consulted, and the category label in the digital canon should not be taken as settled evidence of the work's genre. Readers should treat the identification of the text with any similarly named work as unconfirmed.
