The *Navaślokī* ("Nine Verses") is a short Sanskrit poem on the Perfection of Wisdom (*prajñāpāramitā*). In the digital edition it is headed as the "essential meaning" (*piṇḍārtha*) of the *Āryāṣṭasāhasrikā prajñāpāramitā*, the Perfection of Wisdom in Eight Thousand Lines, and its colophon names its author as Śrī Kambalāmbarapāda. The text as edited runs to fourteen verses: four introductory verses that present the Perfection of Wisdom as the "mother" of all buddhas, free of conceptual elaboration and beyond apprehension, followed by nine numbered verses that use familiar similes (an illusion, a dream, an echo, a mirage, a reflection, the moon in water) to show that perceived phenomena lack an inherent existence, and a closing verse on the aspiration to awakening.[^gretil-navasloki]

Kambalapāda (also called Kambala or Lawapa in Tibetan) is known in the Buddhist tantric tradition as a mahāsiddha placed in about the tenth century, to whom songs and a *Kambalagītikā* have been attributed.[^wiki-kambalapada] Whether the author of this poem is the same person, and where and when it was composed, is not established by the sources consulted here. Little documentation on the work itself, including any Tibetan or Chinese translation or any manuscript, could be found in the research behind this introduction, so none is described.
