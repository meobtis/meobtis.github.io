# catuṣpariṣat sūtram (The Sūtra of the Four Assemblies)

The Sūtra of the Four Assemblies

The bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Urubilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of `bodhi` (awakening), acting continuously and prudently, dwells devoted to the practice of cultivation in the qualities pertaining to awakening. In the first watch of the night, he directs his `citta` (mind) to the `abhijñā` (experiential understanding) of the direct realisation of the `jñāna` (knowledge) of the domain of extraordinary abilities, and he experiences the manifold domain of extraordinary abilities. Namely, having been one, he becomes many; having been many, he becomes one; he becomes manifest, and through knowledge and vision he experiences disappearance. Through walls, through rocks, and through ramparts he goes unhindered with his body, just as in `ākāśa` (space); he rises up and sinks down in the earth just as in water. He goes unhindered on water with his body just as on the earth. He moves cross-legged through ākāśa just as a winged bird. Even these moon and sun, so greatly powerful, of such great might, he touches and strokes with his hand, and he exercises mastery with his body even up to the world of Brahmā. Thus the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Uruvilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of awakening, acting continuously and prudently, dwelling devoted to the practice of cultivation in the qualities pertaining to awakening, experiences the manifold domain of extraordinary abilities in the first watch of the night. Then the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Uruvilvā, as before, and so forth up to, dwells devoted. In the first watch of the night, he directs his citta to the abhijñā of the direct realisation of the knowledge of the recollection of past abodes, and he recollects manifold past abodes. Namely, he recollects one `jāti` (birth), two, three, four, and so forth up to even many aeons of contraction. Thus the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Uruvilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of awakening, acting continuously and prudently, dwelling devoted to the practice of cultivation in the qualities pertaining to awakening, recollects past abodes in the first watch of the night. Then the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Uruvilvā, as before, and so forth up to, dwells devoted. In the middle watch of the night, he directs his citta to the abhijñā of the direct realisation of the knowledge of the celestial ear; with the purified celestial ear that surpasses the human, he hears both, and human ones, whether those far away or those nearby. Thus the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Uruvilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of awakening, acting continuously and prudently, dwelling devoted to the practice of cultivation in the qualities pertaining to awakening, experiences the knowledge of the celestial ear in the middle watch of the night. Then the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Uruvilvā, as before, and so forth up to, dwells devoted. In the middle watch of the night, he directs his citta to the knowledge of the direct realisation of the knowledge of the celestial eye. With the purified celestial eye that surpasses the human, he sees beings passing away and rearising, fair and ugly, inferior and superior, going to a good destination and to a bad destination too; he understands as they really are beings faring according to their actions. Thus: these worthy beings endowed with misconduct of body, endowed with misconduct of speech and mind, revilers of the āryas, holders of wrong `dṛṣṭi` (view), because of the `hetu` (primary cause) of undertaking `karman` (actions) and practices of wrong view, with that as primary cause and with that as cause, upon the breakup of the body, after death, rearise in a place of deprivation, a bad destination, a downfall, in the hells. Or again, these worthy beings endowed with good conduct of body, endowed with good conduct of speech and mind, not revilers of the āryas, holders of `samyagdṛṣṭi` (right view), because of the hetu of undertaking actions and practices of right view, with that as hetu and with that as cause, upon the breakup of the body, rearise in a good destination, in the heavenly world, among the devas. Thus the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Urubilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of awakening, acting continuously and prudently, dwelling devoted to the practice of cultivation in the qualities pertaining to awakening, experiences the knowledge of the celestial eye in the middle watch of the night. Then the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Urubilvā, as before, and so forth up to, dwells devoted. In the last watch of the night, he directs his citta to the experiential understanding of the direct realisation of the knowledge of the ways of others' cittas. He understands as it really is what is thought, what is deliberated, and the mental state by citta of other `sattva` (beings) and of other `pudgala` (persons); he understands as it really is a citta with `rāga` (passion) as a citta with passion. He understands as it really is a mind free of passion as free of passion; and likewise a mind with `dveṣa` (hatred), free of hatred, with `moha` (delusion), free of delusion, distracted, contracted, sluggish, exerted.

Agitated, not agitated, unquieted, quieted, focused, unfocused, uncultivated, cultivated, and an unliberated citta he understands as it really is as an unliberated citta. Thus the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Urubilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of awakening, acting continuously and prudently, dwelling devoted to the practice of cultivation in the qualities pertaining to awakening, experiences the knowledge of the ways of others' minds in the last watch of the night. Then the bodhisattva Bhagavān, dwelling at Urubilvā, as before, and so forth up to, dwells devoted. In the last watch of the night, he directs his citta to the abhijñā of the direct realisation of the knowledge of the destruction of the `āsrava` (defilements). He understands as it really is: 'This is the `āryasatya` (realisation) of `duḥkha` (dissatisfaction).' 'This is the `samudaya` (origin) of dissatisfaction.' 'This is the `nirodha` (cessation) of dissatisfaction.' He understands as it really is: 'This is the āryasatya of the path leading to the cessation of dissatisfaction.' The citta of him who thus knows and thus sees is liberated from the defilement of sensual desire; his citta is liberated from the defilement of `bhava` (existence) and from the defilement of `avidyā` (ignorance). In him who is liberated, there is the knowledge and vision: 'I am liberated.' 'Jāti is destroyed for me, `brahmacarya` (the optimal life) has been lived, what had to be done has been done, and I understand there is no further bhava beyond this.' Thus Bhagavān, dwelling at Urubilvā on the bank of the river Nairañjanā at the root of awakening, having accomplished what was to be accomplished, having done what had to be done, having fully awakened to awakening, entered into the fire `dhātu` (component).
