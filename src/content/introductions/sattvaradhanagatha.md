The *Sattvārādhanagāthā* ("verses on the propitiation of beings") is a short poem in Sanskrit verse. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is filed among the hymns (*stotra*) addressed to Śākyamuni, and the digitised text runs to eleven numbered verses.[^dsbc187] The verses take the form of a first-person address in which the speaker argues that service to living beings is the proper way of honouring the Buddha: compassion toward beings, rather than ritual alone, is presented as what pleases the speaker, and the text asks its hearers to keep the teaching alive for the sake of others.[^dsbc187]

Little reliable information about the work could be found beyond its own text and edition record. The heading of the digitised text names the Buddhist author Nāgārjuna as its composer, while its closing line describes the verses as spoken by the fully awakened one; these two statements are not reconciled in the text, and no independent scholarship on the attribution, date, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation was consulted, so none is asserted here.[^dsbc187]
