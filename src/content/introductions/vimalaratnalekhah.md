The *Vimalaratnalekha* ("Letter of the Stainless Jewel") is a short Sanskrit letter in verse. The opening of the digital text gives its Sanskrit title and a Tibetan title (transliterated as *dri ma med pa rin po che-i phrin yig*), and its first verses refer to a king, Nayapāla, who protected the Buddhist teaching and ruled in accordance with the Dharma.[^dsbc879]

Reference works list the *Vimalaratnalekha* among the writings of the Indian Buddhist master Atiśa Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna (c. 982–1054), describing it as a Sanskrit letter addressed to Nayapāla, king of Magadha.[^banglapedia] Atiśa is said to have left India for Tibet in 1040 and to have remained there until his death; the same source notes that several of his Sanskrit works survive only in Tibetan translation.[^banglapedia] Little else about the letter is documented in the sources consulted: its date, the circumstances of its composition, its manuscript tradition and its Tibetan and Chinese translations were not established here, and the attribution should be treated as traditional rather than proven.
