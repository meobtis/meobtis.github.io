The *Bhadracaryādeśanā* is a short Sanskrit text in verse, listed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the hymns (*stotra*) and given as edited by Jes Peter Asmussen (Copenhagen, 1961).[^dsbc842] The title can be rendered roughly as "the exposition of good conduct". Asmussen's book is known from other citations as *The Khotanese Bhadracaryādeśanā*,[^vasylieva2024] so the Sanskrit text in this edition is presumably the Sanskrit counterpart edited alongside the Khotanese version; the edition's own front matter does not say so.

The verses open with homage to all the buddhas of the ten directions and the three times, offered with body, speech and mind, and the second verse names the "bhadra-carī-praṇidhāna", the vow of good conduct.[^dsbc842] This is the vocabulary of the *Bhadracarī* (*Bhadracaryāpraṇidhāna*), a 62-verse aspiration prayer that concludes the *Gaṇḍavyūhasūtra*, the last part of the *Avataṃsakasūtra*, and also circulates as an independent text.[^anandajoti2018] A recent study treats the *Bhadracaryāpraṇidhāna* as one of the most important aspiration prayers of Mahāyāna Buddhism and examines its Sanskrit versions and its Tibetan translations, including one among the Dunhuang manuscripts.[^vasylieva2024]

How the *Bhadracaryādeśanā* relates to the *Bhadracarī* in detail, and its date, authorship and transmission, are not established by the sources consulted here; little is documented beyond the edition's bibliographic record. Readers should compare it with the standard *Bhadracarī* editions before relying on it as a distinct work.
