The *Khasarpaṇasādhana* is a brief Sanskrit prose manual of visualisation and worship (*sādhana*) devoted to Khasarpaṇa, a form of the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara. It opens with an origin story: a lay devotee named Śubhaṅkara, on his way to Potalaka, stayed in a village called Khasarpaṇa in the Khāḍī region, where Avalokiteśvara told him not to go on but to install the deity there according to the procedure of the *Vairocanābhisambodhi* tantra, for the great benefit of beings. The text closes with a colophon stating that it was composed by Śubhaṅkara.[^gretil-khs]

The practice described begins with right view and the contemplation of the emptiness of all phenomena, followed by compassionate resolve to relieve beings' suffering. The practitioner places five seed syllables in the heart, offers the sevenfold worship and cultivates the four *brahmavihāras*, then generates himself as Avalokiteśvara, white like the autumn moon, with Amitābha in the hair, holding a lotus and bestowing nectar. The deity is accompanied by Tārā, Sudhanakumāra, Bhṛkuṭī and Hayagrīva, a group of five. The text also gives mantras for protection, offerings, homage, dismissal and a bali offering, and ends with a short moral exhortation on purifying the mind of desire, hatred and delusion.[^gretil-khs]

Little reliable information on this work could be gathered beyond the edition itself: its date, Śubhaṅkara's identity, any Tibetan or Chinese translation and its place in the wider Khasarpaṇa tradition are not documented in the sources consulted, and are therefore left out here. The Sanskrit text is available through the GRETIL corpus, based on the 1903 edition by Thomas.[^gretil-khs]
