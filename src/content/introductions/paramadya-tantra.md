The Paramādya-tantra (*Paramādya-tantra*) is a short Sanskrit Buddhist tantric text. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is catalogued among the works of the *śāstrapiṭaka* in the category *yogottaratantra*, that is, the "higher" or "later" Yoga tantras.[^dsbc971] The text opens with the title *paramādya-tantra* and the sub-title *mahāsukhavajraguhya*, and its first verses address Vajrasattva and Samantabhadra, warn against the passions of the three realms, and announce an exposition of a means of accomplishment (*sādhana*) leading to lasting success (*siddhi*).[^dsbc971]

Little reliable information about this work could be found in the sources consulted. Its authorship, date, place of composition and its Tibetan and Chinese translations are not documented in the catalogue records, and no printed edition is named there: the bibliographic fields of the record are left blank.[^dsbc971] The text should therefore be read as a transcription whose provenance is not stated, and any identification with a work in the Tibetan canon would need to be confirmed against the text itself.
