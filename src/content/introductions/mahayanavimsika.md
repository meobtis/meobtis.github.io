The *Mahāyānaviṃśikā* ("Twenty [Verses] on the Great Vehicle") is a short Sanskrit work in verse. The text as keyed in the digital editions is incomplete or irregularly numbered: it opens with seven verses, then gives a second, separately numbered series, with a gap marked in the text after verse 7.[^gretil-mvim] Little detailed scholarship on it could be found in the sources consulted, and no established English title was identified.

Judging from the Sanskrit text itself, the opening verses discuss emptiness as the absence of duality in cognition, speak of "mind only" (*cittamātra*) and of the seven bodhisattva stages (*bhūmi*) with an eighth, and the later verses compare beings and phenomena to illusions and reflections, describe saṃsāra as produced by false imagining, and end with the buddhas, moved by compassion, establishing beings in awakening.[^gretil-mvim] This summary is drawn from a reading of the verses, not from a commentary.

The work is traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna, and both digital editions are catalogued under his name or in the Madhyamaka section.[^gretil-mvim][^dsbc245] According to a Wikipedia article on Nāgārjuna, which reports the views of Christian Lindtner and K. Ruegg, it is cited as his in the *Tattvasaṃgraha*, is also cited by Atiśa, and is among works of uncertain authenticity that Lindtner thought "perhaps authentic".[^wiki-nagarjuna] As that is a tertiary source, its claims should be checked against the primary scholarship. Tibetan and Chinese translations, manuscripts and commentaries were not verified here.
