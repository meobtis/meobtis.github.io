The *Madhyamakāvatāra* ("Entry into the Middle Way", commonly *Introduction to the Middle Way*) is a work of the Indian Buddhist philosopher Candrakīrti, who is placed at the beginning of the seventh century.[^hayes2023] It is a verse text accompanied by a prose commentary by Candrakīrti himself, the *Madhyamakāvatārabhāṣya*.[^hayes2023]

Its ten chapters correspond to the ten stages (*bhūmi*) of the bodhisattva path, each centred on a virtue to be perfected. The sixth chapter, on wisdom, contains Candrakīrti's most detailed exposition of Madhyamaka philosophy, and the work thus joins the doctrine of emptiness to the practice of the path.[^hayes2023]

According to the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, the work is available only in Tibetan translation, so no Sanskrit original survives.[^hayes2023] The same source says it predates Candrakīrti's commentary on Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*, which repeatedly refers readers to it for fuller arguments.[^hayes2023] The Sanskrit editions in this collection should therefore be understood in that light: they open with a Sanskrit title paired with a Tibetan one, which suggests a Sanskrit rendering made from the Tibetan, and their precise provenance is not documented in the records consulted.[^dsbc243]

English translations exist, including one by the Padmakara Translation Group accompanied by the commentary of Jamgön Mipham.[^hayes2023]
Little further detail on the Sanskrit editions could be verified from the sources available.
