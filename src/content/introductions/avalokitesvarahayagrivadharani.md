The *Avalokiteśvarahayagrīvadhāraṇī* is a short Sanskrit text in the form of a dhāraṇī, a formula of invocation and protection. It opens with a series of salutations to the Three Jewels and to the bodhisattva Avalokiteśvara, praised as one who destroys the sufferings of beings, removes fear and cuts bonds, and it then presents the formula as uttered from Avalokiteśvara's own mouth. The main body consists of mantra phrases addressed to Hayagrīva, with repeated imperatives and a series of invocations ending in *phaṭ* and *svāhā*; the opening salutations and the stated aims of the formula (protection and the removal of obstacles and poisons) can be read directly in the text itself.[^dsbc120]

Very little is documented about the work beyond its inclusion in a modern printed anthology. The digital record gives no author, date or place of composition, and searches did not locate a Tibetan or Chinese counterpart or a manuscript description for this title under this name, so its origin and transmission remain unknown here. Related but distinct Avalokiteśvara dhāraṇīs exist in the Tibetan canon, but they should not be assumed to be the same work. The digital edition derives from Pandey's collection of Buddhist hymns, in which the text appears among Avalokiteśvara-related items.[^dsbc120]
