The *Madhyamakahṛdaya* (more fully *Madhyamakahṛdayakārikā*, "Verses on the Heart of the Middle Way") is a verse treatise in eleven chapters attributed to the Madhyamaka master Bhāviveka, also called Bhavya. Bhāviveka also wrote an auto-commentary on it, the *Tarkajvālā* ("Blaze of Reasoning"). The first three chapters present his own Madhyamaka position, beginning with the aspiration to awakening and the ascetic's vow and turning to the search for knowledge of reality. The remaining chapters survey and criticise other doctrines: those of the Śrāvakas, the Yogācāras, the Sāṃkhyas, the Vaiśeṣikas, the Vedānta and the Mīmāṃsā, followed by a chapter on omniscience (which touches on Jain views) and a closing chapter of praise and characteristics.[^wiki-bhaviveka]

Bhāviveka is generally dated to roughly the sixth century; one scholar's estimate, cited in a reference work, is about 500–560 CE, and parts of the *Tarkajvālā* may be the work of a later author.[^wiki-bhaviveka] These details come from a tertiary source only, and no further scholarly confirmation could be consulted for this introduction, so they should be treated with caution.

The work does not survive complete in Sanskrit. An incomplete Sanskrit manuscript exists, and a Tibetan translation by Atiśa and the translator Jayaśīla has allowed a full Sanskrit text to be reconstructed.[^wiki-bhaviveka] English translations are partial and appear in studies of individual chapters, including Malcolm David Eckel's rendering of chapters 4 and 5 with the *Tarkajvālā*, and Christian Lindtner's edition and study of the Mīmāṃsā chapter.[^wiki-bhaviveka] The text offered here is the one catalogued as edited by Lindtner and published at Adyar in 2001.[^dsbc240] Little else about this particular digital edition is documented.
