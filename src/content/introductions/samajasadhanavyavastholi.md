The *Samājasādhanavyavastholi* is a short Sanskrit prose text that the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon classifies among its commentarial (ṭīkā) works on the Yogottara tantras.[^dsbc758] It opens with homage to Vajrasattva and announces itself as a statement of the order (*vyavastholi*) of the limbs of the Samāja practice, which, judging from the cycle it refers to, is the Guhyasamāja tradition. The text then runs through the stages of a visualised sādhana: the practitioner dissolves ordinary entities into emptiness, builds up the successive elemental maṇḍalas, begins from the wind maṇḍala, and goes on to the palace (*kūṭāgāra*) and the deities, beginning with Akṣobhya.[^dsbc758]

The sources consulted state neither the author nor the date or place of composition, and the editions give no translation, manuscript description or parallel Tibetan title. Only the printed edition is known: Kimiaki Tanaka's edition, published in Tokyo in 2016.[^dsbc758] Little else about the work is documented in the sources available, so its relationship to the better-known Guhyasamāja ritual and commentarial literature remains to be established.
