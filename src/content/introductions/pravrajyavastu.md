The *Pravrajyāvastu* ("the chapter on going forth", often rendered *The Chapter on Going Forth*) is the first of the seventeen chapters of the *Vinayavastu*, the first of the four scriptural divisions of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya.[^84000toh11] It deals with the entry of men into the Buddhist renunciant order and follows how the rite of admission developed from the Buddha's simple invitation, "Come, monk", to a more elaborate formal procedure. Along the way it introduces the roles of preceptor and instructor and recounts the difficulties caused by untrained novices.[^84000toh11]

According to the 84000 introduction, the chapter falls into three broad parts: the spiritual quest of Upatiṣya (Śāriputra) and Kolita (Maudgalyāyana) and their meetings with rival teachers; the stages of the admission rite itself; and the origin stories behind the "impediments" that disqualify candidates, including the story of Saṅgharakṣita and a nāga who sought ordination.[^84000toh11]

The complete Sanskrit *Vinayavastu* is lost. The only complete version is the ninth-century Tibetan translation, and a partial Chinese translation by Yijing covers at least seven of the chapters.[^84000toh11] In 1931 a chest of Buddhist manuscripts, dated to about the fifth or sixth century CE, was found near Gilgit, and most of its Vinaya material belongs to the *Vinayavastu*. For this chapter, Sanskrit survives only in fragments at the beginning (folios 2–12) and the end (folios 43–53) of the manuscript, together corresponding to about one hundred of the 261 pages of the chapter in the Degé Kangyur.[^84000toh11] Claus Vogel and Klaus Wille published these fragments in successive instalments, including the final leaves with the *Saṃgharakṣitāvadāna* and the *Nāgakumārāvadāna*.[^gretil-pravr]

Little else about the Sanskrit fragments could be consulted in preparing this note, so questions of date and authorship beyond the manuscript's approximate age are not addressed here. An English translation from the Tibetan, with a draft translation of the surviving Sanskrit portions, is available from 84000.[^84000toh11]
