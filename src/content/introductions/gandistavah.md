The *Gaṇḍīstava* ("Hymn of the Gaṇḍī") is a short Sanskrit verse text of the stotra (praise-poem) genre. It is catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the stotra section of the śāstrapiṭaka, in a sub-category named for the "gaṇḍībhaṭṭāraka", and it is a single unit without chapter divisions.[^dsbc150] The gaṇḍī is a percussion instrument used in Buddhist monasteries to call the community together; the hymn's opening verses refer to its sound as the signal for all beings to assemble and listen.[^dsbc150]

In the text as keyed, the heading ascribes the work to the teacher (ācārya) Āryadeva.[^dsbc150] The opening verses address gods, humans, garuḍas, demons, gandharvas, yakṣas, siddhas, vidyādharas and nāgas, who are invited to hear the sound of the gaṇḍī, and later verses praise the Buddha, lord of the three worlds, and the Śākya lion.[^dsbc150] The text also includes onomatopoeic syllables imitating the instrument's sound.[^dsbc150] Beyond this front matter and the text itself, little reliable scholarly information on its date, the identity of its author, or any Tibetan or Chinese translations could be found, so the attribution is reported here as given in the edition and not independently verified.
