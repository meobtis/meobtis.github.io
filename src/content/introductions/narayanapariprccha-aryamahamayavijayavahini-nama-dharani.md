The *Nārāyaṇaparipṛcchā* ("Nārāyaṇa's Questions"), whose full title in the digital edition is *Āryamahāmāyāvijayavāhinī nāma dhāraṇī*, is a short Sanskrit text of the dhāraṇī type, a genre of Buddhist protective and incantatory literature. The edition, which is the only one on which this account rests, was published in 1941 by A. C. Banerjee at the University of Calcutta under the title *Nārāyaṇaparipṛcchā sutra*.[^dsbc18]

The text opens in the usual sūtra manner ("Thus have I heard") with the Buddha dwelling on a golden-peaked mountain at the house of Vaiśravaṇa. The god Nārāyaṇa, who has been defeated and robbed of his power by the asuras, approaches him and asks for a teaching that will bring victory in battle and protection from calamities to all who hold it. The Buddha then begins a narrative of a past age, set in Magadha and involving a king named Ratnaśrī and a Tathāgata named Sarveśvara, which leads into the dhāraṇī itself.[^dsbc18]

Little reliable information about this work could be gathered beyond the edition's own front matter and text. Its date, place of composition, any Tibetan or Chinese translations, and its manuscript transmission are not documented in the sources consulted, and no established English title was found, so none is given here.
