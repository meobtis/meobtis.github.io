The *Mahogratārāstutiḥ* ("Hymn of Praise to the Greatly Fierce Tārā") is a brief Sanskrit stotra addressed to Ugratārā, the "fierce" form of the goddess Tārā. In the digital text it opens with a salutation to Śrī Ugratārā and an introductory verse describing her terrifying appearance, followed by fifteen numbered verses, and closes with a colophon.[^dsbc202] No established English title appears to exist.

The introductory verse portrays the goddess with bared fangs, a roar like thunderclouds, a garland of human heads, a sword and a skull-cup. The numbered verses mostly repeat the refrain of taking refuge in her. They praise her as a destroyer of enemies and a giver of boons, as one honoured by gods, demons and perfected beings, and as identical with other goddesses such as Gaurī, Lakṣmī, Umā and Sarasvatī; the speaker confesses weakness and ignorance and asks for protection. The final verses promise that reciting the hymn on particular lunar days brings success, liberation, wealth or learning, and that it protects in battle and in danger.[^dsbc202]

Ugratārā is known as one of the best-known forms of Tārā, alongside Ekajaṭā and Nīlasarasvatī.[^wiki-ugra] She is worshipped in both Buddhist and Hindu tantric traditions, and the goddess Mahācīnakrama-Tārā, also called Ugra-Tārā, has been studied by Bühnemann in that dual context.[^buhnemann1996] I did not find reliable information on the author, date or place of composition of this particular hymn, nor on its manuscripts or translations; its identification as Buddhist rests on its inclusion in a collection of Buddhist hymns and in the DSBC's Buddhist canon, and its content (for example the equation of the goddess with Hindu goddesses) shows the overlap between the two traditions.[^dsbc202]
