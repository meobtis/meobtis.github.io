The *Jñānasiddhi* ("Accomplishment of Knowledge") is a Sanskrit Buddhist treatise on Vajrayāna practice. In the digital text it is divided into twenty chapters (*paricchedas*), and the colophon formula "jñānasiddhau …" closes chapters with titles such as the denial of meditation on form and the characteristics of the disciple. The text opens with an homage to Vajrasattva and a verse praising the "protector of the world" (*jagannātha*) before announcing a supreme method of accomplishment (*sādhana*) taught in the *yogatantras*, and it closes by stating that it is the work of Indrabhūti.[^dsbc860] An English title is not established in the sources consulted, so only the Sanskrit name is given here.

The work is attributed to a King Indrabhūti, and a secondary source quotes the modern scholar Donaldson as calling it his "famous work" and noting its opening invocation of Jagannātha, a figure linked to Orissa.[^wiki-indrabhuti] The same source stresses that the name Indrabhūti covers several figures in Buddhist tradition and gives no single date for the author, so the identification, date and place of composition are uncertain and are not settled here.[^wiki-indrabhuti] Little further reliable information on the text's contents, its Tibetan or Chinese translations, or its reception could be found in the sources available, and it is best approached through the printed edition and the Sanskrit text itself.

The printed edition behind both digital versions is that of Benoytosh Bhattacharyya, published as "Two Vajrayāna Works" by the Oriental Institute in Baroda in 1929.[^dsbc860]
