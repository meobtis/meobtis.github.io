The *Vinayasūtra* is a Sanskrit text on monastic discipline (*vinaya*) in the form of concise aphorisms (*sūtra*). The text itself names its author as the venerable Guṇaprabha and its school as the Mūlasarvāstivāda, and the surviving Sanskrit manuscript, written in Tibetan script, is described in the GRETIL record as a manuscript of 62 folios found in Tibet by Rahula Sankrityayana.[^gretil-vinayasutra] The edition used for the other digital texts presents the sūtras together with an auto-commentary (*svavyākhyāna*) in Sanskrit, and is organised by the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinaya, beginning with the *pravrajyāvastu*, which treats going forth as a novice (*śrāmaṇera*) and ordination.[^gretil-vinayasutra-1][^dsbc93]

The Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya is the monastic code followed in Tibetan Buddhism and in the Himalayan regions that share its tradition; it survives in full in Tibetan, in a Chinese translation, and in an incomplete Sanskrit manuscript.[^wikivinaya] Beyond these editorial facts, little reliable information on the *Vinayasūtra* could be verified here: its date, the dates of Guṇaprabha, its Tibetan translation and its commentarial reception are not documented in the sources consulted, and so are not stated.
