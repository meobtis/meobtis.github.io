The *Ratnakīrtinibandhāvali* ("Garland of Ratnakīrti's Treatises") is a modern editorial collection of short Sanskrit philosophical works by Ratnakīrti, a Buddhist monk and teacher of the eleventh century.[^mcallister2020] The title belongs to the printed edition rather than to a single composition: the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon record of the text lists ten numbered pieces, beginning with the *Sarvajñasiddhi* (a proof of omniscience) and ending with the *Santānāntaradūṣaṇa* (a critique concerning other mindstreams).[^dsbc515]

The collection opens by taking up the question of whether a person can know everything. Its first treatise begins with a verse of homage to Tārā and engages the Mīmāṃsaka thinker Kumārila, who denied that omniscience could be proved while allowing that a person might know what is right (*dharma*). The treatise then argues, in the form of a syllogism, that a mind that has been cultivated attentively, continuously and over a long time can become fully clear, taking the four noble truths as the object of such cultivation.[^dsbc300] The record also shows that the *Kṣaṇabhaṅgasiddhi*, on momentariness, appears twice in the collection (as items 4 and 5), presumably in two versions.[^dsbc515] Beyond this outline, little about the contents of the individual treatises could be verified in the sources consulted here.

Ratnakīrti is described in the scholarly literature as an eleventh-century Buddhist author, known above all for the *Apohasiddhi*, a work on the theory of exclusion (*apoha*).[^mcallister2020] No reliable account of the collection's own history, such as manuscript discoveries or Tibetan and Chinese translations, was found in the sources that could be consulted, and nothing is asserted about them here.

All four digital editions derive from the printed edition prepared by Anantalal Thakur and published in Patna by the Kashiprasad Jayaswal Research Institute in 1975. The SARIT record identifies it as the second revised edition in the Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series.[^sarit-nibandhavali]
