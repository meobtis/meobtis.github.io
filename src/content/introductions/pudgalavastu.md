The *Pudgalavastu* ("the section on persons") is, by its title and its place in the edition, one of the sections (*vastu*) of the Vinayavastu, the part of the Mūlasarvāstivāda Vinaya that deals with matters of monastic discipline. The Sanskrit text opens with mnemonic verses (*uddāna*) summarising its stories, and it carries references to folios of the Gilgit manuscript, so it belongs to the Sanskrit material recovered at Gilgit.[^gretil-pudgala]

Judging from the opening of the text itself, the work is set at Śrāvastī, in the Jetavana monastery, and concerns monks who have committed an offence of the *saṃghāvaśeṣa* class. It begins with the monk Udāyin, whose offence is concealed for a fortnight, and sets out the penalties the Buddha prescribes in such cases: a period of probation and a further period of six nights called *mānāpya*, followed by the other persons who incur the same offence.[^gretil-pudgala]

Little detailed, reliable scholarship on this particular section could be consulted in preparing this note, so its date, its Tibetan and Chinese counterparts and its place in the wider Vinaya are not described further here.
