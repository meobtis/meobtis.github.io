The *Bhāvanāyogam* ("the practice of cultivation") is a very short Sanskrit text of about a page. It is catalogued in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the Madhyamaka and Yogācāra section of the treatises, but the record supplies no author, edition or source.[^dsbc913] No established English title was found for it, and no scholarly discussion of a work under this name could be located in the sources consulted.

Judging from the text itself, it is written in the manner of a sūtra rather than a treatise. It opens "at one time" (*ekaṃ samayam*) with the bodhisattva, the Blessed One, staying at Urubilvā on the bank of the Nairañjanā river at the foot of the tree of awakening, "devoted to the cultivation of the factors conducive to awakening" (*bodhipakṣikeṣu dharmeṣu bhāvanāyogam anuyukto*). It then describes, in a repeated pattern, how in the first watch of the night he directs his mind to the higher knowledges (*abhijñā*): the various kinds of supernormal power and recollection of past lives, followed in the middle watch by the divine ear.[^dsbc913] The text as keyed breaks off after the divine ear passage, and many passages are enclosed in round brackets, which suggests a fragmentary source, although the record does not explain the convention.[^dsbc913]

Little else is documented. Neither the date, the school, the manuscript from which the passage comes, nor any Tibetan or Chinese parallel could be verified, so nothing more is claimed here. The two digital versions, in Roman transliteration and Devanagari, were prepared from the same input in 2023.[^dsbc913][^dsbc912]
