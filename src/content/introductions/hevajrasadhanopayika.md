The *Hevajrasādhanopāyikā* ("Means of Accomplishing Hevajra") is a short Sanskrit ritual-meditation text (*sādhana*) on the deity Hevajra. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it among the commentarial works (*ṭīkā*) on Yoginī tantras and attributes it to Saroruhavajra.[^dsbc953] The text opens with homage to Śrī Hevajra and Nairātmyā, and then sets out the stages of practice: the practitioner, already initiated, takes refuge, generates the awakening mind, contemplates emptiness, and visualises a circular maṇḍala in which he arises as Hevajra (Hekāravajra), surrounded by a retinue of goddesses such as Gaurī, Caurī and Vetālī.[^dsbc953]

Hevajra is the central deity of the *Hevajra Tantra*, a Yoginī tantra of the *anuttarayoga* class that scholars date between the late eighth and the early tenth century, and which has attracted a number of Indian commentaries and practice manuals.[^wiki-hevajra] I could find little reliable scholarly documentation on this particular work, its author's identity and date, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, so none is claimed here. The digital editions record no printed source, so it is not known which manuscript or edition they derive from.[^dsbc953]
