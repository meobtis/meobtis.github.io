# Ajitasenavyākaraṇa (The Prediction of Ajitasena)

Oṃ, homage to the `sarvajña` (All Knowing).

Thus heard by me. At one time Bhagavān was dwelling at Śrāvastī in the Jetavana, in the park of Anāthapiṇḍada, together with a great saṃgha of bhikṣus, with twelve thousand five hundred bhikṣus, namely, āyuṣmān Ājñātakauṇḍinya, āyuṣmān Mahānāman, āyuṣmān Revata, āyuṣmān Vakkula, āyuṣmān Śāriputra, and āyuṣmān Pūrṇa Maitrāyaṇīputra, along with niyutas of śrāvakas. All of them approached where Bhagavān was and where the Jetavana monastery was; having bowed with their heads at the feet of Bhagavān, they stood before Bhagavān together with thirty-two thousand bodhisattvas, namely, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Sahacittotpādadharmacakrapravartana, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Anikṣiptadhura, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Maitreya, the bodhisattva mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, and the bodhisattva mahāsattva Mahāsthāmaprāpta. With thirty-two thousand bodhisattvas headed by these, all of them approached where Bhagavān was and where the Jetavana monastery was.

Having bowed with their heads at the feet of Bhagavān, they stood before Bhagavān. Then indeed Bhagavān, having dressed in the forenoon time, taking only his bowl and robe, entered the great city of Śrāvastī for alms. Then Bhagavān addressed āyuṣmān Ānanda: "Go, Ānanda, bring the bowl, the ring, and the `śikya` (carrying sling)." Then āyuṣmān Ānanda, as soon as he heard from Bhagavān, presented the bowl, the ring, and the śikya to Bhagavān. Then āyuṣmān Ānanda, with palms joined before Bhagavān, addressed Bhagavān in `gāthā` (verses).

When you enter, `piṇḍapātika` (alms-food collector).

Indeed you release many breathing beings. May I ferry across.

You carry across many beings.

From the fear of hell, from the great fear of `jāti` (birth) and old age.

From the great fear of the tangle of the `duḥkha` (dissatisfaction) of the `saṃsāra` (cycle of rebirth).

{vimocayeyaṃ tava lokanāyakāḥ}  
The mahānubhāva, supreme worthy of offerings.

Having released indeed many beings.  
From the great fear of the tangle of the dissatisfaction of the cycle of rebirth.

Having released them, he will indeed return again.

Then āyuṣmān Ānanda, having spoken these gāthās to Bhagavān, stood silent. Then Bhagavān stood not far from the great city of Śrāvastī. Then all those round windows, archways, and pinnacles appeared made of gold, made of crystal, and made of silver. Likewise, in the great city of Śrāvastī, a great crowd of people gathered. Then that crowd of people became filled with doubt: "What is the `hetu` (primary cause), what is the primary `pratyaya` (cause) that this `śubhanimitta` (auspicious sign) of the city has appeared? May this city not be dissolved into ashes!" Then in that crowd of people stood a decrepit, aged, elderly man of many hundreds of thousands of years. Then that man, reassuring that crowd of people, spoke thus: "Do not fear, bhoḥ kulaputras! In this very region of the earth there is a monastery named Jetavana. There dwells Śākyamuni by name, Tathāgata, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha, accomplished in `vidyā` (knowledge) and conduct, Sugata, lokavid, unsurpassed puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstṛ of devas and humans.

And Buddha, Bhagavān. This very one has entered the great city of Śrāvastī for alms. At the `kāla` (time) of his arrival this śubhanimitta occurred." Then that crowd of people, with palms joined before that decrepit man, spoke thus: "Since this śubhanimitta occurred at the time of proclaiming the praise of the qualities of that Bhagavān, Tathāgata, arhataḥ, Samyaksaṃbuddha, what sort of `puṇyābhisaṃskāra` (meritorious mental construction) will there be merely upon seeing that Tathāgata, arhataḥ, Samyaksaṃbuddha?" Then that decrepit man addressed that crowd of people in gāthās, proclaiming the praise of the qualities of Bhagavān.

Whoever indeed hears the name of the lokanātha.

That person is released from the dissatisfaction of the cycle of rebirth;  
never will he be one who goes to a bad destination.

And swiftly indeed he will go to heaven.  
Whoever indeed hears the name of the lokanātha.

Firm in his vow through many tens of millions of aeons,  
the mahānubhāva, the Sugata, of great self.

For inconceivable niyutas of tens of millions of aeons.

(Asv 106)

That bodhisattva has stood firm, like the sands of the Gaṅgā.

Does he ever go to the fear of a bad destination,  
whoever indeed hears the name of the lokanātha?

Never will he be one who goes to a bad destination.

For inconceivable niyutas of tens of millions of aeons,  
he always becomes a `cakravartin` (universal monarch) king,  
whoever indeed bears in mind the name of the lokanātha.

Whatever bad deed was ever done before,  
all of this will swiftly go to exhaustion;  
he becomes even Śakra, lord of devas, mahānubhāva.  
For inconceivable niyutas of tens of millions of aeons.

He goes to Sukhāvatī, the Buddha `kṣetra` (field),  
and that bodhisattva sits cross-legged,  
Brahmasvara, sweet-voiced, gentle-toned;  
they endure for tens of millions of thousands of years.

Never will he be one who goes to a bad destination.

Whoever indeed bears in mind the name of the lokanātha.

Then that decrepit man, having proclaimed to the crowd of people the praise of the qualities of Bhagavān, stood silent. Then Bhagavān entered the great city of Śrāvastī by the eastern city gate. And at that city gate one hundred and twenty million lotuses appeared. And on those lotuses one hundred and twenty million bodhisattvas appeared seated cross-legged with palms joined. Then, as soon as Bhagavān had entered the great city of Śrāvastī, ninety-nine hundred thousand niyutas of tens of millions of `sattva` (beings) were established in the `lokadhātu` (world system) system of Sukhāvatī, and eighty-four hundred thousand niyutas of tens of millions of beings were established in the Buddha field of Tathāgata Akṣobhya in the world system system of Abhirati. Then Bhagavān, together with Ānanda, arrived at the house of the city-beggar girl. Then Bhagavān rattled the ring at the house of the city-beggar girl. Then that girl, having heard that sound of the ring, became filled with doubt: "What is the hetu, what is the hetu? Never before has a piṇḍapātika come to my house." Then that city-beggar girl, seated in an empty-looking house, with throat choked with tears, weeping and lamenting, stayed there searching for a sharp-edged sword, lamenting and weeping. Then that girl, seated in the empty-looking house, lamenting, with throat choked with tears, weeping, spoke in gāthās.

Oh, alas, the duḥkha in a poor house!

Better for me is death, and not even life;  
and what use even is life to me.

Since my body is oppressed by duḥkha?  
... indeed there is no protection, no ultimate recourse;  
I am indeed without a nātha today, since I was born in a poor house.

(Asv 108)

Then that city-beggar girl, seated in the empty-looking house, having spoken these gāthās, remained silent. Then a deva-son of the Pure Abodes class, standing in the sky, reflected: "If only Bhagavān would look upon her! That Śākyamuni, the Tathāgata, who has practised a course of many hundreds of thousands of niyutas of tens of millions of difficult deeds, has stood at the house of the city-beggar girl." Then the deva-son of the Pure Abodes class, taking a pearl necklace worth a hundred thousand, taking a basket of food of a hundred flavours, and taking garments from Kāśī, approached where the house of the city-beggar girl was. Then the deva-son of the Pure Abodes class spoke thus to the city-beggar girl: "Put on, `dārike` (girl), these garments from Kāśī and these ornaments worth many hundreds of thousands. Having put them on, taking this pearl necklace worth a hundred thousand and taking this basket of food of a hundred flavours, present them to Bhagavān." Then that girl, having put on those garments from Kāśī, taking the pearl necklace worth a hundred thousand and taking that basket of food of a hundred flavours, approached where Bhagavān was. Having approached.

She presented them to Bhagavān. Then Bhagavān spoke thus to that city-beggar girl: "Dedicate it, `dārike` (girl), just as it has been dedicated by the true Tathāgatas, arhats, Samyaksaṃbuddhas beginning with Vipaśyin, Śikhin, Viśvabhū, Kakutsunda, Kanakamuni, and Kāśyapa. There will be an end to female `bhāva` (existent); there will be an end to a poor house." Then that girl, having dedicated that `piṇḍapāta` (alms-food)-food, presented it to Bhagavān: "By this root of wholesome merit of alms-food, may I not be reborn in any poor house." Then that city-beggar girl, having dedicated that piṇḍapāta-food and given it to Bhagavān, set out to return to her own house. Then Bhagavān spoke thus to that city-beggar girl: "Turn back, `dārike` (girl). I recollect the origin of your former birth; I shall proclaim that." Then that girl, having turned back, prostrated herself with all her limbs before Bhagavān. Then that city-beggar girl addressed Bhagavān in verses.

Inevitably, through `karman` (action) done by me before,  
by which I was born in a poor house;  
have compassion indeed on me, who am afflicted with dissatisfaction,  
turn back indeed the guardians of hell.

Have compassion on me, afflicted with dissatisfaction,  
reborn in the female state, nāyaka;  
you, lokanātha, from old age, illness, and sorrow,  
release me, afflicted with dissatisfaction.

Be a protection, a refuge, an ultimate recourse;  
release me, afflicted with dissatisfaction.  
for a vow was made by you, nātha:  
"Whatever beings there are here in Jambudvīpa.

(Asv 110)

And who indeed abide in the ten directions.

Indeed I shall make all beings happy,  
and I shall release all from the ocean of dissatisfaction."

Be a protection, a refuge, an ultimate recourse.

But indeed you, nātha, release the world and me.  
Inevitably, through karman done by me before,  
by which I was born in a poor house,  
be a protection for me, afflicted with dissatisfaction.

Be, nātha, a releaser from old age and illness,  
be a protection, foremost in accumulated qualities;  
and I shall never again do a bad deed,  
by which I experience these feelings.

Generate compassion... foremost of beings.  
Be a protection, a refuge, an ultimate recourse.  
Whatever beings here in Jambudvīpa  
indeed bear the name in mind in the later kāla.

In that later time after your final extinguishment,  
when the ruin of the teaching will occur,  
whatever bad deed was done before,  
all of this will swiftly go to exhaustion.

Then that city-beggar girl, having spoken gāthās to Bhagavān, once again set out for her house. Then Bhagavān spoke thus to that city-beggar girl with a resonant voice like the cry of a kalaviṅka bird: "Turn back, `dārike` (girl); I recollect the origin of your former birth and I shall proclaim it." Then that girl, having turned back, spoke thus.

Proclaim today, guide of the `loka` (world),  
whatever very dreadful bad deed was always done;  
inevitably a very dreadful bad deed was done by me,  
by which I was born in a poor house.

You are the caravan leader here in the entire world;  
release me here from the female state;  
Be a protection, a refuge, an ultimate recourse;  
I am permanently grateful to the nāyake.

Cause me to hear this Dharma guide;  
he never enters a bad destination;  
purify the karman just as it was done by me;  
Be a protection, a refuge, an ultimate recourse.

One of unobstructed knowledge, supreme world-nāyaka.

I bow to you, nātha, my refuge, with palms joined.

Then Bhagavān spoke thus to that girl: "Formerly, `dārike` (girl), in past time, innumerable aeons ago, there was a Tathāgata named Ratnaśikhin, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, Sugata, lokavid, unsurpassed puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstṛ of devas and humans, Buddha, Bhagavān. Indeed at that very time there was a royal capital named Padmāvatī. Indeed at that very time in the royal capital of Padmāvatī there was a householder named Padmaprabhu. You were the daughter of that householder Padmaprabhu. Indeed at that very time a piṇḍapāta-food collector bhikṣu was going round the villages, towns, market-towns, and districts for piṇḍapāta. When he came to your house, then you, `dārike` (girl), having taken piṇḍapāta and stepped out of the house, went straight back inside again, saying, 'And I will not give piṇḍapāta to an unlucky shaven-head.' Through that accumulation of action, `dārike` (girl), for twelve thousand aeons you have experienced duḥkhas again and again in a poor house. Through this one.

`dārike` (girl), root of wholesome merit, you will obtain a prediction of `bodhi` (awakening). Because the `rūpa` (form), marks, and bearing of that bhikṣo were seen by you, you will become, `dārike` (girl), in future time, after inconceivable, immeasurable aeons, a Tathāgata named Nagaradhvaja in the loka, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, Sugata, lokavid, unsurpassed puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstṛ of devas and humans, Buddha, Bhagavān." Then that girl, having circumambulated Bhagavān three times to the right, spoke thus: "Of what sort, Bhagavan, will my Buddha field be, where indeed I shall become a Buddha?" Bhagavān said: "That Buddha field will be named Aparimitaguṇasaṃcaya. And just as that lokadhātu system of Sukhāvatī is, such will that Buddha field be. There will be spontaneously born ārya bodhisattvas seated cross-legged. Of such a sort will that Buddha field be." (Asv 113)

And then that girl, satisfied, elated, delighted, overjoyed, filled with joy and gladness, set out for her own house. Then Bhagavān spoke thus to that girl: "You, `dārike` (girl), will pass away on the seventh day. Having passed away— in the eastern direction, in the country of Magadha, there is a king named Ajitasena. There are a thousand women in the inner quarters of that King Ajitasena. Indeed you will be born as the son of that very King Ajitasena. This very one will be your last dwelling in a womb." Then Bhagavān departed from the great city of Śrāvastī by the western city gate and approached where the Jetavana monastery was. Then āyuṣmān Ānanda, seeing Bhagavān coming from afar, having bowed with his head at his feet and circumambulated him three times to the right, addressed Bhagavān in gāthās.

Golden-coloured, adorned with supreme `lakṣaṇa` (characteristics),  
bearing a form with twenty-two characteristics,  
when you have come back as a piṇḍapātika,  
having released all beings here.

You have established all beings in happiness;  
the power of `maitrī` (goodwill) has been extended by you to the entire world.  
That supreme piṇḍapāta, nāyaka of the sphere,  
and the world has been released by you from fear.

Whatever bodhisattvas are here in Jambudvīpa.

All of them point out your `mārga` (path).  
(Asv 114)

Always in the later time after your final extinguishment.

May one bear in mind this `sūtra` (discourse) praised by the Buddha.

In the later time after your final extinguishment,  
there will be the ruin of the teaching;  
one should bear in mind this method, this jewel of a sūtra.

Then āyuṣmān Ānanda, having spoken these gāthās and circumambulated Bhagavān three times to the right, stood before Bhagavān. Then Bhagavān addressed āyuṣmān Ānanda: "Go, Ānanda, strike the gong; those śrāvakas will partake of the piṇḍapāta-food." Then āyuṣmān Ānanda addressed Bhagavān: "Of what sort, Bhagavan, will the `kuśalamūla` (root of wholesome merit) of the sound of the gong be?" Bhagavān said: "Listen, Ānanda, I proclaim the kuśalamūla of the sound of the gong. Whoever, Ānanda, shall hear the sound of the gong, their five deeds of immediate retribution will go to complete exhaustion; they will become non-returning, and quickly they will fully awaken to unsurpassed `samyaksaṃbodhi` (perfect awakening)." Ānanda said: "What sort of kuśalamūla, bhagavan, has been planted by those sattvas?" Bhagavān said: "Listen, Ānanda. Those sattvas who in the last kāla, in the last age, after my final extinguishment, when the ruin of the `saddharma` (True Dharma) is underway, at the kāla of the disappearance of the True Dharma, in villages, towns, market-towns, districts, kingdoms, and royal capitals.

Or those beings who in a forest abode shall hear the sound of the striking of the gong and say 'Homage to the Buddha', their five karmans of immediate retribution will go to complete exhaustion. Of such a sort, Ānanda, are the roots of wholesome merit of the sound of the gong." Then āyuṣmān Ānanda calmly and serenely struck the gong. Then by that sound of the gong all those mahāśrāvakas assembled. Seated each on his respective seat, they partook of the piṇḍapāta-food. Then right there in the midst of the śrāvakas a mahāśrāvaka named Nandimitra had assembled and sat down. Then Bhagavān addressed āyuṣmān Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "Go, Nandimitra, mahāśrāvaka, to the country of Magadha in the eastern direction, and perform the service of a `kalyāṇamitra` (spiritual friend) to King Ajitasena." Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka spoke thus to Bhagavān: "I shall not be able, Bhagavan, to go to that region of the earth. Those sattvas are difficult to approach; they will deprive me of life." Then Bhagavān spoke to āyuṣmān.

That Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka thus: "Those sattvas will not be able to shake even the tip of a hair of yours, much less deprive you of life." Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, at dawn time, having put on a golden-coloured garment, went along toward where the royal capital of King Ajitasena was, in the country of Magadha in the eastern direction. Then King Ajitasena, upon seeing that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, became satisfied, elated, delighted, overjoyed, and filled with joy and gladness. Then a minister was sent by King Ajitasena: "Go, bring this bhikṣu." Then that minister approached where Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka was. Then that minister spoke thus to Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "Come, mahāśrāvaka, bhikṣo, the king commands you." Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka spoke thus to the minister: "What business have I with the king? What will the king do for me?" Then the minister approached where King Ajitasena was, and spoke thus to that King Ajitasena: "And that bhikṣu does not come to your side."

Then five hundred ministers were sent by King Ajitasena, and that bhikṣū did not come to the side of King Ajitasena. Then that king in his own very bodily form approached where that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka was. Having approached, with palms joined, he spoke thus: "Come, bho bhikṣo, enter my royal capital." Then King Ajitasena, taking that bhikṣu by the right hand, entered his own royal capital. Then a lion throne was given by King Ajitasena to the bhikṣu Nandimitra. Then King Ajitasena, having sat down on a fine seat, spoke thus to that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "Where are you going, bhikṣo? What is the hetu, what is the hetu?" Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka spoke thus to King Ajitasena: "Whoever have gone forth as bhikṣus, all of them live on piṇḍapāta and partake of piṇḍapāta as they go their rounds." Then King Ajitasena spoke thus to that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "Partake of piṇḍapāta in my house. As long as I live I shall give you piṇḍapāta. If, bhikṣo, you have need of my.

Own flesh, I shall give my own flesh." Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka addressed King Ajitasena in gāthās. (Asv 117)

I eat that food which you give,  
savoury food and drink, supreme flavourful drink;  
indeed you will become free from `kleśa` (defilements),  
having gained the very rare attainment of human birth.

Very rare is the teaching of the nāyaka;  
supreme serenity of `śraddhā` (confidence) is very rare;  
and those bhikṣus who have gone forth in the teaching—  
very rare is the teaching of the nāyakas.

Very rare is the sight of the supreme Sugata;  
homage be to you, Buddha, mahānubhāva;  
homage be to you, consisting of Dharma, `mahāmune` (great sage);  
homage be to you, ārya discerning of defilements.

Homage be to you for releasing from all old age.

Homage be to you, ārya pointing out the path.

Homage be to you, pointer of the way of the path.

Homage be to you, pointer of the path of awakening.

Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, having proclaimed to King Ajitasena the praise of the qualities of Bhagavān, stood silent. Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka addressed that King Ajitasena in gāthās.

Very rare indeed likewise is the sight of a bhikṣu.

Very rare would be the sight of him.  
(Asv 118)

Whoever gives a gift to the saṃgha of bhikṣus.

For him neither yakṣas nor rākṣasas.

(And not {pretakumbhāṇḍamahoragāś}?)  
never do they make an obstacle for them.  
Those who give a gift to the saṃgha of bhikṣus,  
for them is the very rare attainment of human birth.

Whoever sees the rare state of a bhikṣu,  
very rare through inconceivable hundreds of aeons,  
whoever indeed bears in mind the name of the lokanātha.  
For inconceivable niyutas of tens of millions of aeons.

Never would he go to downfall and a bad destination,  
whoever sees such a king of bhikṣus;  
for him there is no downfall and bad destination,  
whoever sees such a king of bhikṣus.

My kalyāṇamitra, pointer of the path,  
who has come to me as a piṇḍapātika—  
whoever indeed shall give piṇḍapāta to him,  
released, they will not be afflicted by old age and illness.

Rather, they will always be free from kleśas.

They shall give piṇḍapāta to him here.

Then that King Ajitasena, having proclaimed the praise of the qualities of Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, stood silent. Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka spoke thus to King Ajitasena: "So be it, mahārāja; I shall eat piṇḍapāta in your house." Then King Ajitasena satisfied that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka with hard and soft food. Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka spoke thus to King Ajitasena: "Do you have, mahārāja, a park ground in this region of the earth?" The king said: "I have, mahāśrāvaka, a delightful, very beautiful park ground." Nandimitra said: "I am going, mahārāja; I shall look at that park ground." The king said: "Go, Nandimitra; look at the park ground." Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka approached where the park ground of King Ajitasena was. Now in that park ground all the qualities of a park were present: the summer lotus ponds were completely filled with cool water, and the rainy-season ponds were completely filled with water neither too hot nor too cold. And those lotus ponds were covered with golden steps, heavenly and delightful. And all the qualities of enjoyment in Jambudvīpa were present: there were adhimuktaka, campaka, aśoka, mucilinda, pāṭala, sumanā, and saugandhika flowers. The birds of the animal realm—parrots, mynahs, ruddy geese, peacocks, cuckoos, and the like—made various cries. Then right there in the park ground.

Golden-coloured birds with golden beaks, golden wings, and golden feet appeared. All of them uttered the sound "Buddha". Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka approached where the royal capital of King Ajitasena was. Then King Ajitasena spoke thus to that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "You have returned, mahāśrāvaka. Have you seen the park ground?" Nandimitra said: "I have seen the delightful, very beautiful park ground." Then King Ajitasena addressed Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka in gāthās.

Whatever enjoyments there are in Jambudvīpa—  
adhimuktaka, campaka, dhānuṣkārikā,  
aśoka, mucilinda, and likewise pāṭalā flowers,  
and saugandhikā lotuses, jasmine, and vārṣikā flowers—.

Then pāriyātra trees trees formed on the river banks.

There were always golden-coloured birds,  
and they pointed out the mārga to supreme awakening.

Heavenly, charming, and sweet-voiced.

And they will proclaim it at all times.

Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, having spoken gāthās to King Ajitasena, stood silent. Then King Ajitasena had the drum beaten. Then, having assembled at that sound of the drum, the host of ministers spoke thus to that King Ajitasena: "For what purpose, mahārāja, has the drum been beaten?" Then that king said: "Let the elephant-chariot and horse-chariot be made ready. I shall go to the park ground for amusement." Then those tens of millions of ministers, having heard his word, very swiftly made ready that elephant-chariot and horse-chariot. Then in just a moment, a trice, an instant, King Ajitasena approached where that park ground was. And that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka also approached right there and spoke thus to King Ajitasena: "In this region of the earth, mahārāja, a hut should be built for me, where, seated, I shall partake of piṇḍapāta from your house." Then King Ajitasena spoke thus to Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "Of what sort should a hut be built for you?" Nandimitra said: "Such as, mahārāja, are your.

Great `utpāda` (arising) of `citta` (mind), confidence, and serenity, have a hut of such a sort built." Then King Ajitasena spoke thus to the chief minister: "In this very region of the earth have a hut built." Then the chief minister spoke thus to the king: "Of what sort, mahārāja, shall I have the hut built?" King Ajitasena spoke thus: "Have a hut built thirty yojanas in length and six yojanas in height, made of the seven jewels and covered with gems and pearls." Then that minister had the hut built. Having had it built of the seven jewels and covered with gems and pearls, he approached where King Ajitasena was and spoke thus to King Ajitasena: "The hut has been built by me, mahārāja, just as was commanded." The king said: "In that very region of the earth a walkway should be built, four yojanas in length and two yojanas in width." Then that minister, having had that walkway built, approached where King Ajitasena was and addressed the king in gāthās.

A very beautiful walkway has been made by me,  
and just as the command was indeed given by you before;  
he releases beings from auspicious and inauspicious nature,  
may he release all these breathing beings.

Oh, well-gained is the vow made by you!  
He releases the entire world together with its devas;  
may such a vow made by you come to be;  
the `dharmabhāṇaka` (Dharma reciter) illuminates the Dharma for me.

Seated and standing in the hut and on the walkway,  
just such as the command was given by you,  
that very beautiful walkway indeed has been made,  
and that ground is covered with gems and jewels.

Then King Ajitasena approached where his own royal capital was. Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, having turned back and sat down in that very hut, entered into the bodhisattva `samādhi` (focus) named Dispersion. He entered it with his hair in one place, his eyes in another, his teeth in another, his neck in another, his arms in another, his heart in another, his belly in another, his thighs in another, his calves in another, and his feet in another. Then after the passing of seven days, that King Ajitasena did not see that bhikṣu. Then the king spoke thus to his eldest son: "Come, kulaputra, I shall go to that hut; I shall approach where that bhikṣu is." Then that king, together with his son, approached where that hut was. Then that King Ajitasena, seeing that bhikṣu's `ātmabhāva` (bodily form) cut into piece after piece, became filled with doubt. With hair standing on end in terror, tearing his garments, lamenting, weeping, his throat choked with tears, he spoke thus to his son: "Bring, putra (etym. doubtful, perhaps fr. √ 2. puṣ, a sharp-edged sword; I shall deprive myself of life." Then that prince, having joined his palms, addressed the king in gāthās.

Do not become sorrowful of mind, Nṛpendra;  
do not experience such feelings.  
For having killed yourself, you will go to hell;  
and even to the very dreadful Raurava hell you will go.

... This one is worthy of offerings in the loka, a releaser from old age and illness;  
and he has not been slain by yakṣas, nor by ghosts, nor by rākṣasas;  
this one is indeed a bodhisattva in the loka, a releaser from old age and illness.

This one is worthy of offerings in the loka, a releaser from old age and illness;  
rare is the sight of him, the pointer of the path of awakening.

This one was your kalyāṇamitra, who came on your account;  
this one is worthy of offerings in the loka, bringing `sukha` (happiness) to all beings.

Sent by the sarvajña lokanātha who has reached perfection,  
by Dṛḍhavīrya, of firm strength, the lokanātha, the great sage.

Whoever bears in mind his name does not go to a bad destination;  
they will not go to a bad destination, being bound for rebirth in the heavenly world.

Then that prince, having spoken gāthās to his father, stood silent. Then King Ajitasena spoke thus to his own son: "How do you know, kumāra as, (fr. 1. ku + māra, √ mṛ? ‘easily dying’, that this bhikṣu has entered into samādhi?" Then that prince spoke thus: "Look, mahārāja, this bhikṣu has entered into a bodhisattva focus; completely free from all defilements, a Bhavasāgarapāraṃgata, for the benefit of all existences he points out the auspicious mārga." Then that prince spoke thus to the king: "Come, tāta, let us go to the walkway." Then that king and that prince, together with many hundreds of youths, approached where that walkway was. Then that bhikṣu, having risen from that samādhi, spoke thus to King Ajitasena: "Come, mahārāja, what are you doing in this place?" (Asv 125)

Then the king, upon seeing that bhikṣu, gave the royal diadem to the prince: "Let the kingdom be yours; rule by Dharma, not by non-Dharma." The prince said: "Many innumerable royal duties have been performed by me, and never was there satisfaction. Let the kingdom be yours, tāta; I have no need of a kingdom, no need of enjoyment, no need of sovereignty and dominion. Let the kingdom be yours, tāta; rule by Dharma, not by non-Dharma." Then the king approached where that bhikṣu was and, with palms joined, spoke thus: "Very rare is the sight of you, sage.

With palms joined facing the nāyakas;  
the sight of you, sage, brings about `mokṣa` (release).

Very rare through inconceivable hundreds of actions.

Whoever you grant a sight of yourself to, sage,  
he will be released for tens of millions of aeons;  
Never would he go to downfall and a bad destination,  
whoever hears the name even for an instant."

The king said.

Seated in a beautiful jewelled carriage.

I go toward that royal capital;  
I give abundant food.

I give this garment from Kāśī.  
(Asv 126)

And fine, closely woven nets.

The robe which I give to you today.

Then that bhikṣū, having mounted the jewelled carriage, approached where the royal capital was, and was satisfied with food. Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka addressed King Ajitasena in gāthās.

Satisfied with abundant food,  
with supreme, auspicious, savoury food, drink, and flavours—  
Those who give a gift to the saṃgha of bhikṣus,  
before long they go to the seat of awakening.

Very rare is the sight of the nāyaka; not  
long does he take to go to the Buddha field;  
to the supreme Buddha field of Amitāyus,  
swiftly he goes to this Sukhāvatī.

Then Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, having spoken gāthās to King Ajitasena, stood silent. Then King Ajitasena had the drum beaten. Then at that sound of the drum all those tens of millions of ministers spoke thus to the king: "For what purpose, mahārāja, has the drum been beaten?" The king said: "On the seventh day let the elephant-chariot and horse-chariot be made ready. On the seventh day I shall go to the monastery named Jetavana to see, pay homage to, attend upon, and hear the Dharma from Śākyamuni, the Tathāgata, arhataḥ, Samyaksaṃbuddha." Then the tens of millions of ministers spoke thus to that King Ajitasena: "We have made ready, mahārāja, the elephant-chariot and horse-chariot." Then King Ajitasena, having mounted an elephant-chariot and having seated that Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka in a jewelled chariot, approached where the Jetavana monastery was. Then Bhagavān, seeing King Ajitasena coming from afar, addressed all those śrāvakas: "Various displays of psychic power should be shown by all." Then all those mahāśrāvakas.

Entered into the bodhisattva focus named Garland of Flames. Then the king, seeing that Garland of Flames from afar, spoke thus to Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka: "For what reason do I see this mountain become wreathed in flames?" Nandimitra said: "Here Śākyamuni, the Tathāgata, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha, endures, abides, maintains himself, and teaches the Dharma; and those bodhisattvas have entered into the bodhisatvā focus named Garland of Flames." Then King Ajitasena, having dismounted from the elephant-chariot, approached on foot together with his thousand sons where Bhagavān was. Now Bhagavān was walking up and down with his golden-coloured body and a fathom-wide halo. Then King Ajitasena, seeing the form, complexion, marks, and bearing of Bhagavān, fainted and fell down on the surface of the earth. Then Bhagavān, stretching out his golden-coloured arm, raised that king up: "Rise, mahārāja; for what reason have you fallen?" The king said. (Asv 128)

For many inconceivable aeons,  
inconceivable hundreds of tens of millions of births,  
never before have I seen such a form here:  
you are the lokanātha, supreme worthy of offerings.

You are the caravan leader, releaser from old age and illness.

Golden-coloured, with limbs of supreme characteristics,  
bearing the thirty-two characteristics, `mune` (sage).

Never does he go to the realm of bad destinations,  
whoever indeed sees the rūpa of the lokanātha.

To you, lokanāthā, I bow with my head.

Then King Ajitasena, with palms joined, spoke thus to Bhagavān: "I, Bhagavan, shall go forth in your teaching." Then Bhagavān became satisfied, elated, delighted, overjoyed, and filled with joy and gladness: "They gain what has not been gained, where my teaching becomes widespread." He spoke thus to that King Ajitasena: "Go, mahārāja, to your own house; on the seventh day I shall come." Then King Ajitasena, satisfied, elated, delighted, overjoyed, and filled with joy and gladness, placing that kalyāṇamitra bhikṣu in front and going to his own house, addressed all the ministers: "By all the villages, towns, market-towns, and districts the road should be cleared; at house after house banners should be raised; at house after house jewelled pitchers should be filled completely." Then it was commanded by all those ministers. By all the villages, towns, market-towns, and districts the road was cleared, banners were raised, and jewelled pitchers were filled completely. Where King Ajitasena resided, there one hundred and twenty million banners were raised and one hundred and twenty million jewelled pitchers were filled completely. In due course, on the seventh day, the Tathāgata, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha, surrounded and attended by saṃghas of mahāśrāvakas headed by Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, Ānanda, and Pūrṇa Maitrāyaṇīputra, arrived at the royal capital of King Ajitasena. Then King Ajitasena, taking a basket of flowers and placing his kalyāṇamitra in front.

Strewing Bhagavān with flowers, spoke thus to Bhagavān: "By this root of wholesome merit may all beings attain unsurpassed samyaksaṃbodhi." Then seats were arranged by King Ajitasena, and a lion throne was given to that Bhagavān. Then Bhagavān, seated on the lion throne, taught the Dharmas to King Ajitasena. Then he was satisfied by King Ajitasena with abundant nourishment, with hard and soft food. Then King Ajitasena gave his eldest prince: "Ordain this prince; then I shall go forth afterwards." Then Bhagavān addressed āyuṣmān Ānanda: "Go, Ānanda, ordain this prince." Then that prince was ordained by āyuṣmān Ānanda. Immediately upon going forth, the `phala` (fruit) of an arhat was attained by him, and he saw all the Buddha fields. Then that prince, rising into the sky, addressed his father in gāthās.

Do not delay, tāta;  
do not feel any weariness.  
Oh, well-gained is the sight of the Sugatas!  
Oh, well-gained is the attainment of the Sugatas!

Oh, well-gained indeed is the supreme attainment,  
the attainment of going forth, praised by the Sugata!  
Release from the cycle of rebirth has been praised by the Sugata;  
go forth swiftly, do not delay, tāta.

Do not weary Lokavināyakendra.

The very rare attainment of human birth has been gained.

... Very rare is the sight of the nāyakas;  
and having gone forth swiftly, indeed I have gained.

I have attained the highest supreme awakening.  
Then the king, having heard his son's words.

He went forth in the teaching of the nāyaka.

Then the prince, standing in the sky, having spoken the gāthā, remained silent. Then King Ajitasena, having heard his son's words, satisfied, elated, delighted, overjoyed, and filled with joy and gladness, had a monastery built dedicated to Bhagavān. Tens of millions of musical instruments were offered. Of his thousand women of the inner quarters, their female `indriya` (faculty) disappeared and the male faculty appeared. Then King Ajitasena went forth in the teaching of Bhagavān. And those tens of millions of ministers went forth in the teaching of Bhagavān. And that thousand men of the inner quarters went forth. Bhagavān, having ordained King Ajitasena, set out toward where the Jetavana monastery was. Then āyuṣmān Ānanda spoke thus to Bhagavān: "This King Ajitasena, having renounced his kingdom and had a monastery built, has gone forth in the teaching of Bhagavān. What sort of kuśalamūla will he have?" Bhagavān said: "Well done, well done, Ānanda, for what you have proclaimed! This King Ajitasena, having gone forth in my teaching, will become in future time, after immeasurable, inconceivable, boundless aeons, a Tathāgata named Ajitaprabha in the loka, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha, endowed with knowledge and conduct, Sugata, lokavid, unsurpassed puruṣadamyasārathi, Śāstṛ of devas and humans, Buddha, Bhagavān." Ānanda said: "This Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka was the king's kalyāṇamitra. What sort of kuśalamūla will he have?" Bhagavān said: "This Nandimitra the mahāśrāvaka, Ānanda, at that very time will become a Tathāgata named Nandiprabha in the loka, arhan, Samyaksaṃbuddha." Ānanda said: "Of what sort, Bhagavan, will the Buddha field of those Tathāgatas be?" Bhagavān.

Said: "That Buddha field where these Tathāgatas will be will be named Aparimitaguṇasaṃcaya." Ānanda said: "Whoever shall illuminate this entire, complete `dharmaparyāya` (method of teaching) in the last kāla, in the last age, what sort of `puṇyaskandha` (mass of merit) will he have?" Bhagavān said: "Just as bodhi was fully awakened to by me, Ānanda, after practising hundreds of thousands of niyutas of tens of millions of difficult deeds, so those beings will fully awaken to bodhi. Those who shall hear even a four-line gāthā from this dharmaparyāya, those beings will become non-returning in unsurpassed samyaksaṃbodhi." Ānanda said: "Those dharmabhāṇakas who shall illuminate this dharmaparyāya, what sort of kuśalamūla will they have?" Bhagavān said: "Listen, Ānanda. Such a one will become a cakravartin king, lord of the four continents. Those who shall illuminate this entire, complete dharmaparyāya will be released, and will be completely released from birth, old age, illness, lamentation, dissatisfaction, `daurmanasya` (displeasure), and `upāyāsa` (despair)." Ānanda said.

"Those beings who in the last kāla, in the last age, shall reject this dharmaparyāya and not trust it, what will be their `gati` (going), what their ultimate recourse?" Bhagavān said: "Enough, enough, Ānanda! Do not ask me about that bad `karmaskandha` (mass of actions). It is not possible for me to proclaim it; apart from tens of millions of Buddhas, it is not possible to proclaim it." Ānanda said: "Proclaim it, Bhagavan, proclaim it!" The Sugata, Bhagavān, said: "Listen, Ānanda, I shall proclaim the gati of one who rejects the True Dharma. In the great hell Raurava, in the great hell Hāhaha, in the great hell Avīci, in the animal realm, in the world of Yama, and in the realm of hungry ghosts, duḥkha must be experienced for many thousands of aeons. If ever he is reborn in the human world, he will have a long, parched palate and throat; his tongue will be twelve yojanas long; twelve ploughs will plough across it, and those who speak such words will say: 'Never, bhoḥ kulaputras, bhoḥ, reject the saddharma anywhere!' Such is the gati of one who rejects the True Dharma." Ānanda said: "By what hetu, Bhagavan, is the saddharma.

Rejected?" Bhagavān said: "Those sattvas who in the last kāla, in the last age, shall revile, abuse, disparage, and deprecate these dharmabhāṇakas who uphold the sūtra, and shall produce a corrupted mind towards that dharmabhāṇaka, by them the saddharma will have been rejected. Even if a sattva were to gouge out the eyes of all sattvas in a three-thousandfold great-thousandfold lokadhātu, this one would have a far greater mass of demerit than that; just so, whoever looks with a corrupted mind upon these dharmabhāṇakas who bear the sūtra will generate a far greater mass of demerit than that." Then Ānanda replied to Bhagavān in gāthās.

Many thousands of sūtras have been heard by me face to face with the teacher.

And never before has such a sūtra been heard by me.

In the very terrifying later time after the teacher's final extinguishment.

I shall illuminate this sūtra and bear in mind this method.

Wherever this jewel of a sūtra will be in the later time.

I shall protect them in the very terrifying later time.

I shall not reject this profound utterance of the Buddha.

Then āyuṣmān Ānanda, having spoken gāthās to Bhagavān, stood silent. Then Kāśyapa the mahāśrāvaka addressed Bhagavān in gāthās.

Golden-coloured, with limbs of supreme characteristics,  
the Jina bearing the thirty-two characteristics,  
well-spoken is the sūtra of great majesty,  
the profound Dharma, subtle and very hard to see.

(Asv 135)

This unsurpassed sūtra has been illuminated.

In the later time when the Jina has passed into final extinguishment,  
I shall bear in mind this unsurpassed sūtra method.

Then āyuṣmān Śāradvatīputra addressed Bhagavān in gāthās.

Homage be to you, Buddha, mahānubhāva;  
This unsurpassed sūtra has been illuminated.  
After your final extinguishment, lokanāyakā,  
whoever shall write out this unsurpassed sūtra.

Never even does he go to the fear of a bad destination.

And swiftly he goes to this heaven.

Then āyuṣmān Pūrṇa Maitrāyaṇīputra addressed Bhagavān in gāthās.

Grateful to many beings, releaser from the fear of bad destinations.

A profound sūtra, a Buddha's gift, has been illuminated by you.

Indeed, in the last kāla, when you, nāyake, have attained extinguishment.

I shall illuminate this sūtra for the benefit of all breathing beings.

Thus.

Then Brahmā Sahāṃpati addressed Bhagavān in gāthās.

Homage be to you, Buddha, mahānubhāvā.

This auspicious sūtra has been illuminated by you.  
(Asv 136)

After your final extinguishment, lokanāyaka.

I shall indeed protect this jewel of a sūtra.

Then the four mahārājas addressed Bhagavān in gāthās.

Oh, very delightful is the supreme jewel of a sūtra,  
illuminated in such a way for the later time!  
... the sūtra-gem,  
sees it illuminated in the later time.

Of the Sugatas indeed, guides of the world,  
I indeed shall bear in mind the jewel of a sūtra;  
I shall protect the jewel of a sūtra.  
Bhagavān spoke this; delighted.

And that entire assembly rejoiced in what was spoken by Bhagavān.  
The Mahāyāna sūtra named the Exposition of the Prediction of Ajitasena is complete.
