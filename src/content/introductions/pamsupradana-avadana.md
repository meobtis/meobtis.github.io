The *Pāṃśupradāna-avadāna* is a fragmentary Sanskrit text of the avadāna (edifying narrative) genre, preserved in the Gilgit Buddhist manuscripts. The digital text is transcribed line by line from two groups of leaves, numbered GBM 1508–1513 and GBM 1514–1517 in the Gilgit facsimile edition, and each line is annotated with the corresponding passage of the *Divyāvadāna* (Divy 351.9–353.21 and Divy 359.28–362.29).[^gretil-pamsu] The title, which can be read as "the gift of dust", is not explained in the edition's front matter, and no English title appears to be established.

The opening lines preserved in the transcription deal with the perfumer Gupta (*gāndhika*), who promises an elder to give him a son as his attendant, and with the births of his sons, among them Aśvagupta and Upagupta, who is described as a remarkable child.[^gretil-pamsu] The manuscript text is incomplete, with many lacunae marked in the transcription, and some lines have no counterpart in the *Divyāvadāna* parallel. I could not consult further scholarly sources on the work's date, authorship, translations or reception, so little beyond what the edition itself records is documented here.
