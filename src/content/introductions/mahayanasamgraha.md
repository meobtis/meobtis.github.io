The *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* ("Summary of the Great Vehicle", also rendered "A Compendium of the Mahāyāna") is a systematic treatise of the Yogācāra school, traditionally attributed to Asaṅga and conventionally dated to about 310–390 CE.[^wiki-mahayanasamgraha] It is arranged in ten chapters: the first three treat the basis of knowable things (the storehouse consciousness, *ālayavijñāna*), their characteristics (the three natures, *trisvabhāva*) and the penetration of those characteristics through the doctrine of representation-only. The remaining chapters move through the six perfections, the ten bodhisattva stages, the three trainings in discipline, concentration and wisdom, and end with the fruits of practice: the transformation of the basis and the wisdom of buddhahood with the Dharma-body (*dharmakāya*).[^wiki-mahayanasamgraha]

According to the sources consulted, no Sanskrit original has been found, and the work survives in Tibetan (Tohoku 4050) and in Chinese translations, including that of Paramārtha (499–567), which became the central text of the Shelun school (Taishō 1592–1594).[^wiki-mahayanasamgraha] Commentaries are ascribed to Vasubandhu and Asvabhāva, and modern translations include Lamotte's French version (1973), Keenan's English version from the Chinese (1992) and Brunnhölzl's English compendium with commentaries (2019), as listed in the same reference article.[^wiki-mahayanasamgraha] Little further reliable information about the digital Sanskrit text itself could be found.

Because the Sanskrit original is reported as lost, the nature of the Sanskrit text in these digital editions (for example whether it is a reconstruction) is not stated in their records, and users should treat it with caution.[^dsbc867]
