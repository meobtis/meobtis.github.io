The *Śākyasiṃhastotra* ("Hymn to the Lion of the Śākyas") is a short Sanskrit stotra, a verse hymn of praise addressed to the Buddha under the epithet Śākyasiṃha. The title carries the qualifier *surapatikṛtam*, "made by Surapati", an epithet of Indra, so the hymn presents itself as the praise of the Buddha offered by the lord of the gods. It consists of nine numbered verses, and the closing verse describes the hymn as written in the *sragdharā* metre.[^dsbc184]

Judging from the verses, the hymn follows the Buddha's life in outline: his descent from the Tuṣita heaven, where he was known as Śvetaketu, and his entry into the womb of Māyā; his birth, marriage and departure from the household life; and his defeat of Namuci (Māra), with the earth goddess bearing witness. Later verses praise his body and its marks, and affirm that even the combined learning of the wise could not describe his greatness. The final verse promises that those who recite the hymn will be protected by Śakra and the world-guardians, enjoy happiness in this life and reach the realm of the Sugata's children.[^dsbc184] Little else about the work appears to be documented in the sources available for this introduction. No reliable information was found on its date, its authorship beyond the attribution in the title, or any Tibetan or Chinese translation, and none is claimed here. The site's catalogue lists similarly titled hymns with other qualifiers (such as *brahmaṇā kṛtam* and *navagrahakṛtam*), which are separate entries.
