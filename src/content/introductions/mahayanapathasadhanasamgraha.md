The *Mahāyānapathasādhanasaṃgraha* ("Compendium of the Practices of the Mahāyāna Path") is a very short Sanskrit poem of about ten verses, attributed in the colophon of the digital texts to the master Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, that is, Atiśa.[^dsbc266] The same colophon gives the title in a longer form, *Atisaṃkṣiptamahāyānapathasādhana*, and states that the Sanskrit was translated and settled together with the Tibetan translator Dge ba'i blo gros.[^dsbc266] The work is listed among the writings attributed to Atiśa (c. 982–1054), the Bengali scholar of Vikramaśīla who later taught in Tibet and is best known for the *Bodhipathapradīpa*.[^wiki-atisha] The DSBC edition is drawn from a printed collection of eleven works by Atiśa.[^dsbc266]

The verses themselves, which open with homage to all buddhas and bodhisattvas, outline in compressed form a path to awakening. They move from going for refuge and abandoning harmful action, through training in ethics as the basis of the awakening mind (*bodhicitta*), to the vow to benefit beings, the six perfections and the union of wisdom and skilful means, and close with a dedication of merit and an aspiration to become a guide of the world. This account is drawn from the text itself.[^dsbc266] Little else about the work, such as its date, Tibetan translation or reception, could be established from reliable sources consulted here, and it has no established English title.
