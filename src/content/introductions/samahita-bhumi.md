The *Samāhitā Bhūmi* ("the stage of one who is in meditative concentration") is the fourth book of the Basic Section of the *Yogācārabhūmi*, a very large Yogācāra compendium organised as a sequence of seventeen "stages" (*bhūmi*) of the path.[^wiki-ybh] The text opens with a verse summary (*uddāna*) and announces that the stage is to be understood under five headings: a statement of its subject (*uddeśa*), its classification (*vyavasthāna*), the kinds of mental attention (*manasikāra*), the kinds of mental sign (*nimitta*), and a collection of relevant sūtra passages (*sūtrāntasaṃgraha*).[^dsbc751]

According to a general survey of the *Yogācārabhūmi*, this book presents meditation in a systematic and exhaustive way, drawing largely on canonical sūtras that are not specifically Mahāyāna. Its contents include the four key terms (*dhyāna*, *vimokṣa*, *samādhi*, *samāpatti*), the five hindrances and the four meditative absorptions, forty kinds of meditative observation, and the emptiness, wishlessness and signlessness concentrations.[^wiki-ybh]

The *Yogācārabhūmi* as a whole is traditionally attributed to Asaṅga or to Maitreya, but modern scholars generally regard it as a composite work of several layers and authors, with a final redaction commonly placed around the fourth century CE.[^wiki-ybh] Most of the Basic Section survives in Sanskrit, while the whole treatise is preserved in Tibetan (translated in the ninth century) and in Xuanzang's Chinese version, with a partial earlier Chinese translation by Paramārtha.[^wiki-ybh] Beyond this, little reliable documentation of this particular book's editorial history could be consulted. The bibliographic record of the digital texts points to a 2009 Vienna publication, which probably corresponds to Martin Delhey's German study and edition of the chapter, *Samahita Bhumi: Das Kapitel über die meditative Versenkung im Grundteil der Yogacarabhumi*, but the record itself names no editor, so this identification is an inference.[^dsbc751][^wiki-ybh]
