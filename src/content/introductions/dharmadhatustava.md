The *Dharmadhātustava* is a Buddhist hymn (*stava*) in praise of the *dharmadhātu*, the "realm of phenomena" or ultimate reality. In the Tibetan canon it is catalogued as Toh 1118 under the heading of eulogies, with the English title *Praise of the Dharmadhātu*.[^84000toh1118] Its Sanskrit text is written in verse, as the digital edition shows.[^dsbc796] The work is also known in English through the translation and commentary by Karl Brunnhölzl, which presents it together with a commentary by the Third Karmapa, Rangjung Dorje.[^wikidharmadhatu]

The opening verses pay homage to the *dharmadhātu* as present in all beings and say that those who do not recognise it wander through the three realms of existence. They add that the same element, when purified, is nirvāṇa.[^dsbc796] The theme is thus the identity of the basic nature of beings with that of awakening, a line of thought related to the Buddhist idea of an innate buddha-nature.

The hymn is traditionally attributed to Nāgārjuna, although Wikipedia notes that this attribution is questioned.[^wikidharmadhatu] Little reliable detail on its date, the survival of its Sanskrit manuscripts or its Chinese translations could be verified from the sources consulted here, so these are not discussed.
