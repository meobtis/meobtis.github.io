The *Śālistambakakārikā* (*Āryaśālistambakakārikā*), here called the Verses on the Rice Seedling, is a short work in Sanskrit verse that summarises the *Śālistamba Sūtra* (*The Rice Seedling*), one of the best-known Mahāyāna sūtras on dependent arising (*pratītyasamutpāda*). It is attributed to Nāgārjuna. The opening verses pay homage, announce the work, and set the scene at Vulture Peak near Rājagṛha, where the Buddha, looking at a rice seedling, taught that whoever sees dependent arising sees the Dharma and the Buddha, and then fell silent; the text then proceeds through the twelve links.[^gretil-salistamba]

The sūtra it versifies is considered one of the most important on dependent arising. It is not known to survive in a complete Sanskrit text, though reconstructions from quotations in later treatises exist, the most thorough being N. Ross Reat's 1993 edition. Four Chinese translations are listed (Taishō 709–712), and a Tibetan translation was made by the late eighth century or soon after.[^84000toh210] The 84000 introduction records three Indian commentaries on the sūtra that survive in Tibetan and Mongolian: Kamalaśīla's *Śālistamba ṭīkā*, and two works attributed to Nāgārjuna, a *Śālistamba mahāyānasūtraṭīkā* and this *Śālistambakakārikā*; Jeffrey D. Schoening studied and translated them in 1995.[^84000toh210]

Little else about the verses is documented in the sources consulted. Their attribution to Nāgārjuna rests on the tradition reported there and has not been independently verified here, and the date and circumstances of composition are not established in the sources read. The Sanskrit text available in digital form is taken from Sonam Rabten's 2004 printed edition.[^dsbc320]
