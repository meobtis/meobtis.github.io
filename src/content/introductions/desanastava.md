The *Deśanāstava* ("hymn of confession") is a brief Sanskrit verse hymn (*stava*) classified in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the *stotra* works on the practice of the bodhisattva (*bodhisattvacaryā*).[^dsbc946] No established English title was found. The text opens with the salutation "namo buddhāya" and then addresses the Buddha, whom the speaker approaches for refuge as the teacher of the world and as the supreme physician, free of every fault and a mine of good qualities.[^dsbc946]

In the opening verses the speaker declares ("*deśayāmi*") his own faults, describing the mind as a river hard to cross, troubled by currents of passion and by the fire of hatred, and finding no peace even within itself; the hymn then turns to the attempt to cultivate a mind of friendliness, which in turn sinks into the mud of attachment.[^dsbc946] This is the language of confession (*deśanā*) familiar from Mahāyāna ritual and devotional literature, but this account rests only on the opening verses of the edition.

The catalogue record names no author, editor, publisher or year, and no scholarly discussion of the work, no manuscript information and no Tibetan or Chinese counterpart could be identified in the sources consulted.[^dsbc946] Its authorship, date and tradition therefore remain undocumented here, and the work should be identified against printed catalogues of Buddhist Sanskrit stotras before any attribution is relied upon.
