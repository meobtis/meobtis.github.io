The *Ahorātravratakathā* is a short Sanskrit Buddhist text on the *ahorātravrata*, a vow or observance kept for a day and a night. In the digital edition it opens with a salutation to the *dharmadhātu* and a verse in honour of the stūpa, followed by the usual narrative frame: the Buddha is staying at Jetavana in Śrāvastī, and the elder Subhūti asks him at what time the observance is performed and what its procedure is. The Buddha then answers in verse, so the text mixes prose and verse.[^gretil-ahoratra]

Little reliable information about the work's date, authorship or transmission could be found. Its edition belongs to a volume of three Sanskrit texts on *caitya* (stūpa) worship in relation to the *ahorātravrata*, edited by Ratna Handurukande, and a separate prose version of the story also exists as another text in the same edition.[^gretil-ahoratra-prose] Beyond what the editor's front matter and the opening of the text show, nothing about Tibetan or Chinese translations, manuscripts or reception could be verified, so none is claimed here.
