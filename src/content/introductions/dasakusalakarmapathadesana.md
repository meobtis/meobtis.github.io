The *Daśākuśalakarmapathadeśanā* ("Teaching on the ten non-virtuous courses of action") is a brief Sanskrit text on the ten unwholesome paths of karma. It opens with a salutation to the Three Jewels and a single verse stating that bodily action is threefold, verbal action fourfold and mental action threefold, making ten in all.[^dsbc101] The text then defines each in prose. No established English title was found in the sources consulted, and the Sanskrit title is used here.

The prose explains the three bodily acts (killing, taking what is not given, sexual misconduct), the four verbal acts (false speech, divisive speech, harsh speech, idle chatter) and the three mental acts (covetousness, ill will, wrong view). Several are analysed by their constituent factors; killing, for example, is said to be complete when five conditions are present. The text closes by citing the *Āryasaddharmasmṛtyupasthāna* and stating that following these ten paths leads beings to the great hells.[^dsbc101]

The colophon attributes the work to the great teacher Dīpaṅkaraśrījñāna and says that it was translated and edited by him together with the Indian preceptor Jayaśīla.[^dsbc101] GRETIL likewise gives the author as Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna, that is, Atīśa.[^gretil-dasakusala] The DSBC record places the text in an edition of Atīśa's *Ekādaśagrantha* ("eleven works").[^dsbc101] Beyond this front matter and the text itself, little reliable information about its date, Tibetan translation or manuscript sources was found, and none is claimed here.
