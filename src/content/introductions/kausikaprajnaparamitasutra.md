The *Kauśikaprajñāpāramitāsūtra* is a short text of the Prajñāpāramitā ("perfection of wisdom") literature, preserved in Sanskrit in P. L. Vaidya's 1961 collection of Mahāyāna sūtras. It opens with the usual setting: the Buddha is at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak with a great assembly of monks and bodhisattvas, and addresses Śakra, lord of the gods, by his epithet Kauśika, which gives the text its name.[^gretil-kaus][^dsbc69]

The text explains the meaning of the perfection of wisdom as something that cannot be seen through any pair of opposites, such as existence and non-existence or sign and signlessness. It likens the perfection of wisdom to all phenomena in their sameness, emptiness and inconceivability, lists the eighteen kinds of emptiness, and then gives a verse on conditioned things being like stars, a lamp, an illusion and a dream, followed by a number of invocations and dhāraṇī formulas addressed to the perfection of wisdom.[^gretil-kaus] Little reliable information about its date, authorship, translations or manuscript sources was found, and none is asserted here; the editions' own records give only the printed edition they derive from.
