The *Mahāmantrānusāriṇī* is a short Sanskrit protective text of the dhāraṇī-sūtra type. In the digital edition it opens with an invocation of the goddess "Āryamahāmantrānusāriṇī", and the narrative has the Buddha send Ānanda to Vaiśālī to proclaim a series of mantra formulas ("mantrapadāni") and verses ("gāthāḥ"). The text then moves through commands to harmful beings to depart, strings of mantra syllables, and verses wishing the hearers well-being.[^gretil-mmanu]

The name is that of one of the five protective goddesses of the Pañcarakṣā, the group of protective texts that circulated in Nepal, Tibet, Central Asia, East Asia and Indonesia and that Newar Buddhists of Kathmandu still use in ritual.[^84000toh563] A Tibetan text of this group, Toh 563 in the Degé Kangyur, is translated into English as *Great Upholder of the Secret Mantra*. Its translators treat the Sanskrit *Mahāmantrānusāriṇī* edited by Skilling as a different text for the corresponding goddess, with a name that differs from the one implied by the Tibetan titles, and they note that no reliable Sanskrit edition reflects the Tibetan translation.[^84000toh563] The Sanskrit work described here should therefore not be assumed to be the same as the Tibetan sūtra, although both belong to the same protective tradition.

Little further is documented about this Sanskrit version in the sources consulted: its date, authorship and manuscript witnesses are not discussed in the edition front matter available here, and no English title is established for it. Readers interested in the wider group of texts will find an overview of the Pañcarakṣā and of the Tibetan sūtra in the 84000 translation and its introduction.[^84000toh563]
