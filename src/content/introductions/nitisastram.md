The *Nītiśāstram* is a short Sanskrit work in verse on *nīti*, the practical conduct of life and of rulers, filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the *śāstrapiṭaka* in its *nīti* section.[^dsbc274] It opens with a salutation to the omniscient one (*sarvajñāya namaḥ*), and its first verses present it as a science of prudence meant to sharpen the understanding of people and to bring them well-being in this world.[^dsbc274]

The verses give brief maxims of the familiar *nīti* type: avoid anger, rely on the learned, speak truthfully, honour brahmins and cows, never despise an enemy, and avoid bad company, together with advice on how a ruler should treat and guard against his servants and ministers.[^dsbc274] I could not find scholarly discussion of the work in the sources available to me, so its author, date, place of composition and any Tibetan or Chinese translation remain undocumented here; the text's own opening verse names a speaker, but its reading is uncertain and is not relied upon. Neither digital edition names the printed edition or manuscript from which it was prepared, and the Devanagari and romanized versions are two renderings of one text.[^dsbc274][^dsbc491]
