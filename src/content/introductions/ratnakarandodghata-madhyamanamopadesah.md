The *Ratnakaraṇḍodghāṭa*, subtitled *Madhyamanāmopadeśa* ("instruction called the Middle"), is a short Sanskrit work classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the Madhyamaka-Yogācāra treatises (*śāstrapiṭaka*).[^dsbc892] The text opens with a homage to Bhaṭṭāraka Mañjuvajra and announces itself as an oral instruction (*avavāda*) of the lineage of Ācārya Nāgārjuna. It then describes the practitioner who, remembering the beginningless suffering of the world, renounces worldly activity, keeps the threefold vow of discipline, and seeks out a qualified teacher of that lineage; it cites verses attributed in the text to Nāgārjuna.[^dsbc892]

Little reliable scholarship on this work could be located in the sources consulted, so its author, date, place of composition, Tibetan or Chinese translations and manuscript tradition are not described here. The only edition on which this digital text rests is the 2018 printed edition by Dr. Penpā Dorje, issued by the Central Institute of Higher Tibetan Studies in Varanasi; the attribution of the teaching to a Nāgārjuna lineage is the text's own claim and should not be taken as an established attribution of authorship.[^dsbc892]
