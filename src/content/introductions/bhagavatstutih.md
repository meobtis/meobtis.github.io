The *Bhagavatstutiḥ* ("Praise of the Blessed One") is a short Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) addressed to the Buddha, whom it calls by epithets such as "bull among men" (*puruṣarṣabha*) and "knower of the six higher knowledges" (*ṣaḍabhijña*). The digital text comprises forty numbered verses and closes with a colophon, "iti bhagavatstutiḥ". It opens with the author's resolve to praise a small part of the qualities of an ocean of virtues, and closes by saying that no one in the world can reach the far shore of that ocean of qualities.[^dsbc743]

Very little is documented about the work. The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon records its printed source as an issue of the journal *Dhīḥ*, edited by Geshe Ngawang Samten and published in 2013 by the Central University of Tibetan Studies in Sarnath, Varanasi.[^dsbc743] Its bibliography record gives the page range 2–7 and lists no author.[^dsbcbib] The records name no author, date or manuscript, and searches did not locate a translation, a Tibetan or Chinese counterpart, or scholarly discussion of the text, so its authorship, date and tradition remain unknown here.
