The *Jñānaśrīmitranibandhāvali* ("Collected Treatises of Jñānaśrīmitra") is the title under which Anantalal Thakur edited the Sanskrit works of the Buddhist philosopher Jñānaśrīmitra, subtitled *Buddhist Philosophical Works of Jñānaśrīmitra*, in the Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series published in Patna in 1959.[^sarit-jna] The SARIT digital edition, keyed from this book, divides the collection into eleven numbered sections: the *Kṣaṇabhaṅgādhyāya*, *Vyāpticarcā*, *Bhedābhedaparīkṣā*, *Anupalabdhirahasya*, *Sarvaśabdābhāvacarcā*, *Apohaprakaraṇa*, *Īśvaravāda*, *Kāryakāraṇabhāvasiddhi*, *Yoginirṇayaprakaraṇa*, *Advaitabinduprakaraṇa* and *Sākārasiddhiśāstra*, followed by the *Sākārasaṃgrahasūtra* and an appendix of corrections and concordances (*saṃśodhana*).[^sarit-jna]

Jñānaśrīmitra is described in a general reference as flourishing around 975–1025 CE, as a gate-keeper scholar (*dvārapaṇḍita*) at Vikramaśīla, and as a thinker of the Buddhist epistemological tradition descending from Dignāga and Dharmakīrti. He is best known for defending the view that cognitive images (*ākāra*) are real (Sākāravāda), against Ratnākaraśānti, and for his own position of "variegated non-duality" (*citrādvaita*); he also wrote on exclusion (*apoha*).[^wiki-jna] The titles in the collection reflect these concerns: momentariness, pervasion, non-apprehension, the exclusion theory, the refutation of a creator god, causality and the doctrine of images.

Little more is documented about the collection itself in the sources consulted here. The SARIT file records two manuscript witnesses, one of them a second manuscript of the *Kṣaṇabhaṅgādhyāya*, both described in Thakur's preface, and notes that Thakur's editorial footnotes contain some irregularities that the encoders partly resolved.[^sarit-jna] Detailed scholarship on individual treatises, such as editions of the *Anupalabdhirahasya* and *Sarvaśabdābhāvacarcā* and studies of the *Apohaprakaraṇa*, exists but was not consulted for this introduction.[^wiki-jna]
