The *Bodhipathapradīpa*, usually rendered *A Lamp for the Path to Enlightenment*, is a short Buddhist text in verse by Atiśa (Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna), an eleventh-century teacher, and is described as his best-known work. It reconciles the doctrines of different Buddhist schools and sets out three levels of spiritual aspiration, which later became the basis of the Tibetan *lamrim* ("stages of the path") tradition.[^wiki-bpp] In Tibetan it is called *Byang chub lam gyi sgron ma*.[^wiki-bpp]

The verses open with homage to Mañjuśrī and a statement that the work is composed at the request of a disciple, and they distinguish three kinds of person: one who seeks only worldly happiness for himself, one who turns from worldly happiness and seeks only personal peace, and the supreme person, who is moved by his own suffering to end the suffering of all beings. The text then moves through refuge and the generation of the awakening mind, ethical discipline, concentration, wisdom and skilful means, and emptiness, and closes with tantric initiation.[^lionsroar] The Sanskrit text in these editions follows the same opening, with the three persons introduced in its first verses.

The work is attributed to Atiśa, named Dīpaṃkaraśrījñāna in its colophon.[^lionsroar] Tibetan sources reported in the research literature place its composition at Tholing shortly after his arrival in Tibet in 1042, and state that the original Sanskrit is lost, so that the Tibetan version is the main surviving witness; the Sanskrit in circulation is therefore a modern restoration, and its wording should be treated with caution.[^tsadra-sherburne] The digital editions here derive from Losang Norbu Shastri's 1984 Sanskrit edition. Little else about the Sanskrit editing history is documented in the sources consulted. English translations exist, including Richard Sherburne's *A Lamp for the Path and Commentary* (1983).[^tsadra-sherburne]
