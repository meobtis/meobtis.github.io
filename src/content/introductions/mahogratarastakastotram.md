The *Mahogratārāṣṭakastotram* is a hymn (*stotra*) of eight verses, as its title ("eight verses to Mahogratārā") indicates, addressed to the fierce goddess Ugratārā, here called Mahogratārā. The digital text is filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon under the stotras to Ugratārā, and its source is a printed anthology of Buddhist hymns edited by Janardan Shastri Pandey.[^dsbc201] The opening verses address the goddess as Nīlasarasvatī, "blue Sarasvatī", standing on a corpse and holding a sword, a chopper and a skull-bowl, and ask for protection and for the gift of eloquence.[^dsbc201]

Ugratārā is one of the best-known fierce forms of Tārā, alongside Ekajaṭā and Nīlasarasvatī; she is described as blue, tiger-skin-clad and standing on a corpse, and is worshipped in both Hindu and Buddhist tantric traditions, so the hymn belongs to a shared devotional world rather than to one school only.[^wiki-ugratara] Nothing reliable about the author, date or place of composition of this particular hymn could be found, and it was not possible to trace it to an earlier manuscript or to Tibetan or Chinese translations. Little about the work appears to be documented beyond the edition record, and it should be treated as a text of uncertain origin.
