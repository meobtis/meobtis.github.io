The *Madhyamakāloka* is a Sanskrit work of the Madhyamaka tradition attributed to Kamalaśīla, the Indian Buddhist scholar of the eighth century. The text opens with a verse and a prose passage stating that it is composed out of compassion to counter attachment to things, and then argues for the thesis that all phenomena lack intrinsic nature (*niḥsvabhāva*), addressing objections about the role of scripture and reasoning in establishing that thesis.[^gretil-mal]

Little detailed, verified information about the work could be gathered for this introduction. Tibetan tradition holds that Kamalaśīla wrote it in Tibet at the request of King Trisong Detsen, together with the first two of his *Bhāvanākrama* treatises; Birgit Kellner is reported to consider such claims worth taking seriously but not literally. The same source states that the *Madhyamakāloka* was not received in India itself until Abhayākaragupta's *Munimatālaṅkāra* in the twelfth century, and that Atiśa is said to have first found the work in Tibet and brought a copy back to India.[^wiki-kamalasila] These statements come from a general encyclopaedic article and should be checked against the scholarly literature it cites, including the entry on Kamalaśīla in *Brill's Encyclopedia of Buddhism*.
