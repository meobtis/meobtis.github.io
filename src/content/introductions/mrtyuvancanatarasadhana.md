The *Mṛtyuvañcanatārāsādhana* ("the sādhana of Tārā for cheating death") is a brief practice text in six Sanskrit verses, which opens with a salutation to White Tārā (*sitatārā*). It describes the visualisation of the goddess seated on a white lotus and a moon disc, in the vajra posture, holding a lotus in her gesture of giving, and having the form of a sixteen-year-old. A white wheel with eight spokes and eight syllables is then visualised in her heart. The practitioner is to meditate on it with one-pointed concentration and to recite the ten-syllable mantra *oṃ tāre tuttāre ture svāhā* for six months. The closing verse promises that the practitioner will not be tainted by the faults of death and will gain all accomplishments.[^gretil-mvts]

Little is documented about this particular text beyond its edition. The GRETIL record gives no author and no date, and states only that the text is taken from Johannes Schneider's 2010 book on Vāgīśvarakīrti's *Mṛtyuvañcanopadeśa*, a work on averting death published as a Sanskrit and Tibetan text with a German commentary.[^gretil-mvts][^gbooks-schneider] The sādhana opens with the words "as before, up to emptiness", which presupposes a preceding practice text that is not included here. The edition therefore presents it as a short supplement to that larger work. Its authorship, date and relation to the Tibetan tradition could not be established from the sources consulted and are left open.
