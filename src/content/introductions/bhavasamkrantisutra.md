The *Bhavasaṅkrāntisūtra* is a short sūtra of the early Mahāyāna that has been translated into English as "The Sūtra on Transmigration Through Existences". It consists of a prose section followed by seven verses, and its prose and verse parts differ in theme.[^84000toh226]

In the prose, King Bimbisāra asks the Buddha how an action performed long ago and since ceased can appear to the mind at the time of death, if all conditioned things are empty. The Buddha answers with the analogy of a dream, and explains rebirth without any self or phenomenon passing from one life to the next. The closing verses turn to emptiness and the two truths.[^84000toh226] The Sanskrit prose of the digital texts opens in the usual way, with the Buddha at the Bamboo Grove in Rājagṛha, and then moves directly to the king's question and the dream of a man with a beautiful woman.[^gretil-bhavasamkranti]

The date and authorship of the sūtra are not established. The 84000 introduction gives no composition date, but notes that the Tibetan translators were active in the eighth and ninth centuries and that the Denkarma and Phangthangma catalogues, from the early ninth century, list the work.[^84000toh226] There are three Chinese translations in the Taishō canon: T. 575 by Bodhiruci (sixth century), T. 576 by Buddhaśānta (sixth century) and T. 577 by Yijing (seventh to eighth century). They broadly agree in structure and meaning, although Bodhiruci's version uniquely says that one phenomenon transmigrates.[^84000toh226] The prose has a close parallel in the *Pitāputrasamāgamasūtra*, and the verses are quoted in many Kangyur and Tengyur works and resemble verses in three treatises attributed to Nāgārjuna.[^84000toh226]

For a long time no Sanskrit witness of the whole sūtra was known; the surviving Sanskrit was found in quotation, notably in Śāntideva's *Śikṣāsamuccaya*. A manuscript of twenty sūtras later found in the Potala Palace in Lhasa was edited and translated into English by Bhikṣuṇī Vinītā in 2010, although its origin and date are unknown.[^84000toh226] The two printed-edition-based digital texts described here derive from N. Aiyaswami Shastri's 1938 edition, published by the Adyar Library.[^gretil-bhavasamkranti][^dsbc37] Little further is documented about that edition in the sources consulted.
