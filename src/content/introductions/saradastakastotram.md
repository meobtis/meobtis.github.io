The *Śāradāṣṭakastotram* ("Eight-verse hymn to Śāradā") is a short Sanskrit devotional poem addressed to the goddess Śāradā, whom the text calls the venerable lady (*bhagavatī*). The Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon files it under the hymns (*stotra*) to goddesses and gives its source as Pandey's anthology of Buddhist hymns.[^dsbc137] The opening verses praise her as white like a conch, the moon, jasmine and snow, seated on a swan and adorned with fine garments and jewels, and as a deliverer of beings sunk in the ocean of saṃsāra; the refrain of each verse is a salutation to her.[^dsbc137] Later verses describe her as the one known in the world as Bhāratī, a mother who dispels the darkness of delusion, shown playing the vīṇā and holding a crystal rosary and a golden book.[^dsbc137]

Little reliable information about the hymn's author, date, place of composition or reception could be found in the sources consulted, and no translation or manuscript tradition is documented here. Its imagery (the swan, the vīṇā and the book) belongs to the iconography of Sarasvatī, the goddess of speech and learning, but this identification is an inference from the text and is not drawn from a cited study. The only edition information available is that given in the front matter of the digital texts: the Romanized and Devanāgarī versions are parallel files of the same work.[^dsbc137][^dsbc664]
