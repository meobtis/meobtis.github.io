The *Sarvatathāgatādhiṣṭhānavyūhasūtra* is a short Mahāyāna sūtra transmitted in Buddhist Sanskrit. The digital editions here reproduce the text as printed in Nalinaksa Dutt's edition of the Gilgit manuscripts (pp. 49–89 of volume I), which marks the page breaks of that edition within the text.[^gretil-sarvatathagata] The title can be rendered roughly as the "array of the empowerment of all the tathāgatas"; no established English title was found in the sources consulted.

According to the Sanskrit text itself, the sūtra opens on Mount Potalaka, in the abode of Avalokiteśvara, where the Buddha is assembled with monks, bodhisattvas, lay followers, gandharvas, yakṣiṇīs and the world-guardians. The Buddha enters a samādhi that lights up the world and relieves the suffering of beings. Mañjuśrī then urges Avalokiteśvara to request a teaching for the sake of beings in a degenerate later age. The Buddha speaks of a samādhi he once heard from an earlier tathāgata and describes the benefits of hearing, writing, reciting and honouring the discourse, including relief from illness, poverty and the obscurations of past actions, and rebirth in Sukhāvatī. The text goes on to give dhāraṇī and mantra phrases for protection and prosperity.[^gretil-sarvatathagata]

Little reliable information on the date, authorship, Tibetan or Chinese translations or reception of this work could be found in the sources consulted, so none is claimed here. The Sanskrit text comes from the Gilgit manuscripts, a collection of Buddhist texts found in the Gilgit region in the 1930s and dated on palaeographic grounds to roughly the fifth and sixth centuries CE, although later manuscripts were also grouped with them.[^unesco-gilgit] It is not stated in the edition records consulted which manuscript contains this sūtra or how it is dated.
