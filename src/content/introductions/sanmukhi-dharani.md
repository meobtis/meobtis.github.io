The *Ṣaṇmukhī dhāraṇī* (in full, the *Ārya-ṣaṇmukhī-nāma-dhāraṇī*, "the dhāraṇī called Ṣaṇmukhī") is a very short Sanskrit Buddhist text. In the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon it is classed among the dhāraṇī texts of the *sūtrapiṭaka*, and the printed source named for it is the Journal of Indian and Buddhist Studies (Tokyo, 1977), with Katsumi Mimaki as editor.[^dsbc19] Its title is a Sanskrit compound meaning "six-faced"; no established English title was found.

The text opens in the manner of a sūtra, with the Buddha dwelling in a jewelled array above the Śuddhāvāsa heavens among a great assembly of bodhisattvas. He urges them to take up the dhāraṇī for the benefit of all beings. A series of aspirations follows, asking that the sufferings of saṃsāra not befall the speaker, that worldly happiness be shared with all beings, that unwholesome karma and Māra's works be dispelled, and that wholesome roots be turned towards the knowledge of all beings. A mantra-like formula then follows, beginning *oṃ kṣame kṣame*, and the text closes with the promise that anyone who recites it three times by night and three by day will exhaust their karmic obstructions and swiftly attain supreme awakening.[^dsbc19]

Little reliable information on its date, authorship, transmission or translations into Tibetan or Chinese could be found in the sources consulted, so none is claimed here. The two digital editions give only the bibliographic details and credits recorded above.[^dsbc19][^dsbc390]
