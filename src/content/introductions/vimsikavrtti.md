The *Viṃśikāvṛtti* is the prose commentary on the *Twenty Verses* (*Viṃśikākārikā*), a short work attributed to Vasubandhu that defends the Yogācāra thesis that what appears to us is representation-only (*vijñaptimātra*). The verses open with the claim that everything in the three realms is only appearance, and the work is often regarded as among the most readable and analytical of Vasubandhu's philosophical texts.[^gold2021]

In the argument as summarised in the *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, Vasubandhu first answers objections to the idea that mental appearances can lack external objects, such as why they are restricted in place and time, why several beings may share them, and why they have causal effects. He replies with examples drawn from dreams and from the shared experiences of beings in hell, then argues that accounts of physical sense-objects are less parsimonious than the representation-only view, and reads scriptural statements about the sense bases as teachings given with a special intention.[^gold2021] Vasubandhu is generally dated to the fourth or fifth century CE.[^gold2021]

Little further about the transmission of this text could be verified from the sources consulted. Many of Vasubandhu's works survive only in Chinese and Tibetan translation,[^gold2021] and the details of translations and printed Sanskrit editions of this work are not documented here.
