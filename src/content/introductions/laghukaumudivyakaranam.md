The *Laghukaumudī*, also called *Laghusiddhāntakaumudī*, is a short Sanskrit grammar in the Pāṇinian tradition. The text opens with an invocation of Sarasvatī and a statement that the author composes it as an entrance to Pāṇini's system, and it then begins with the *Māheśvara* sūtras and the chapter on technical terms (*saṃjñāprakaraṇa*).[^dsbc881] It follows the method of the *Siddhāntakaumudī* of Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita, which rearranges Pāṇini's rules under topical heads instead of following the order of the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*.[^wiki-sk]

The work is traditionally ascribed to Varadarāja, a pupil of Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita, who is reported to have composed an abridged *Siddhāntakaumudī*.[^wiki-sk] Beyond this, little reliable information on its date, structure or transmission could be found in the sources consulted, and it is not a Buddhist work in origin; it appears among the grammatical texts of the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon.[^dsbc881] The edition behind both digital texts is that of James R. Ballantyne, reprinted by Motilal Banarsidass in 1976.[^dsbc881]
