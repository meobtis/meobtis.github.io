The *Vidyākṣarastotram* ("Hymn of the Knowledge Syllables") is a very short Sanskrit stotra of seven verses. It is classed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon among the hymns attributed to Caityabhaṭṭāraka, and the colophon calls it the *vidyākṣarastotra* of the "venerable Buddhabhaṭṭāraka".[^dsbc141] No established English title for the work was found.

The verses praise the five Tathāgatas of the maṇḍala in turn: Vairocana in the centre on a lion throne, Akṣobhya in the east on an elephant, Ratnasaṃbhava in the south on a horse, Amitābha in the west on a peacock and Amoghasiddhi in the north on a Garuḍa. Each is said to arise from a seed syllable (such as *oṃ*, *hūṃ* and *trāṃ*), with a colour and a hand gesture. The hymn then names four goddesses (Rocanā, Māmakī, Pāṇḍarā and Tārā) at the corners, and ends with homage to Vajrasattva within the caitya.[^dsbc141] Little reliable information on its author, date or transmission could be found in the sources consulted, so these are left unstated here.
