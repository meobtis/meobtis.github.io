The *Chandoratnākara* is a Sanskrit work on prosody, the science of metres (*chandas*). The edition in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon presents it as a work of Ratnākaraśānti (*Ratnākaraśāntikṛtaḥ chandoratnākaraḥ*), in verse with a prose commentary (*vṛtti*), and its title page describes it as "accompanied by the author's own commentary" (*svopajña samanvitaḥ*).[^dsbc108] The DSBC files it under the heading of poetics (*alaṃkāra*) within the treatises (*śāstrapiṭaka*).[^dsbc108] No English title for the work appears to be established.

The opening of the commentary explains the author's purpose: to teach readers how to compose and understand verse. It criticises the earlier metrical works of Piṅgala and Jayadeva for lacking, among other things, concision, clear definitions of heavy and light syllables, and a convenient order of presentation, and it claims that the new treatise remedies these faults.[^dsbc108] The first chapter then defines the verse (*padya*) as a four-quarter unit divided into syllable-counted metres (*vṛtta*) and mora-counted metres (*jāti*), and goes on to the classification of vowels and consonants and of light and heavy syllables.[^dsbc108]

Ratnākaraśānti is generally identified as the Buddhist scholar of Vikramaśīla who was active from the late tenth to the mid eleventh century and who wrote on Prajñāpāramitā, tantra and epistemology.[^wiki-ratnakarashanti] Whether this prosodist is the same person is not discussed in the sources consulted here, and little else about the date, transmission or translations of this treatise could be found.
