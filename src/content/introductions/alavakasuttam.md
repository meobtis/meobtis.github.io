The *Ālavakasūttam* on this site is a Sanskrit version of the Āḷavaka Sutta, a short discourse of the Pāli canon. Its own heading describes it as a word-for-word Sanskrit rendering made from the original Pāli, and it is not a Sanskrit Buddhist sūtra transmitted in that language.[^dsbc893] The discourse is found in the Sutta Nipāta (Sn 1.10, verses 181–192) and, identically, in the Saṃyutta Nikāya (SN 10.12).[^thanissaro] No established English title for this Sanskrit version was found; the Pāli discourse is usually called the Āḷavaka Sutta, "Discourse to Āḷavaka".

The narrative has the Buddha staying at Āḷavī in the haunt of the yakkha (a spirit) Āḷavaka. Three times the yakkha orders him out and then back in, and the Buddha complies; the fourth time he declines and invites the yakkha to put his questions, after the yakkha has threatened to unsettle his mind, tear open his heart or hurl him across the Ganges. The questions concern the best wealth, how to cross the flood and the sea, how to overcome suffering, how to be purified, and how to gain discernment, wealth, honour and friends and avoid grief after death. In reply the Buddha names conviction, heedfulness, persistence and discernment, and truth, self-control, endurance and generosity. The yakkha declares himself convinced and vows to wander from place to place honouring the Buddha and the Dhamma.[^thanissaro]

According to the commentarial tradition summarised in a dictionary of Pāli proper names, Āḷavaka was a man-eating yakkha who lived near Āḷavī and was supplied with a human victim at intervals, until the Buddha was sent to him when the turn came to the king's son. The commentaries are said to add that the questions were handed down from the earlier Buddha Kassapa. The same entry notes that the discourse belongs to the Pāli *Paritta* collection of protective texts.[^palikanon] That narrative frame is later commentarial material rather than part of the discourse itself.

Nothing could be verified about who made this Sanskrit rendering beyond the information in the digital edition. The front matter of the text names Padmanābha Śāstrī and Trivandrum, and the record gives 1960, but it does not identify the publication.[^dsbc893]
