The *Caṇḍamahāroṣaṇa tantra* ("The Tantra of the Fierce Great Wrath") is a Buddhist tantra of the Unexcelled Yoga class, listed as Toh 431 in the Degé Kangyur. It is presented as revealed teaching: the Lord Vajrasattva speaks in the form of Acala to his consort Vajradhātvīśvarī, and the human author is anonymous.[^84000toh431] The opening lines of the digital text show this setting, with Vajrasattva dwelling among a retinue of vajra-yogins and yoginīs and uttering a verse on the four joys.[^dsbc762]

The soteriological part of the work, roughly its first sixteen chapters, is centred on the four joys. After the opening chapters on the introduction, the maṇḍala, empowerments and the deity, it covers development-stage and completion-stage practice, and later chapters turn to mantras, magical rites (above all rites of enthralment), materia medica and unusual practices. Chapter 13 treats inverted conduct.[^84000toh431] The deity is the wrathful Acala, who also appears in earlier literature such as the *Acalakalpa*, and the tantra is a primary source for his iconography.[^84000toh431][^wiki-acala]

The translator's introduction to the English version classes the text as a late Yoginītantra and dates it to about the tenth or eleventh century, with evidence pointing to Nepal, probably a Newar milieu. It draws on the *Guhyasamāja*, *Hevajra* and *Siddhaikavīra* tantras and on Āryadeva's *Cittaviśuddhiprakaraṇa*, and it influenced the *Vidyādharavinoda*.[^84000toh431]

More than a hundred Sanskrit manuscripts are said to survive, all copied in Nepal, the oldest being a palm-leaf manuscript of 1380 in the Royal Asiatic Society, London. The only known Tibetan translation, found in all major Kangyur editions, is credited in the Degé to Trakpa Gyaltsen and the Indian scholar Ratnaśrī. The introduction mentions no Chinese version. The only known commentary is the *Padmāvatī* by Mahāsukhavajra, which was never translated into Tibetan. Christopher Starr George translated chapters 1–8 into English (1974), and Gäng published a German translation (1981).[^84000toh431]
