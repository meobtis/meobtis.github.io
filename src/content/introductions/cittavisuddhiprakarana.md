The *Cittaviśuddhiprakaraṇa* ("Treatise on the Purification of the Mind") is a short Sanskrit work in verse, numbering 134 verses in the digital edition, which names Āryadeva as its author in the closing colophon.[^gretil-cvp] It opens by praising a buddha beyond duality and concept, then promises to explain, "from the observation of one's own mind", the doctrine of the Yogācāra.[^gretil-cvp] The text is concerned with the purification of mind, stressing non-duality, emptiness and the illusory nature of conceptual distinctions, within a Mahāyāna framework that also contains tantric elements such as mantra, deity yoga and maṇḍala practice. It also criticises ritual purity, ritual bathing and caste distinctions.[^gretil-cvp]

Little reliable information on the work could be gathered beyond the edition records. The Āryadeva to whom tantric works are attributed is generally distinguished from the third-century Madhyamaka author of that name, and recent research is reported to date such tantric works to the late ninth or early tenth century.[^wiki-aryadeva] Whether this text belongs to that tantric author, and its Tibetan and Chinese translations, were not verified here and are left undocumented. The Sanskrit is known through Patel's 1949 edition, on which both digital texts are based.[^gretil-cvp][^dsbc354]
