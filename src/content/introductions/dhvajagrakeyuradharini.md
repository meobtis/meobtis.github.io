The *Dhvajāgrakeyūrādhāriṇī* ("Dhāraṇī of the Banner-Top Armlet") is a short Buddhist dhāraṇī, a spell-like formula used for protection. In the longer of the two Sanskrit versions available here, the Buddha is staying among the gods of the Thirty-Three on the Pāṇḍukambala rock when Śakra, ruler of the gods, comes to him after being defeated by the asura lord Vemacitrin and asks for a means of protection. The Buddha answers with the dhāraṇī, which the text calls invincible (*aparājitā*).[^gretil-alt]

The formula calls upon a goddess, addresses her as three-eyed and four-faced, and asks her to crush enemies and hostile armies. The prose frame promises victory in battle, quarrels and disputes to whoever carries it, and instructs that it be tied to a banner or worn at the neck. A short heart-mantra (*hṛdaya*) follows in the same manuscript.[^gretil-alt] The other text consists of the dhāraṇī formula itself, opening with homage to the Three Jewels and to the Tathāgata Dhvajāgrarāja, and also contains an appeal to the truth of the Buddha, Dharma and Saṅgha.[^gretil-dhvaja] Little reliable secondary literature on this text could be consulted, so its date, authorship, Tibetan and Chinese translations and reception are not described here.
