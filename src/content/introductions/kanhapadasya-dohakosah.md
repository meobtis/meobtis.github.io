The Kāṇhapādasya dohākoṣaḥ is a short collection of dohās, couplets in an Apabhraṃśa vernacular, attributed to Kāṇhapāda (Kāṇha, Kṛṣṇācārya), one of the Buddhist siddhas. In the digital edition each verse is followed by an explanation in Sanskrit that begins with a formula such as "asyāyamarthaḥ" ("this is its meaning"), glossing the vernacular words and sometimes quoting tantric scriptures in support.[^dsbc344] The first verse, for example, speaks of those who boast of knowing the ultimate truth, and the commentary glosses the "stainless" (*nirañjana*) state as the innate body free of passion, aversion and other afflictions.

Little reliable information about this work could be gathered. Kāṇhapāda is also named as the author of thirteen songs in the Old Bengali Caryāpada collection, and the Tibetan translation of dohās of this kind has been studied by P. C. Bagchi, who published a Tibetan version containing fifty verses in 1938.[^wiki-charyapada] The date of the author, the authorship of the Sanskrit commentary, and the relationship of this text to the Tibetan translation are not established in the sources consulted here, so they are left unstated.
