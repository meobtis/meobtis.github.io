# Pratītyasamutpādamahāyānasūtra

*(**Vaidya 119**)* The sūtra of the Great Vehicle, by name Ārya `pratītyasamutpāda` (Contingent Coarising). Thus heard by me. At one time the Bhagavān was dwelling in the midst of the Trāyastriṃśa devas on the stone slab resembling a pale woollen blanket, together with the great śrāvakas headed by Jitāśva; the bodhisattvas, mahāsattvas headed by Āryamaitreya, Avalokiteśvara, and Vajrahasta, adorned with immeasurable jewels of virtues; the devas headed by Mahābrahmā Sahāmpati, Nārāyaṇa, and Maheśvara; Śakra, Indra of the devas; and Pañcaśikha, king of the gandharvas. Then the bodhisattva, mahāsattva Avalokiteśvara, rising from his seat, arranging his upper robe over one shoulder, resting his right knee on the surface of Meru, and bowing with joined palms in the direction of the Bhagavān, said this to the Bhagavān: "Indeed, Bhagavān, these devas, having homage to shrines as their adornment, assembled in the circle of this assembly, somehow generating the `puṇya` (merit) of `brahmacarya` (the optimal life), and in the world with its devas, Māras, and Brahmās, among generations with their `śramaṇabrāhmaṇa` (religious practitioners), the bhikṣus, bhikṣuṇīs, upāsakas, and upāsikās generating abundant merit, request a teaching of the Dharma from the Bhagavān." Then the Bhagavān spoke the verse of contingent coarising to them.

Whatever phenomena arise from a `hetu` (primary cause), the Tathāgata indeed has declared their primary cause,  
and whatever is their `nirodha` (cessation); thus speaks the great śramaṇa.

Namely, Avalokiteśvara, this pratītyasamutpāda is the Dharma-body of the Tathāgatas. He who sees pratītyasamutpāda sees the Tathāgata. And, Avalokiteśvara, whatever kulaputra or daughter of a good family, endowed with `śraddhā` (confidence), having made in an unestablished region of the earth a shrine the size of an āmalaka fruit, a `bodhi` (awakening) tree the size of a needle, and a parasol the size of a bakula flower, recites the verse of the Dharma `dhātu` (component) of contingent coarising, that person produces the puṇya of Brahmā. Having passed away from here and met the time of death, that person is reborn in the world of Brahmā. Having passed away from there and completed their time, that person is reborn in the company of the devas of the Pure Abode host.

Thus spoke the Bhagavān. And all those śrāvakas, and those bodhisattvas, mahāsattvas, and that entire assembly, and the world with its devas, humans, asuras, and gandharvas, rejoiced in what was spoken by the Bhagavān.

The sūtra of the Great Vehicle, by name Ārya Contingent Coarising.
