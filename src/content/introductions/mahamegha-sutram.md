The *Mahāmegha sūtra* in this edition is a brief Mahāyāna discourse of the dhāraṇī-sūtra type. It opens with the Buddha staying in the palace of the nāga kings Nanda and Upananda, where a very long list of nāga kings gathers and makes vast offerings, and one nāga sovereign then asks how all nāgas might be freed from suffering so that rain falls in season and crops grow in Jambudvīpa.[^dsbc47] The Buddha answers that nāgas who dwell in loving-kindness (*maitrī*) are relieved of suffering, and he then gives a dhāraṇī named as the bestower of all happiness, which is to be recited and retained.[^dsbc47]

The text appears to correspond to the short rainmaking sūtra preserved in the Tibetan Kangyur as *The Great Cloud (2)* (Toh 235), which 84000 describes as an addendum to the longer *Great Cloud* (Toh 232) and as having been associated by Indian Buddhists with rainmaking. According to 84000, that text was translated into Chinese four times between the sixth and eighth centuries, was catalogued in the early ninth-century Denkarma, survives in five Dunhuang manuscripts, and is partly preserved in Sanskrit.[^84000toh235] The identification of the Sanskrit text given here with Toh 235 rests on matching content (the nāga setting and the rain dhāraṇī) and has not been confirmed in a scholarly source consulted; its date, authorship and the history of the Sanskrit manuscript are not documented in the sources found.
