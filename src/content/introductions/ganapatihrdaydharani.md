The *Gaṇapatihṛdaydhāraṇī* is a very short Buddhist Sanskrit text. In its own closing words it is the *Āryamahāgaṇapatihṛdaya*, the "heart" of the noble Great Gaṇapati.[^gretil-ganapati] It opens with the usual salutation and the "Thus have I heard" formula, set at Rājagṛha on Vulture Peak, and then presents the Buddha addressing Ānanda.

The teaching consists of a sequence of mantras that invoke Gaṇapati, the "lord of the hosts", through repeated formulae ending in *svāhā*, followed by a promise of benefits: the success of undertakings such as mantra practice, worship of the Three Jewels or travel, and a favourable reception at court, among other benefits listed for those who recite it seven times.[^gretil-ganapati]

Little reliable information on the origin, date or transmission of this text could be gathered for this introduction, so its authorship and date are left unstated. The GRETIL header refers readers to a translation by R. Duquenne, "Gaṇapati Rituals in Chinese" (*Bulletin de l'École française d'Extrême-Orient*, 77, 1988), which suggests that the text or related material also exists in Chinese; this has not been verified here.[^gretil-ganapati]
