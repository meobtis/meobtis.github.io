The *Madhyamārthasaṃgraha* ("Summary of the Meaning of the Middle") is a very short Sanskrit work in verse, filed in the Digital Sanskrit Buddhist Canon (DSBC) among the Madhyamaka treatises. The digital text consists of eleven verses preceded by an opening salutation to Samantabhadra and a verse of homage.[^dsbc244] No established English title for it was found, and very little independent documentation of the work could be located in the sources consulted.

According to the text itself, its aim is to make the two truths, the ultimate (*paramārtha*) and the conventional (*saṃvṛti*), understood. The verses divide the ultimate truth into kinds and describe it as free from conceptual elaboration (*niṣprapañca*), beyond the four alternatives (*catuṣkoṭi*) and beyond origination from the four kinds of conditions. They then divide the conventional into the true or functional (*tathya*) and the false or mistaken (*mithyābhūta*), the latter being illustrated by the perception of two moons. The ninth verse is incomplete in the DSBC text, which marks the gap with dots.[^dsbc244]

The closing colophon of the text attributes it to the teacher (*ācārya*) Bhavya, a name that probably refers to the Madhyamaka author known as Bhāviveka or Bhavya. It also says that the work was translated, revised and finalised by the translator (*lotsāva*) Śīlarāja, which suggests a connection with the Tibetan translation tradition.[^dsbc244] These statements are taken from the colophon alone; no independent confirmation of the attribution, the date or the translation was found, and they should be treated with caution.
