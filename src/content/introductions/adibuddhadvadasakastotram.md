The *Ādibuddhadvādaśakastotram* is a brief Sanskrit hymn (*stotra*) addressed to the Ādibuddha, the "primordial Buddha". It opens with a salutation to the Ādibuddha and consists of twelve verses of homage, each ending in *namaḥ*, followed by a thirteenth verse promising the reward of reciting the hymn at dawn.[^dsbc109] No established English title was found in the sources consulted; the title may be rendered "Twelve-Verse Hymn to the Ādibuddha".

The verses salute the Ādibuddha as the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṃgha, as the five Buddhas, as the four elements and space, as Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Maheśa, as the senses and their objects, and as time, birth, old age and death, ending with a salutation to him as nirvāṇa and as the many-formed.[^dsbc109] The colophon names Mañjuśrī as the composer.[^dsbc109] Beyond what the edition itself says, little about the hymn's date, provenance, manuscripts or translations could be found; the sources consulted yielded only catalogue records, and a similarly titled twelve-deed praise in Tibetan tradition is a different work. It is not otherwise documented here and its authorship should be treated as a traditional attribution.
