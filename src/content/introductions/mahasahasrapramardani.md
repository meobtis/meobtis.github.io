The *Mahāsāhasrapramardanī* ("She Who Crushes the Great Thousand") is a Buddhist protective text of the kind known as a *dhāraṇī* sūtra, and it is counted as the first of the five texts of the Pañcarakṣā, the "Five Protectresses". The Tibetan version is catalogued as Toh 558 in the Degé Kangyur, among the action (*kriyā*) tantras, and has been translated into English as *Destroyer of the Great Trichiliocosm*.[^84000toh558] Some lists of the Pañcarakṣā give it second place, after the *Mahāpratisarā*.[^wiki-pancaraksa]

The text opens in the usual sūtra manner, with the Buddha staying at Rājagṛha with a large assembly of monks. It then moves to Vaiśālī, which is struck by calamities, and the Licchavis and other inhabitants are afflicted by spirits.[^iwamoto1937] According to the 84000 summary, the work deals with illnesses attributed to spirit beings (*graha* and *bhūta*) that drain the vitality of people and animals. These beings are grouped under the four great kings, and the Buddha teaches incantations and rituals to prevent or cure the afflictions. The same summary notes that the text also contains monastic rules on avoiding alms tainted by the "five impure foods", generally understood as meat, because eating them is said to weaken the incantations.[^84000toh558]

No author is known, and the sources consulted here give no firm date or place of composition. The Tibetan translation was made in the early ninth century under imperial patronage by Bandé Yeshé Dé with the Indian preceptors Śīlendrabodhi, Jñānasiddhi and Śākyaprabha, and the Degé edition was later re-edited by Gö Lotsāwa Zhönnu Pal (1392–1481).[^84000toh558] The 84000 translation, by James Gentry for the Dharmachakra Translation Committee, was made mainly from the Degé Tibetan and compared with Iwamoto's 1937 Sanskrit edition.[^84000toh558]

Iwamoto's Kyoto edition is the printed Sanskrit text on which the digital version rests.[^gretil-mps] Little else about the text's manuscript tradition, Chinese translations or reception could be verified from the sources consulted, so this introduction is brief.
